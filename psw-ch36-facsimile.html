A disability is any loss (or absence) of physical or mental function (see Chapter 6). A developmental disability is a permanent disability that a person is born with or that begins before 18 years of age. The term disability is becoming increasingly objectionable to many people, who urge replacing that word with one that is considered to have a less negative connotation. However, because governments and insurers still use this term in their benefit descriptions, it is the term we will continue to use within this chapter.
残疾是指身体或心理功能的任何丧失（或缺失）（见第 6 章）。发育迟缓残疾（developmental disability）是个体先天罹患或在 18 岁之前发生的永久性残疾。对于许多人而言，“残疾（disability）”一词正变得越来越令人反感，他们敦促使用一个负面涵义较少的词来取代它。然而，由于政府机构和保险公司在其法定福利描述中仍在使用该术语，因此我们在本章中将继续沿用这一概念。
Developmental disabilities and disorders can be caused by various conditions, illnesses, accidents (e.g., poisoning, trauma), diseases, or developmental (genetic) disorders (e.g., cerebral palsy, Down syndrome) that injure or affect the normal development of the brain or body before birth, during birth, or during childhood or adolescence. Many disabilities begin in adulthood as a result of diseases, medical conditions, and injuries.
发育迟缓残疾和障碍可由在出生前、分娩期间或儿童期/青春期阶段损伤或干扰大脑及身体正常发育的各种状况、疾病、事故（如中毒、创伤）、器质性病变或发育（遗传）障碍（如脑瘫、唐氏综合征）引起。许多其他类型的残疾则是在成年期因后天疾病、身体疾患以及外伤所致。
Developmental disorders and disabilities can be mild, moderate, or severe. They can affect physical function, mental function, or both, limiting the person’s ability to function in at least three of the following life activities:
发育障碍和发育迟缓残疾可分为轻度、中度或重度。它们可能损害身体功能、心理功能或两者兼有，限制个体在以下至少三项日常关键生活活动中的功能表现：
  • • Self-care (e.g., eating, dressing, hygiene)
    • 自我照料（例如进食、穿衣、个人卫生与如厕）
  • • Understanding and expressing speech and language
    • 理解与表达言语及语言交流
  • • Learning
    • 认知与知识技能学习
  • • Mobility (i.e., getting around independently)
    • 身体移动能力（即独立走动或转移位移）
  • • Self-direction (e.g., solving problems, making choices and decisions)
    • 自我主导与自我定向（例如解决问题、进行自主选择和决策）
  • • Independent living
    • 社区独立生活能力
  • • Economic self-sufficiency (i.e., earning enough income to support oneself financially)
    • 经济自立能力（即赚取足够收入在经济上维持自我生存）
This chapter will discuss the causes of the most common developmental disorders and disabilities and how they affect an individual’s functioning. As with all other clients, it is important to understand that while one client with a developmental disorder may demonstrate certain abilities or require certain types of care, another client with the same type of developmental disorder may be very different. Most clients with developmental disorders and disabilities need lifelong assistance, support, and special services, which are provided by the health care team involved in the client’s care.
本章将深入探讨最常见的发育障碍与残疾的病因及其如何影响个人的生理与社会功能。与其他所有服务对象一样，至关重要的是要认识到：即使患有同一种发育障碍，一位服务对象所展现的能力或所需的照护方式可能与另一位患者截然不同。大多数患有发育障碍与残疾的服务对象需要全生命周期的终身协助、支持与专门服务，这些均由参与照护的跨专业医疗护理团队提供。

CARING FOR A CLIENT WHO IS LIVING WITH A DEVELOPMENTAL DISABILITY OR DISORDER

照料患有发育迟缓残疾或障碍的服务对象
Clients who are living with developmental disorders and disabilities often have complex care needs, especially if their disabilities are severe. As a support worker, you must be familiar with special equipment and self-help devices needed for each client. Follow the client’s care plan and consult with your supervisor if you have questions.
生活在发育障碍和残疾中的服务对象通常具有复杂的照护需求，特别是当残疾程度严重时更是如此。作为支持工作者，您必须熟悉每位服务对象所需的专用设备与自理辅助工具。请严格遵循照护计划，如有疑问及时向主管请示。

For example, Gordon, age 10, has severe cerebral palsy. He cannot walk and has limited use of his hands. He tends to slide down in his wheelchair, so a postural support is used to help keep him in good body alignment. He must be repositioned frequently to prevent pressure ulcers from developing. Gordon also has a hearing impairment and a speech disorder; he wears hearing aids in both ears and uses a computer to communicate. Gordon has splints on his ankles to prevent contractures. He uses a special spoon strapped to his wrist to help him eat.

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) causes inappropriate levels of inattention, as well as hyperactive and impulsive behaviour. Fragile X syndrome
脆性X染色体综合征
The most common inherited cause of mental impairment and the most common known cause of autism spectrum disorder, fragile X syndrome is a genetic condition caused by changes in the X chromosome that prevent the chromosome from making the protein that it normally does. Some individuals are carriers who have a small defect in the gene (called permutation) and show no symptoms. One in 259 women carry the fragile X gene, and there is a 50% chance of her passing it on to her children; about 1 in 800 men carry the fragile X gene and pass it on to all of their daughters but not to their sons. Most males with full mutation will have developmental disabilities. Common symptoms include: mental impairment ranging from learning disabilities to developmental disabilities, ADHD, anxiety and unstable mood, autistic behaviour, long face, large ears, flat feet, hyperextension of joints (especially fingers), and seizures (affecting about 25%).
作为最常见的智力缺陷遗传病因以及自闭症谱系障碍最明确的致病因，脆性X综合征是由于X染色体基因突变导致无法正常合成所需蛋白质引起的。部分人群为突变基因携带者且无明显临床症状。女性携带者有50%概率遗传给下一代；男性携带者则会遗传给其所有女儿，但不会遗传给儿子。男性全突变患者绝大多数伴有发育残疾。常见临床表现包括：从学习困难到重度发育迟缓的智力受损、注意力缺陷多动（ADHD）、焦虑与情绪不稳定、自闭样孤僻行为、长面容、大耳朵、扁平足、关节（尤其是手指）过度过伸、以及癫痫发作（约占25%）。

Hydrocephalus is a condition in which fluid collects in the brain (hydro means 'water,' and cephalo means 'head'). While ADHD has always been considered a children’s disorder, increasingly, in the past 25 years, adults, too, have been diagnosed with and medicated for this condition. It is believed that an adult who is newly diagnosed with ADHD likely had the disorder in childhood but it went undiagnosed. Although symptoms can be caused by other events, such as a head injury, hyperthyroidism (see Chapter 33), or seizure disorders, true ADHD appears during early childhood and may be diagnosed even before age 7 years. It used to be thought that the majority of children ultimately grew out of ADHD before reaching adulthood. However, it is now known that these disorders often do not disappear as children mature.
虽然过去人们一直将 ADHD 视为仅存在于儿童期的障碍，但在过去的 25 年中，越来越多的成年人也被确诊并接受药物治疗。临床普遍认为，新近确诊为 ADHD 的成年人很可能在童年期就已患有该障碍，只是当时未被及时诊断。尽管某些症状可能由其他因素引起（如头部外伤、甲状腺功能亢进症[见第 33 章]或癫痫发作），但真正的 ADHD 通常始发于幼儿期，甚至在 7 岁前即可确诊。过去曾误以为大多数儿童成年后会“自愈”，但现代医学证实，随着年龄增长，这些神经发育障碍往往并不会自行消失。
ADHD symptoms exist long term, occur in more than one environment, and are severe enough to impair the affected person’s everyday functioning. Hyperactive people often appear as if they need to move. Therefore, they are almost constantly moving and frequently make excessive noise. People who have chronic difficulties in maintaining focus or completing tasks may struggle academically and socially.
ADHD 的症状长期持续存在，且表现在多重生活环境（如学校、家庭、工作单位）中，其严重程度足以损害当事人的日常社会功能。多动型个体通常表现出一种不可遏制的身体运动冲动；因此，他们几乎时刻处于活动状态，并且经常发出过大的声响。而在保持专注或完成指定任务方面存在长期慢性困难的人，往往在学业、职场与人际交往中面临沉重挫折。

AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (ASD)

孤独症谱系障碍（自闭症，ASD）
Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental disorder that impairs a person’s ability to communicate and interact with others. It is called a spectrum disorder because it affects each person differently and in varying degrees. Symptoms can range from mild to severe. In Canada, approximately 1 in 66 children and youth aged 5 to 17 years has been diagnosed with ASD.
孤独症谱系障碍（ASD/自闭症）是一种严重损害个体与他人沟通和社交互动能力的神经发育障碍。之所以被称为“谱系（spectrum）”障碍，是因为它对每个人的影响方式和受累程度千差万别。其症状表现从轻度到极重度不等。在加拿大，5 至 17 岁的儿童和青少年中，大约每 66 人中就有 1 人被确诊为自闭症。
Symptoms of ASD typically appear in early childhood—often between 12 and 36 months of age. While the exact cause of ASD is unknown, genetic and environmental factors are believed to play a role. ASD is four times more common in boys than in girls.
自闭症的症状通常在幼儿早期显现——通常在 12 至 36 个月之间。虽然自闭症的确切病因尚不完全明了，但医学界认为遗传易感性与环境因素共同起到了关键作用。在性别分布上，男孩的患病率是女孩的四倍。

Characteristics of Autism Spectrum Disorder

孤独症谱系障碍的核心临床特征
Children and adults with ASD commonly display difficulties in two main areas: (1) social communication and social interaction, and (2) restricted, repetitive patterns of behaviour, interests, or activities.
患有自闭症的儿童和成人通常在两大核心领域表现出显著障碍：(1) 社交沟通与人际社交互动；(2) 局限、刻板重复的行为模式、兴趣或活动。
  • • Social communication and interaction challenges: Avoiding eye contact; difficulty interpreting nonverbal cues (such as facial expressions and body language); delayed speech or lack of speech; difficulty holding conversations; repeating words or phrases verbatim (echolalia); and difficulty making friends or understanding others’ perspectives.
    • 社交沟通与互动障碍：主动回避眼神接触；难以解读面部表情和肢体语言等非语言社交线索；语言发育迟缓或完全缺乏语言表达能力；难以维持对话；机械重复听到的词语或短语（模仿言语/鹦鹉学舌）；难以结交朋友或无法理解他人的情感视角。
  • • Repetitive behaviours and restricted interests: Engaging in repetitive movements (such as hand flapping, rocking, or spinning); insisting on strict, identical routines and becoming extremely distressed by minor changes; intense, obsessive focus on specific topics; unusual sensory sensitivities (such as extreme distress at loud sounds, bright lights, or certain textures); and lack of imaginative or pretend play.
    • 刻板重复行为与狭隘局限兴趣：反复做出刻板动作（如拍手、摇晃身体或转圈）；强烈固守刻板一致的作息路线，微小的环境变动即可引发极度痛苦与情绪崩溃；对特定物件或狭隘主题表现出强烈的执念沉迷；异常的感官知觉反应（如对刺耳噪音、强光或特定衣服质地极度过敏痛苦）；缺乏象征性或假装性的想象力游戏能力。

Supporting a Client Who Is Living With Autism Spectrum Disorder

支持与照料患有孤独症谱系障碍的服务对象
There is no cure for ASD, but early behavioural, educational, and speech therapy interventions can significantly improve functioning and quality of life. As a support worker, your role is to provide consistent, calm, and structured care tailored to the client’s unique needs. Follow these guidelines:
自闭症尚无治愈方法，但早期的行为干预、结构化特教训练与言语康复治疗能显著改善其自理能力与生活质量。作为支持工作者，您的职责是根据服务对象的独特需求，提供恒定、冷静且高度结构化的专业照护。请遵循以下实操准则：
  • • Maintain a predictable, structured environment. Changes in routine can cause severe anxiety; notify the client well in advance of any schedule changes.
    • 维持可预测的结构化生活环境：日常秩序的突然改变会引发强烈的惊恐与焦虑；如有任何行程调整，必须提前以视觉化方式充分告知服务对象。
  • • Minimize sensory overload. Keep background noise, bright lighting, and chaotic activity to a minimum.
    • 尽量减少感官知觉超载：将环境背景噪音、刺眼强光和嘈杂走动限制在最低限度。
  • • Use clear, concrete communication. Use simple words, short sentences, and visual aids (such as picture communication boards or schedules). Avoid figures of speech, sarcasm, or idioms, which can be interpreted literally.
    • 采用直观、具象的沟通方式：使用简明词汇、简短句子并配合图卡沟通板等视觉辅具；切勿使用隐喻、讽刺反语或成语俚语，因为自闭症个体往往会对其字面意思进行机械化硬解。
  • • Learn the client’s triggers and calming strategies. Be alert for early signs of sensory or emotional distress, and use techniques documented in the care plan (such as weighted blankets or a quiet corner) to help the client regulate.
    • 熟知服务对象的情绪触发源与安抚策略：敏锐捕捉感官或情绪烦躁的早期迹象，严格应用照护计划中记录的脱敏或安抚措施（如重力毯、安静角深压拥抱等）协助其重获情绪平静。

CEREBRAL PALSY (CP)

脑性瘫痪（脑瘫，CP）
Cerebral palsy (CP) is a disorder affecting muscle control (palsy) caused by an injury or abnormality in the motor region of the brain (cerebral). It occurs before birth, during birth, or in the first few years of life while the brain is developing. In Canada, approximately 2 to 3 out of every 1000 children are diagnosed with CP.
脑性瘫痪（脑瘫，CP）是由于大脑运动中枢（cerebral）受到损伤或发育异常，导致肌肉控制受损、姿势与运动协调障碍（palsy=瘫痪/麻痹）。它发生在出生前、分娩过程中或生命早期大脑发育阶段。在加拿大，每 1000 名儿童中约有 2 至 3 人被诊断为脑瘫。
The brain damage that causes CP is permanent and does not get worse over time, although the symptoms and functional difficulties can change as the person grows older. Certain body movements and body parts are affected by CP. The following types of CP are the most common:
导致脑瘫的脑部器质性损伤是永久性的，且其原发病理通常不会随时间进行性恶化，但随着服务对象年龄增长，其外在运动症状与继发性骨骼肌肉挛缩困难可能会发生动态变化。脑瘫主要分为以下几种最常见的运动类型：
  • • Spastic cerebral palsy. Spastic means uncontrolled contractions of skeletal muscles (spastic comes from the Greek spastikos, meaning “to draw in”). Muscles contract or shorten and remain stiff and unable to relax. One or both sides of the body may be involved, so posture and balance are affected, and movement is stiff and jerky.
    • 痉挛型脑瘫（Spastic CP）：痉挛是指骨骼肌不受控制的强直收缩（源自希腊语 spastikos，意为“紧缩牵拉”）。肌肉持续异常收缩缩短，僵硬紧绷且无法自主放松。身体的一侧或双侧均可能受累，导致姿势与平衡严重失调，运动步态僵硬、笨拙且呈痉挛性剪刀步态。
  • • Athetoid (dyskinetic) cerebral palsy. The client has slow, involuntary, writhing (weaving) movements of the trunk, arms, legs, feet, or hands. The movements are constant and worsen when the client is stressed or trying to make a voluntary movement.
    • 手足徐动型（不随意运动型）脑瘫（Athetoid CP）：服务对象的躯干、手臂、腿部、双手或双足出现缓慢、不自主且如蠕虫波浪样扭曲缠绕的怪异动作。这些不随意动作往往持续不断，并在精神紧张或试图做自主动作时显著加剧。
  • • Ataxic cerebral palsy. The client has difficulty with balance, coordination, and depth perception. Movements are clumsy and unsteady, and the person may walk with a wide-based gait.
    • 共济失调型脑瘫（Ataxic CP）：小脑功能受损，导致平衡感、动作精细协调以及空间深度感知严重障碍。动作笨拙摇晃，行走时步基极宽、蹒跚不稳。
  • • Mixed cerebral palsy. The client exhibits symptoms of more than one type, most commonly a combination of spastic and athetoid movements.
    • 混合型脑瘫（Mixed CP）：服务对象同时表现出一种以上类型的症状，最常见的临床组合为痉挛型与手足徐动型的混合表现。

Anatomical Distribution of Cerebral Palsy

脑性瘫痪的躯体瘫痪部位解剖分类
Depending on which limbs are affected, cerebral palsy is also classified anatomically:
根据受累肢体的具体部位分布，脑瘫在解剖学上进一步划分为：
  • • Diplegia: Loss of ability to move corresponding parts on both sides of the body; both legs or both arms are affected, but usually the legs are affected much more severely than the arms.
    • 双瘫（Diplegia）：身体两侧对称部位的运动功能丧失；双上肢或双下肢受累，但临床绝大多数为双下肢受损明显重于双上肢。
  • • Hemiplegia: Paralysis of one side of the body (arm, leg, and trunk on the same side), typically caused by brain damage to the opposite cerebral hemisphere.
    • 偏瘫（Hemiplegia）：身体同侧半边肢体（同侧手臂、腿部和躯干）发生瘫痪，通常由对侧大脑半球运动区器质性损伤引起。
  • • Quadriplegia: Paralysis of all four limbs (both arms and both legs), often accompanied by severe trunk and neck muscle impairment.
    • 四肢瘫（Quadriplegia）：四肢全部受累瘫痪（双侧手臂及双侧下肢），通常伴有重度躯干与颈部肌肉控制障碍。
In addition to motor challenges, clients with cerebral palsy may experience associated health conditions, including: intellectual disability (although many individuals with CP have normal or superior intelligence); hearing, speech, or vision impairment; difficulty swallowing (dysphagia); drooling due to poor oral-motor control; bowel and bladder incontinence; seizures; breathing difficulties due to poor posture; and pressure injuries resulting from immobility.
除严重的运动控制障碍外，脑瘫服务对象还常合并出现一系列伴随性健康问题，包括：智力障碍（但必须强调，许多脑瘫患者的智力完全正常甚至超常）；听觉、言语或视力功能受损；吞咽困难（dysphagia）；因口周肌力控制薄弱导致的流涎；膀胱及肠道排泄失禁；癫痫抽搐发作；因胸廓姿势不良引起的呼吸受限；以及长期活动受限继发的压力性损伤。

Supporting a Client Who Is Living With Cerebral Palsy

脑性瘫痪服务对象的综合支持照护
Care depends on the severity of the CP and the needs of the client. The goal is for the client to be as independent as possible. As a support worker, you may be required to assist the client with CP with range-of-motion (ROM) exercises and ADLs and to reduce the client’s risk of injury. Other supports may come from physio-, occupational, and speech therapy teams as well as the use of eyeglasses or hearing aids. Depending on the client’s needs, surgery or medications to reduce muscle challenges may also be ordered.
照护方案取决于脑瘫的严重程度以及服务对象的实际需求。照护的核心目标是促使其尽可能维持最高水平的生活自立。作为支持工作者，您可能需要协助脑瘫服务对象进行关节活动度（ROM）维持性练习与日常自理活动（ADL），并积极预防跌倒或外伤。其他专业支持来自物理治疗师（PT）、作业治疗师（OT）及言语病理治疗团队（SLP），并配合眼镜或助听器等辅具。根据医疗指征，有时还会采取外科肌腱松解术或注射抗痉挛药物以减轻肌张力障碍。

DOWN SYNDROME (DS)

唐氏综合征（21三体综合征，DS）
Down syndrome (DS) is a disorder caused by an extra chromosome, so the affected person has 47 chromosomes instead of 46. It occurs because of an error at fertilization and is a congenital (present at birth) condition. In Canada, DS is one of the most common congenital chromosomal disorders. For every 10 000 births in Canada, 15 babies are born with DS.
唐氏综合征（DS）是由受精卵染色体分离异常多出一条染色体引起的先天性遗传病，受累个体的体细胞中含有 47 条染色体（即多出一条第 21 号染色体，称为 21 三体），而非正常的 46 条。它是出生时即存在的先天性状况。在加拿大，唐氏综合征是最常见的先天性染色体疾病之一；每 10,000 名新生儿中约有 15 名唐氏综合征婴儿出生。

许多唐氏综合征儿童合并患有先天性心脏缺陷，且极易并发视力与听力障碍。他们患中耳炎、呼吸道感染及甲状腺功能减退的风险极高。患有唐氏综合征的个体在 35 岁之后可能开始显现失智症状，并在 40 多岁时常被确诊为阿尔茨海默病。遗传学研究表明，这一早发性痴呆高发倾向与第 21 号染色体上淀粉样前体蛋白基因的过度表达直接相关。

Supporting a Client Who Is Living With Down Syndrome

唐氏综合征服务对象的支持照护
Care depends on the severity of the DS and the needs of the client. The goal is for the client to be as independent as possible. Early intervention programs ideally begin soon after birth and will help identify the best methods for teaching each particular child. With good nutrition, adequate physical activity, and consistent mental stimulation, many people with DS live well into their 60s.
照护方案取决于唐氏综合征的个体差异与具体需求，其核心目标是促使其实现最大可能的自主生活。早期干预项目若在出生后尽早展开，将有助于确定最适合每个孩子的教学与康复途径。在优良的营养、充足的体育活动以及持续的心智刺激下，许多唐氏综合征人士能够享有长寿，安享 60 岁以上的晚年生活。
  • • Encouraging activities outlined by a physiotherapist, a speech-language therapist, as well as an occupational therapist
    • 积极鼓励并落实物理治疗师、言语病理师与作业治疗师制定的各项功能促进训练

    • 耐心训练穿衣、洗漱、如厕与进食等日常自理技能

    • 依据其认知接受水平开展适度的健康宣教与性教育指导

EPILEPSY

癫痫
Epilepsy is a condition characterized by recurrent (occurring repeatedly from time to time) seizures (epilepsia means “seizure”). A seizure is a brief disturbance in the brain’s normal electrical function, which affects awareness, movement, and sensation. Seizures that affect only one part of the brain are called partial seizures. Seizures that affect the whole brain are called generalized seizures.
癫痫是一种以反复发作性抽搐（recurrent seizures）为特征的慢性脑部神经系统疾病（epilepsia 意为“发作/抽搐”）。癫痫发作是大脑正常生物电活动的短暂紊乱，会急剧波及意识认知、躯体运动或感觉功能。仅累及大脑某一个局灶区域的发作称为部分性发作（局灶性发作）；若波及整个大脑皮质的发作则称为全面性发作。
The area of the brain affected by the seizure temporarily loses its ability to function normally, so seizures may cause different reactions, depending on the part of the brain affected. For example, some seizures cause the person to briefly stare and appear unresponsive; others (as in tonic–clonic seizures) involve a violent and sudden contraction or tremor of muscle groups. The person loses consciousness and falls to the floor. All muscle groups contract and relax, causing jerking and twitching movements of the body. Urinary and bowel incontinence may occur. A tonic–clonic seizure usually lasts for 1 to 7 minutes.
受发作累及的脑区会暂时丧失正常功能，因此发作表现因受波及的脑部位不同而差异极大。例如，某些发作仅表现为短暂失神、凝视且反应迟钝（如失神小发作）；而另一些发作（如强直-阵挛性大发作）则伴有肌群突然剧烈的僵直收缩与痉挛抽搐。当事人突然丧失意识并倒在地上，全身肌肉阵发性强烈痉挛抽动，常伴有大小便失禁。强直-阵挛性发作通常持续 1 至 7 分钟。
A single seizure does not mean that the person has epilepsy, since a number of factors can cause a single seizure. For a diagnosis of epilepsy, the person must have recurring seizures. The electrical system in the brain is permanently damaged, making it susceptible to seizures.
单次孤立的抽搐发作并不意味着当事人患有癫痫，因为多种急性诱因（如高热、低血糖、急性中毒）均可诱发单次抽搐。只有当出现反复、刻板发作的无诱因抽搐时，临床上才能正式诊断为癫痫。此时大脑的神经电传导系统存在器质性或永久性紊乱，使其具有自发异常放电的易感性。

Children and young adults are commonly affected, but epilepsy can develop at any time during a person’s life. Often, the cause of epilepsy in a particular person is not known. It can be caused by any condition affecting the brain—for example, CP, intellectual disability, ASD, Alzheimer’s disease, stroke, tumours, and acquired brain injury (see TABLE 36.1, on page 842).
儿童和青年人常受累，但癫痫可在个体生命周期的任何阶段发生。在很多患者中，癫痫发作的确切致病原因无法明确探知。任何累及脑部器质性病变的疾患均可诱发癫痫——例如脑性瘫痪、智力障碍、孤独症谱系障碍、阿尔茨海默病、中风、脑肿瘤以及获得性脑损伤（参见第 842 页表 36.1）。
There is no cure for epilepsy; medications are prescribed by a physician to try to prevent seizures. These medications may control seizures in many people but may not work in others, so some individuals may need brain surgery to reduce the frequency of seizures. When controlled, epilepsy usually does not affect learning and the ability to perform ADLs. Clients with epilepsy have an increased risk for premature death. They also have higher rates of suicide; sudden, unexplained death; or accidental death, especially drowning.
癫痫目前尚无法根治；医生通过开具抗癫痫药物以预防抽搐发作。这些药物能够在多数患者中有效控制发作，但对某些难治性癫痫则可能收效甚微，部分个体可能需要接受神经外科手术以减少发作频次。当病情得到良好控制时，癫痫通常不会损害认知学习能力与日常自理能力。然而，癫痫患者面临更高的过早死亡风险；其自杀率、突发不明原因死亡（SUDEP）以及意外伤害致死（尤其是溺水）发生率显著高于常人。

Supporting a Client Who Is Living With Epilepsy

癫痫服务对象的专业照护与急救安全规范
Care for a client with epilepsy depends on the severity of the epilepsy and the needs of the client. The goal is for the client to be as independent as possible.
癫痫服务对象的照护方案取决于病情的严重程度以及个体的实际需求，核心目标是促使其实现最大程度的生活自理与安全：
  • • As a support worker, you must observe and report all activity before, during, and after a seizure. Call for help immediately if needed.
    • 作为支持工作者，您必须密切观察并精准记录抽搐发作前、发作中及发作后的全部具体细节与表现；必要时立即呼叫医疗急救支援。
  • • Clients with frequent or unpredictable seizures may injure themselves if they fall. Safety measures are thus required, whether the client is at home, in the workplace, taking transportation, or engaging in recreation activities.
    • 频繁或不可预测发作的患者跌倒时极易受严重外伤。因此，无论服务对象身处家中、工作场所、搭乘公共交通工具或参与文娱活动，均须落实环境防撞防跌安全防护措施。
  • • Close supervision is required at all times when the client is swimming, boating, or in a hot tub, to prevent drowning.
    • 当服务对象在游泳、划船或泡热水澡/温泉时，必须全程保持一对一严密视线监护，绝对不可离人，以防发作导致溺水窒息。
  • • If your client has a seizure, ensure that there is nothing within reach that could harm the client. Do not hold the client down; keep track of the duration of the seizure and the parts of the body affected. If the seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes, call the emergency medical service in your area. After the seizure has stopped, place the client on their left side in case the client vomits; stay with the client until they recover.
    • 若服务对象突发抽搐，立即移开其周围所有尖锐硬物以防磕伤。绝对不可强行按压约束肢体，切勿往口中塞入任何物品；准确记录抽搐持续时间与身体受累部位。若抽搐发作持续超过 5 分钟（癫痫持续状态急症），立即拨打 911 急救电话。抽搐停止后，协助其保持左侧卧位（侧卧复苏体位）以防呕吐物误吸，全程陪伴直至其完全恢复神志。

FETAL ALCOHOL SPECTRUM DISORDERS (FASD)

胎儿酒精谱系障碍（FASD）
Fetal alcohol spectrum disorders (FASD) are a group of physical and mental abnormalities that occur in a child as a result of alcohol consumption by the birth parent during pregnancy. In Canada, one child is born with FASD every day. It is the most common preventable developmental disorder.
胎儿酒精谱系障碍（FASD）是由于孕母在妊娠怀孕期间饮用酒精导致胎儿出现的一组复杂的身体与心智发育异常。在加拿大，平均每天就有一名患有 FASD 的婴儿出生。它是全社会最常见的完全可以预防的发育迟缓残疾。
Physical challenges include low birth weight, weak muscle tone, and poor weight gain. Cardiac (heart) disorders, hearing impairment, and abnormalities of the spine and joints may also occur. The client may have characteristic facial features, including an abnormally small head, small eye openings, a thin upper lip, and a small chin.
身体躯体受损表现包括出生低体重、全身肌张力低下以及体重增长迟缓。此外还常并发先天性心脏缺损、听力受损以及脊柱与骨关节畸形。患儿往往具有特征性的特殊面容：小头畸形、眼裂狭小、上唇菲薄且人中平滑、以及下颌短小。
A child with FASD may not have any outward signs of a disability, but may have an intellectual disability or other behavioural, learning, and emotional disorders, including poor attention span, hyperactivity, poor motor skills, and slow language development. Older children and adults with FASD may often have memory impairment, poor judgement, difficulties with ADLs, inability to manage anger, poor social skills, and mental health challenges. Some adults with FASD work and live independently, but others will need support and help from family and community services. Women with FASD can have a normal baby as long as they do not consume alcohol during pregnancy.
患有 FASD 的儿童可能在外貌上没有任何显性残疾特征，但却存在深层智力障碍或严重的注意力、行为、学习和情绪调控缺陷（如注意力极度短暂、多动冲动、精细运动协调不良以及语言发育滞后）。随着年龄增长，患有 FASD 的青少年和成人常表现出严重记忆受损、判断力低下、日常自理困难、愤怒失控、社交技能拙劣以及精神心理并发症。部分成年患者能够独立工作和生活，但另一些人则终身需要家庭与社区支持服务。患有 FASD 的女性只要在孕期严格杜绝饮酒，完全能够孕育健康的正常婴儿。

Supporting a Client Living With Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorders

胎儿酒精谱系障碍服务对象的支持照护
FASD is a lifelong condition. Care depends on the severity of the FASD and the needs of the client. The goal is for the client to be as independent as possible. With appropriate interventions and support, people living with FASD can be successful in many areas of their lives. It is most helpful to intervene as early as possible and get a diagnosis. You can support clients living with FASD by: encouraging self-esteem (focus on what the client can do and not on what they cannot do); and encouraging self-awareness (the client may overreact to normal everyday stresses or may feel “different” than other people; encouraging self-awareness can be very empowering for the client).
FASD 是一种终身不可逆的永久性状况。照护方案取决于受累严重程度与实际需求，目标是促使其实现最大可能的自主生活。在适宜的干预与支持下，患有 FASD 的人士能够在生活的诸多领域取得卓越成就。尽早获得明确诊断并开展早期干预至关重要。支持工作者可通过以下方式赋能：树立自尊心（聚焦于当事人“能做什么”，而非其“不能做什么”）；培养自我认知（当事人可能对日常微小压力反应过度，或产生异于常人的疏离感；帮助其清晰理解自身特质具有极强的赋能疗愈价值）。

INTELLECTUAL DISABILITIES

智力障碍（认知障碍）
An intellectual disability (cognitive disability) is an impaired ability to learn and results in below-average intelligence and limitations in the ability to function in certain areas of daily life. (Intelligence relates to learning, thinking, and reasoning.) The client can learn new skills but does so at a slower than normal rate. A client with an intellectual disability often has difficulties with communication, self-care, and social interaction. In the past, intellectual disability was called mental retardation, a term now considered unacceptable in Canada.
智力障碍（认知障碍，cognitive disability）是指个体学习、理解与推理能力的显著受损，导致智力低于平均水平，并在日常生活的适应性功能上表现出显著受限。（智力涵盖认知学习、逻辑思考与抽象推理。）当事人完全能够掌握新的生活技能，但其学习速率明显慢于常人。患有智力障碍的服务对象在人际交流、日常自我照料及社会人际互动方面常常面临多重挑战。过去，智力障碍曾被称为“精神发育迟滞（mental retardation）”，该词汇如今在加拿大被认为具有严重贬损性而严禁使用。
An intellectual disability can be caused by any genetic abnormality, injury, or disease that impairs the development of the brain. Some common causes of intellectual disabilities are listed in BOX 36.1 on page 849. People with an intellectual disability often have other disabilities as well.
智力障碍可由干扰大脑正常发育的任何遗传基因异常、外伤创伤或器质性疾病引起。专栏 36.1 列出了智力障碍的常见致病诱因。患有智力障碍的人士往往合并伴有其他伴随性躯体残疾。

BOX 36.1: Causes of Intellectual Disability / 专栏 36.1：导致智力障碍（认知障碍）的常见病理诱因

  • • Genetic Conditions: Abnormal genes inherited from one or both parents (e.g., fragile X syndrome and phenylketonuria [PKU]); missing or extra chromosomes (e.g., Down syndrome).
    • 遗传基因疾患：自双亲遗传的异常基因突变（如脆性X综合征、苯丙酮尿症[PKU]）；染色体缺失或多余（如唐氏综合征）。

  • • Conditions After Birth: Childhood diseases such as whooping cough, chicken pox, and measles; central nervous system infections such as meningitis and encephalitis; acquired brain injury caused by accidents, disease, or abuse; severe childhood malnutrition or profound psychosocial neglect.

Intellectual disabilities range from mild to severe. Tests that measure intelligence are called IQ (intelligence quotient) tests. An average person without an intellectual disability has an IQ score of about 90 to 110. People with IQ scores between 55 and 70 are considered to have mild intellectual disability. They may learn slowly but are able to attend regular schools. As adults, they can function in society with some support. They can work and live in the community and need only occasional support. People who have IQ scores below 55 are considered to have moderate intellectual disability and need daily support at home and at work. People with IQ scores below 25 are considered to have severe intellectual disability and need constant support in all areas.
智力障碍程度从轻度到极重度不等。测量智力的标准化测验称为智商（IQ）测验。无认知障碍的普通人群平均智商在 90 至 110 之间。智商在 55 至 70 之间被判定为轻度智力障碍：他们学习较慢，但能够就读普通融合学校；成年后在适当指导下能够完全融入社会，胜任普通工作并独立生活，仅需偶尔的咨询支持。智商在 25 至 55 之间被判定为中度智力障碍：在居家和工作场所需要日常支持性协助。智商低于 25 则属于重度/极重度智力障碍：在所有日常生活自理与安全领域均需要 24 小时恒定监护与全面照料。
The Canadian Association for Community Living is a national association dedicated to serving people with intellectual disabilities and their families. The association’s goal is to ensure that people with intellectual disabilities have opportunities to live meaningful, dignified lives. The association’s philosophy is that people with intellectual disabilities can and should be allowed to participate in all aspects of community living. Children should live in families, be integrated into regular schools whenever possible, and interact with other children with or without disabilities. Adults with intellectual disabilities have the right to control their lives to the fullest extent possible—that is, they should make choices and decisions about their care and how they live. They should have friends, work at jobs, enjoy adult activities, and contribute to their communities.
加拿大社区生活协会（CACL）是一家致力于服务智力障碍人士及其家庭的全国性权威机构。该协会的终极宗旨是确保智力障碍者享有过上有意义、有尊严生活的全部机会。协会的核心哲学理念主张：智力障碍者能够并且应当被完全允许参与社区生活的方方面面。儿童应当生活在家庭温暖中，尽可能融入普通学校，与健康同龄人平等互动；成年智力障碍者享有最大限度掌控自我生活的不可剥夺的权利——即有权对自身照护方式和生活模式做出自主选择与决策；他们应当拥有友谊、从事体面工作、享受成年人的社交休闲，并为所在社区贡献独特的个体价值。

SPINA BIFIDA

脊柱裂
Spina bifida is a congenital disorder caused by improper closing of the spine before birth (spina means “spine”; bifida means “split into two parts”). Spina bifida is a neural tube defect that occurs during the first month of pregnancy. The neural tube is the structure in a developing embryo that eventually forms the baby’s brain, spinal cord, and the protective coverings for the brain and spinal cord (the meninges).
脊柱裂是一种因胎儿在母体内出生前脊椎骨未能正常闭合导致的先天性缺陷（spina 意为“脊柱”，bifida 意为“分裂成两半”）。脊柱裂属于神经管缺陷（NTD），发生在受孕后的第一个月内。神经管是发育中胚胎形成婴儿大脑、脊髓以及脑和脊髓保护膜（脑脊膜）的关键初级神经解剖结构。
Bones of the spinal column (vertebrae) protect the spinal cord. In spina bifida, the vertebrae do not close properly, leaving the spinal cord unprotected. The spinal cord contains nerves that send messages to and from the brain, and because in spina bifida the spinal cord is unprotected, nerve damage occurs. Infection of the meninges and the spinal cord is also a risk.
正常情况下，脊柱的骨骼（椎骨）环绕并严密保护着椎管内的脊髓。在脊柱裂中，脊椎背侧的椎弓板未能正常融合闭合，导致脆弱的脊髓失去骨性屏障的保护而暴露。脊髓内布满了在脑与身体各部之间传导神经冲动的感觉和运动神经纤维；由于脊柱裂中脊髓失去保护，不可避免地会发生神经纤维损伤与受压坏死。此外，脑脊膜和脊髓发生致命性感染（脑膜炎）的风险也极高。
Spina bifida can occur anywhere in the spine. The lower back (lumbar and sacral region) is the most common site. Types of spina bifida include:
脊柱裂可发生在脊柱的任何节段，其中下腰部（腰骶椎段）是最常见的病变部位。脊柱裂主要分为以下两大基本临床类型：

Spina Bifida Occulta

隐性脊柱裂（Spina Bifida Occulta）
This type is the mildest form of spina bifida. A vertebra or vertebrae have a small gap or defect. However, the spinal cord and the membranes (meninges) remain inside the spinal column (FIGURE 36.2). The defect is hidden (occulta means “hidden”) under the skin. Skin over the defect is intact, although a dimple, a tuft of hair, or a birthmark may be present on the lower back. The spinal cord and nerves are normal. It rarely causes disability, and many people do not know they have it until an X-ray is taken for another reason.
这是脊柱裂中最轻微的一种形式。一个或多个椎骨存在微小的骨性裂隙或骨质缺损。然而，脊髓和保护膜（脊膜）完全完整地保留在椎管内部（图 36.2）。该骨性缺损完全隐藏（occulta 意为“隐藏”）在皮肤下方。缺损部位表面的皮肤完好无损，但局部下腰部皮肤表面可能伴有一处小凹陷、一小簇毛发或色素沉着胎记。脊髓和马尾神经通常形态与功能正常。隐性脊柱裂极少导致肢体功能障碍，许多人终生并不知道自己患有此病，往往是在因其他原因拍摄腰椎 X 光片时偶然发现。

囊性脊柱裂（Spina Bifida Cystica）
With spina bifida cystica, the client has a defect in the vertebrae and a sac-like protrusion (cystica means “sac”). Part of the spinal column is in a pouch or sac that protrudes through the opening in the spine. The sac is covered by a thin layer of skin or by meninges.   • • Meningocele: With this type of spina bifida, a sac containing meninges and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) protrudes from the spine (FIGURE 36.3, A). The sac does not contain nerve tissue, so the spinal cord and nerves are not usually damaged. The person usually has normal mobility and bladder/bowel function.     • 脊膜膨出（Meningocele）：在此类囊性脊柱裂中，膨出的囊袋内仅含有脑脊膜和脑脊液（图 36.3, A）。囊袋内并不包含脊髓神经实质，因此脊髓和外周神经通常未遭实质破坏。患儿通常具有正常的下肢运动能力和大小便控制功能。出生后尽早通过外科手术闭合骨裂孔并将脑脊膜回纳修复至椎管内。
  • • Myelomeningocele: This is the most severe and dangerous form of spina bifida (myelo means “spinal cord”). With this type of spina bifida, a sac containing nerves, a part of the spinal cord, meninges, and cerebrospinal fluid protrudes through the spine (FIGURE 36.3, B). Severe nerve damage occurs because the spinal cord is exposed and damaged. Paralysis, loss of sensation below the level of the lesion, and loss of bowel and bladder control are common.
    • 脊髓脊膜膨出（Myelomeningocele）：这是脊柱裂中最严重、最危险且致残率最高的形式（myelo 意为“脊髓”）。在此类型中，膨出的囊袋内包含暴露的脊髓组织、马尾神经根、脑脊膜及脑脊液（图 36.3, B）。由于脊髓神经直接暴露并发生实质性发育缺损，导致病变平面以下发生不可逆的永久性重度神经损伤，患儿通常出现下肢完全瘫痪、感觉缺失以及神经源性膀胱与肠道失禁。
Clients with spina bifida may have other disorders as well. Hydrocephalus (fluid buildup in the brain) occurs in 80 to 90% of children with myelomeningocele. Latex allergy is also very common in children with spina bifida; this allergy can cause life-threatening anaphylactic reactions, so clients must avoid all products made of natural rubber latex.
脊柱裂患者还常常合并并发其他严重疾患。在脊髓脊膜膨出患儿中，高达 80% 至 90% 并发脑积水（hydrocephalus，脑脊液在颅脑内积聚压迫脑组织），必须行脑室-腹腔分流管手术。此外，乳胶过敏（latex allergy）在脊柱裂儿童中极其高发；接触医用乳胶手套、导尿管或橡皮筋等制品可引发致死性过敏性休克，因此对这类服务对象必须严格执行无乳胶安全防护规程。

脊柱裂服务对象的专业照护重点
Care depends on the type of spina bifida and the severity of nerve damage. Key care measures include: assisting with braces, crutches, or wheelchairs for mobility; implementing neurogenic bladder programs (such as clean intermittent catheterization [CIC]) and bowel management routines; preventing pressure ulcers over insensate skin areas; monitoring shunt function for signs of hydrocephalus or shunt malfunction (headache, vomiting, lethargy); and ensuring a strictly latex-free environment.

IMPACT ON THE CLIENT AND THE FAMILY

发育障碍对服务对象个体与家庭全生命周期的深远影响
A developmental disorder or disability affects the client and the family across the client’s entire lifespan. The needs of the client change as they age, and the ability of family members to provide physical and emotional care also changes as parents and caregivers grow older.
发育障碍或发育迟缓残疾在服务对象的整个生命周期中持续对本人和家庭产生深刻影响。随着当事人年龄的增长，其照料与医疗需求不断演变；而随着父母和家庭照顾者自身步入老龄，其提供重体力搬运与情感照料的身体机能也在逐渐衰退。
Families often experience intense grief, shock, denial, and guilt following a child’s diagnosis. Over time, chronic sorrow, financial strain from specialized medical equipment and therapies, social isolation, and exhaustion can lead to severe caregiver burnout. Support workers play a vital role by providing respite care, emotional encouragement, and dependable professional assistance that allows families to sustain their loving care without collapsing under physical exhaustion.
在孩子被确诊初期，家庭成员往往会经历剧烈的悲恸、震惊、否认与内疚自责。随着时间推移，旷日持久的慢性悲伤、专用医疗器材与高昂康复治疗带来的沉重经济负担、社交生活孤立隔绝以及长期体力透支，极易引发严重的“照顾者耗竭综合征（caregiver burnout）”。支持工作者通过提供专业的暂托照料（respite care）、给予情感共情支持并承担日常重体力照护操作，发挥了不可或缺的支撑作用，使家庭得以在不至于身心崩溃的前提下维系持续温暖的照护纽带。

Providing Compassionate Care: Caring for Clients Who Are Living With Developmental Disorders and Disabilities / 提供同理照护：照料患有发育障碍与发育迟缓残疾的服务对象（DIPPS核心原则）

  • • Dignity. Adults and children with developmental disabilities deserve to be treated with respect and in a manner that promotes their dignity. Remember to consider the client before the disability. The words you use are an important way to show respect. For example, say, “clients with disabilities.” Do not refer to them as “handicapped,” “disabled,” or “crippled.” Also, do not identify the client by the disability. For example, it is correct to say, “Mr. Joshi lives with autism spectrum disorder.” Do not say, “Mr. Joshi is autistic.” Say, “Susan lives with an intellectual disability,” not “Susan is retarded.” Have empathy, not pity. Remember, empathy is understanding the experiences and feelings of others; pity is feeling sorry for others, which implies that you are superior to the person. Treat them like adults. If appropriate, shake hands or nod your head and smile when introduced. Speak directly to the client in a normal tone of voice.
    • 尊严（Dignity）：患有发育障碍的成人和儿童理应受到充分尊重，并以维护其尊严的方式对待。时刻牢记“先看到人，再看到残疾”（以人为中心的原语）。您所使用的言辞是展现尊重的关键桥梁：例如，使用“患有残疾的服务对象（clients with disabilities）”，绝不可称其为“残废”、“残疾人”或“畸形跛足”。此外，绝不要用疾病标签来指代人：可以说“乔希先生生活在孤独症谱系障碍中”，不可说“乔希是自闭儿”；可以说“苏珊生活在智力障碍中”，绝不可称“苏珊是个弱智”。怀抱同理心（empathy），切忌施舍同情怜悯（pity）——同理心是平视并理解对方的境遇，而怜悯暗示了自身的居高临下与优越感。像对待普通成年人那样对待他们：正常对视微笑握手，用正常音调直接与本人对话。
  • • Independence. The goal for clients with disabilities is for them to live as independently as possible. Do not make assumptions about what the client can and cannot do. Follow their care plan. Encourage the client to use self-help devices whenever possible. Be patient, and do not rush the client.
    • 独立（Independence）：残障服务对象的核心目标是实现最大可能的自主生活。切勿凭空假设服务对象“能做什么”或“不能做什么”；严格遵循照护计划；鼓励尽可能使用自理辅助设备；保持耐心，切勿催促进度。
  • • Individualized care. When working with children and adults with developmental disorders or disabilities, remember to consider the client before the disorder or disability. Each client is unique. The effects of disabilities vary, depending on the client. Your supervisor and the care plan will tell you how to meet each client’s needs in the best way. For care to be truly client-centred, it must be planned or adapted to fit the client’s choices, physical and cognitive strengths and limitations, personality, and care plan.
    • 个性化照护（Individualized care）：每位服务对象都是独一无二的生命个体，残疾的影响因人而异。要实现真正的以人为本，照护必须量身定制以契合当事人的个性偏好、身体及认知优劣势以及照护计划规范，绝不可千篇一律机械套用。
  • • Preferences. Clients have the right to personal choices. Provide opportunities for the client to make choices and decisions. Ask first if the client wants help and how you can provide help. All clients must consent to procedures before you start. If the client does not want you to continue, stop. Contact your supervisor. Also, adapt your work to allow for the family’s choices and preferences.
    • 偏好尊重（Preferences）：服务对象享有自主选择权。创造机会让其自主做决定。在提供协助前先礼貌询问对方是否需要帮助以及希望如何被协助。在开始任何操作前必须获得知情同意；若当事人拒绝继续，必须立即停手并向主管汇报。
  • • Privacy. Medical and personal information about the client is confidential. Talk about the client only with members of the health care team who need to know. Do not expose the client. Provide privacy when performing procedures. Drape the client so as not to expose the client’s body. Remember that adults with disabilities have sexual needs. Many have partners or are married. Allow for privacy for sexual activity.
    • 隐私保护（Privacy）：所有医疗与个人信息严格保密。仅在必要时与直接照料团队成员讨论。执行照护操作时妥善拉帘关门遮蔽身体以防暴露。必须清醒认识到：患有残疾的成年人同样享有完全正常的性需求与情感欲望；许多人拥有伴侣或组建了家庭；必须为其合法私密生活提供充分的空间与隐私保障。
  • • Safety. Most developmental disorders or disabilities create safety hazards for the client. Check with your supervisor and the care plan to determine how to ensure the client’s safety. Practise the safety measures described in Chapter 20. Also, be alert to signs of abuse. Report any suspected abuse to your supervisor immediately. Remember, you also must report child abuse directly to a public authority (see Chapter 12).
    • 安全第一（Safety）：发育残疾往往潜藏跌倒、烫伤、走失与误吸风险；严格践行第 20 章防跌安全规程。同时，残障群体极易沦为虐待的受害者：时刻警惕身体或情感虐待征象，一旦怀疑虐待必须立即向主管汇报；请牢记，对于虐待儿童的任何嫌疑，法律强制要求必须直接向公共保护机构（如儿童援助协会 CAS）紧急法定报告（参见第 12 章）。

CHAPTER REVIEW: KEY POINTS

本章核心复习要点
  • • Developmental disorders and disabilities can affect physical, mental, and intellectual functions.
    • 发育障碍与发育迟缓残疾可全面影响个体的躯体生理、心理情感以及认知智力功能。
  • • Developmental disorders and disabilities are always permanent and may be mild, moderate, or severe.
    • 发育障碍与残疾终身存在且不可逆（永久性），其受损程度分为轻度、中度或重度。
  • • There are various developmental conditions, and each condition can have a wide range of symptoms.
    • 存在多种不同的发育疾病谱系，且每种状况都具有跨度极广、高度个体差异的临床症状表现。
  • • A developmental disability can occur in the child because of the condition of the birth parent during pregnancy, or it can occur during birth, after birth, or during childhood or adolescence. The disability can interfere with or impair normal development in a child.
    • 发育残疾可由孕母妊娠期母体状况、分娩围产期损伤、产后疾病或儿童/青少年期（18岁前）伤害引起，阻碍儿童正常身心发育里程碑。
  • • The care plan for each client will differ, depending on the client’s level of functioning, age, and symptoms.
    • 照护计划因服务对象的功能等级、实际年龄及独特症状而各不相同，必须严格落实个性化定制。
  • • A developmental disorder or disability can affect the client and the family across the client’s lifespan as the needs of the client change and the ability of family members to care for the client change as they age.
    • 发育障碍在服务对象全生命周期中持续对本人和家庭产生重大影响，随着当事人需求演变与父母照顾者衰老，照护支持重心随之转移。
  • • The family and the client (depending on the client’s age) should be included in planning care.
    • 服务对象本人（视认知年龄而定）及其家庭必须被充分纳入照护计划的制定与决策全过程。

CRITICAL THINKING IN PRACTICE / 临床反思实践：脑瘫青少年女孩的指甲修剪与安全防抗拒照护

  • Scenario: A teenage client who has cerebral palsy has long fingernails that she likes to have filed and polished. Today, you notice that some of her fingernails are broken and have rough edges. You quietly try to file her nails before beginning her personal care, but as you begin, she resists. She swings her arms at you and yells. What should you have done prior to beginning nail care? Why is nail care important for this client? How might you provide care safely?
    临床情境：一位患有脑性瘫痪的青少年女孩留着长指甲，平时非常喜欢修锉指甲并涂上指甲油。今天，你注意到她的某些指甲断裂并出现了粗糙边缘。在开始基础个人卫生照护前，你没有出声直接试图帮她修锉指甲；然而刚一上手，她便剧烈反抗，挥舞双臂向你打来并大声尖叫。问题：在开始指甲护理前，你本应该先做什么？为什么指甲护理对该服务对象至关重要？你应当如何确保安全地提供照护？
  • Clinical Analysis & Action: (1) Prior action: You should have communicated with her first—explained what you noticed, asked for her permission, and explained the procedure gently. Touching a person with spasticity without warning can trigger startle reflexes, sudden violent involuntary muscle contractions, and profound fear. (2) Importance: Nail care prevents scratch injuries to her skin (especially during spastic episodes or involuntary movements) and prevents infections. (3) Safe delivery: Calm the client, apologize for surprising her, wait for her muscles to relax, involve her in selecting nail file or polish, position her comfortably with adequate support, and file slowly while continually speaking to her gently.
    临床深度解析与行动准则：(1) 事先应做的规范操作：在触碰前必须先与她沟通！温和解释你发现她指甲边缘有破损、征得她的知情同意并解释接下来的动作步骤。在毫无预警的情况下突然抓握触碰脑瘫患者，会直接诱发惊吓反射（startle reflex），引发剧烈的不随意痉挛抽搐并造成当事人极大惊恐。(2) 护理重要性：痉挛或不自主扭动极易导致破损指甲抓伤自身面部与眼球皮肤，造成皮肤破损感染。(3) 安全照护途径：先暂停退后、温和道歉安抚情绪、等待肌张力自然恢复平缓；邀请她挑选指甲锉或指甲油颜色以建立掌控感；在体位支撑稳定、双臂舒适放松时，一边轻柔对话一边缓慢轻柔修锉。
