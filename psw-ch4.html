The term ethics refers to the rules of conduct that guide us when deciding what is right and what is wrong (immoral). These rules come from society’s expectation of how people should conduct themselves. Morals are the fundamental principles of behaviour each person believes to be right or wrong for themselves without concern for legalities or customs.

伦理（Ethics）一词特指在指引我们判定何者为正当（Right）、何者为不当或不道德（Wrong / Immoral）时所恪守的行为守则（Rules of conduct）。这些守则源于整个社会对于人类应当如何立身行事的普遍期望。道德（Morals）则是每个人内心深处坚信对自己而言何者为是、何者为非的根本行为准则，且通常不受法律条文或世俗习俗的约束。

Support workers must be aware that people’s culture and personal circumstances may influence their ethical beliefs, morals, and values, and all of these influence their actions. Values are what an individual considers to be worthwhile or desirable qualities in a person. A belief is a conviction that something is true.

支持工作者必须深刻清醒地认识到，每个人的文化背景与个人独特境遇都会深刻塑造其伦理信念、道德观与价值观，而这一切又反过来直接支配其日常行为。价值观（Values）指一个人所认同的、在人格中具有价值或令人向往的内在品质。信念（Belief）则是坚信某事为真实的内在确信。

Ethics and morals are often incorrectly used to mean the same thing, and some people think of morals as focusing specifically on standards of behaviour related to sexual activity. Morals and ethics play a part in our everyday lives, and we apply them when making both big and small decisions. Whether we realize it or not, they have a great impact on our personal and professional relationships. As a support worker, when you have to make difficult choices or decisions, you will rely on your ethical beliefs, morals, and values to guide your conduct.

在日常生活中，“伦理”与“道德”常被错误地混为一谈，甚至有人狭隘地认为道德仅指与性行为规范相关的戒律。事实上，道德与伦理贯穿于我们日常生活的方方面面，在我们做出无论是大是小的决断时无时无刻不在发挥指引作用。无论我们是否自觉意识到，它们都在深刻影响着我们的私人人际关系与职业执业交往。作为一名支持工作者，当你面临两难的艰难抉择或临床决策时，你必将依靠自身的伦理信念、职业道德与核心价值观来规范指引自己的言行举止。

BOX 4.1: A Sample Code of Ethics for Support Workers

专栏 4.1：支持工作者专业伦理守则范本（全真九大道德准则）
• Support workers provide high-quality personal care and support services. They work within their scope of practice. They promptly report to their supervisors any concerns and observations about clients’ health and well-being. They perform only those tasks for which they have received the necessary education. They know and follow employer policies as well as federal and provincial or territorial laws. They work within the parameters of national and provincial or territorial laws at all times.

• The support worker needs to be aware of the policies and procedures for each area. If the support worker is employed for more than one client or agency, the required skills may vary. It should never be assumed that the skills allowed at one agency can be automatically transferred to another agency. When unsure, the support worker should always ask for direction from their supervisor or regulated health care professional.

• Support workers provide compassionate care to all clients. They promote the client’s physical, emotional, intellectual, social, and spiritual well-being. They encourage clients to maintain as much independence as possible during times of normal health and in situations of illness, injury, or disability or while dying. They respect and promote the family’s roles and relationships.

• Support workers value the dignity and worth of all clients. They advocate for their client whenever necessary. They strive to treat all clients in an honest, fair, and just manner. They do not discriminate against a client on the basis of age, race or ethnicity, colour, religion, sexual orientation, or culture.

• Support workers respect their clients’ choices about how they receive or participate in their care. They respect and promote the client’s wishes and use discretion to avoid causing distress or embarrassment to a person (see the Supporting Mrs. Glass: Recognizing the Need for Discretion box).

• Support workers respect their clients’ right to privacy and confidentiality. Information about the client learned while providing care is not shared with anyone outside the health care team.

• Support workers do not misuse their position of trust. They do not accept gifts or tips from their clients. They do not buy property from their clients, nor do they sell products to them. They do not try to impose their own religious or other beliefs on their clients. Their behaviour toward clients and co-workers is professional at all times (see the Supporting Mr. Creishilo: Maintaining a Professional Relationship box).

• Support workers are reliable. They arrive at work on time and complete all assignments. They are patient and courteous with clients. They notify their supervisor if they are going to be late or are unable to work, or if they are working in the community or in a home care setting, they inform their client. Their conduct is professional and based on ethical principles.

• Support workers promote and maintain their clients’ safety. They report mistakes and unsafe situations immediately to their supervisors. They consider their clients’ safety when performing all tasks and activities.

BOX 4.2: Ethical Dilemmas (Scenarios 1 to 3)

专栏 4.2：临床一线伦理困境实战案例深度剖析（情境 1 至情境 3）
Scenario 1
You are assigned to work for a family with a 2-year-old boy and newborn twins. You are responsible for helping with care of the 2-year-old. The mother mentions that since the birth of the twins, the toddler has been throwing temper tantrums around lunchtime. She wants to stop this problem behaviour and tells you that if the toddler throws a tantrum or misbehaves, he is to be sent to his room for 15 minutes or longer if he has not settled down by the end of that time.
Discussion
Consider how applying the main principles of health care may cause conflict. To ensure the child’s safety, discuss this with the supervisor.
• Autonomy: The mother has the right to make parenting decisions.
• Justice: Would it be fair to the child to leave him alone in his room for so long?
• Beneficence: Will the disciplining help the child improve his behaviour? Can other actions improve his behaviour?
• Nonmaleficence: Could leaving the child in his room cause harm?

Scenario 2
Miki works in a long-term care facility. Mr. Petrova is a resident on her resident care unit. Miki smells alcohol on Mr. Petrova’s breath following his son’s visit. She comments on this to Mr. Petrova, and he tells her that his son had brought him a bottle of liquor. Residents keeping alcohol in their rooms is against facility rules. Mr. Petrova says that alcohol eases his pain and asks Miki to promise not to tell anyone about the liquor.
Discussion
Telling her supervisor about the liquor would disregard Mr. Petrova’s wishes and his autonomy. However, not telling her supervisor could harm Mr. Petrova. For example, alcohol may interfere with his medications or cause adverse effects. Not telling could also harm Miki. She could lose her job for knowing a resident has broken facility rules and not reporting it.
Miki explains to Mr. Petrova that she is ethically and legally obligated to tell her supervisor about the bottle of liquor in his room. She explains her reasons, but Mr. Petrova still gets upset. Miki, however, knows that she has followed the ethical principle of nonmaleficence. Also, she has avoided betraying Mr. Petrova’s trust by refusing to make a promise she cannot keep.

Scenario 3
John’s home care client is Mrs. Jessop, whose care plan states that she is not allowed sugary foods because she has uncontrolled diabetes; however, she often snacks on sugary foods when she is by herself. Today is Mrs. Jessop’s birthday, and her neighbour brings her a cake. Mrs. Jessop tells John that every year on her birthday she chooses to go off her diet. She asks him to cut her a piece of cake. Serving Mrs. Jessop a piece of cake would respect her autonomy. However, the cake could harm Mrs. Jessop’s physical health.
Discussion
John decides not to serve the cake to Mrs. Jessop. He knows that she can serve herself or her neighbour can serve it to her. John says that he should perhaps call Mrs. Jessop’s case manager about the situation, but Mrs. Jessop does not want John to make the call. She goes ahead and eats the cake. John observes her carefully for any ill effects but notices no change in her condition. Later, he telephones his supervisor to report the incident. His written notes also describe what happened exactly.
John upholds the principle of autonomy by allowing Mrs. Jessop to decide to eat the cake. He also upholds the principle of nonmaleficence by not serving her the cake and by advising that they consult her case manager before she eats a piece of cake. Because he has reported what happened, he has also followed the principle of beneficence.

Justice

公平正义原则（Justice）

The principle of justice means that all people should be treated in a fair and equal manner. Justice is an ideal that is central to Canada’s universal health care system—all Canadians, regardless of their ability to pay, receive equal access to the same medical services. Unfortunately, this ideal is not a reality for all people across Canada. Clients who can afford it can buy services that are not readily available to everyone. Examples of such services include private clinics, medical tests and procedures (e.g., magnetic resonance imaging [MRI], hip replacement), and elective procedures (e.g., plastic surgery).

公平正义原则特指所有受助案主都应当获得毫无偏袒的公平、公正与平等对待。正义是加拿大全民公共医疗体制的最核心崇高理想 —— 无论个人经济支付能力如何，全体国民依法享有同等获取必需医疗卫生服务的平等权利。然而令人遗憾的是，在现实维度的全加拿大，这一宏伟理想并未在所有人身上得到绝对实现。财力雄厚的富裕案主往往能够自费购买那些普通公众无法轻易获取的高阶快捷服务，典型范例包括私立名医诊所、免排队的加急高端影像检查（如私人 MRI 磁共振）、境外私立全髋置换外科手术以及非必需的自费医美整形等。

As a support worker, you can uphold the principle of justice by showing concern for all clients, regardless of their conditions or temperaments. For example, some people are easier to work with than others, so you may be tempted to spend less time with a client who is demanding and ungrateful. Or you may wish to avoid a client whose lifestyle is very different from your own. However, doing so would be unjust and unethical. Every client deserves your attention and care equally.

作为一名一线支持工作者，你捍卫公平正义原则的最佳实践，在于对你所照料的每一位案主展现出始终如一的平等关切，绝不因其病情轻重或性格脾气而厚此薄彼。例如，某些脾气温和的案主相处起来轻松愉快，而另一些案主则可能百般挑剔苛刻、甚至冷漠不知感恩；在潜意识中，你可能会不由自主地想在挑剔的案主身上少花时间，或者刻意疏远躲避那些生活方式与你格格不入的案主。然而，这种区别对待在职业伦理上是完全非正义且严重违背医德的。每一位案主都完全同等地配得你的全神贯注与专业照料。

Treating people justly also means that you do not betray their trust. Clients trust that you will handle their possessions with care, respect their privacy, perform your services competently and skillfully, and keep all conversations and health information confidential. Beneficence means doing or promoting good. The principle of beneficence is central to your work. Nonmaleficence

不伤害原则（Nonmaleficence）

The principle of nonmaleficence means seeking to do no harm. In health care, this harm can be intentional or unintentional. Support workers must never intentionally harm a client. Negligence, carelessness, and failing to follow instructions can cause unintentional harm. To apply nonmaleficence, you must work within your scope of practice. Sometimes clients or family members may ask you to perform functions that could be dangerous if not performed correctly. Most often, such requests are made innocently.

不伤害原则（Nonmaleficence）的核心精髓在于竭尽一切所能避免对受助者造成任何伤害。在医疗卫生与支持服务领域，伤害既可能源于蓄意故意（Intentional），亦可能源于无心疏忽（Unintentional）。支持工作者绝不可以蓄意实施任何形式的身体虐待、言语侮辱或情感伤害；然而在临床中，粗心大意、玩忽职守或盲目擅自违抗标准作业流程，同样会导致极度惨烈的非蓄意人身损害。 践行不伤害原则的最关键前提，在于时刻严守法定执业范围（Scope of practice）。在临床一线，案主或其家属有时会随口请求你执行某些一旦操作失误极具致命危险的高阶医疗动作（如无菌伤口复杂换药、调节输液滴速或注射药物等）。

The client may forget that you are not qualified to do certain tasks. Although this confidence in you as a support worker is to be appreciated, it is not safe or wise to take on tasks that you are not trained to do, even if you have the best of intentions. Clients and their family members may also ask you for information about diagnoses or medical, surgical, or treatment plans. You must never reveal these details, whether or not you are asked. In doing so, you could give the wrong information and cause harm or distress. Not only is giving or discussing medical information outside your scope of practice, it is also unethical. Refer all such questions to your supervisor.

在绝大多数情况下，家属提出这些越权要求完全是无心的，他们往往遗忘了支持工作者并非持牌医生或注册护士。尽管案主家庭对你个人专业能力的充分信任值得感激，但盲目逞能接揽那些你未曾接受过法定系统培训与考核的操作是极其危险且极其愚蠢的，哪怕你的出发点满怀善良的好意！ 此外，案主及其家属常会焦急地向你追问有关疾病确诊诊断、医学检查化验指标、外科手术方案或专科预后等核心临床细节。请时刻牢记：无论案主或家属如何软磨硬泡，你绝对不可以擅自向其泄露或解读任何诊断预后信息！盲目违规探讨医疗诊断不仅会给出完全错误的虚假信息从而导致案主遭受极度恐慌应激与误导损害，更属于严重的非法越权非法行医，在伦理与法律上皆属严惩违规。遇到此类所有医学咨询，必须委婉而坚定地讲明职责红线，并立刻引荐其向责任护士或主管医生咨询。

To provide safe and effective care, you must keep your skills and knowledge current. Participate in educational programs offered by your employer. Consider enrolling in courses or workshops relevant to your work. Support work is continually changing. What you are trained to do this year may become outdated in a few years. The more knowledge and practice you get, the better and safer your skills will be. As a support worker providing care, you must keep clients as safe as possible. You can protect them from harm by practising infection control techniques (see Chapter 19) and by recognizing common safety hazards and knowing how to prevent accidents (see Chapter 20).

为了保障照护的绝对安全有效，你必须终身学习并保持自身专业技能与临床知识的与时俱进。积极参加用人单位开设的各项继续教育培训项目；踊跃报读与工作密切相关的专科进阶认证研习班。支持工作行业正在飞速演进革新，你今年所学到的某些操作标准可能在短短数年后就已被全新循证指南所替代。你所储备的专业学识越渊博、实操模拟越扎实，你的床旁照护手速与安全系数就越无可挑剔。 作为一线照料者，将案主置于最高级别的安全防护之下是你的天职。你必须通过一丝不苟地严格执行院感控制技术（如无菌洗手、穿脱隔离衣，详见第 19 章）、敏锐识别日常起居环境中形形色色的危险隐患并精通防范意外跌倒与伤害的全部金科玉律（详见第 20 章），筑起捍卫案主生命的钢铁长城。

Codes of ethics provide only guidelines for ethical behaviour. They do not have the answers or rules for every situation.

职业伦理守则仅仅为我们的日常行为提供了崇高的方向指引。它们绝不可能为现实世界中千变万化的每一个棘手困局都预先准备好现成的标准答案或机械教条。

    该方案是否会直接对案主造成身体心理损伤，或者隐性推高案主遭受意外伤害的风险？

Mr. Adamson’s illness has progressed to a point at which he is unable to stand, turn himself in bed, or toilet himself. He is being cared for at home, and you are one of the support workers at the agency that has been contracted to provide care and support for him.

You feel frustrated by your inability to change Mr. Adamson’s situation. KEY POINTS

第 11 题（C）：当案主的自决权（因剧痛抗拒搬动）与不伤害原则（长期卧床导致严重压疮溃烂）发生剧烈冲突时，首要步骤是召开多学科个案研讨会，携手案主、家属与护理团队共同探寻人性化的折衷解决方案（如预先镇痛后使用特级防压疮气垫床或轻柔减压翻身法）。
