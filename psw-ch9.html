LEARNING OBJECTIVES · 执业胜任力标准

OBJECTIVES

本章学习与执业考核目标 (10 大核心能力指标)
After reading this chapter, the learner should be able to do the following:
完成本章研读后，学习者应当能够达成以下核心专业能力考核目标：
  • • Describe the communication process.
    详尽阐述包含发送者、信息编码、接收者与即时双向反馈在内的人际沟通动态闭环机制。
  • • Describe verbal and nonverbal communication.
    深入辨析言语沟通与非言语沟通（面部表情、眼神、姿势体态与触碰）的深层心理内涵与临床互补机制。
  • • List common defence mechanisms and explain how they can interfere with effective communication.
    系统列举临床常见的心理防御机制（否认、置换、退行、压抑等），并深度剖析其如何阻碍人际良性交流。
  • • Define professional communication and describe how it differs from other types of communication.
    界定专业医疗护理沟通（Professional Communication）的核心本质，并清晰区分其与日常社交沟通的界限。
  • • Explain the methods of and barriers to effective communication.
    熟练掌握促进有效沟通的核心实操技巧，并高度警惕识别破坏医患信任的各类严重沟通障碍（Barriers）。
  • • Explain how messages can be misinterpreted when conveyed electronically.
    深入剖析通过电子邮件、即时通讯或社交媒体等电子渠道传递信息时容易发生语义曲解的深层诱因。
  • • Describe how to communicate with an angry client.
    熟练掌握面对情绪狂躁、愤怒或具有潜在攻击倾向案主时的危机去升级（De-Escalation）黄金降维法则。
  • • Explain why assertive communication is important.
    全面阐明坚定自信沟通风格（Assertive Style）在维护专业界限与保护案主核心权益中的不可替代价值。
  • • Learn how to explain procedures and tasks to clients.
    系统掌握在执行日常护理与操作流程前及进行中，循序渐进向受助者解释沟通的实操规范与技巧。
  • • Explain ways to effectively communicate with clients living with dementia.
    深入掌握与罹患阿尔茨海默病等认知障碍（Dementia）长者建立同理心、维持人格尊严的黄金沟通策略。

FACTORS THAT INFLUENCE COMMUNICATION

影响沟通效果的核心生理、心理与环境多元因素

Interpersonal communication is the exchange of information between two people, usually face to face. A message is sent by one person (the sender) and is received and interpreted by another person (the receiver). Often, the receiver provides information in response to the message (feedback). During the exchange of information, each person usually acts as both a sender and a receiver of messages. As they send and receive messages, they try to understand each other (FIGURE 9.1). Numerous factors can influence both the sending and the receiving of messages:

人际沟通（Interpersonal Communication）本质上是两个人之间——通常是面对面进行的——信息双向交换流转过程。信息最初由一个人（信息发送者，Sender）完成思想编码并发出，由另一个人（信息接收者，Receiver）接收并展开认知解读与释义。随后，接收者往往会向发送者回馈响应信息（即双向反馈回路，Feedback）。在信息的动态交互中，沟通双方通常同时兼具发送者与接收者的双重角色；随着信息的往返穿梭，双方努力达成深度的互信与共识（见图 9.1）。然而在真实的临床实践中，诸多深层主客观因素会强烈干扰信息的发送与解码过程：

Perception. Perception is the process of organizing and interpreting information to give it meaning. Two people can experience the exact same event and interpret it in completely different ways based on their perceptual filters.

知觉感知（Perception）：知觉是个体对外界感官刺激进行组织整合并赋予特定主观意义的认知加工过程。两个人置身于完全相同的客观物理场景中，往往会因知觉过滤器的不同而得出截然相反的主观解读！

Experience. Past life experiences shape the way people view the world and how they interpret communication from others. A person who has experienced trauma in a hospital setting may view routine nursing actions with deep suspicion or fear.

既往经验（Experience）：个人过往的人生阅历、童年遭遇与医疗经历会从根本上塑造其审视世界的视角。一位曾经历过严重医源性创伤的案主，可能会对护理人员完全合规的日常查房产生极度的戒备与恐惧。

Physical and mental health. Severe pain, fatigue, sensory loss (such as hearing impairment), depression, or anxiety can severely distort communication.

身心健康状况（Physical and Mental Health）：剧烈的躯体疼痛、深度躯体疲劳、感官机能衰退（如严重听力损伤）、临床抑郁或急性焦虑，都会直接瓦解或严重扭曲信息的正常传递。

Emotions. Intense feelings—such as anger, fear, grief, or euphoria—directly influence how a person expresses themselves and how they decode incoming messages.

情绪状态（Emotions）：极端强烈的情感波澜——如暴怒、濒死恐惧、丧亲剧痛或狂躁兴奋——会直接操纵个体的言语表达方式，并阻碍其理性解码对方的善意信息。

Values and beliefs. Cultural, spiritual, and personal values govern what people consider appropriate to discuss, how eye contact is made, and which topics are considered taboo.

价值观与信仰（Values and Beliefs）：文化传统、宗教信仰与个人伦理价值观直接界定了何种话题属于公开禁忌、眼神交汇应当保持何种距离、以及身体部位的触碰界限。

Beliefs, Cultural Influences, Gender, and Age

文化信仰、性别沟通风格差异与代际沟通特征

Beliefs and cultural influences. As a support worker, you will be working with clients from different cultures. The health care team will need to research these cultures to be able to communicate appropriately with clients and to respect their dignity and their willingness to share information.

信仰与多元文化影响：作为一名专业支持工作者，你将每天与来自多元文化、宗教信仰与种族背景的服务对象密切互动。跨学科医疗照护团队必须主动钻研各文化的民俗传统与沟通礼仪，以便与案主建立得体和谐的沟通桥梁，全方位捍卫其人格尊严，并鼓励其坦诚倾诉病情与实际照护诉求。

Gender. Males and females communicate in different ways. Numerous books and other sources of information are available to help you understand the differences in communication styles between the genders. Males tend to use less verbal communication and are more likely to initiate conversations and address issues directly. Females tend to disclose more personal information, use more active listening (see page 134), and respond in ways that encourage and maintain conversations.

性别沟通范式差异：男性与女性在沟通表达与信息加工习惯上存在显著的统计学特征差异。大量专业文献表明：男性在日常交流中倾向于使用较少的话语量，更偏好就事论事、直接切入问题核心并提出解决方案；而女性则倾向于分享更多内心感受与个人生活细节，更善于运用积极倾听技巧（详见第 134 页），并以富于同理心的互动模式持续维系、深化对话的温度。

Age. People within an age group or culture may use expressions and terms that are unfamiliar to you. Younger people, for example, often use slang or abbreviations that older generations do not understand. Similarly, older clients may use dated terms that younger caregivers might misunderstand. Treat generational differences with patience and respect.

年龄与代际语境差异：特定年龄群体的个体往往会高频使用同龄人专属的俗语、俚语或流行术语。例如，年轻一代常常习惯使用网络缩写或现代流行俚语，这对于高龄长者而言往往犹如天书；与此相对，经历过特定历史时期的长者可能习惯使用古老传统的典故词汇，常令年轻护理人员一头雾水。面对代际文化差异，必须始终秉持极大的同理耐心与由衷尊重，切忌盲目主观臆断。

VERBAL COMMUNICATION

言语沟通：词汇选择、语音语调与倾听契约

Verbal communication is the exchange of messages sent through the spoken word. The words you choose, how you pronounce them, the tone and pitch of your voice, and the speed at which you speak all convey meaning. Spoken words can heal, comfort, and build trust—or they can inflict profound emotional wounds, intimidate, and destroy therapeutic relationships.

言语沟通（Verbal Communication）是指借助有声口语词汇所进行的思想信息传递。你所精选的每一个词句、发音的清晰度、语音的抑扬顿挫、语调的高低急缓、乃至说话的语速节律，都在时刻向案主辐射着深层的心理信号。温暖严谨的话语能够抚平病痛创伤、安抚恐惧心灵并构筑牢不可破的医患信赖；反之，轻率刺耳的措辞则会造成不可逆的心理创伤、激发防御敌意，并彻底摧毁宝贵的治疗性医患关系。

Electronic Communication and Social Media Electronic communication involves transmitting information through digital channels such as emails, text messages, and electronic health record (EHR) systems. In modern Canadian health care, electronic communication enhances efficiency and multidisciplinary coordination. However, digital messages lack nonverbal cues (such as facial expressions and tone of voice), making them exceptionally vulnerable to misinterpretation. Always maintain a professional, courteous, and objective tone in all electronic correspondence.

电子通讯规范与社交媒体执业红线： 电子通讯（Electronic Communication）是指借助数字化渠道（如电子邮件、加密短信以及电子病历 EHR 系统）所展开的信息交互。在当代加拿大医疗卫生体系中，数字化通讯极大赋能了跨学科协同效率。然而必须清醒认识到：数字化纯文本彻底剥离了至关重要的非言语线索（如面部表情与语调体温），极易诱发严重的语义曲解。在所有电子通讯往来中，必须始终恪守客观、严谨、专业与礼貌的基本原则。

Guidelines for Effective Verbal Communication

言语沟通黄金准则：严谨选词、觉察情绪与声调管理

To effectively communicate with words, you need to do the following:

为了确保言语沟通达成最高水准的临床精确度，你必须严格恪守以下黄金准则：

• Choose your words carefully. Words must have the same meaning for both you and the other person. Try to avoid words with more than one meaning. For example, the words small, moderate, and large mean different things to different people. Is 'small' the size of a pea or the size of a walnut? Instead, use words that are specific and descriptive: Telling your supervisor that a client's temperature is 37.9°C is clearer than saying, 'His temperature is up.'

• 慎重精准选词：所选词汇在双方认知中必须指代完全相同的客观概念！坚决摒弃模棱两可、主观模糊的多义词汇。例如，“轻微、中度、大量”在不同人心目中的尺度大相径庭。“小创口”是指豌豆大小还是核桃大小？支持工作者必须始终采用具体量化的描述性语言：向主管护士汇报“长者体温为 37.9°C”，远比含糊其辞地说“他体温有点高”更严谨可靠！

• Be aware that emotions can affect your communication. A person who is angry or upset can unintentionally convey these feelings when communicating with others. This display of emotion may, in turn, confuse or upset the listener, who is not aware of the background context of why you are communicating in such a way.

• 深度觉察情绪对沟通的潜移默化：处于愤怒或委屈挫败中的人，往往会在言谈间不自觉地外泄负面攻击性气场。这种情绪外溢会瞬间让不知背景内情的听者感到困惑、难堪或本能抗拒。

• Control the tone and volume of your voice. How your voice sounds sends a powerful message. Do not shout, because shouting can suggest irritation or anger (FIGURE 9.3). Similarly, do not talk in a harsh or abrupt manner. As well, avoid speaking to adults in high-pitched tones as you might when speaking to children (an offensive habit known as 'elderspeak').

• 严格调控语音语调与音量分贝：你的声音质感所传递出的信息往往压倒言语字面本身！严禁对着案主大声呼喊呵斥，因为叫嚷会被本能解读为暴躁、厌烦或敌意（见图 9.3）。同样，说话切忌生硬粗暴。此外，面对成年或高龄服务对象时，严禁使用哄骗婴幼儿般的尖细高音调（即严重侵犯人格尊严的所谓‘幼化老人语’Elderspeak）！

• Be brief and concise. Do not add unrelated or unnecessary information. Focus on the core message and keep explanations simple.

• 言简意赅，直击要点：切勿夹带冗余琐碎、与照护主题毫无关联的信息。突出核心事实，解释流程务必简明易懂。

• Speak distinctly and slowly. Enunciate your words clearly, especially when communicating with older adults who may experience gradual age-related hearing decline.

• 吐字清晰，语速从容：字正腔圆地咬字发音，控制匀速平稳的节奏，这对于伴随听力生理性衰退的高龄长者尤为关键。

• Do not pretend to understand. If you do not understand what the client has said, ask them politely to restate or rephrase the message. Repeat the message, if needed, to indicate that you have understood it correctly.

• 绝不弄虚作假、假装听懂：如果你由于口音、方言或环境噪音未能听清案主的话，切忌装模作样点头附和！应当诚恳礼貌地请求对方：“请原谅，我刚才没能听清，您能换个说法再讲一遍吗？”并在必要时复述其核心词句以闭环确认。

NONVERBAL COMMUNICATION

非言语沟通：肢体语言、面部表情、眼神与沉默的力量

In nonverbal communication, messages are sent without words through body language, touch, and the use of silence. The meaning and interpretation of messages can be affected by the relationship between the sender and receiver, cultural backgrounds, and physical environment. Nonverbal communication often reflects a person's true feelings more accurately than spoken words, because body movements, facial micro-expressions, and involuntary postures are far harder to consciously control or disguise.

非言语沟通（Nonverbal Communication）是指彻底摆脱有声词汇载体，纯粹借助身体语言、物理触碰以及神圣的沉默留白所实现的信息辐射与情感交融。非言语信息的深层内涵与最终解读，深刻受制于沟通双方的既有信赖程度、深层文化根基以及周遭物理环境。在临床心理学上，非言语沟通往往比精雕细琢的口头言语更能毫无保留地折射出一个人的真实心理与潜意识情感，因为瞬息万变的面部微表情、本能的肌肉紧绷与身体姿态，远远难以被人为主观刻意伪装或掩饰！

Body Language: Facial Expressions, Eye Contact, Posture, and Demeanor

肢体语言深度解析：面部表情、眼神交汇、站坐姿势步态与专业仪态

Body language sends messages through facial expressions, gestures, posture, body movements, and general appearance.

肢体语言（Body Language）通过面部表情、手势动作、躯体姿势、运动步态以及整体外表仪容时刻向外界发射着海量信息：

• Facial expressions. The face is the most expressive part of the human body. Smiles convey warmth and acceptance, while frowns, rolled eyes, or tight lips can convey disapproval, impatience, or disgust. Clients constantly scan your face to see if you genuinely care about them or if you are repulsed by their illness or physical limitations.

• 面部微表情：面孔是整个人体最具表达力与穿透力的器官。由衷的微笑传递着温暖、关怀与全然接纳；而紧锁的双眉、轻蔑的翻白眼或紧抿的唇角，则无情宣泄着内心深处的否定、不耐烦乃至嫌恶反感。案主无时无刻不在紧盯你的面庞，试图捕捉你究竟是真的在关爱呵护他们，还是在内心对他们的残缺病躯或排泄气味感到厌烦反感！

• Eye contact. Eye contact shows interest and trustworthiness in Western culture. However, staring can make clients feel uncomfortable, intimidated, or scrutinized. In many Indigenous, Asian, and Middle Eastern cultures, prolonged direct eye contact with an elder or authority figure is considered disrespectful or aggressive. Always adapt your eye contact to the client's cultural background and personal comfort level.

• 眼神交汇（Eye Contact）：在主流西方文化中，坦荡自然的目光接触象征着诚恳、专注与高度值得信赖；然而毫无节制的死死凝视则会让案主感到极度不适、被审判或遭遇心理威慑。在许多加拿大原住民（First Nations）、亚裔以及中东传统文化中，与长者或医护权威长久直视被视为无礼、傲慢与冒犯挑衅。你必须根据案主的文化脉络与舒适阈值，灵活微调目光接触的频度与柔和度。

• Posture and gait. How you stand, sit, and walk communicates your attitude. An upright, relaxed posture conveys confidence and readiness to help, whereas slumping or leaning heavily can suggest boredom, fatigue, or indifference. Walking briskly may convey competence, but rushing frantically into a client's room can provoke severe anxiety.

• 姿势体态与步态（Posture & Gait）：你的站立、端坐与行走姿态直接折射出你的执业态度。挺拔而松弛的端正姿势彰显着专业自信与随时施以援手的热忱；而无精打采的弯腰驼背或瘫倒倚靠，则赤裸裸暴露出厌烦、懈怠与漫不经心。沉稳从容的步伐彰显干练专业，但火急火燎、风风火火冲进病房则会瞬间引发案主巨大的惊恐与焦虑！

• Professional Demeanor and Appearance. Your uniform, grooming, and general presence communicate volumes before you speak a single word. Wrinkled uniforms, overwhelming perfumes, visible provocative tattoos, or unkempt hair can undermine client confidence in your clinical competence.

• 专业仪态与外表着装：在你开口说出第一个单词之前，你的护士服整洁度、仪容仪表已然向案主与家属完成了第一轮专业背书。褶皱邋遢的制服、刺鼻浓烈的香水（易诱发呼吸道哮喘）、带有攻击性暴力图案的裸露纹身或蓬乱未束的头发，都会严重摧毁案主对你临床照护能力的信任基石！

Providing Culturally Sensitive Communication

专栏 9.1 提供文化敏感性沟通的临床黄金法则
Canada is an ethnically diverse nation. To provide culturally safe and sensitive communication:
1. Educate yourself regarding the client's cultural customs, greeting rituals, and dietary/hygiene practices.
2. Respect personal space norms—some cultures prefer close proximity, while others require generous physical distance.
3. Understand gender roles: In certain conservative cultures, personal care may only be performed by a caregiver of the same biological sex.
4. Do not make assumptions or rely on stereotypes; treat every client as an individual with unique needs.
5. When language barriers exist, use certified medical interpreters rather than relying on young family members for complex clinical discussions.
加拿大是一个多元文化交融的国家。为了落实文化安全与文化敏感性沟通：
1. 主动学习案主所在族群的民俗传统、问候礼节以及饮食卫生习惯；
2. 严格尊重人际空间距离（Personal Space）规范——部分文化习惯亲近接触，而另一些文化则要求保持严谨的物理距离；
3. 深刻理解性别角色禁忌：在特定保守传统文化中，私密身体护理必须绝对由同性别护理人员执行，切忌硬性勉强；
4. 坚决摒弃任何刻板成见（Stereotypes），将每一位案主视为拥有独特诉求的独立个体；
5. 当面临语言障碍时，必须通过正规合法的专业医疗翻译人员进行重大医疗沟通，绝不可轻率依赖年幼的未成年家属充当翻译！

Humour in Health Care

幽默（Humour）在临床照护中的双刃剑效应与应用禁忌

Humour is an important, but often underused, technique in communicating with clients and co-workers. Research has shown that laughter shared among clients, family members, and the support worker can reduce tension, increase trust, and promote bonding.

幽默（Humour）是一门在医患交流与同仁协作中至关重要、却常常未被充分善用的高阶沟通艺术。临床心理学实证研究表明：支持工作者与案主及家属之间由衷爆发的笑声，能瞬间融化紧绷的焦虑防线、大幅增进人际信任并构筑深厚的情感共鸣。

When caring for clients whose first language is not the same as your own, however, you may not be able to use verbal humour, because it may be misunderstood or misinterpreted. Gauge whether the use of humour would be both comfortable for you as a support worker and appropriate for your client.

然而，在面对第一语言与你不同的多元文化案主时，口头言语幽默必须慎之又慎——因为双关语与文化隐喻极易被误解为嘲笑或轻浮冒犯！你必须极其审慎地权衡：幽默的时机是否恰到好处，既符合你本人的专业角色，又能完全契合案主当下的心理承载能力与文化语境。

Supporting Mr. Reyes: Verbal and Nonverbal Behaviours Send Differing Messages

临床思维修炼：当案主的言语表达与非言语肢体信号发生剧烈冲突时如何破局？
You have been providing supportive services in the home of Mr. Reyes for several months. His physical condition has deteriorated, and he now requires 24-hour nursing facility care. You are making your last home visit today and ask him how he is doing. Mr. Reyes says: 'I am looking forward to moving to the nursing home. I am sure I will make some new friends.' However, you observe tears welling in his eyes and he quickly looks away down at the floor. His verbal statement suggests optimism, but his body language radiates intense grief, abandonment, and loss.

Professional Response: Never take conflicting verbal statements at face value. Acknowledge and validate the unspoken emotion gently: 'Mr. Reyes, you are telling me you look forward to making friends, but I see tears in your eyes. It is completely normal and heartbreaking to leave your home of 40 years. Would you like to sit together and talk about it?' This therapeutic intervention honors the client's emotional truth without humiliating him.
数月以来，你一直在雷耶斯先生（Mr. Reyes）家中为其提供上门照护服务。由于躯体机能出现不可逆的严重恶化，雷耶斯先生如今必须迁入 24 小时全日制长期照护机构。今天是你在他家中执行的最后一次家访，你关切地询问他感觉如何。雷耶斯先生挤出笑容说道：“我很期待搬去养老院呢，我相信在那里一定能结识许多新朋友。”然而，你敏锐地捕捉到他眼眶中噙满了泪水，并迅速将目光闪躲避开、低下头凝视地板。他的口头言语貌似乐观从容，但他的非言语肢体信号却赤裸裸宣泄着无尽的悲伤、眷恋与被遗弃感！

专业破局对策：坚决不可对这种自相矛盾的言语敷衍了事！优秀的支持工作者应当以极尽温柔的语调验证其未曾言说的深层情感：“雷耶斯先生，您口中虽然说着期待结识新朋友，但我看到了您眼中的泪光。生活了四十年的老宅要舍弃离开，换作任何人内心都会痛彻心扉。愿意跟我坐下来好好聊聊您的心事吗？”这一同理干预既精准守护了案主的情感真实性，又绝不伤及男人的自尊边界！

Touch: The Healing Power, Professional Boundaries, and Cultural Taboos

触碰（Touch）：治愈体温、专业执业边界与文化禁忌红线

Touch is a very important form of nonverbal communication. It can convey warmth, comfort, concern, affection, trust, and reassurance. For example, holding a person's hand can provide comfort, and gently stroking a person's shoulder or back can promote rest and relaxation. The use of touch is very personal. While most people respond well to a touch on their hand because it helps them feel less lonely, not everyone would feel comfortable being hugged or patted on the head. Your touch should be gentle, not hurried or rushed, and should be restricted to 'public' areas of the body, such as the hands or shoulders. It is a good rule to ask permission from a client to hug them. Your employer and the care plan will direct your care.

物理触碰（Touch）是非言语沟通中极具穿透力与治愈潜能的核心形式。真诚专业的触碰能够传递出无尽的温暖、慰藉、同理关怀、由衷信赖以及强大的心理支撑。举例而言，轻轻握住长者颤抖的手能瞬间驱散冰冷的孤独感；轻柔地抚慰案主的肩膀或背部，能显著缓解骨骼肌痉挛并诱导身心松弛。然而，触碰也是人类最具隐私性与个体差异的身体互动。虽然绝大多数长者对握手报以温暖的感激回应，但绝非所有人都愿意接受拥抱或被拍打头部！你的触碰动作必须始终轻柔、坚定且从容不迫，严禁慌张粗暴；在非私密护理操作期间，身体接触必须严格限制于所谓的‘社交公共区域’（如手部、前臂或肩部）。如果意图给予案主安慰性的拥抱，事前征询其明确口头同意是一条不可动摇的黄金铁律！一切身体接触必须严格受雇主管理章程与护理计划的指引。

Touch can mean different things to different people. Pulling away or tensing of the body may indicate that the client does not want to be touched because they are in pain and it hurts to be touched. Or they may be uncomfortable with touch because of their cultural background. For some people who have experienced torture, spousal or child abuse (physical, sexual, or both), rape, or bullying, any touching from another person may be beyond uncomfortable—it may trigger flashbacks of profound trauma. It is very important that you ask your clients for permission each time you touch their body or possessions.

更为重要的是：触碰在不同人群心目中承载着截然相悖的深层隐喻！当案主本能地缩回肢体或全身肌肉剧烈紧绷时，往往意味着他们正遭受着剧烈的器质性疼痛（害怕触碰诱发更剧烈的绞痛），或是由于深层文化禁忌而对异性触碰感到极度羞耻；而对于那些曾经不幸经历过战争酷刑、家庭暴力虐待（躯体虐待、性虐待或精神虐待）、强暴创伤或恶性霸凌的创伤后应激（PTSD）幸存者而言，任何外在突如其来的肢体触碰都可能瞬间诱发极其可怕的创伤闪回与重度惊恐发作！因此，每一次触碰案主的身体或移动其私人物品之前，必须始终礼貌征求其书面或口头知情同意！

Silence: The Power of Therapeutic Presence

沉默（Silence）：治疗性静默的同理力量与心智安抚价值

The use of silence can convey messages of acceptance, rejection, fear, or the need for quiet and time to think. As a support worker, you may find it difficult to accept silence when you want to comfort your client. Sometimes, however, especially during sad times, you do not need to say anything; just being there shows that you care.

沉默（Silence）绝非沟通的缺失，而是非言语互动中极富张力的一种高级形式。沉默可以传递出彻底的无声接纳、冷酷的拒绝隔离、极度的惊恐窒息、或是极度渴望独处静思的深层心理诉求。

Silence can give you and others time to organize thoughts and choose words. It is also useful when the client is making difficult decisions or is upset and is trying to regain control. In these situations, silence on your part shows respect and empathy for the client.

作为一名初入临床的支持工作者，当眼见案主深陷悲伤痛苦时，你往往会感到一种坐立不安的冲动，迫切想要说点什么来‘打破僵局’。然而现实中——尤其在生离死别的至暗悲恸时刻——任何贫瘠苍白的口头言语往往都是徒劳无力的；此时此刻，你的纯粹守候与安详静默，本身就是对案主最深沉的关切与陪伴！沉默赋予了双方沉淀思绪、重组语言的宝贵窗口；当案主正面临人生重大医疗抉择、或是情绪处于崩溃边缘正在努力重拾自控力时，你的神圣静默是对案主人格尊严与悲伤权利的最大敬意与同理！

CASE STUDY: Silence and Touch During Sad Times

真实案例研析：在案主至亲离世的极度悲恸时刻——善用沉默与温暖执手的治愈奇迹
How do you show your client that you care? Words may not be enough. You may have to be completely silent and comfort the client just with your touch if it is culturally allowed and the client gives permission. Often, holding the client's hand can provide more comfort than words can. Jessica, a support worker in a long-term care facility, relates this experience:

'Mrs. Robinson has lived in our facility for 3 years. She is severely disabled by osteoarthritis. She is a friendly, cheerful woman who rarely complains. One morning, she didn't reply when I knocked on her door. After knocking three times, I opened the door. I was afraid that she was ill. She was sitting in her wheelchair. She looked very sad. I sensed that something was wrong.

'I sat down in a chair beside her and asked if I could help. When there was no reply, I placed my hand on hers. I didn't say anything. After a few minutes, she told me that her son had just called. Her grandson had been killed in a car accident. He was 19, and he had just finished his first year at university. I told Mrs. Robinson that I felt very sad for her. We sat there quietly, my hand on hers, for 5 minutes. I asked her if anyone else at the facility knew and if there was anything I could do. She asked me to tell the nurse. Then she said, 'You are very kind to sit with me. I know how busy you are.''
你该如何向悲恸欲绝的案主证明你发自内心的深切关怀？在许多时刻，苍白的辞藻显得如此无能为力。如果文化允许且案主欣然接纳，你或许只需保持彻底的静默，仅凭充满体温的执手触碰来抚慰案主受创的心灵。在长期照护机构执业的支持工作者杰西卡（Jessica）深情分享了她的真实临床经历：

“罗宾逊夫人（Mrs. Robinson）在我们机构已经安养了三年整。她因罹患严重的骨关节炎而重度肢体失能，但她平日里总是一位性格温婉、见人就笑、从不轻易抱怨的长者。一天清晨，当我像往常一样敲击她的房门时，房内却一片死寂。在连续叩门三次无应答后，我怀着担忧轻轻推开房门，生怕她突发急性疾病。我发现她独自孤零零坐在轮椅上，眼神中透着无尽的悲凉。我直觉意识到一定发生了某种可怕的变故。

“我拉过一张椅子安详坐在她的身旁，轻声询问是否需要我的协助。面对她漫长的沉默无言，我没有急躁追问，而是轻轻将自己的手掌覆盖叠放在她冰凉苍老的手背上，静默无声。良久，她眼眶泛红、颤抖着向我倾诉：她的儿子刚刚打来国际长途，告知她最心爱的年仅 19 岁、刚刚读完大一的孙子在一场惨烈车祸中不幸当场罹难！我握紧她的手，真挚地对她说：‘听到这个消息，我的心同您一样痛。’随后整整五分钟里，我们谁也没有再多说一句话，就那样静静依偎坐着，我的手掌始终温暖地覆盖在她的手背上。在情绪平复后，我询问是否有其他员工知晓以及我能帮她做些什么，她请求我将噩耗告知主管护士，并眼含热泪动情地对我说：‘杰西卡，你能在这么忙碌的早班抽出宝贵时间静静陪我坐在这里，你真的太善良了……’”

COMMUNICATION METHODS

高效沟通核心技术与专业护患同盟建立

Certain communication skills help you share information more effectively with others and result in better relationships with people.

熟练掌握特定的专业沟通技巧，能够协助你更为高效、精准地与案主及团队共享诊疗信息，并在人际互动中建立起更加牢固、和谐的信赖关系。

Active Listening

积极倾听（Active Listening）：身心同在的无审判倾听准则

As discussed on page 134, active listening means paying close attention to a client's verbal and nonverbal communication—listening to the content, the intent, and the feelings behind the words. Remember, nonverbal clues may reveal the client's true feelings. For example, Mrs. Gorecki tells you that her knees do not hurt today. However, you observe that she is rubbing her knees and grimacing. Her nonverbal behaviour indicates that she is in pain.

正如第 134 页所系统阐述的那样，积极倾听（Active Listening）绝非仅凭耳朵被动接收声波，而是全身心、全神贯注地聚焦于案主的言语表达与非言语肢体线索——细致品味其言辞背后的真实意图与激荡情绪。请永远牢记：非言语肢体微动作往往泄露了案主内心深处未曾言说的真相！举例而言，戈雷茨基夫人（Mrs. Gorecki）微笑着对你说她今天的膝盖一点也不痛；然而你敏锐地观察到她正不由自主地用力揉搓着膝关节，面部肌肉因剧痛而剧烈扭曲抽搐——她的非言语行为赤裸裸揭示了她正遭受着剧痛的折磨！

Active listening requires you to be interested in your client and to show that you care. The following are guidelines for active listening:

积极倾听要求你发自内心地对案主充满由衷关切，并通过行动向其传递你的支持。积极倾听的五大临床黄金准则如下：

• Face the client. Your client will often need to see your facial expressions and body language to be assured that you are listening.

• 正对案主身体站立或端坐：案主必须清晰看清你的面部表情与身体姿态，方能确信你正在全神贯注地倾听其倾诉；

• Make eye contact. However, do consider cultural preferences in regard to eye contact.

• 保持坦然温和的目光接触：但必须时刻观照案主的深层文化背景与个性舒适度；

• Lean toward the client. Do not sit back with your arms crossed. Crossed arms can be interpreted as an indication that you are not open to what the client is telling you.

• 身体前倾微倾向案主：严禁后仰倚靠并紧抱双臂！胸前交叉双臂在心理学上是极其典型的防御、抗拒与封闭信号，会瞬间切断案主的倾诉欲望；

• Respond to your client. Note, however, that sometimes your client just may not want to talk or may not respond to your normal communication style.

• 给予积极有温度的即时回应：同时必须敏锐觉察：有时案主只想静默独处，并不愿立刻多言；

• Avoid communication barriers. See page 139.

• 全力清除各类人为沟通壁垒（详见第 139–140 页）。

Paraphrasing

释义复述（Paraphrasing）：用自己的话印证核心语义

Paraphrasing is restating another person's message in one's own words. A paraphrase usually has fewer words than the original message. Paraphrasing serves three purposes:

释义复述（Paraphrasing）是指运用你自己的语言，将对方刚刚表达的核心信息与思想精炼地重述出来。释义复述通常比原话更加言简意赅。在临床沟通中，释义复述发挥着三大不可替代的战略功能：

1. It shows that you are listening. 2. It lets both you and the sender know that you understood the message. 3. It promotes further communication.

1. 明确向案主证明你正全神贯注在倾听； 2. 促使你与信息发送者双方闭环核实信息是否被精准领会； 3. 强力促进对话的进一步纵深展开。

People usually respond well to a paraphrased statement. For example:

绝大多数服务对象对准确的释义复述都会产生由衷的信任与共鸣。典型对白范例如下：

Mrs. Cummings: I was a keen reader when I could see. I miss books so much. Those talking books are hard to follow. You: You love stories, but talking books are not as good as real books. Mrs. Cummings: Exactly. I wish you had time to read to me.

卡明斯夫人（Mrs. Cummings）：“在我双眼还能看清东西的时候，我是一个极其狂热的书迷。我太怀念捧着纸质书阅读的日子了。现在那些听书有声读物听起来总是让人抓不住要点。” 你（支持工作者）：“您一直由衷深爱着书籍故事，但那些冰冷的发声读物完全无法替代真正纸质书的质感。” 卡明斯夫人：“一点没错！我真希望你有充裕的时间能天天为我朗读一段。”

When paraphrasing, try not to interpret the client's words. Guide the conversation in such a way that the client feels comfortable expressing thoughts or feelings. If you misinterpret a client's meaning, you could put an end to the conversation or cause offence (see the Case Study: Using Paraphrasing and Questioning Skills box for an example of effective paraphrasing).

在执行释义复述时，切忌过度主观脑补或随意篡改案主的原意！巧妙引导对话节奏，让案主在彻底放松的安全感中继续倾诉内心波澜。假若你严重歪曲了案主的主旨，可能会瞬间招致案主的戒备反感并彻底终结对话。

Empathetic Listening

共情倾听（Empathetic Listening）：深层情感共鸣与禁忌语避坑

Empathetic listening requires being attentive to the speaker's feelings. Empathy means being open to and trying to understand the experiences and feelings of others. It involves acknowledging the other person's point of view without judging. Clients need to know that they are understood. Empathy can help reduce feelings of loneliness and sadness and can create bonds of trust between a support worker and the clients they support.

Focusing

聚焦技巧（Focusing）：在漫无边际的跑题中锚定核心诊疗议题

Focusing means limiting the conversation to a certain topic. It is useful when a client rambles, wanders from topic to topic, or avoids talking about difficult but critical subjects. For example, if a client is discussing a minor cut on their finger while ignoring their worsening chest tightness, you might say gently: 'Mr. Chen, we will make sure your finger is bandaged, but let's talk more about the pressure you mentioned feeling in your chest.' Focusing directs the client back to urgent health priorities without being dismissive.

People usually interrupt others when they: • Jump to conclusions about what the speaker is trying to say • Become impatient with the speaker or the way the story is being told • Become bored and wish to change the subject • Are in a hurry to get things done Interrupting communicates that you think what you have to say is far more important than what the client has to say.

粗暴打断插话（Interrupting）： 在他人话音未落时强行插话会瞬间掐断沟通的生机！人们之所以打断他人，通常源于以下劣质心态： • 盲目臆断、急于妄下结论； • 对长者缓慢迟钝的语速失去耐心； • 感到枯燥厌倦、迫不及待想要转移话题； • 火急火燎赶时间、满脑子只想着快点干完活跑路！ 中途打断向案主赤裸裸宣示着：你自以为你嘴里的废话，远比案主一生的心声重要千百倍！

Answering Your Own Questions Do not ask a question and then answer it yourself before the client has had time to process and reply: 'Did you sleep well? You must have because you look rested.' This common mistake takes control away from the client and silences their true voice.

自问自答（Answering Your Own Questions）： 绝不可在提出问题后，甚至不等案主大脑反应就迫不及待自作主张替案主回答：“您昨晚睡得挺香吧？我看您气色不错肯定睡得很好！”这种极其低级的沟通漏洞直接剥夺了案主的表达主权，强行逼迫案主咽下自己的真实苦楚。

Giving Advice Do not tell clients what they should or should not do about personal matters. Giving advice places you in a position of false authority and can lead to resentment if your advice turns out poorly. Instead, empower clients to explore their own solutions and refer complex questions to the case manager or supervisor:

Minimizing Problems Do not minimize a client's problems. Avoid making comments like these: 'Everything will be fine,' 'Don't worry—it's really not that bad,' 'Look on the bright side,' and 'It could be worse.' These comments block communication and imply that the client is complaining or exaggerating the problem. They also show that you are judging the client or the situation when you have no right to do so. Minimizing problems makes people feel that you are ridiculing their concerns, feelings, and fears. Clients could believe that you do not care about what they think or feel.

轻描淡写淡化痛苦（Minimizing Problems）： 坚决禁止在案主面临危难时随意淡化其痛苦！严禁口吐以下敷衍套话：“别瞎操心了，一切都会好起来的”、“真的没那么严重，放宽心”、“多往好的一面想想看嘛”、“情况比这更糟糕的人多得是呢”！这些言辞是极其残忍的沟通阻断剂，潜台词赤裸裸嘲弄案主在无病呻吟或小题大做。这种冷酷的轻蔑会彻底摧毁案主对你的信任，让其认定你对其生死苦难漠不关心！

Using Patronizing Language: Elderspeak and Degrading Terms

居高临下式幼化语言（Patronizing Language）：摧毁人格尊严的潜规则

Sometimes, the words you use can make a person feel unimportant and inferior. These words are patronizing.

在临床执业中，某些脱口而出的词句会在不知不觉中让受助者感到极其微不足道与低人一等。这类词句被称为居高临下的幼化语言（Patronizing Language）。它无声地宣告着你自命不凡、凌驾于对方之上。为了彻底杜绝居高临下的幼化语言： • 严禁称呼成年服务对象为“小可爱（sweetie）”、“老头子（gramps）”、“亲爱的（honey/dear）”或任何腻人的所谓昵称！

They imply that you are better than the other person. To avoid using patronizing language: • Do not address clients as 'sweetie,' 'dude,' 'gramps,' 'love,' 'dear,' 'honey,' or any other term of endearment (or 'sweet talk'). • Do not use a client's first name without their permission. • Do not use terms such as 'good girl' or 'good boy' or 'you guys' with adults. • Do not use the term 'we' when you really mean 'you' (e.g., 'Are we ready for our bath today?'). • Do not use 'baby talk' or expressions such as 'There, there.' • Do not talk to co-workers or family members as if the client were not present. • Do not correct a client's speech or language.

• 未经案主明确书面或口头同意，严禁擅自直呼其名（必须始终尊称“某某先生/女士”）； • 坚决禁止对着成年人甚至古稀长者使用“真乖、真是好孩子（good girl/boy）”等侮辱性词汇； • 当你指代案主个人时，严禁使用所谓的包揽式“我们”（例如“我们今天准备好洗澡了吗？”）； • 坚决摒弃哄婴儿式的叠词儿语（Baby Talk）或拍哄式的“好啦好啦乖乖（There, there）”； • 严禁当着案主的面与同事或家属交头接耳，仿佛病榻上的案主是个毫无知觉的透明空气！• 严禁当面吹毛求疵纠正案主的语法失误或发音口音。

Introduction to Defence Mechanisms

潜意识心理防御机制概述与适应性功能

Defence mechanisms are used subconsciously in communications with others in order to delay—or avoid altogether—uncomfortable feelings such as anxiety, guilt, stress, or embarrassment. Defence mechanisms may be used for a short term or long term and may be used in positive ways or negative ways as a coping means. For example, a client who is awaiting a big surgical procedure may use denial and tell others that 'everything will be all right' in order to reduce his own presurgery anxiety.

心理防御机制（Defence Mechanisms）是个体在日常人际交往与面对惊涛骇浪般的危机时，潜意识中自动启动的心理护盾，旨在暂时推迟——或彻底逃避直面——令其极度痛苦的急性焦虑、内疚负罪感、沉重精神重压或尊严扫地般的尴尬难堪。心理防御机制既可以作为短期应急避险手段，也可能绵延数年成为长期处世风格；既可以发挥正向减震的积极适应功能，也可能走向消极病态的自我毁灭。举例而言，一位即将推入手术室面临开颅大手术的危重案主，可能会动用否认机制（Denial）对探视的亲友坚称“我这只是一场小感冒，一切都会顺风顺水”，以此竭力平复内心近乎崩溃的术前濒死焦虑。

As a support worker, you may experience difficulty when trying to communicate effectively with a client who is using a defence mechanism instead of accepting reality or facing a painful truth. Some examples of defence mechanisms are the following:

作为一名专业支持工作者，当你竭力与一位通过动用潜意识防御机制来逃避现实或抗拒直面残酷真相的案主沟通时，你往往会遭遇巨大的心理挫败感。原版教材系统归纳的十项经典防御机制如下：

• Denial: An unwillingness to accept the truth or a certain reality. For example, a person with a terminal illness refuses to believe that he is ill.

• 否认机制（Denial）：拒绝接纳客观事实或特定残酷现实。例如，一位被正式确诊为恶性肿瘤晚期的长者，坚决拒绝相信自己身患绝症。

• Displacement: A transfer of feelings (usually of anger) onto another person or item instead of onto the person who is the source of the anger. For example, a woman who is abused by her partner abuses her children instead of dealing with the conflict between her and her partner.

• 置换/转移机制（Displacement）：将针对真正压力源头的情绪冲动（通常为愤怒）无意识转移宣泄到相对安全、不易招致反击的无辜替代对象或物品身上。例如，遭受家暴虐待的女性将怒火发泄到年幼的孩子身上，而不是去正面解决伴侣冲突。

• Fantasizing: Daydreaming as an escape instead of dealing with reality. For example, a person who wishes to be a singer one day daydreams about his future singing successes instead of practising his singing.

• 幻想机制（Fantasizing）：通过脱离现实的白日梦寻求心理逃避，而不去采取实际行动直面现实。例如，梦想成为歌星的人整日沉溺于未来万众瞩目的成功幻象，却从不肯脚踏实地练习发声。

• Passive-aggressiveness: The practice of expressing negative emotions, resentment, or anger toward others through passive, indirect ways (such as through procrastination or manipulation) instead of by verbalizing feelings in an assertive manner. For example, a student who is not happy with the role her project group assigns her submits her part of the project late, affecting the rest of her team's grades.

• 被动攻击（Passive-aggressiveness）：通过消极、拖延、推诿、暗中破坏或情感操纵等间接隐蔽手段，向他人宣泄负面情绪、怨恨或怒火，而不是以坚定自信的姿态坦诚表达。例如，对小组分工不满的学生故意拖延提交自己的作业模块，从而拉低整支团队的考试总分。

• Projection: Blaming someone else for one's own behaviour. For example, a student blames her bad test marks on her roommate's noise, when in reality, the student did not prepare adequately for her test.

• 投射机制（Projection）：将自身的不良行为、过失或卑劣冲动，反咬一口归咎并硬套在他人头上。例如，考试挂科的学生抱怨室友昨晚太吵，而客观事实是该生平日根本没有认真复习备考。

• Rationalization: Justifying a behaviour or action by explaining it as being less harmful or hurtful than it really is. For example, a woman does not admit that her bad cough is a result of her heavy smoking but instead blames it on the dry air produced by the air conditioner.

• 合理化机制（Rationalization）：为自身的错误行为或不良习惯寻找看似体面、合乎逻辑的说辞，以淡化其危害程度。例如，长期重度吸烟的患者坚决不承认顽固剧烈咳嗽是烟草所致，硬把责任推给空调房间空气干燥。

• Regression: A return to an earlier developmental stage as a way to escape from stress. For example, a toddler who was already toilet-trained begins to wet her bed after a new sibling is born.

• 退行机制（Regression）：面对无法承受的沉重重压，倒退回个体生命早期幼稚不成熟的发展阶段行为模式。例如，早已完成如厕如期训练的幼儿，在母亲诞下新生二胎后突然开始频繁尿床。

• Repression: The involuntary action of blocking painful memories from the conscious mind. It is not uncommon for people with repressed painful memories to suffer from depression or other mental health disorders. For example, a victim of an assault 'forgets' that he was assaulted.

• 压抑机制（Repression）：潜意识不由自主地将极端痛苦、创伤性的记忆或道德禁忌彻底屏蔽在意识阈限之下。具有深层压抑创伤记忆的个体极易继发重度抑郁症或其他精神心理疾患。例如，暴力袭击的受害者在大脑保护机制下彻底“遗忘”了受袭细节。

• Submissiveness: A show of obedience, meekness, and compliance in the face of conflict or a difference of opinion, instead of assertiveness.

• 顺从顺从（Submissiveness）：面对人际冲突或意见分歧时，彻底放弃坚定维护自身权益的立场，表现出近乎盲目的顺从、怯懦与唯唯诺诺。

• Suppression: A conscious refusal to acknowledge a painful or upsetting situation. For example, a teenager tells her friends that she 'doesn't want to talk about it' after her boyfriend ends their relationship.

• 抑制机制（Suppression）：在清醒的意识层面，自主、理性地决定暂时拒绝直面或谈论令人痛苦难堪的危机。例如，遭遇分手重创的少女对朋友明确表示“我现在一个字也不想谈这事”。

COMMUNICATING WITH ANGRY PEOPLE

与愤怒或狂躁案主的危机化解与去升级沟通策略

Anger is a common response of both clients and family members to illness and disability (see Chapter 6). The many underlying causes of anger include frustration, anxiety, fear, and pain. Another common cause is hurt feelings—people may react with anger if they feel their self-esteem is being attacked. Loss of body function and of one's independence can also incite anger. People who are angry are often feeling helpless about a situation.

愤怒（Anger）是服务对象及其家属在直面器质性重疾与突发失能时极其普遍的心理应激反应（详见本书第 6 章）。诱发愤怒的深层根本诱因极其繁复，包括挫败绝望、剧烈焦虑、濒死恐惧以及难以忍受的肉体剧痛。另一大极其关键的根源是自尊心遭受重创——当案主感到自己的独立人格与尊严受到挑衅践踏时，往往会以滔天怒火作为防御反击。身体机能的永久丧失以及对他人全方位依赖的屈辱感，会剧烈点燃愤怒之火；愤怒的躯壳之下，往往深藏着一颗极度无助彷徨的脆弱灵魂！

Anger also is a symptom of diseases that affect thinking and behaviour. For example, people who abuse alcohol or drugs can show anger. Some people are often angry or unhappy, and few things please them. There could be numerous reasons for their demeanour. Do not judge an angry client, but do report a client's angry behaviour to your supervisor. Provide these clients with the same high-quality, compassionate care that you give all your clients.

2. Stay calm and professional: Keep your voice low, steady, and soothing. Never match the client's anger or yell back.

Comparing Communication Styles: Assertive, Passive, and Aggressive

三大沟通风格临床横向对比：果断坚定 vs 消极退缩 vs 侵犯攻击

Understanding the differences between communication styles is vital for professional practice:

深刻洞悉不同人际沟通风格的本质差异，是衡量一名支持工作者职业成熟度的试金石：

• Assertive Style: Thoughts and emotions are stated clearly, calmly, and respectfully. Body language is open, posture is relaxed and upright, and eye contact is steady. 'I understand your request, but our clinical protocol requires that two staff members assist with this transfer for safety.'

• 果断坚定型风格（Assertive Style）：以极其清晰、从容且充满由衷尊重的语调陈述事实与边界。身体姿态挺拔松弛，目光接触坦荡沉稳：“我完全理解您想尽快下床的急切心情，但按照临床安全转移规程，这项操作必须由两名护理人员协同执行以确保绝对安全，请允许我立刻去呼叫同仁协助”。

• Passive Style: Individuals fail to stand up for their rights, yielding meekly to others. They hesitate, apologize excessively, speak softly, and avoid eye contact. Passive caregivers risk extreme burnout, exploitation, and compromising client safety because they fear speaking up.

• 消极退缩/顺从型风格（Passive Style）：个体彻底丧失捍卫自身权益与患者安全的底线，唯唯诺诺盲目妥协。言语犹豫吞吐、动辄低声下气道歉、声音微弱颤抖、目光躲闪游移。消极顺从的护理人员极易深陷职业耗竭、遭受不公剥削，并因不敢向主管直言而酿成重大医疗安全隐患！

• Aggressive Style: Individuals express thoughts in a demanding, rude, or hostile manner that violates the rights of others. They shout, glare, interrupt, point fingers, and use intimidation to dominate conversations. Aggression destroys therapeutic rapport and creates toxic workplaces.

• 侵犯攻击型风格（Aggressive Style）：以跋扈专横、粗暴无礼或充满敌意攻击的姿态将自身意志强加于人，彻底践踏他人的基本权益。叫嚷咆哮、怒目而视、粗暴打断插话、手指戳点并施加心理威慑。侵犯攻击会瞬间瓦解医患信任并毒化团队协作生态。

• Passive-Aggressive Style: Expressing hostility through covert, manipulative procrastination or sullen unhelpfulness rather than open dialogue.

• 被动攻击型风格（Passive-Aggressive Style）：在表面上伪装温顺迎合，背地里却通过故意拖延、推诿懈怠、暗中使绊子或阴阳怪气等隐蔽卑劣手段泄私愤。

It is a good practice to explain every task that you are doing, prior to and while doing it, regardless of how 'routine' it might feel to you. For example, you might have assisted your client, Mrs. Jones, with toileting every day that week, but because she has dementia, she may not remember what you are doing and why you need to remove her clothing prior to toileting. Tips for Communicating With a Person With Dementia

1. Approach from the front: Never approach a person with dementia from behind or from the side, which triggers severe startle reactions.
2. Maintain gentle eye contact at eye level.
3. Address the client by their preferred name warmly.
4. Speak slowly, clearly, and in a calm, soothing, low tone.
5. Use short, simple sentences with one clear thought at a time.
6. Allow plenty of time (at least 20–30 seconds) for the client to process what you said before repeating.
7. Use familiar words and concrete nouns rather than abstract pronouns ('here is your sweater' rather than 'put this on').
8. Use positive statements rather than negative commands ('Please walk with me' instead of 'Don't run').
9. Support your words with gentle visual gestures and pointing.
10. Never argue, confront, or attempt to force logic on delusions or hallucinations.
1. 始终从正前方温和走近长者：严禁从长者正后方或侧面无声逼近，这会瞬间触发重度惊恐反击反应；
2. 保持平视视线齐平，维持柔和专注的目光交汇；
3. 每次开口前热情温和地呼唤长者喜爱的尊称；
4. 放缓语速，咬字发音清晰，保持低沉抚慰的从容语调；
5. 每次只说一句简短纯粹的短句，一次只传达一个最核心的概念；
6. 给予极度充裕的大脑处理缓冲期（每次发问后耐心等待至少 20–30 秒），切忌连珠炮似地反复催问；

What to Say and What NOT to Say to Clients Living With Dementia

认知症沟通言语规范：肯定性表达 vs 禁忌质问

What to Say: • Try to be positive. • Avoid asking too many direct questions. People with dementia can become frustrated if they can't find the answer. If you have to, ask questions one at a time, and phrase them in a way that allows for a 'yes' or 'no' answer. • Try not to ask the person to make complicated decisions. Giving someone a choice is important when they can cope with it, but too many options can be confusing and frustrating. • If the person doesn't understand what you are saying, try to get the message across in a different way. Use simpler words, or demonstrate with actions.

沟通提倡规范（What to Say）： • 始终保持积极正向的肯定语气； • 坚决避免提出过多连环直问！认知症长者若在大脑中搜寻不到答案会产生极大的挫败与焦虑。如确需问询，一次仅提一个问题，并尽量设计为只需回答“是”或“否”的闭合选项； • 切莫强迫长者做出复杂的权衡决策！当其认知尚能负荷时给予适度选择固然重要，但过多选项会瞬间导致认知超载与狂躁； • 如果长者未能听懂你的话，切忌大声重复原话！尝试换用更浅白质朴的词汇，或辅以缓慢生动的肢体示范动作。

What NOT to Say: • Do not argue with the person. If they believe it is 1952, entering into a debate will only cause severe agitation. • Do not tell them what they cannot do. Phrase statements in positive terms. • Do not ask: 'Don't you remember?' This painful question highlights their profound cognitive deficit and provokes despair. • Do not take insults personally: Remember that catastrophic reactions are symptoms of neurodegenerative disease.

绝对禁忌言论（What NOT to Say）： • 严禁与长者发生逻辑争辩！如果长者坚信现在是 1952 年自身待字闺中，强行逼其承认现实只会诱发狂躁应激； • 绝不要生硬命令长者“不能做什么”，而应以正向动作指引其“做什么”； • 坚决禁止质问：“难道您不记得了吗？（Don't you remember?）”这句残忍的话像把尖刀直插长者痛处，残酷揭开其大脑功能丧失的伤疤并诱发绝望！ • 绝不可将长者的辱骂攻击归咎于个人恩怨：请永远铭记，灾难性神经心理爆发纯属脑部不可逆退行性病变的病理症状！

Special Communication Challenges: Sensory Impairments and Speech Disorders

Hearing Impairment Guidelines: • Ensure hearing aids are clean, turned on, and properly inserted with functioning batteries. • Face the client directly with good lighting on your face so they can read lips. • Reduce background environmental noise (close doors, mute television). • Lower the pitch of your voice (high-pitched tones are lost first in presbycusis). • Use written notes or communication boards if necessary (see FIGURE 9.4).

Vision Impairment Guidelines: • Always identify yourself by name immediately upon entering the room. • Explain everything you are going to do before touching the client. • Use the clock-face technique to describe food locations on a meal plate (e.g., 'Your chicken is at 6 o'clock, carrots at 2 o'clock'). • Never move personal items or furniture without the client's explicit knowledge.

视力障碍（Vision Impairment）照护沟通准则： • 步入房间的第一秒钟，必须立刻主动清晰自报家门与姓名； • 在身体触碰到案主之前的每一瞬间，务必提前清晰预告即将执行的动作； • 采用经典‘钟表盘布局法’（Clock-Face Technique）向盲人长者描述餐盘中的菜肴定位（如“清蒸鸡肉在 6 点钟方位，胡萝卜在 2 点钟方位”）； • 严禁在案主不知情的情况下擅自挪动病房内的任何家具或生活私人物品！

Speech Disorders (Aphasia and Dysarthria): Aphasia is a neurological language disorder often resulting from a stroke. Receptive aphasia affects comprehension, while expressive aphasia affects speech production. Dysarthria is difficulty speaking due to muscle weakness. • Allow extensive time for the client to speak without interrupting or finishing their sentences. • Use visual communication boards, picture cards, and tablets. • Ask simple questions that require nodding or pointing.

言语障碍与失语症（Aphasia & Dysarthria）照护准则： 失语症（Aphasia）是脑卒中脑中风或外伤后常见的神经语言中枢损害；感受性失语症损害语言理解力，而表达性失语症损害言语吐字组织能力；构音障碍（Dysarthria）则是面部肌肉瘫痪所致的吐字不清。 • 给予极其充裕的言语组织时间，坚决禁止中途打断或自以为是地抢话替案主把话说完！ • 广泛采用高清晰度图文沟通板、实体照片卡或数字化平板电脑辅助； • 尽量采用点头、摇头或手指指示即可完成的极简提问。

  • • The communication process is the means by which people exchange information. Communication skills are used in all environments, including at work, school, and home.
    沟通循环（Communication Process）是人类传递思想信息的动态核心载体；卓越的沟通技巧在临床职场、学业研修与家庭生活中无处不在。
  • • Interpersonal communication is the exchange of information between two people, usually face to face.
    人际沟通（Interpersonal Communication）本质上是两个人之间——通常为面对面进行的——信息双向交换流转。
  • • Messages sent are sometimes misunderstood by the receiver because factors such as perception, experience, physical and mental health, emotions, values, beliefs, culture, gender, and age can influence understanding.
    发出的信息极易被接收者误读失真，因为知觉过滤、既往阅历、身心健康状况、当时情绪、价值观信仰、文化传统、性别及年龄代沟会深刻干扰理解。
  • • To communicate effectively with words, you need to choose your words carefully, be aware of how emotions affect communication, control your tone and volume, be brief and concise, and speak distinctly and slowly.
    为了实现精准高效的言语沟通，必须审慎选词、深刻觉察情绪外溢、严格调控语音语调分贝、言简意赅直击要害，并字正腔圆从容发音。
  • • Nonverbal communication is the sending of messages without words, through body language, touch, and the use of silence.
    非言语沟通纯粹摆脱有声词汇载体，借助身体语言姿态、物理触碰以及神圣的沉默留白向外界辐射真实心理情感。
  • • Communication methods include active listening, paraphrasing, empathetic listening, asking closed questions, asking open-ended questions, clarifying, and focusing.
    促进沟通的七大核心专业技术包括：积极倾听、释义复述、共情倾听、封闭式提问、开放式提问、澄清、以及聚焦。
  • • Communication barriers include interrupting, answering one's own questions, giving advice, minimizing problems, using patronizing language, and failing to listen.
    必须严厉戒除的六大常见沟通壁垒包括：粗暴打断插话、自问自答、好为人师滥给建议、轻描淡写淡化痛苦、使用居高临下的幼化语言、以及心不在焉敷衍倾听。
  • • People subconsciously use defence mechanisms to avoid uncomfortable feelings such as anxiety, guilt, stress, or embarrassment. Defence mechanisms include denial, displacement, fantasizing, projection, rationalization, regression, repression, and suppression.
    个体在潜意识层面动用心理防御机制以逃避直面焦虑、负罪自责、精神重压或难堪尴尬；主要包括否认、置换、幻想、投射、合理化、退行、压抑与抑制。
  • • At times, support workers will be faced with angry clients. Effective communication is important to preventing and dealing with anger.
    前线支持工作者必将在临床火线遭遇愤怒狂躁案主；熟练运用去升级沟通策略是防范事态恶化与化解危机的人身生命线。
  • • Assertiveness is a style of communication in which thoughts and feelings are expressed positively and directly without offending others.
    果断坚定（Assertiveness）沟通风格是指在清晰、自信、坦诚表达自身主张的同时，绝不冒犯或侵犯他人的神圣合法权益。
  • • When teaching clients new tasks, describe to the client the steps in the task, show the client how to do each step, have the client try each step, and review the client's success with each step.
    指导案主掌握全新自理操作流程的四步法则：清晰分解口述步骤、缓慢示范动作、让案主亲身尝试实操、闭环复盘并由衷肯定其点滴成就。
  • • It is especially important to pay attention to your verbal and nonverbal messages when communicating with people living with dementia. As clients lose their language skills, they rely more heavily on following nonverbal cues.
    面对认知症（Dementia）长者时必须时刻自省言行举止；随着大脑言语中枢退化，长者几乎完全依靠外界的非言语肢体线索来感知世界与安全感。

Critical Thinking in Practice: Staff Shortage and Workload Management

临床思维修炼：突发同仁缺勤与工作量倍增时——如何在从容高效与共情沟通间取得平衡？
Scenario: You are employed at a long-term care facility. A co-worker did not show up for work today. You and the other remaining staff members have extra work assigned to you. How do you respond? Can you refuse to do the extra work assigned to you? Do you complain or keep silent about this situation, maintaining a positive attitude? How will you plan, prioritize, and manage the extra work?

Professional Analysis:
1. Professional Response: As a healthcare worker, you cannot unilaterally refuse assigned care duties unless they directly endanger client safety or exceed your authorized scope of practice. Client safety and basic hygiene must be preserved.
2. Attitude & Etiquette: Venting anger in front of clients or spreading toxic negativity damages team morale and terrifies vulnerable residents. Maintain an assertive, constructive attitude.
3. Strategic Prioritization: Meet briefly with the supervising nurse immediately. Prioritize vital morning care: medication administration, urgent toileting, wound dressing, and feeding assistance. Defer non-urgent tasks (deep wardrobe reorganizing, recreational outings) to later in the shift.
4. Transparent Communication: Inform your clients with calm empathy: 'Good morning, Mrs. Gable. We have an unusually busy morning today, but I am here to ensure you are safely washed and comfortable. If you need anything urgent, please ring your call bell.'
临床真实情境：你在一家大型长期照护机构执业。今天清晨，同组的一名同事无故旷工缺勤，你与在岗的其他同仁不得不承担极其繁重的翻倍工作量。你该如何应对？你能够单方面断然拒不接受主管护士分派的额外工作吗？你应当在病房走廊里愤懑抱怨，还是保持沉着冷静、秉持积极建设性的职业态度？你将如何科学规划、区分轻重缓急并闭环管理这笔额外激增的工作负荷？

专业破局与伦理决策指引：
1. 职业合规底线：身为医护从业者，除非所分派的工作严重危及案主生命安全或严重超出法定执业范畴，否则决不可单方面任性拒绝！长者的基本生理生命维持与卫生安全高于一切；
2. 态度与职场礼仪：严禁当着病榻上案主的面大倒苦水或在走廊里散布负能量抱怨，这会严重摧毁团队士气并让无助的长者深感自己是累赘！始终保持果断坚决、积极建设性的专业姿态；
3. 战略性优先级排序（Prioritization）：清晨第一瞬间与主管注册护士举行 3 分钟紧急碰头会；对今日全盘工作施加科学分级：紧急必须立即执行项（生命体征监测、胰岛素与核心药物协同、紧急如厕排泄、压疮翻身与喂食协助）排在最高优先级；将非紧急次要琐事（衣柜深层整理、户外文娱散步）合理推迟至中下午；
4. 透明共情沟通：以气定神闲的从容语调向所负责的长者温和通报：“早安，盖布尔夫人。今天我们组里人手非常紧张忙碌，但我一定会全力确保您的晨起清洁舒适与安全用药。如果您有任何紧急诉求，请随时按响呼叫铃，我立刻就到您身旁”。
