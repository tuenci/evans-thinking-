OBJECTIVES / 执业学习目标

After reading this chapter, the learner should be able to do the following: / 学完本章后，学习者应能够做到以下各项：

  • • Describe disorientation, delirium, and dementia and their causes.
    阐述定向障碍（意识混乱）、谵妄与认知症痴呆的本质界定及其各自的器质与环境致病原因。
  • • Differentiate between normal and abnormal mental changes related to aging.
    准确区分与正常生理衰老相关的脑神经良性变化与由器质性疾病导致的病理性脑功能退化。
  • • List measures that help clients who are disoriented.
    列举能够有效协助定向障碍服务对象恢复时间、空间及人物定向感的实用护理支持措施。
  • • Differentiate between disorientation, delirium, and dementia.
    系统鉴别并对比定向障碍、谵妄（突发可逆急症）与认知症（进行性不可逆慢性病程）的核心临床差异。
  • • Describe dementia and its signs and symptoms.
    详尽描述认知症的概念内涵及其在记忆、思维、语言、判断与日常生活能力方面的典型临床症状。
  • • Explain why depression is often associated with dementia.
    解释为何抑郁障碍频繁与认知症共病，以及假性痴呆（Pseudo-dementia）的临床鉴别意义。
  • • List different forms and causes of dementia.
    列举认知症的不同临床分型（阿尔茨海默病、血管性认知症、路易体痴呆、额颞叶痴呆等）及其病理机制。
  • • Describe the common stages of dementia.
    系统阐述认知症的三阶段病程演进（早期轻度、中期中度、晚期重度）及其各自特征与支持重点。
  • • Describe how to support a client living with dementia.
    阐述如何依据 DIPPS 原则为认知症长者提供涵盖沟通、身体卫生、进食、睡眠与安全赋能的全人照护。
  • • List examples of responsive behaviours and possible causes of them.
    列举常见的反应性行为（如激越、徘徊、日落综合征、妄想、囤积等）及其背后的生理、心理与环境未满足需求。
  • • Describe how caring for family members with dementia can affect their primary caregivers.
    阐明长期照护认知症长者对家庭主要照顾者身心健康造成的深重负担、情感撕裂与照顾者倦怠风险。
  • • Apply the principles learned in this chapter to the care and support of clients with disorientation, delirium, or dementia.
    将本章所学的专业沟通原则、GPA 保护技巧与 ABCD 行为管理模型融会贯通，指导前线临床实操。

Disorientation (Confusion)

定向障碍（意识混乱）的临床界定
Disorientation, also known as confusion, is the impaired ability to recall or recognize people, time, or places as a result of physiological changes in the brain, underlying disease processes, emotional trauma, or chemical toxicity. A disoriented person may not recognize family members, may not know where they are, or may believe it is decades earlier.
定向障碍（Disorientation，亦称意识混乱 Confusion）是指由于脑部生理退化、器质性疾病进程、心理情绪重创或药物化学毒性等因素，导致个体识别或回忆人物、时间或空间地点的能力严重受损。定向障碍者可能认不出朝夕相处的亲人，不知道自己身处何方，或坚信当前仍处于几十年前的年轻岁月。

BOX 38.1: A Comparison of Normal and Abnormal Nervous System Changes / 专栏 38.1：神经系统正常生理衰老与异常病理性改变对照

  • Normal aging changes: Slower reflexes, mildly decreased sensory perception (sight, hearing, touch), slightly longer time required to process new complex information or recall familiar names.
    正常衰老改变：神经反射稍变慢、感官知觉（视力、听力、触觉）轻度迟钝、处理复杂全新信息或回忆熟人姓名需要稍长的时间，但完全能够独立安全生活并做出正确判断。
  • Abnormal changes (Disease): Inability to remember recent events or conversations, severe disorientation to time and place, loss of ability to manage finances or cook safely, personality deterioration, and loss of language.
    异常改变（器质性疾病）：对刚刚发生的事情或对话完全失忆、对时间与空间产生严重定向障碍、丧失理财或做饭自理能力、人格性格恶化，以及进行性语言表达丧失。

Measures to Help Disoriented Clients

协助定向障碍长者的照护支持措施
  • State your name and explain what you are doing before initiating care.
    在开始任何照护操作前，始终先主动报上自己的姓名并清晰解释即将进行的步骤。
  • Provide clear orientation cues: Place large-face clocks, large-print calendars, and clear season markers in the client's line of sight.
    提供清晰的环境定向线索：在长者视线所及之处放置大表盘时钟、大字号日历以及当季标志物。
  • Maintain consistent, calm daily routines for meals, hygiene, and bedtime.
    在日常进餐、个人卫生清洁和就寝时间上，保持高度稳定、连贯且安静的生活作息节律。

Delirium: An Acute Medical Emergency

谵妄：必须紧急救治的急性精神医疗急症
Delirium is a state of temporary, acute mental disorientation that develops suddenly—over hours or a few days. Unlike dementia, which progresses slowly over years, delirium has an abrupt onset and fluctuating course. Delirium is NOT a normal part of aging; it is an urgent medical emergency that signals severe underlying physiological distress.
谵妄是一种在数小时或数天内急性突发、可逆的暂时性严重精神定向障碍与意识状态混乱。与持续数年缓慢恶化的认知症截然不同，谵妄起病急骤且病情在日间剧烈波动。谵妄绝非正常衰老的一部分；它是一种危急的医学急症，标志着长者身体深层正在遭受严重的急性生理危机。

Common Causes of Delirium

诱发谵妄的常见急性病因
  • Systemic infections: Urinary tract infections (UTIs) and pneumonia are the leading causes of acute delirium in older persons.
    全身性急性感染：隐匿性尿路感染（UTI）与肺部肺炎是导致老年长者突发谵妄的首要最常见诱因。
  • Adverse drug reactions or toxicity: Interactions between multiple medications (polypharmacy), sedatives, or narcotics.
    药物不良反应或蓄积中毒：多重用药相互作用、镇静安眠药或阿片类镇痛药中毒。
  • Physiological imbalances: Severe dehydration, electrolyte collapse, acute hypoxia (low oxygen), hypoglycemia, or severe uncontrolled pain.
    生理生化失衡：重度脱水、电解质紊乱、急性低氧血症、低血糖休克或无法言说的严重剧烈疼痛。
Signs of delirium include: sudden restlessness, acute confusion, dramatic fluctuations in alertness (hyperactive vs. hypoactive), terrifying visual hallucinations, incoherent speech, and disrupted sleep-wake cycles. If delirium is recognized and its underlying physical cause treated promptly, mental functioning usually returns to baseline.
谵妄的临床体征包括：突发极度坐立不安、急性意识混乱、警觉水平剧烈摇摆（过度亢奋躁动或极度嗜睡迟钝）、生动恐怖的视幻觉、胡言乱语以及昼夜节律颠倒。如果能够及早识别并将潜在的躯体感染或生化紊乱迅速根治，长者的智力心智通常能够完全恢复至原有基线水平。

Understanding Dementia

深入认识认知症（综合征界定）
Dementia is a general, overarching clinical term that describes the progressive loss of brain functions, including cognitive, emotional, and social abilities. Dementia is not a single disease; it represents a wide umbrella of disorders that affect memory, reasoning, language, learning, judgment, and communication.
认知症（Dementia，亦称痴呆）是一个涵盖性综合征临床总称，用于描述大脑高级中枢机能（包括认知思考、情绪调节及社会交往能力）进行性丧失的病理状态。认知症绝非单一疾病，而是由多种慢性脑部疾患构成的庞大家族，波及记忆、逻辑推理、语言表达、学习吸收、辨别判断与人际沟通。

DSM-5 Classification: Neurocognitive Disorders

DSM-5 分类体系：神经认知障碍分级
In the DSM-5, dementias are categorized under Neurocognitive Disorders (NCDs), distinguished by the severity of functional impairment:
在现代 DSM-5 诊断体系中，认知症被统一归类于“神经认知障碍（NCD）”大类，依据其日常功能受损的严重程度划分为两大等级：
  • Mild Neurocognitive Disorder: Modest cognitive decline from a previous level of performance. The individual notices memory or thinking lapses, but these deficits do NOT interfere with independence in everyday activities (e.g., complex IADLs may require greater effort or compensatory strategies).
    轻度神经认知障碍（Mild NCD）：认知机能较既往基线出现轻度减退。长者本人或家属察觉到记忆或思考迟钝，但这些缺陷尚未剥夺其日常生活独立自理能力（在处理复杂金融、用药等工具性活动时可能需要借助备忘录或付出更多心力）。
  • Major Neurocognitive Disorder: Significant cognitive decline that DOES interfere with the individual's ability to live independently. The client requires progressive assistance with complex instrumental activities and basic personal activities of daily living (ADLs).
    重度神经认知障碍（Major NCD，即传统临床意义上的认知症痴呆）：认知机能发生显著病理性衰退，且直接剥夺了独立生活的能力。服务对象在完成复杂家务以及基础个人自理（穿衣、沐浴、进食、排泄）时需要不同程度的外部照护协助。

Apraxia: Motor Planning Deficits

失用症（Apraxia）：动作规划机能丧失
Apraxia is the loss of the ability to carry out learned, purposeful motor acts despite having intact physical strength and sensation. A person with apraxia cannot coordinate the brain signals required to brush their teeth, button a shirt, or use a spoon, even though their muscles are fully functional.
失用症是指在肌力、肌肉协调和感觉神经完全正常的前提下，大脑丧失了规划并执行已习得的有目的性熟练动作的能力。患有失用症的长者尽管手臂有力，却无法协调大脑信号去完成刷牙、系纽扣或使用调羹进食等连贯动作。

Primary Versus Secondary Dementias

原发性认知症与继发性认知症
Primary dementias are progressive, irreversible brain disorders that do not result from any other underlying physical disease. The disease originates directly within brain cells, causing primary neuronal death. Alzheimer’s disease is the most common primary dementia.
原发性认知症是指非由任何其他全身系统性疾病所继发的进行性不可逆脑部病变。该病直接起源于大脑皮层神经细胞本身，导致神经元进行性变性坏死死亡。阿尔茨海默病是临床上最典型的原发性认知症。
Secondary dementias, in contrast, occur as a direct consequence of an underlying physical condition, injury, or neurotoxin—such as multiple ischemic strokes, chronic thiamine deficiency, advanced Parkinson's disease, or chronic traumatic encephalopathy.
相比之下，继发性认知症则是明确由其他身体器质性疾病、外伤或神经毒物损害所直接引发的脑功能衰退——如多次缺血性中风脑梗、慢性维生素B1缺乏、晚期帕金森病或慢性脑创伤等。

Alzheimer’s Disease and Related Dementias (ADRD)

阿尔茨海默病及相关认知症（ADRD）
Alzheimer’s disease is a progressive, degenerative, and fatal brain disease that accounts for 60% to 70% of all dementia diagnoses. It gradually destroys brain tissue, causing severe memory loss, cognitive collapse, and functional dependence.
阿尔茨海默病是一种进行性、退行性且最终致死的器质性脑部疾病，占全社会所有认知症确诊病例的 60% 至 70%。它逐步侵蚀摧毁大脑组织神经元，导致严重的不可逆性记忆丧失、认知机能崩解与生活全面依赖。

Pathological Hallmark: Plaques and Tangles

阿尔茨海默病微观病理金标准：老年斑与神经纤维缠结
Under microscopic autopsy examination, the hallmark features of Alzheimer's disease are: (1) Amyloid Plaques (abnormal extracellular deposits of beta-amyloid protein fragments accumulating between neurons) and (2) Neurofibrillary Tangles (twisted microscopic strands of tau protein inside dying neurons). These abnormal structures block communication between synapses and starve nerve cells of vital nutrients, resulting in massive cortical atrophy.
在病理学尸检显微镜下，阿尔茨海默病的标志性病理特征为：（1）淀粉样蛋白老年斑（β-淀粉样蛋白片段异常沉积在神经元细胞间隙形成的毒性硬化斑块）；以及（2）神经纤维缠结（神经元细胞内部过度磷酸化的 Tau 蛋白发生扭曲缠结）。这些异常病理结构彻底阻断了神经突触之间的信息传递，切断了脑细胞的营养供应，最终导致脑皮层发生广泛萎缩与神经元大面积坏死。

Risk Factors for Alzheimer’s Disease

阿尔茨海默病的危险因素与致病机制
  • Age: Age is the single greatest known risk factor. The likelihood of developing Alzheimer's doubles every 5 years after age 65; by age 85, roughly one in three older adults is affected.
    年龄因素：年龄是目前已知最大的单一危险因素。65岁以后，个体罹患该病的概率每5年翻一倍；到了85岁以上，每3位高龄长者中就有1人受其困扰。
  • Genetics and Family History: Individuals with a first-degree relative (parent or sibling) with Alzheimer's have a higher risk. Mutations in genes such as APOE-e4 elevate susceptibility, while rare mutations in APP, PSEN1, and PSEN2 cause familial early-onset Alzheimer's.
    遗传基因与家族史：拥有患病直系亲属（父母或兄弟姐妹）的人群患病风险显著增高。携带 APOE-ε4 等位基因会大幅提高患病易感性；而 APP、PSEN1、PSEN2 基因的罕见突变则直接引发早发型家族性阿尔茨海默病。
  • Cardiovascular and Lifestyle Factors: Hypertension, high cholesterol, diabetes, obesity, smoking, and lack of physical/cognitive exercise severely impair cerebral blood flow and accelerate neuropathology.
    心血管与生活方式因素：长期高血压、高胆固醇血症、糖尿病、肥胖症、吸烟嗜好以及缺乏身心锻炼，会严重损害脑脑血流灌注并加速神经退行性病变。
  • Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI): Severe concussions or repetitive head impacts increase long-term risk of developing amyloid neuropathology.
    创伤性脑损伤（TBI）：严重脑震荡或反复头部遭受撞击，会显著增加脑内淀粉样蛋白沉积变性的长期风险。

Clinical Stages of Alzheimer’s Disease

阿尔茨海默病的三大病程阶段
Alzheimer’s disease generally progresses through three identifiable stages over an average span of 8 to 12 years: (1) Early (Mild) stage, (2) Middle (Moderate) stage, and (3) Late (Severe) stage. Each stage presents unique clinical challenges and requires adjusted support worker strategies.
阿尔茨海默病在平均 8 至 12 年的病程中，通常经历三个可清晰识别的临床演进阶段：（1）早期（轻度阶段）；（2）中期（中度阶段）；以及（3）晚期（重度终末阶段）。每个阶段均呈现独特的照护挑战，需要支持照护人员针对性调整支持策略。

Vascular Dementia

血管性认知症（Vascular Dementia）
Vascular dementia (multi-infarct dementia) is the second most common form of dementia, accounting for roughly 15% to 20% of cases. It is caused by brain damage resulting from impaired blood flow to the brain—typically following a major stroke, a series of small "silent" mini-strokes (transient ischemic attacks, TIAs), or chronic small-vessel arteriosclerosis. Its clinical course often presents a distinct "stepwise" decline, where functioning drops abruptly after each cerebrovascular event.
血管性认知症（多发梗死性痴呆）是临床第二常见的认知症类型，约占全部病例的 15% 至 20%。该病是由于脑部供血受阻引起脑组织缺血缺氧坏死所致——通常发生在大面积严重中风、一系列无声微小中风（短暂性脑缺血发作 TIAs）或慢性脑微血管硬化之后。其病程往往呈现特征性的“阶梯式”恶化（Stepwise decline），即每次脑血管小发作后功能骤然下跳一步，随后维持平台期。

Dementia with Lewy Bodies (DLB)

路易体认知症（DLB）
Dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB) is characterized by abnormal microscopic protein deposits (Lewy bodies) accumulating inside neurons in areas of the brain that regulate cognition and motor control. DLB is defined by three hallmark features: (1) marked fluctuations in attention and alertness from hour to hour; (2) recurrent, detailed visual hallucinations (e.g., seeing animals or people); and (3) spontaneous Parkinsonian motor symptoms (rigid muscles, shuffling gait, tremors). Clients with DLB are extremely sensitive to antipsychotic medications.
路易体认知症（DLB）的病理特征为异常的微观球形蛋白质沉积物（路易小体）积聚在调节认知思维与运动控制的大脑神经元胞体内部。DLB 具有三大标志性诊断特征：（1）警觉水平与注意力在日间按小时剧烈波动；（2）反复出现极其生动逼真的视幻觉（如看到不存在的小动物或陌生人）；以及（3）自发出现的帕金森样运动症状（肢体僵直、慌张步态、静止性震颤）。DLB 患者对传统抗精神病药物具有极其致命的过度敏感性。

Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD)

额颞叶痴呆（FTD）
Frontotemporal dementia (FTD, formerly Pick’s disease) is a progressive disorder involving degeneration of the frontal and temporal lobes. It frequently strikes younger individuals (ages 40 to 65). The primary symptoms are dramatic personality changes, socially inappropriate disinhibition (e.g., shoplifting, rude remarks, loss of empathy), apathy, or severe language deterioration (primary progressive aphasia).
额颞叶痴呆（FTD，曾称匹克病）是一组以大脑额叶和颞叶发生进行性萎缩变性为特征的障碍。该病常在中年早发（40至65岁）。其首发核心症状往往不是失忆，而是戏剧性的人格蜕变、严重的社会去抑制失控言行（如公然偷窃、言语粗鄙冒犯、同理心丧失）、深度淡漠，或严重的进行性语言功能丧失（原发性进行性失语症）。

Other Progressive Secondary Dementias

其他进行性继发性神经退行性认知症
  • Parkinson’s Disease Dementia (PDD): Up to 80% of individuals with advanced Parkinson's disease eventually develop dementia. Cognitive decline begins years after well-established motor symptoms (resting tremors, pill-rolling, bradykinesia, rigid posture).
    帕金森病认知症（PDD）：高达 80% 的晚期帕金森病患者最终会继发认知症。与路易体痴呆不同，PDD 的认知衰退通常是在确诊帕金森运动症状（静止性震颤、搓丸样动作、行动迟缓、僵直步态）数年之后才逐步显现。
  • Huntington’s Disease: A fatal, inherited autosomal dominant disorder beginning between ages 30 and 50. Involves progressive chorea (uncontrollable jerky, writhing body movements) combined with severe personality changes, depression, and profound dementia.
    亨廷顿舞蹈症（Huntington's Disease）：一种致命的常染色体显性遗传性脑变性疾病，多在30至50岁发病。表现为进行性加剧的舞蹈样不自主剧烈扭动痉挛，并伴有暴躁抑郁的人格剧变与重度痴呆。
  • Creutzfeldt–Jakob Disease (CJD): A rare, rapidly fatal brain disorder caused by infectious misfolded proteins called prions. Destroys brain tissue into a sponge-like matrix, causing rapid mental decline, severe myoclonus (muscle spasms), blindness, and death typically within a year.
    克雅氏病（CJD）：一种由具传染性的错误折叠朊病毒（Prions）引发的罕见急性致命性脑病。病理改变导致大脑实质呈海绵样空洞化坏死，表现为闪电般恶化的精神崩溃、严重肌阵挛抽搐、失明，患者通常在确诊一年内死亡。
  • Chronic Traumatic Encephalopathy (CTE): Progressive neurodegeneration resulting from repetitive concussions or subconcussive blows to the head (common in athletes and veterans), leading to mood lability, memory loss, and dementia decades later.
    慢性创伤性脑病（CTE）：因头部遭受反复多次脑震荡撞击（多见于职业橄榄球/拳击运动员及战地退伍军人）而引起的进行性神经退行性病变，在数十年后逐渐演变为严重情绪失控、记忆崩溃与痴呆。

Wernicke–Korsakoff Syndrome (WKS)

韦尼克-柯萨可夫综合征（WKS）
Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome is a severe neurological brain disorder caused by chronic thiamine (vitamin B1) deficiency, almost universally associated with chronic heavy alcohol abuse and severe malnutrition. It consists of two stages: (1) Wernicke's encephalopathy (acute confusion, ataxia/unsteady gait, and ophthalmoplegia/eye muscle paralysis) which is a medical emergency, and (2) Korsakoff’s syndrome (chronic, irreversible anterograde amnesia, profound inability to form new memories, and severe confabulation—making up stories to compensate for memory gaps).
韦尼克-柯萨可夫综合征是一种因长期重度酗酒与严重营养不良导致体内硫胺素（维生素B1）极度匮乏引发的严重神经系统器质性综合征。它由两个连续阶段组成：（1）急性韦尼克脑病（表现为急性意识混乱、共济失调步态不稳、眼外肌麻痹视物成双），属医疗急症；若未获及时大剂量维生素B1抢救，则不可逆转地滑入（2）慢性柯萨可夫综合征（表现为无法建立新记忆的永久性严重顺行性遗忘、虚构症——不由自主地编造荒谬离奇的虚假故事来填补记忆空白）。

Pseudo-Dementia (Depression Mimicking Dementia)

假性痴呆（抑郁症假扮的认知衰退）
Pseudo-dementia ("false dementia") occurs when severe clinical depression in older adults produces cognitive deficits that closely resemble true dementia—such as slowed thinking, impaired recall, indecisiveness, and memory complaints. Unlike true dementia, where clients typically try to mask deficits or confabulate, clients with pseudo-dementia readily answer "I don't know," appear deeply distressed by their memory slips, and show normal brain imaging. Most importantly, pseudo-dementia is REVERSIBLE when the underlying depression is treated.
假性痴呆（虚假痴呆）是指当老年长者患有严重临床重度抑郁症时，其脑神经受抑产生的一系列极似真实认知症的智力衰退表象——如思维极度迟缓、反应迟钝、无法决断与注意力涣散。与真正认知症患者千方百计掩饰遮盖记忆漏洞或虚构故事不同，假性痴呆长者在被测试时往往频繁消极回答“我不知道”，对自己的健忘感到深切自责痛苦，且脑影像学检查无实质退行萎缩。最关键的是，一旦通过抗抑郁治疗驱散抑郁阴霾，假性痴呆是完全可以逆转康复的！

Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH)

正常压力脑积水（NPH：可逆性痴呆）
Normal pressure hydrocephalus involves an abnormal accumulation of cerebrospinal fluid in the brain's ventricles. It is characterized by the classic clinical triad: (1) Gait apraxia (wide-based "magnetic" shuffling walk, as if feet are glued to the floor), (2) Urinary incontinence, and (3) Dementia. NPH is one of the rare surgically treatable forms of dementia via ventriculoperitoneal (VP) shunting.
正常压力脑积水（NPH）涉及脑室内脑脊液循环吸收障碍造成的病理性液体蓄积。它具有经典的临床三联征：（1）步态失用（双脚如磁铁吸附在地面般的磁性慌张步态）；（2）尿失禁；以及（3）认知机能倒退。NPH 是临床上极少数可通过脑室-腹腔分流手术（VP Shunt）引流多余积液从而实现功能完全逆转康复的认知症亚型之一。

Stages of Dementia: Overview

认知症病程分期全景概述
Dementia typically progresses through three distinct, overlapping stages: Stage 1: Early (Mild), Stage 2: Middle (Moderate), and Stage 3: Late (Severe). The duration of each stage varies widely from person to person. As a support worker, understanding what abilities are retained and what functions are declining in each stage enables you to tailor compassionate, empowering care that upholds client dignity.
认知症的病程演进通常经历三个特征鲜明且相互重叠的阶段：第一阶段：早期（轻度）；第二阶段：中期（中度）；以及第三阶段：晚期（重度终末期）。每个阶段持续的时间在不同个体之间存在显著差异。作为支持照护人员，深入理解长者在各个阶段仍保留哪些功能、哪些能力正在衰退，能够帮助你量身定制充满同理心与赋能的专业照护，坚定捍卫长者的人格尊严。

Stage 1: Early (Mild) Stage Dementia

第一阶段：早期（轻度阶段）临床特征
In the early stage, the individual remains aware of their diagnosis and often experiences intense anxiety, grief, fear, and depression about their declining memory. They usually live at home, manage most personal hygiene independently, but struggle with complex instrumental activities of daily living (IADLs) such as balancing financial accounts, remembering medication dosages, or navigating unfamiliar transit routes.
在早期阶段，长者通常对自己的患病诊断保持着清醒的病识感，往往会对自身正在衰退的记忆力感到强烈的焦虑、哀伤、恐惧和抑郁。他们通常能够继续在自己家中生活，独立完成绝大部分个人卫生洗漱，但在处理复杂的工具性日常生活活动（IADLs）时开始遇到困难，如核对银行账单、按医嘱规律服药或在陌生路线上乘坐交通工具。

Supporting the Client in the Early Stage

早期阶段的支持与赋能原则
Clients living with mild dementia need help getting organized, solving problems, and remembering appointments, occasions, and medications. They need discreet support and supervision—for example, if a client wants to bake cookies, as their support worker you will need to make sure the setting is safe, ingredients are assembled, and the oven is safely turned off afterward.
处于轻度认知症阶段的长者需要外部协助来整理生活条理、解决日常问题以及备忘预约、重要节日和服药时间。他们需要温和、不露声色的支持与安全监护——例如，如果长者想烘焙饼干，作为支持照护员，你需要确保厨房环境安全无绊倒隐患、协助备齐食材，并在烘烤完毕后确保烤箱已被安全关闭。

Transition to Middle (Moderate) Stage Dementia

向中期（中度阶段）认知症的过渡
As neural degeneration spreads deeper across the cerebral cortex and hippocampus, the individual transitions into the middle stage. Memory gaps widen substantially, self-care abilities deteriorate, and 24-hour supervision becomes increasingly necessary for safety.
随着神经变性损伤进一步向大脑皮层深部及海马体蔓延扩散，长者逐渐滑入中度认知症阶段。此时记忆漏洞急剧扩大，个人日常生活自理能力发生实质性退化，为了防止走失、烫伤或跌倒，全天候 24 小时监护变得愈发不可或缺。

Stage 2: Middle (Moderate) Stage Dementia

第二阶段：中期（中度阶段）认知症
The middle stage is typically the longest stage, often lasting several years. Cognitive decline is marked: the client fails to recognize familiar acquaintances or extended family, forgets personal history, becomes severely disoriented to time and location, and struggles with apraxia, agnosia (inability to recognize familiar objects or sounds), and aphasia (loss of language). Responsive behaviours like wandering, pacing, hoarding, and sundowning emerge prominently.
中期阶段通常是病程中历时最长的一个时期，往往持续数年之久。长者的认知衰退极其显著：认不出熟人甚至大家庭亲友、遗忘个人重要人生经历、对当前年份季节及身处地点彻底丧失定向感，并饱受失用症、失认症（认不出朝夕相处的日常物品或声音）以及失语症的困扰。诸如游走、踱步、囤积藏物及日落综合征等反应性行为在中期达到高峰。

Stage 3: Late (Severe) Stage Dementia

第三阶段：晚期（重度终末阶段）认知症
In the severe final stage, the client is entirely incapable of remembering, verbal communication, or performing self-care. The individual becomes bedridden, develops severe dysphagia (difficulty swallowing), loses bowel and bladder control, and becomes completely dependent for all physiological survival needs. Death typically results from secondary complications such as aspiration pneumonia or systemic infection.
在重度终末阶段，长者完全丧失了记忆、言语交流以及任何个人生活自理的能力。患者最终卧床不起、出现严重的吞咽障碍（极易误吸呛咳）、完全大小便失禁，一切生理存活需求完全依赖人工照料。生命终点通常由继发性并发症（如吸入性肺炎、褥疮脓毒症或系统性感染）所致。

    营养进食与水分补充：长者常遗忘进食或因失用症不会使用餐具。提供营养丰富、易于拿取的手拿食物（Finger foods，如小三明治、切块水果、小肉丸），使长者可以在走动中自行进食。在全天中多次分批提供小口温水，确保水分充足。

Providing a Safe and Enabling Physical Environment

  • Eliminate visual hazards: Avoid highly patterned carpets or rugs, which clients perceive as deep holes, steps, or snakes, causing sudden hesitation or falls.

Understanding Responsive Behaviours

深刻理解“反应性行为”（应答性行为）的本质
In modern person-centred dementia care, actions previously labelled as "difficult," "problem," or "aggressive" behaviours are now formally termed responsive behaviours (or reactive/protective behaviours). This progressive shift emphasizes that words and actions—such as pacing, calling out, resisting care, or striking out—originate as a direct human response to an illness, infection, discomfort, fear, confusion, or unmet need in the client's environment. The client is not acting out maliciously; they are communicating the only way their damaged brain allows.
在现代以人为本的以全人为中心的认知症照护体系中，过去被歧视性定性为“问题行为”、“具有攻击性”或“难以管教”的举动，现已被统称为反应性行为（Responsive behaviours，亦称应答性保护行为）。这一专业认知理念的重大跃升深刻表明：长者的徘徊踱步、高声呼喊、抗拒穿衣洗澡甚至挥拳击打，本质上是个体对其内在疾病疼痛、感染不适、未知恐惧、意识混乱或周围环境中未满足需求所做出的直接本能反应。长者绝非出于恶意找茬，而是在用其受损大脑仅存的唯一方式向外界求救呼喊！

Gentle Persuasive Approaches (GPA) in Dementia Care

温和劝导法（GPA）在认知症照护中的核心应用
Gentle Persuasive Approaches (GPA) is an evidence-based training program that equips healthcare workers with respectful, person-centred, and protective techniques to de-escalate and safely manage responsive physical behaviours (e.g., grabbing, hitting, pinching, or biting). GPA emphasizes understanding the person behind the disease, recognizing environmental triggers, and using non-restrictive body positioning to protect both client and worker from injury.
温和劝导法（GPA）是一套循证培训体系，旨在为一线医护与照护人员提供充满尊重、以人为本且注重防护的临床沟通与物理脱困技能，以化解并安全应对反应性躯体攻击行为（如突然抓扯衣物、挥拳击打、掐掐或咬人）。GPA 强调透过疾病表象看到长者完整的“人”，识别环境与未满足需求触发因素，并运用非侵犯性身体站位与解脱技巧，最大限度保护服务对象与照护人员免受职业伤害。

Validation Therapy: Connecting with the Client's Reality

确认疗法（验证疗法）：接纳融入长者的内在真实知觉
Developed by Naomi Feil, Validation Therapy is a compassionate communication philosophy that emphasizes empathy and active listening, advocating that caregivers accept the client’s perceived reality rather than trying to force them into our reality. When a 90-year-old client insists she must go home to cook dinner for her young children, reality orientation ("Your children are grown up and you live in a nursing home") only provokes panic, humiliation, and terror. Validation acknowledges the underlying emotion: "You must love your children very much. Tell me what their favourite dinners were." This calms distress and preserves dignity.
由内奥米·菲尔（Naomi Feil）创立的确认疗法（Validation Therapy，亦称验证疗法）是一种充满至真同理心的沟通哲学，主张照护者应当由衷接纳并融入长者主观感知的“现实世界”，而非强行用客观事实将其拽回我们的世界。当一位90岁的高龄老妇焦虑地哭喊着必须赶回家给年幼的孩子做晚饭时，生硬的现实定向（“您的孩子早就六十岁了，您现在住在养老院”）只会让长者感到羞辱、惊恐与绝望；而确认疗法则共情其背后的深层爱意：“您一定是一位无比疼爱孩子的慈母，能跟我讲讲孩子们最喜欢吃您做的什么菜吗？”这种接纳能迅速抚平焦虑并守护其尊严。

Wandering: Causes and Safety Risks

徘徊游走：病因机制与潜在安全风险
Wandering is a frequent and dangerous responsive behaviour in moderate-to-severe dementia. A client who wanders has no conscious awareness of direction, traffic dangers, or extreme weather, placing them at catastrophic risk for hypothermia, falls, dehydration, or fatal traffic accidents. Wandering often represents an unexpressed goal: looking for a deceased spouse, searching for a childhood home, looking for a toilet, or trying to walk off chronic joint pain or restless legs.
徘徊游走是中重度认知症长者极为常见且最具危险性的反应性行为之一。游走的长者完全丧失了空间方向感、车流危险意识以及恶劣天气防范能力，极易发生低体温冻死、摔伤骨折、脱水虚脱或恶性交通事故。徘徊往往代表着某种无法用语言表达的诉求：寻找早已过世的伴侣、寻找童年的老家、急于寻找卫生间，或是试图通过走动来缓解慢性关节疼痛或下肢不宁腿综合征。

Sundowning: Twilight Agitation and Care

日落综合征（黄昏狂乱）的机理与照护策略
Sundowning refers to the pronounced escalation in confusion, agitation, anxiety, restlessness, and aggressive behaviour that occurs late in the afternoon, during twilight, or into the evening hours. Contributing factors include physical and mental exhaustion accumulated throughout the day, diminishing daylight that casts confusing shadows, shift changes among facility staff, and disruptions to internal circadian clocks.
日落综合征（Sundowning，亦称黄昏狂乱）是指认知症长者的意识混乱、激越躁动、焦虑不安及攻击性行为在午后晚期、薄暮黄昏或夜幕降临阶段出现显著恶化加剧的临床现象。其促发诱因包括：全天身心疲惫积累、室外自然光线减弱导致室内暗影重重、机构早晚班工作人员换班嘈杂，以及大脑松果体昼夜生物节律中枢的退行变性。

Interventions to Alleviate Sundowning

缓解日落综合征的循证干预措施
  • Turn on indoor lights before dusk: Close blinds and turn on bright, cheerful indoor lighting before the sun goes down to prevent confusing twilight shadows.
    在黄昏降临前提前打开室内照明：在太阳落山前提前拉上窗帘并打开温暖明亮的室内顶灯，阻断窗外昏暗暮色与室内诡异阴影的形成。
  • Provide relaxing afternoon routines: Schedule calm, low-demand activities (e.g., listening to gentle acoustic music, soft hand massage, folding warm towels).
    安排舒缓的午后平静活动：在午后组织低耗脑力的放松活动（如聆听轻柔老歌、双手温和精油抚触、叠温热的毛巾等）。
  • Do not argue with a client who insists on leaving: Walk with the client outdoors safely, and guide them back inside gently after a few minutes.
    绝不与坚决要外出的长者争执：为长者穿戴保暖衣物后陪其在安全庭院中散步几分钟，顺应其步调并在其情绪平复后温和引导回房。

Agitation and Catastrophic Reactions

激越躁动与灾难性情绪爆发（Catastrophic Reactions）
A catastrophic reaction is an extreme, explosive, and disproportionate emotional outburst (screaming, sobbing, cursing, striking out, or destroying objects) in response to what seems to an observer like a trivial event. It occurs when the client’s damaged brain experiences acute cognitive and sensory overload—they cannot process what is asked of them, cannot communicate their distress, and feel completely overwhelmed by panic.
灾难性情绪反应（Catastrophic reaction）是指认知症长者在面对在旁人看来微不足道的普通琐事时，所爆发出的极其剧烈、失控且完全不成比例的情绪崩溃与攻击行为（如狂暴尖叫、嚎啕大哭、破口大骂、动手打人或摔砸物品）。当长者受损的大脑遭遇急性认知与感官过载——无法理解外界向其发出的复杂指令、无法用语言表达自身痛苦，且内心被压倒性的惊恐淹没时，就会被迫触发这种原始的应激自卫爆发。

De-escalation Strategies During Outbursts

平息激越爆发的黄金应对法则
  • Remain calm and speak softly: Speak in a gentle, slow, low-pitched voice. Never shout, scold, or display anger, which escalates client panic.
    保持绝对镇静并轻声发声：用低沉、缓慢、温和而坚定的语调说话。绝不能大声斥责、训诫或表露怒气，那只会让长者更加恐慌狂躁。
  • Eliminate excessive stimuli: Guide the client away from crowds, dim blazing lights, turn off the television, and reduce environmental noise.
    迅速撤离清除过度刺激：温和将长者引导离开拥挤的人群，调暗刺眼灯光，关闭电视机，消解环境中的喧闹噪音。
  • Use gentle, reassuring touch when appropriate: A soft hand on the client’s shoulder or arm can anchor them and restore emotional security.
    在长者允许时运用温和的治疗性触抚：轻轻将手搭在其肩膀或手臂上轻抚，能够传递温暖的安全感，平息剧烈风暴。

PAGE 919 · 第 919 页 Hallucinations, Delusions, Paranoia Support / 幻觉、妄想与多疑偏执的去对抗同理应对准则

Hallucinations, Delusions, and Paranoia

幻觉、妄想与多疑偏执的专业应对
Hallucinations (perceiving sensory stimuli that do not exist, such as seeing spiders on the bed or hearing threatening whispers) and Delusions (holding false, unshakeable beliefs, such as believing staff are poisoning food or stealing jewelry) are common manifestations of cortical and neurotransmitter breakdown in dementia.
幻觉（在没有外界客观刺激时感知到虚幻知觉，如看见床上爬满毒蜘蛛或听见耳边有人密谋威胁）与妄想（坚信不疑的顽固错误信念，如坚信护工在饭菜里下毒、或是怀疑房间财物被室友偷窃）是认知症中脑皮层萎缩与神经递质严重失衡的常见病理表现。

Support Worker Action Protocols

前线支持照护人员操作规范
  • Never argue with delusions or hallucinations: You cannot reason with a damaged brain. Arguing makes the client feel threatened, defensive, and abandoned.
    绝对不要与长者的妄想或幻觉争辩对质：你绝不可能通过逻辑说理去说服一个器质性受损的大脑；辩驳对质只会让长者感到自身受到严重威胁并陷入极度敌视。
  • Acknowledge and validate their emotional feeling: Say: "That must feel very frightening. I am here with you, and I will make sure you are safe."
    同理接纳其内心情感感受：说出：“那听起来真的太吓人了。我就在这里陪伴着您，我一定会保护您的安全。”
  • Ensure sensory aids are worn: Ensure eyeglasses are clean and hearing aids have fresh batteries. Sensory deficits severely distort ambient shadows and sounds into frightening hallucinations.
    确保佩戴感官辅具：确保眼镜镜片擦拭明亮，助听器电量充足；视力与听力障碍是导致长者将普通阴影和杂音曲解为恐怖幻觉的首要诱因。
  • Gently distract and redirect: Redirect their attention toward an enjoyable, comforting activity (e.g., listening to music, having a warm snack).
    温和转移注意力并实施重定向：将长者的注意力轻柔引向其喜爱的舒缓活动（如听老歌、享用温热点心或看看相册）。

Hoarding and Rummaging

藏匿囤积与翻箱倒柜（Hoarding & Rummaging）
Clients living with dementia often rummage through drawers and collect objects (eating utensils, napkins, clothing, soap) in their rooms, handbags, or wastebaskets. This hoarding behaviour stems from an overwhelming sense of loss, insecurity, or a forgotten purpose. Never punish or scold the client. Check wastebaskets before emptying them, maintain a designated "rummage drawer" filled with safe tactile objects, and discreetly return missing facility items.
认知症长者常常会反复翻乱抽屉，并将搜集到的各种零碎物品（如塑料餐具、餐巾纸、多余衣物、小肥皂等）严密藏匿在自己的枕头下、手提包或废纸篓里。这种囤积行为往往源于其内心深沉的丧失感、极度不安全感或某种被遗忘的本能动机。绝不能斥责或惩罚长者；清倒垃圾篓前务必仔细检查有无贵重假牙或首饰；为长者专门设立一个装满安全布料杂物的“趣味翻找抽屉”，并在长者不知晓时悄悄将公共物品物归原位。

Inappropriate Sexual Behaviours

非适宜性行为（去抑制性行为）
Disinhibition from frontal lobe damage may cause clients to undress in public areas, use sexually explicit language, touch staff inappropriately, or masturbate in common rooms. Recognize that undressing often simply indicates the client is hot, needs to use the toilet, or has uncomfortable clothing. Inappropriate touching is rarely sexually motivated; it is usually a desperate reaching out for human warmth, touch, or comfort. Remain non-judgmental, escort the client calmly to their private bedroom, ensure dignity, and redirect them gently.
大脑额叶损伤导致的去抑制（Disinhibition）可能会使长者在公共区域当众脱光衣物、言语露骨轻薄、不当抚摸工作人员或在客厅手淫。支持照护员必须清醒认识到：当众脱衣通常仅仅是因为长者身体感到燥热、衣物粗糙刺痒或憋尿急于如厕；而不当触碰他人也极少源于性冲动，大多是个体对人类最基础温存触抚与心理慰藉的渴求。保持非评判的专业沉稳，平静而尊严地为其披上披肩并护送回其私人卧室，温和引导转移注意。

The ABCD Model: Systematic Behaviour Analysis

ABCD 行为分析与管理黄金模型
The ABCD model is an objective, structured clinical tool utilized by healthcare teams to investigate, decode, and manage responsive behaviours by analyzing four interconnected dimensions:
ABCD 模型是跨学科医疗护理团队广泛采用的高度客观、结构化的临床分析工具，通过拆解四大核心维度来解码并化解反应性行为背后的真正诉求：
  • A = Activating Event (Antecedent / Trigger): What immediately preceded the behaviour? Was the client awakened suddenly? Was the shower water too cold? Was the dining room overcrowded? Were multiple staff shouting orders at once?
    A = 激活事件（Activating Event，诱因/前因）：在该行为暴发前的那一瞬间究竟发生了什么？长者是否被突然摇醒？洗澡水是否过烫或冰冷？餐厅是否过于嘈杂拥挤？是否有多名员工同时向其发号施令？
  • B = Behaviour: What was the exact, observable, objective behaviour? (Avoid subjective labels like "aggressive" or "nasty"). Describe objectively: Client pushed the washcloth away and struck the worker forearm with an open hand.
    B = 行为表现（Behaviour）：长者展现出的确切、客观可观察的行为是什么？（严禁使用“脾气暴躁”、“故意刁难”等主观定性词汇）。应客观如实记录：“长者推开洗脸毛巾，并用手掌推击了照护员的前臂”。
  • C = Consequences: What occurred immediately after the behaviour? How did staff, family, or other residents react? Was the client scolded, restrained, isolated, or spoken to in a soothing tone? Staff reactions directly influence whether the behaviour escalates or resolves.
    C = 后果与反应（Consequences）：行为发生后现场立即出现了什么后果？工作人员、家属或其他住户做出了何种反应？长者是被当面训斥、隔离压制，还是获得了轻声安抚？工作人员的第一反应直接决定了局势是迅速降温还是灾难性升级。
  • D = Decide and Debrief: The health care team collaborates to interpret the root cause (e.g., hidden pain or cold water), modifies the care plan to eliminate the trigger, and debriefs to ensure staff safety and emotional support.
    D = 决断与复盘（Decide & Debrief）：跨学科团队共同复盘该事件，挖掘深层根本原因（如排查出隐匿性关节剧痛或洗澡水温过凉），即刻修正照护计划以消除该前因诱因，并组织团队复盘支持员工心理健康。

The Impact of Dementia on Family Caregivers

认知症对家庭照顾者的多维重创
Caring for a family member with progressive dementia is an immense, exhausting, and heartbreaking journey often described as "the long goodbye" or "ambiguous loss." Spouses and adult children watch their loved one’s personality, memories, and identity gradually slip away while their physical body remains.
照料一位患有进行性认知症的挚爱亲人是一段漫长、筋疲力竭且令人心碎的历程，常常被形容为“漫长的告别”（The long goodbye）或“模糊性丧失”（Ambiguous loss）。配偶与成年子女眼睁睁看着亲人熟悉的人格、珍贵的回忆以及完整的自我认同在眼前逐步消逝，而肉体躯壳却依然留在人世。
Caregiver burnout is exceptionally prevalent. Caregivers experience chronic sleep deprivation from nighttime wandering, acute physical strain from lifting, overwhelming financial burdens, and profound emotional guilt whenever they feel frustrated or consider long-term care placement. Support workers must offer genuine validation, never judge family stress, and encourage the caregiver to seek medical and emotional support.
照顾者倦怠（Caregiver burnout）在认知症家庭中极其普遍。主要照顾者长年因长者夜间游走而严重失眠、因搬抬协助而遭受腰肌劳损、承受沉重的经济负担，且每当自己产生烦躁怨恨情绪或考虑将长者送入养老机构时，内心又被排山倒海的负罪感所吞噬。支持照护员必须提供真挚的同理肯定，绝不对家属的疲惫不耐烦横加道德评判，积极鼓励照顾者寻求喘息照护与专业心理支持。

Community Support Services: Respite Care and Day Programs

社区支持服务体系：日间照护中心与喘息照护
Adult day programs provide structured, engaging social activities, cognitive stimulation, and nourishing meals in a safe, supervised community setting for individuals with dementia. Concurrently, these programs provide primary family caregivers with vital hours of respite to rest, run errands, and attend to their own medical and personal wellness.
成人日间照护中心（Adult day programs）在安全受监护的社区环境中，为认知症长者提供结构化、富有趣味的社交互动、认知益智训练及营养膳食。与此同时，这些日间项目为长期紧绷的家庭照顾者争取到至关重要的数小时喘息时间（Respite care），使其得以补觉休息、处理个人事务并关照自身的健康。

Transitioning to Long-Term Care

向长期照护机构过渡的心理支持
As the dementia advances into the middle and late stages, home safety risks often exceed what a family can manage. Moving a loved one to a long-term care facility is frequently the most agonizing decision a family makes, fraught with intense feelings of failure and abandonment. Support workers in long-term care should welcome families warmly, involve them in care routines, and assure them that their presence and love remain vital.
随着认知症演进至中晚期，居家照护的安全隐患往往远远超出单个家庭所能承受的生理极限。将长者转送至长期照护机构（LTC）通常是家属一生中最艰难痛苦的抉择，内心充斥着深刻的失败感、遗弃感与背叛感。机构支持照护员应当热情拥抱并欢迎家属探访，鼓励家属参与温馨的日常喂饭或梳发陪伴，向其保证：专业机构接管的是繁重的物理护理，而家属的深情陪伴与爱是任何机构都无法替代的。

Chapter Review: Key Points

第 38 章核心知识要点总结
  • Disorientation, delirium, and dementia are NOT normal parts of aging.
    定向障碍、谵妄与认知症痴呆绝非人类正常衰老的必然组成部分。
  • Delirium is an acute, fluctuating medical emergency often caused by reversible infections (UTIs) or medications.
    谵妄是一种突发急性、剧烈波动的医疗急症，通常由可逆的感染（如尿路感染）或药物中毒所诱发。
  • Dementia involves progressive, irreversible loss of cognitive and functional abilities across mild, moderate, and severe stages.
    认知症涉及在大脑轻度、中度与重度三个阶段中进行性、不可逆的大脑高级机能与自理能力衰竭。
  • All responsive behaviours communicate meaning and unmet physical or emotional needs.
    所有反应性行为皆具有深层沟通含义，反映了长者未被满足的生理或心理需求。
  • Validation therapy and Gentle Persuasive Approaches (GPA) maintain client dignity and prevent caregiver injury.
    确认疗法与温和劝导法（GPA）能够有效捍卫长者尊严，并防止照护人员遭受职业伤害。

CRITICAL THINKING IN PRACTICE: Detecting Acute Changes in Mrs. Uma / 临床批判性思维实战：识别乌玛夫人晨起突发异常改变

  • Scenario: Mrs. Uma is 82 years old and lives in a long-term care facility. Today she seems irritable and does not recall your name. These issues are not normal for her. She says she is tired and does not want to eat breakfast.
    实战情境：82岁的乌玛夫人居住在长期照护机构中。今天早晨她显得异常烦躁易怒，且完全记不得你的名字。这些反常表现对平时的她而言绝非寻常。她主诉非常疲惫，拒绝起床吃早餐。
  • Reflection Questions: When should you report these changes? Why is it vital to monitor for subtle mental status changes in older persons?
    反思要点：你应该在何时向上级主管汇报这些异常变化？为什么在老年长者身上密切监测微妙的意识与精神状态改变如此生死攸关？

Sudden changes in cognition, mood, and appetite in an older adult are classic red-flag signs of acute DELIRIUM, most commonly triggered by a developing urinary tract infection (UTI), pneumonia, or medication toxicity. Prompt investigation and antibiotic treatment can reverse the infection before septic shock occurs.
