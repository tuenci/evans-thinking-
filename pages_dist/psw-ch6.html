Chapter 6 Opener & Learning Objectives

开篇导读与学习目标

Objectives

学习目标

After reading this chapter, the learner should be able to do the following: • Describe the historical perspective of health. • Define holism and describe the five dimensions of health. • Explain how health and wellness lie on a continuum. • List and explain the 12 key determinants of health in Canada. • Differentiate between an acute illness and a persistent (chronic) illness. • Differentiate between an illness and a disability. • Describe common emotional and behavioural reactions to illness and disability. • Explain the impact of stigma and discrimination on individuals living with health challenges. • Identify compassionate, client-centred support strategies that promote dignity, independence, and optimal health.

完成本章研读后，学习者应当能够达成以下核心专业能力考核目标： • 深刻阐述现代健康概念的历史演进脉络，鉴别从 19 世纪传统生物医学观到现代大健康观的历史飞跃。 • 准确定义“全人理念”（Holism），并系统阐述健康的五大核心维度（身体、情绪、社交、认知、精神）。 • 深入阐释健康与安康在“健康连续谱”（Health-Illness Continuum）上的动态流动与平衡调适规律。 • 详尽列举并系统剖析加拿大公共卫生署所确立的 12 项国家关键健康决定因素（Determinants of Health）。 • 准确鉴别与区分急性疾病（Acute Illness）与持续性/慢性疾病（Persistent/Chronic Illness）的病理演进及照护特征。 • 严谨辨析“疾病”（Illness）与“身体/认知残疾”（Disability）在医学与社会学层面的本质界限。 • 详尽阐述案主在遭遇严重疾病与创伤残疾时常见的心理、情绪与行为防御反应阶段。 • 深刻揭示社会污名化（Stigma）与歧视（Discrimination）对患病及残障人士所造成的深重心理创伤与社会隔离。 • 熟练掌握并践行以 DIPPS 核心原则为指导的慈悲关怀（Compassionate Care）实务策略，全力赋能并促进案主的最优安康状态。

第一节：历史演进、全人理念与健康五大维度

Historical Perspective & The Concept of Holism

历史视角的演变与全人健康哲学

A support worker's job is to help clients achieve or maintain optimal health. But what exactly is "health"? In the nineteenth century, good health was defined simply as the absence of disease. If a person was not acutely sick in bed with an infectious illness such as tuberculosis, cholera, or diphtheria, they were considered healthy.

支持工作者的天职，在于竭尽全力协助受助案主达成并维系其生命所能达到的“最优健康状态”（Optimal Health）。然而，究竟什么是真正的“健康”？在 19 世纪工业革命时代，医学界对健康的理解极其狭隘，仅仅将其简单定义为“未罹患疾病”（The absence of disease）。如果一个人没有因肺结核、霍乱或白喉等致命传染病卧床不起，传统观点便将其视为“健康体魄”。

In 1948, the World Health Organization (WHO) revolutionized health philosophy by defining health as: "a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity." Today, health is understood through holism. Holism is a concept that considers the whole person. A whole person has physical, social, emotional, cognitive, and spiritual dimensions. Holistic health recognizes that all five dimensions are deeply interrelated and that a change or crisis in one dimension directly reverberates through all others.

1948 年，世界卫生组织（WHO）以划时代的宪章重塑了全人类的健康哲学，将健康庄严界定为：“一种身体躯体、心理精神与社会适应层面的完全充实与完好状态，而绝非仅仅是没有疾病或虚弱羸弱。” 在当代加拿大医学与支持护理体系中，健康是通过“全人理念”（Holism）来深刻理解的。整体论主张：人绝不是一堆孤立运转的生物器官拼凑，而是一个具有深层灵魂的鲜活整全生命体！一个完整的人必然包含身体（Physical）、社交（Social）、情绪（Emotional）、认知（Cognitive）以及精神（Spiritual）五大共生维度。全人健康哲学明确指出：这五大维度血肉交融、休戚与共；任何一个维度遭受挫败、病变或创伤，必然会引发其他所有维度的剧烈连锁震荡！

Health and wellness lie on a continuum. At one end of the continuum is optimal health or wellness-a state of peak physical vitality, emotional resilience, social integration, and spiritual peace. At the opposite end is severe illness, incapacitating disability, or premature death. An individual's position on this continuum is never static; it fluctuates constantly throughout life based on age, life events, illnesses, personal choices, and coping abilities.

健康与安康不是一种非黑即白的静止标签，而是分布在一个动态滑动的“健康连续谱”（Health-Illness Continuum）之上。在连续谱的一端，是生机盎然的“最优安康状态”（Optimal Wellness）——拥有强健的抗病体魄、开朗的情绪韧性、融洽的社交支持以及内心的宁静自洽；而在连续谱的另一极端，则是重疾缠身、功能衰竭与濒死痛苦。任何人在生命旅途中所处的位置绝非一成不变，而是随着年龄增长、突发生活变故、慢性病进展、个人生活方式抉择以及心理抗挫能力在谱系上不断动态滑动迁移。

The Five Dimensions of Health

健康的五大维度深度解析

The five dimensions of health that constitute the whole person are: 1. Physical Health: Achieved when the body is strong, fit, energetic, and free from active disease or injury. It is influenced by genetics, nutrition, hydration, restorative sleep, regular physical activity, personal hygiene, and safety practices (such as wearing seat belts and avoiding smoking or substance abuse). 2. Emotional Health: Well-being in the emotional dimension, achieved when people feel secure, valued, and positive about themselves.

Clients who face sudden illness or progressive disability experience profound life disruptions. They may vent their frustration on family members or support workers. • Denial: An unwillingness to accept reality. In early stages, denial may protect the person from overwhelming emotional trauma, but prolonged denial hinders medical cooperation.

当案主突遭严重急病或进行性机能衰退残疾时，其整全生命节奏被彻底击碎。支持工作者必须熟稔案主以下常见的身心防御与情绪波动规律： • 恐惧与重度焦虑（Fear and Anxiety）： 即便是偶感微恙都会令人心生忐忑，更何况是面临重大器质性病变的案主。他们内心充斥着对疾病累及家庭的内疚、对日常生活自理能力的担忧、对天文数字般医疗账单的恐慌、对子女未来的茫然，以及对死亡临近的巨大恐惧。有些案主会向您吐露心声，但多数人会选择紧闭心门暗自神伤。 • 悲恸与哀伤反应（Sadness and Grief）： 面对任何形式的丧失，悲伤都是必然的人性流露。身患重疾与残障的案主往往面临着连环剥夺——丧失社会角色、丧失生活自决独立性、丧失尊严与自信心，甚至痛失对未来全部美好愿景的期许。他们正处于病理性哀伤之中，需要被允许宣泄哀悼。支持工作者需保持深切倾听与默默陪伴。 • 反应性抑郁与临床重度抑郁（Depression）： 持续的恐惧与悲恸极易演变为心理抑郁。因特定变故引发的情绪低落被称为“反应性抑郁”（Reactive Depression）；但若案主长期深陷自责自怨、绝望消沉、丧失食欲或出现自残倾向，则可能已恶化为需精神医学干预的“临床重症抑郁”（Clinical Depression）。 • 愤怒与迁怒宣泄（Anger and Frustration）： 案主常会从内心深处发出绝望呐喊：“凭什么倒霉的总是我？！”这种对命运不公的极度愤懑，往往会不自觉地迁怒投射到最亲近的家人或朝夕相处的支持工作者身上。 • 否认与逃避防御机制（Denial）： 拒绝接纳残酷的既成事实。在创伤初期，潜意识的“否认”能够充当心理防震垫，防止案主心理瞬间崩溃；但长期深陷否认则会严重阻碍其遵医配合与康复进程。



Living with a serious illness or life-altering disability disrupts every dimension of an individual's life. It can lead to loss of independence, loss of bodily control, altered self-image, financial distress, and profound grief. People respond to illness and disability in vastly different ways depending on their personality, age, past coping experiences, spiritual beliefs, and available support systems. However, psychologists identify common emotional and behavioural reactions (BOX 6.2): 1. Denial: Refusing to acknowledge the reality of the illness or diagnosis ("There must be a mistake in the laboratory results; I feel perfectly fine!"). Denial can initially protect the person from acute psychological shock, but becomes dangerous if it prevents the client from seeking lifesaving medical treatments. 2. Anger: Expressing frustration, resentment, and bitterness over the unfairness of the situation ("Why me? What did I ever do to deserve this?"). Clients often displace their anger onto support workers, nurses, or family members. 3. Bargaining: Attempting to make deals, often with God, spiritual powers, or health professionals, to postpone death or reverse the illness ("If I can just live to see my daughter get married, I will never complain again"). 4. Depression and Grief: Overwhelming feelings of sadness, mourning the loss of health, physical abilities, career, and independence. Clients may become withdrawn, refuse food, or weep openly. 5. Fear and Anxiety: Dread of the unknown, fear of physical pain, fear of becoming a burden to loved ones, and anxiety over financial ruin or abandonment. 6. Acceptance: Reaching a state of peaceful acknowledgment of reality. The client accepts their physical limitations and focuses on maximizing remaining abilities and finding meaning in life.

面对突如其来的重度疾患或致残性创伤，个体的整全生命维度瞬间遭受毁灭性重创。这意味着生活自理能力的剥夺、对身体排泄与移位失控的羞耻、自我身体意象的崩解、家庭经济支柱的倒塌以及对死亡的深刻恐惧。 没有两个人会对疾病与残疾做出完全相同的心理反应；个体反应深受其天生性格气质、年龄成熟度、过往应对重大危机的经验、精神信仰依托以及身边支持系统的深远影响。然而，临床心理学系统揭示了以下几大最普遍的情绪防御反应阶段（参见 BOX 6.2）： 1. 否认期（Denial）： 坚决拒绝承认疾病诊断的客观残酷事实（“化验单绝对搞错了，肯定是医院系统串号了，我身子骨硬朗得很！”）。否认机制在初期能够充当心理保护性盾牌，缓冲巨大的急性精神休克；但若案主长期深陷否认拒绝配合服药与胰岛素注射，将直接导致不可逆的临床危险。 2. 愤怒期（Anger）： 对命运的不公产生强烈的委屈、怨恨与怒火（“凭什么是我？我一辈子行善积德，老天凭什么这么惩罚我？！”）。深陷无助的长者往往会将内心的怒火无端倾泻发泄在床旁的一线支持工作者、护士或亲近家属身上。 3. 协议妥协期（Bargaining）： 试图与神明、上苍或权威医生进行心理谈判，企图以自身的道德祈祷换取生命期限的延长或病情的逆转（“只要能让我撑到亲眼看着小孙女穿上婚纱出嫁，我以后天天吃素念佛，再也不发脾气了”）。 4. 抑郁与哀伤期（Depression and Grief）： 在严酷现实面前陷入巨大的哀伤，哀悼自身失去的健康体魄、职业尊严、生活自理能力与家庭地位。案主往往沉默寡言、面朝墙壁流泪、拒绝进食，陷入深沉的无望感。 5. 恐惧与焦虑（Fear and Anxiety）： 对不可知未来的极度惶恐：恐惧无休无止的躯体剧痛、恐惧因瘫痪失禁成为儿女沉重的拖累包袱、焦虑庞大的透析护理开支将掏空家庭积蓄。 6. 接纳自洽期（Acceptance）： 历经内心的惊涛骇浪后，逐步平静接纳自身的残障现实。案主不再怨天尤人，而是将有限的精力聚焦于如何借助轮椅和辅助器具最大限度发挥现有肢体功能，在残缺中重塑生命的平静与尊严。

Stigma is social shame associated with a particular circumstance, illness, or physical quality that causes an individual to feel disgraced, embarrassed, or devalued. Discrimination is the unfair treatment of people on the basis of their health condition, disability, or perceived flaws. Clients with mental illnesses, dementia, substance use disorders, human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), incontinence, or physical deformities frequently experience cruel stigma. As a professional support worker, your paramount professional duty is to provide compassionate care that dismantles stigma and restores self-respect. Compassionate care means caring about the person, not just for the medical condition. It involves listening with empathy, respecting choices, preserving privacy, and upholding the DIPPS principles in every encounter.

BOX 6.3 Suggested Guidelines for Providing Compassionate Care • Treat every client as a whole, unique individual, never as "the stroke patient in Room 12" or "the diabetic foot ulcer". • Practice active, nonjudgemental listening: give clients your undivided attention and validate their fears and grief. • Encourage independence: celebrate small triumphs and never rush a client who is trying to dress or eat independently. • Communicate clearly, gently, and with genuine warmth. • Protect dignity and modesty at all times during personal hygiene care. • Involve clients in daily decision-making to restore their sense of personal control. • Support the client's family with patience, understanding, and empathy.
