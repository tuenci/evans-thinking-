Imagine what life would be like if you were unable to speak or communicate! That is a daily reality for people who have speech and language disorders. Speech and language disorders involve difficulty speaking, understanding, reading, or writing, and unfortunately, they can occur at any age.
想象一下，如果您无法说话或交流，生活将会变成什么样！对于患有言语和语言障碍的人来说，这就是残酷的日常现实。言语和语言障碍涉及在说话、理解、阅读或书写方面的困难，不幸的是，它们可能发生于任何年龄段。
Chapter 9 discussed effective communication between clients who have no physical communication disorder. This chapter builds on the information presented in Chapter 9 and applies it to the process of communicating effectively with clients who have speech and language disorders. It should be noted that clients of all ages may have speech and language disorders. Some causes of communication difficulties include hearing impairment (see Chapter 34), neurological disorders and brain injury (see Chapter 33), vocal cord injury, autism, intellectual disability, emotional or psychiatric disorders or drug abuse (see Chapter 37), physical impairments such as cleft lip or palate, and developmental disorders (see Chapter 36). They should not be confused with the difficulty that clients who speak another language may have with understanding English.
第 9 章讨论了与没有身体沟通障碍的服务对象之间的有效沟通。本章在此基础上，将这些信息应用于与患有言语和语言障碍的服务对象进行有效沟通的过程。需要注意的是，任何年龄段的服务对象都可能患有言语和语言障碍。沟通困难的某些病因包括听力受损（见第 34 章）、神经系统疾患和脑损伤（见第 33 章）、声带损伤、自闭症、智力障碍、情绪或精神障碍或药物滥用（见第 37 章）、唇腭裂等身体结构缺陷以及发育迟缓障碍（见第 36 章）。绝不应将这些病理障碍与母语为非英语的服务对象在理解英语时遇到的语言隔阂相混淆。

Health History in Speech and Language Assessment

言语和语言评估中的详尽健康病史采集
Clients with speech or language disorders should always have a thorough health history taken, usually by a doctor or a nurse, as soon as possible before treatment begins. The client’s history will provide details that can help the health care team better understand the health challenges the client has faced.
在治疗开始之前，通常应由医生或护士尽快为患有言语或语言障碍的服务对象采集详尽的健康病史。服务对象的病史将提供详细信息，帮助医疗护理团队更深入地了解服务对象所面临的健康挑战。

APHASIA

失语症
Aphasia is the partial or complete loss (a) of speech and language skills (phasia), whether it is understanding the words or trying to say them correctly. It is caused by brain injury. Stroke (also called cerebral vascular accident, or CVA), head trauma, and brain tumours are the most common causes of aphasia. Some clients with aphasia regain some or all of their language skills, but in some, aphasia is permanent. For example, clients with dementia who develop aphasia do not typically regain verbal communication skills (see Chapter 38).
失语症是指言语和语言技能的部分或完全丧失（a=无/缺失，phasia=言语），无论表现为理解词汇困难还是努力正确说出词汇。它由脑部损伤引起。中风（亦称脑血管意外，CVA）、头部外伤和脑肿瘤是失语症最常见的病因。一些失语症服务对象能够缓慢恢复部分或全部语言技能，但对某些人而言，失语则是永久性的。例如，发展为失语症的失智症服务对象通常无法重新恢复口头言语交流技能（见第 38 章）。
For communication to occur, a message must be sent, received, and interpreted. Clients with aphasia are unable to send messages, unable to understand the message received, or both.
要实现沟通，必须完成信息的发送、接收与解释。失语症服务对象可能无法发送信息、无法理解接收到的信息，或者两者兼而有之。

Types of Aphasia

失语症的类型
There are three basic types of aphasia:
失语症主要分为三种基本类型：
  • 1. Receptive aphasia—difficulty understanding language, including both spoken and written words. Clients with receptive aphasia cannot understand their own words or others’ words, so their speech is mixed up or muddled. They also mix up sounds within words. A client trying to say “hospital,” for example, may say “posital.”
    1. 感受性/感觉性失语症（Receptive aphasia）——理解语言（包括口语和书面文字）存在困难。患有感受性失语症的服务对象既无法理解自己说出的话，也无法理解他人的话，因此他们的言语往往混淆错乱。他们还会混淆词内的发音。例如，一位想说“医院（hospital）”的服务对象可能会说成“posital”。
  • 2. Expressive aphasia—difficulty speaking or writing. Clients with expressive aphasia can understand spoken and written words, but their speech or written language is jumbled or slurred and difficult to understand. They think one thing but say or write another. For example, a client may want food but ask for a newspaper. Clients with expressive aphasia cannot think of the right words or put the right sounds together to form words and sentences.
    2. 表达性失语症（Expressive aphasia）——口头说话或书写困难。患有表达性失语症的服务对象能够完全理解口语和文字，但他们的口语或书写语言杂乱、含混不清且难以辨认。他们心里想的是一回事，说出或写出的却是另一回事。例如，服务对象可能想要食物，脱口而出的却是索要报纸。表达性失语症患者无法准确想起对应的词汇，或者无法将正确的发音拼凑成单词和完整句子。

For example, a client trying to say, “Please give me a glass of water” may actually say “Come. Come. Come. Come.” Some may leave out connecting words. Others can produce only meaningless sounds or syllables. Because clients with expressive aphasia are very aware of their mistakes since they can understand what they are saying, they may become frustrated or depressed, leading them to cry or swear for no apparent reason.
例如，服务对象想要表达“请给我一杯水”，实际说出的可能却是“来。来。来。来。”有些人可能会漏掉连词。另一些人只能发出毫无意义的声音或音节。由于表达性失语症服务对象具有完全的认知理解能力，他们对自己说错的话非常清楚，因而容易感到极度沮丧或抑郁，甚至无缘无故地哭泣或咒骂。
3. Expressive–receptive aphasia (also known as global aphasia)—difficulty speaking and understanding written and spoken language. Some clients with expressive–receptive aphasia can say only “yes” and “no” and make sounds such as “da da.” Some others have lost all speech and language skills.
3. 表达-感觉混合性失语症（亦称全失语症，Global aphasia）——在口语表达以及口语和书面语理解两方面均存在严重困难。某些患有混合性失语症的服务对象只能说出“是”与“否”，或者发出诸如“哒哒”的声音。另一些服务对象则丧失了全部言语与语言技能。

APRAXIA OF SPEECH

言语失用症
Apraxia of speech (verbal apraxia) is the inability (a) to correctly move (praxia) the muscles used to speak, even though the muscles themselves are intact. Clients with this disorder are not able to control their lip, jaw, or tongue movements. As a result, they are not able to say the desired sounds and words. Apraxia of speech is caused by brain injury resulting from stroke, accident, brain tumour, or infection. Some babies are born with this disorder. Apraxia of speech can occur alone or with aphasia.
言语失用症（口头失用症）是指虽然用于发音的肌肉本身完好无损，但无法正确协调和运动（praxia）这些肌肉以进行言语表达（a=不能/缺失）。患有此障碍的服务对象无法自如控制嘴唇、下颌或舌头的动作。结果，他们无法发出想要的声音和词汇。言语失用症是由中风、意外事故、脑肿瘤或感染引起的脑损伤所致。有些婴儿出生时就患有这种障碍。言语失用症可以单独发生，也可与失语症合并出现。
Understanding the speech of clients with apraxia of speech is difficult, as they usually speak very slowly and may use words that sound similar to but not be exactly what they are trying to say. For example, a client may say “me” instead of “see.” As well, the order of sounds within words may be mixed up. For example, a client may say “thootshub” instead of “toothbrush.” Some clients have difficulty putting words in the right order or finding the right words. Inconsistent speech is common—that is, a person may say something correctly one time and incorrectly another time.
理解言语失用症服务对象的话非常困难，因为他们通常说话极其缓慢，并且可能使用发音相似但并非其本意的词语。例如，服务对象可能将“see”说成“me”。此外，单词内的音节顺序可能被颠倒。例如，服务对象可能把“牙刷（toothbrush）”说成“thootshub”。有些人在组织词序或寻找合适词汇方面存在困难。语音表现不一致极为常见——即一个人可能某一次说得很正确，而另一次却说错了。

DYSARTHRIA

构音障碍
Dysarthria is difficulty (dys) speaking clearly (arthria) because of weakness or paralysis in the muscles used for speech. Common causes of dysarthria include cerebral palsy, multiple sclerosis, head injury, tumour, and infection.
构音障碍是指因用于发音的肌肉软弱无力或瘫痪，导致吐字发音不清（dys=困难，arthria=关节/发音结构）。构音障碍的常见病因包括脑瘫、多发性硬化症、头部外伤、肿瘤以及感染。
Clients with dysarthria usually have slurred, slow, and soft speech and speak in flat, harsh, or nasal tones. They often have difficulty forming words, spacing their words, and breathing while speaking. Speech errors are usually consistent and predictable, so as you work with a client with dysarthria you may become familiar with their speech. (See TABLE 35.1: A Comparison Between Aphasia, Apraxia, and Dysarthria).
构音障碍服务对象的言语通常含糊不清、缓慢且声音微弱，并带有单调平淡、粗糙或鼻音过重等声调特征。他们往往在词语成型、断句间隔以及说话时的呼吸协调方面存在困难。其语音错误通常是一致且可预测的，因此随着你与构音障碍服务对象共同工作，你会逐渐熟悉其独特的言语模式。（参见表 35.1：失语症、失用症与构音障碍的对比）。

言语和语言障碍的情绪影响
How would you feel if you were not able to express your thoughts and feelings? How would you feel if you were not able to understand what others are saying? Clients with speech and language disorders experience emotions such as frustration, depression, and anger, as well as low self-esteem, shame, and guilt. Communication is important for functioning and for maintaining relationships with others. Being unable to communicate may cause a client to avoid social situations, or family and friends may avoid visiting the client instead. Speech and language disorders can be very stressful for families, as the relations between all family members are usually affected.

Many of us often find sharing our thoughts and feelings with others difficult. It is even more difficult for someone who has a speech or language disorder since even everyday conversations require great effort. Clients with speech and language disorders may speak slowly, may struggle with words, or may mispronounce words frequently. These communication difficulties might be frustrating for both the affected person and the listener, who may have to struggle to understand. Family members may find themselves trying to correct the person or becoming irritated with the person if the same word or phrase is repeatedly mispronounced. The person who cannot speak clearly might be tempted to avoid speaking.

TREATMENT FOR SPEECH AND LANGUAGE DISORDERS

言语和语言障碍的治疗
A speech therapist (also called a speech–language pathologist) helps the client with a speech disorder learn to communicate and also helps family members learn new communication techniques. Methods used depend on the age of the client, their disorder, its cause, and its severity. Practice and exercises may help the client learn (or relearn) speech and language skills. In the case of dysarthria, the client practises muscle-strengthening exercises and learns how to breathe while speaking.
言语治疗师（亦称言语-语言病理学家，SLP）协助患有言语障碍的服务对象重新学习沟通技能，并指导家属掌握新的沟通技巧。所采取的方法取决于服务对象的年龄、所患障碍、病因及其严重程度。持续的训练与练习有助于服务对象掌握（或重新习得）言语和语言技能。对于构音障碍，服务对象需要进行发音肌肉强化练习，并学习说话时的科学呼吸调节法。
Clients with speech and language disorders also learn how to improve existing skills. For example, a client learns to use body language and facial expressions to make communication more effective. Some clients learn new skills, such as sign language.
言语和语言障碍服务对象还会学习如何提升现有的代偿技能。例如，服务对象学会利用肢体语言和面部表情来增强沟通的有效性。一些服务对象还会掌握手语等全新技能。

SUPPORTING AND COMMUNICATING WITH CLIENTS

支持与沟通实操指导
As a support worker, to effectively communicate with your clients who have speech and language disorders, you must first know how you communicate in general. Do you speak clearly, or do you mumble? Do you use gestures? With these clients in particular, you may have to change the way you speak. Follow the care plan and your supervisor’s instructions. Use the communication methods that are best for your client. While you are speaking, you need to remember to be mindful of your facial expressions, as they can reflect any impatience or frustration you feel with the client.
作为支持工作者，为了与患有言语和语言障碍的服务对象有效沟通，您首先必须了解自己平时的沟通习惯。您说话是否吐字清晰，还是习惯含混不清？您是否善于借助手势？特别是在面对这些服务对象时，您可能必须改变自己平时的说话方式。请严格遵循照护计划以及主管的指导，采用最适合该服务对象的沟通方法。在说话时，您需要时刻注意自己的面部表情，因为它们会敏锐地泄露您对服务对象所产生的不耐烦或挫败情绪。
The effort of understanding others and making oneself understood can be exhausting, especially to clients with speech and language disorders, so they often tire easily. Their other health issues may also cause fatigue. Be alert for signs of fatigue, such as drooping shoulders, irritability, lack of interest, and a decline in understanding. Reduce surrounding distractions (e.g., close doors and windows, turn down the volume of a television or radio), which can make concentrating difficult for the client. Some clients with speech and language disorders will appear withdrawn and uninterested. Spend extra time with these clients. Social interaction can promote self-esteem and recovery. Always include them in conversations, even if they cannot understand you.
费力理解他人以及表达自己会令人精疲力竭，尤其是对于患有言语和语言障碍的服务对象而言，他们往往极易疲倦。他们身体的其他健康问题也可能加重疲劳。请时刻警惕疲劳的迹象，如垂肩、烦躁易怒、丧失兴趣以及理解力下降等。尽量减少周围环境的干扰（例如关紧门窗、调低电视或收音机的音量），这些噪音会让服务对象难以集中注意力。某些言语和语言障碍服务对象可能会显得孤僻退缩、对周围事物漠不关心。请多花时间陪伴他们：持续的社交互动有助于提升自尊并促进康复；即使他们不能完全理解，也务必将他们纳入交谈与互动中。
Clients with speech and language disorders should be treated with respect and empathy. Remember DIPPS—the five principles of support work (see the Providing Compassionate Care: Communicating With Clients Who Have Speech and Language Disorders box; also see the Supporting Mr. Hamilton, Part 1: Communicating With Clients Who Have Expressive Aphasia and Supporting Mr. Hamilton, Part 2: Communicating With Clients Who Have Expressive Aphasia boxes). Follow the guidelines in BOX 35.2 on page 836.
患有言语和语言障碍的服务对象应当受到充分的尊重与同理对待。请牢记 DIPPS——支持工作的五大核心原则（参见“提供同理照护：与患有言语和语言障碍的服务对象沟通”专栏；另见“支持汉密尔顿先生第一部分：与患有表达性失语症的服务对象沟通”与“支持汉密尔顿先生第二部分：与患有表达性失语症的服务对象沟通”专栏）。请遵循第 836 页专栏 35.2 中的沟通指南。

  • • Dignity. Showing respect, warmth, and compassion are important ways to promote dignity. Never talk about a client as if the person were not present. Address questions for and comments about the client to the client, not to others who are present. Some clients with language disorders are embarrassed to speak in front of others, especially strangers, so do not force a client to talk in front of others (see the Supporting Mr. Hamilton, Part 1: Communicating With Clients Who Have Expressive Aphasia box).
    • 尊严（Dignity）：展现尊重、温暖与同情是维护尊严的关键途径。绝不要当着服务对象的面议论他们，仿佛他们根本不存在一样。向服务对象提问或发表与其相关的意见时，必须直接面对服务对象本人，而不是转问在场的旁人。某些语言障碍服务对象在他人（尤其是陌生人）面前开口会感到难堪羞愧，因此切勿强迫他们在众人面前说话（参见“支持汉密尔顿先生（第一部分）”专栏）。
  • • Independence. Clients with speech and language disorders must gain, regain, or maintain independence and control. Support, encouragement, and patience are important. Never show impatience, frustration, or worry when a client is having difficulty speaking or understanding. Do not talk down to the client. (The Supporting Mr. Hamilton, Part 2: Communicating With Clients Who Have Expressive Aphasia box describes a support worker patiently using body language and symbols to learn about a client’s needs.) The care plan may list ways to encourage communication.
    • 独立（Independence）：患有言语和语言障碍的服务对象必须获取、重新获得或维持其生活的独立性与掌控感。支持、鼓励与耐心至关重要。当服务对象说话或理解遇到困难时，绝不可流露出不耐烦、沮丧或担忧。绝不要居高临下轻视对方。（“支持汉密尔顿先生（第二部分）”专栏描述了支持工作者如何耐心利用肢体语言和符号来获知服务对象的需求。）照护计划中可能会列出促进沟通的具体方法。
  • • Individualized care. For care to be truly client-centred, it must be planned or adapted to fit the client’s choices, physical and cognitive strengths and limitations, personality, and care plan.     • 偏好尊重（Preferences）：适当限制选项的数量，以帮助服务对象轻松表达个人喜好。给予充分的鼓励与支持。一个实用的工作技巧是预先将可供选择的事项写在纸上，以便服务对象能够轻松指认选择。
  • • Privacy. Keep information about your client confidential. Remember your role and relationship with the client at all times. Be professional in your communication. Learn to be comfortable with silence. Do not feel that you need to talk when the client is silent.
    • 隐私保护（Privacy）：对服务对象的全部个人信息严格保密。时刻牢记自己的专业角色以及与服务对象之间的执业关系界限。在沟通过程中保持专业操守。学会适应沉默并与安静泰然处之：当服务对象保持静默时，无需觉得必须强行填补空白去搭话。
  • • Safety. Take extra time to explain procedures. Do not explain all the steps at once. Explain the step just before you do it. Avoid using medical terms. Speak clearly and slowly. Be alert for signs that the client has not understood you.
    • 安全第一（Safety）：多花时间解释各项操作程序。切勿一股脑儿将所有步骤全部说完；应当在即将执行某一步骤之前，单独清晰解释该步骤。避免使用生涩的医学术语。说话吐字清晰缓慢。时刻警惕服务对象尚未完全听懂理解的微表情与反应迹象。

  • • Aphasia is the partial or complete loss of speech and language skills. These skills may be slowly regained or may be lost permanently.

  • Mr. Avery had a stroke. He has hemiplegia, receptive aphasia, and dysphagia. Explain how you will plan to perform the care measures listed below from his care plan. Apply learning from this and other chapters.
    埃弗里先生（Mr. Avery）经历了一次严重中风。他遗留有偏瘫（hemiplegia）、感受性失语症（receptive aphasia）以及吞咽困难（dysphagia）。请结合本章及其他章节所学知识，阐述您将如何规划并执行其照护计划中列出的以下各项照护措施：
  • • Transferring him from the bed to the wheelchair: Use positive, simple instructions with physical demonstrations; support his affected side while mobilizing from his strong side; ensure transfer belt application and proper body mechanics.
    • 协助他从床转移至轮椅：使用简明肯定的短句配合肢体动作示范；从健侧主导转移并妥善托扶保护患侧；正确使用转移带并保持符合人体工效学的安全搬运姿态。
  • • Communicating with him: Receptive aphasia means he cannot easily understand spoken or written words; face him directly, minimize environmental distractions, speak slowly in normal tones using gestures, communication boards, and pointing; observe his facial expressions carefully.
    • 与他进行交流沟通：由于患有感受性失语症，他理解口语和文字存在极大困难；应正面对视、清除噪音干扰、放缓语速保持正常音量，综合运用手势示范、图片沟通板及实物指认；细心观察其微表情确认理解程度。
  • • Dressing and undressing him: Allow him time to process steps; dress the affected side first, undress the affected side last (DAF: Dress Affected First); use simple gestures and cues.
    • 协助穿衣与脱衣：给予他充足的反应缓冲时间；严格遵循“穿衣先穿患侧，脱衣后脱患侧”（DAF原则）；借助直观的手势指引与衣物展示。
  • • Assisting him with food and fluids: Dysphagia precautions require an upright 90-degree sitting posture, thickened fluids if ordered, small bites placed on his unaffected oral side, observation for pocketing or coughing, and maintaining upright posture for at least 30 minutes post-meal.
