Respect for Diversity

尊重多样性：支持工作者崇高的职业价值基石

This chapter addresses one of the most important values that all support workers—and all caregivers, for that matter—must possess: respect for diversity. Diversity is the state of different individuals and cultures coexisting.

本章聚焦于全体支持工作者——乃至所有照护专业人员——所必须具备的最崇高核心价值观之一：尊重多样性（Respect for Diversity）。多样性（Diversity）是指不同个体与多元文化和谐共存的社会状态。

During your career as a support worker, you will care for and work with people whose lifestyles, beliefs, customs, and rituals are different from your own. It is necessary that you are always respectful of a person’s age, ethnicity, gender, occupation, sexual orientation, and lifestyle.

在支持工作者的职业生涯中，你将照护并共事各种各样的人群，他们的生活方式、思想信仰、风俗习惯以及宗教礼仪可能与你截然不同。你必须时刻真诚尊重每个人的年龄、族裔背景、性别认同、职业经历、性取向以及个人生活方式。

Ethnicity

族裔归属（Ethnicity）的学术界定与多元认同

Ethnicity refers to the sharing of a common history, language, geography, national origin, religion, or identity. Examples of ethnic groups include the Irish, the Inuit, and the Chinese.

族裔归属（Ethnicity）是指一个群体共同拥有并世代维系的共同历史传统、语言系统、地理渊源、原籍国、宗教信仰或集体身份认同。典型的族裔群体包括爱尔兰人、因纽特人以及华人等。

An ethnic group is not necessarily a nationality. Canada can be proud that it has many ethnic groups within it.

需要严格区分的是：族裔群体绝不等同于法定国籍（Nationality）。例如，你可能土生土长在加拿大，因而你的法定国籍是加拿大公民（Canadian）；但由于你的祖辈移民自乌克兰，且你在家中依然讲乌克兰语、坚守着乌克兰传统民俗节庆，你完全可以自豪地将自己的族裔认同界定为乌克兰裔（Ukrainian）。加拿大拥有如此众多璀璨多元的族裔共同繁衍生息，这是我们国家足以傲立于世界的崇高荣耀！

Culture

Culture makes a society distinctive. Culture refers to the characteristics of a group of people—the language, values, beliefs, habits, ways of life, implied rules for behaviour, music, and traditions—that are shared or perhaps even passed on from one generation to the next. Culture may be influenced by age, ethnicity, gender, occupation, sexual orientation, or lifestyle, or a combination of these factors.

文化（Culture）赋予了一个社会独一无二的精神特质与鲜明烙印。文化是指某一群体所共同享有、并世代相传的特征总和——包括语言交流方式、核心价值观、信仰体系、日常行为习惯、生活方式、心照不宣的行为潜规则、音乐艺术形式以及民俗传统。文化深受个人的年龄代际、族裔血脉、性别认同、所从事职业、性取向或日常生活方式等诸多维度的深刻塑造，往往是上述多维因子交织共振的复合产物。

Cultural characteristics, which are learned by living within a group, influence a person’s attitudes and behaviours. Examples of cultural groups include high school students (numerous subgroups exist within this main group), farmers, and snowboarders. Everyone is part of a culture.

文化的特征绝非与生俱来的遗传生理印记，而是个体在特定社群中通过后天浸润、生活与互动而习得的，它深刻地左右着一个人的内在心理态度与外在行为表现。文化群体的形态极其丰富多样，例如高中生群体（该大群内部还衍生出数不胜数的亚文化圈子）、务农农夫群体、单板滑雪爱好者群体等。世上的每一个人都身处于某种文化之中，许多人甚至同时隶属于多种互有交叉的文化体系。

Unfortunately, prejudice and discrimination do still exist throughout the world. Prejudice is an attitude toward or opinion of a person based on their membership in a group.

Stereotypes, Ageism, and Sexism

刻板印象、年龄歧视与性别歧视的临床表现

Some people stereotype ethnic or cultural groups by believing that all members of the group share the same characteristics. Terms and Phrases to Avoid When Communicating With Indigenous Peoples

原住民沟通尊重新规：严禁使用之五大历史贬损与误导词汇
1. Pre-history. Avoid use of the term pre-history because it implies that the history of Indigenous peoples began with the arrival of Europeans. In reality, each individual Indigenous nation has its own rich, sovereign history spanning thousands of years, passed down through oral traditions and cultural records.

2. Native. Avoid using Native as a blanket noun. While some Indigenous people may refer to themselves this way, it is overly generic and lacks distinct cultural specificity. Instead, use First Nations, Inuit, or Métis, or refer to the specific nation to which the client belongs (e.g., Cree, Haida, Ojibwe, Mohawk).

3. Indian. Do not use the word Indian unless referring to the legal terminology within the federal Indian Act. Historically, this term originated from Christopher Columbus's mistaken belief that he had landed in the East Indies, and it carries heavy colonial baggage and stereotypes.

4. Reservation or Reserve. Many Indigenous communities prefer to be called by their specific community name or nation (e.g., Six Nations of the Grand River, Kahnawà:ke) rather than the bureaucratic term reserve.

5. Traditional Knowledge as Myth or Folklore. Indigenous traditional knowledge, medicinal wisdom, and oral histories are sophisticated, empirical, and deeply sacred systems of understanding the world. Referring to them as mere 'myths,' 'legends,' or 'folklore' trivializes and invalidates their scientific and historical legitimacy.
1. 史前时代（Pre-history）：坚决避免使用“史前时代”这一带有强烈欧洲中心主义偏狭色彩的陈旧词汇！因为该词隐含着一种极度狂妄且错误的预设——仿佛加拿大原住民的文明历史只有在欧洲殖民者坚船利炮登陆美洲之后才算真正拉开序幕。而客观历史事实是：每一个原住民独立部族均拥有长达数千年甚至上万年源远流长、灿烂辉煌且主权独立的悠久文明史，通过神圣口述传统与独特符号世代完整传承！

Eye Contact Across Cultures

Eye Contact Eye contact has different meanings within different cultures. In Western cultures, eye contact is usually a sign of positive self-concept, openness, interest in others, attention, and honesty, and it also communicates warmth.

眼神接触（Eye Contact）的跨文化深层意涵 眼神交汇在人类不同文化宝库中传递着截然相反的精神信号。在西方传统主流社会中，在交谈时保持直接、坦荡的眼神对视通常被视作心理健康、自尊自信、坦诚率真、全神贯注以及正直诚实的崇高象征，并能有效传递出温暖与关爱；

Lack of eye contact in Western society may be interpreted as shyness, lack of confidence, dishonesty, or guilt. However, in many Indigenous, Asian, and Middle Eastern cultures, direct or prolonged eye contact is considered disrespectful, rude, or an aggressive challenge to authority.

反之，在西方语境下若一人交谈时眼神飘忽、刻意回避对视，往往易被刻板误判为羞怯胆小、缺乏自信、心虚不诚实甚至做贼心虚。 然而在众多加拿大原住民、东亚以及中东传统文化中，直勾勾凝视对方眼睛——尤其是直视长辈或权威人士——却被视作极度缺乏教养、粗鲁傲慢甚至公然挑衅权威的大不敬之举！

Among many First Nations peoples, looking down or averting one's eyes when speaking with an elder or a professional is a mark of profound respect and humility. Never force eye contact or assume that looking away indicates dishonesty or disinterest!

在许多加拿大第一民族长者看来，与长辈或医生交谈时低垂眼帘或避开正面直视，正是内心深处流露出的最高崇敬与谦卑顺服之礼。专业支持工作者绝对不可强求案主直视自己的眼睛，更不可轻率将案主的眼神回避误判为缺乏诚意或漫不经心！

You have noticed that the resident uses his call light more often at night, after family visits, and when he is not checked on regularly. How might this information be helpful in care planning? What are ways that you might be able to decrease the resident’s anxiety?

Critical Analysis & Intervention Plan:
• Root Cause Analysis: The resident is experiencing acute language barrier anxiety, isolation, and fear of abandonment, especially after family members leave and darkness falls.
• Care Planning Integration: Schedule regular, proactive 30-minute check-ins so the resident feels secure and does not need to resort to the call light out of panic.
• Nonverbal Reassurance: Introduce a bilingual pictorial communication chart at the bedside for immediate needs (pain, water, toilet, warmth).
• Family Partnership: Encourage family members to leave a recorded comforting audio message in the resident's native language and familiar cultural mementos in the room.
