OBJECTIVES / 学习目标

After reading this chapter, the learner should be able to do the following: / 学完本章后，学习者应能够做到以下各项：

  • • Describe the effects of mental health disorders on everyday life.
    阐述精神健康障碍对服务对象生理、情绪、心理及社会交往等日常生活全方位的深远影响。
  • • List factors that may contribute to mental health disorders.
    列举诱发或促成精神健康障碍的生物学、遗传、大脑神经化学失衡、心理及社会环境因素。
  • • Describe the stigma experienced by people with mental health disorders.
    描述精神健康障碍患者所遭遇的社会病耻感（污名化）、刻板歧视以及去污名化的执业要求。
  • • Describe the effect of mental health disorders on families.
    阐述精神健康障碍对患者家庭成员所产生的心理压力、情感负担、经济冲击与照顾者倦怠。
  • • Explain the importance of DIPPS when supporting clients with mental health disorders and their families.
    阐明在支持精神健康障碍服务对象及其家属时践行 DIPPS（尊严、独立、个性、隐私、安全）的核心价值。
  • • Understand how culture can influence the interpretation and treatment of mental illness.
    理解多元文化背景如何深刻影响对精神病症状的解读、求医态度、接受度及治疗方案的选择。
  • • Define common mental health disorders (including Acquired Brain Injuries, Anxiety, Bipolar, Depressive, Disruptive/Conduct, Eating, OCD, Personality, Schizophrenia, Self-Harm, Sleep-Wake, Substance-Related, Suicidal Behaviour, Trauma/PTSD) and describe how to support clients with these disorders.
    准确界定全书涵盖的各类精神健康障碍（包括获得性脑损伤、焦虑障碍、双相情感障碍、抑郁障碍、破坏性及品行障碍、进食障碍、强迫症、人格障碍、精神分裂症谱系、自伤行为、睡眠-觉醒障碍、物质成瘾、自杀行为、创伤后应激等），并掌握专业支持照护规范。

Introduction to Mental Health Disorders

精神健康障碍导论：全人多维健康视角
Mental health disorders can affect all dimensions of a person’s life—physical, emotional, spiritual, social, and intellectual. As a support worker, you will care for and support clients and family members with physical health challenges, mental health challenges, or both.
精神健康障碍会波及服务对象生命的全部维度——生理、情绪、精神信仰、社会交往以及认知心智。作为支持照护人员，你将为面临身体健康挑战、精神健康挑战或两者兼有的服务对象及其家庭成员提供照护与支持。
There may be no relationship between a client’s physical and mental health issues, but in some clients, physical conditions can result from mental health challenges or the reverse. To be able to provide care to your clients and their families while ensuring dignity, respect, and compassionate care, you need to understand what they are experiencing and be aware of your own attitude toward mental health and mental illness. Be sensitive to the feelings of your clients and their families.
在某些情况下，服务对象的身体与精神健康问题之间可能并无直接关联；但在另一些服务对象身上，躯体病症可能由精神心理困扰诱发，反之亦然。为了能在确保尊严、尊重与同理照护的同时为服务对象及其家属提供专业服务，你必须深刻理解他们的切身体验，并时刻觉察自己对心理健康和精神疾病的内在态度。对服务对象及其家属的情感保持高度敏锐。

Providing Compassionate Care: Supporting Clients With Mental Health Disorders / 提供同理心照护：支持精神健康障碍服务对象（DIPPS核心准则）

  • Dignity. Treat clients with mental health disorders as you would any other client. Show respect, and promote dignity. Do not label them—for example, it is not acceptable to say that a client is "a schizophrenic" or "a drug addict." It is acceptable to say that the client "has schizophrenia" or "has a substance-dependence disorder" or "lives with schizophrenia" or "lives with a substance-dependence disorder."
    尊严（Dignity）：像对待任何其他服务对象一样对待精神健康障碍患者。展现由衷的尊重，积极维护其尊严。严禁给患者贴标签——例如，绝不能称患者为“精神分裂症患者”或“吸毒成瘾者”；合乎伦理的专业表述是“该服务对象患有精神分裂症”、“患有物质依赖障碍”或“正与精神分裂症抗争”。
  • Independence. Help clients do as much for themselves as possible, but do not push them to the point of frustration. Recognize that a person’s ability to perform activities of daily living (ADLs) will change depending on how well they feel on a given day.
    独立性（Independence）：协助服务对象尽可能独立完成力所能及的事项，但切勿强推至令其感到挫败无助的境地。必须清醒认识到，服务对象执行日常生活活动（ADLs）的能力会随着其当天的精神状态波动而动态变化。
  • Preferences. Respect the client's choices whenever safely possible. Allowing clients to make daily decisions about their meals, clothing, and routines fosters autonomy and emotional empowerment.
    偏好与个性化（Preferences）：在确保安全的前提下，充分尊重服务对象的自主选择。允许服务对象在日常饮食、着装及生活作息上自行决断，有助于培养其自主感与心理掌控力。
  • Privacy. Respect personal boundaries and confidentiality. Mental illness carries severe stigma; never share private health information, diagnosis, or behaviours with anyone outside the designated multidisciplinary health care team.
    隐私（Privacy）：严格尊重个人边界与保密原则。精神疾病往往伴随严重的社会病耻感；严禁向跨学科医疗护理团队之外的任何无关人员透露服务对象的私人健康信息、诊断结果或行为表现。
PAGE 859 · 第 859 页 Compassionate Care (cont'd), Stigma & DSM-5 / 同理心照护续篇、病耻感界定与DSM-5分类体系
Privacy (continued): Respect the client’s right to have boundaries, especially when they refuse to discuss certain aspects of their life with you.
隐私（续）：充分尊重服务对象设立心理界限的权利，尤其是当他们拒绝向你谈论其生活或内心世界的某些隐秘方面时，切勿强行窥探。
Safety: Stress, fatigue, and physical illness make mental health disorders worse and may trigger episodes. Observe the client carefully for any signs of stress or illness, and report such signs at once. Remember also that clients with mental health disorders may be more likely to fall or injure themselves if they are unable to focus on their surroundings. Provide a calm, stress-free, and safe environment. The client’s care plan will list activities and situations that should be avoided. Follow the safety guidelines in Chapter 20.
安全（Safety）：心理压力、身体疲劳和躯体疾病会加重精神健康障碍，并可能诱发急性发作。严密观察服务对象是否有任何压力加剧或身体不适的迹象，并立即如实汇报。切记，当精神障碍患者无法将注意力集中在周围环境时，跌倒或受伤的风险显著增高。务必提供平静、无压力且安全的环境。服务对象的照护计划中会详细列明应规避的活动与情境；严格遵循第20章的安全准则。

Mental Health and Mental Health Disorders

心理健康与精神健康障碍
A mental health disorder, which can be caused by a combination of genetic, biological, personality, and environmental factors, is a disturbance in a person’s ability to cope with or adjust to stress; as a result, the person’s thinking, mood, and behaviours are affected, and functioning is impaired. Twenty percent of Canadians (one in five) will personally experience a mental health disorder during their lifetime. Mental health disorders affect people of all ages, cultures, and educational and income levels.
精神健康障碍是由遗传、生物学、人格特质及环境压力等多种因素综合作用所致的病理状态，表现为个体应对或调节压力的能力发生紊乱；结果导致患者的思维、心境和行为受到显著影响，日常生活功能严重受损。全加拿大有 20%（即五分之一）的人口在其一生中会亲身经历精神健康障碍。精神障碍波及所有年龄、文化背景、教育水平及收入阶层的群体。

BOX 37.1: How Do I Know If I Have a Mental Health Disorder? / 专栏 37.1：我如何知道自己是否患有精神健康障碍？

  • Most people, at one time or another, have asked themselves this question: "Are my problems or symptoms getting in the way of my life?" If you think the answer is yes, then you may have a mental health disorder.
    大多数人在生活中的某个时刻都曾自问：“我的心理困扰或症状是否已经妨碍了我的正常生活？”如果你认为答案是肯定的，那么你可能正在经历精神健康障碍。
  • Key reflective indicators include: persistent changes in mood, unmanageable anxiety, withdrawal from social relationships, drastic changes in sleep or eating habits, difficulty coping with daily problems, or substance misuse to numb emotions.
    关键的反思指标包括：持续的情绪低落或剧烈波动、无法控制的焦虑、从人际社交中全面退缩、睡眠或饮食习惯的骤然剧变、难以应对日常琐碎问题，或试图通过滥用成瘾物质来麻痹情感痛苦。
The Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, fifth edition (DSM-5), published by the American Psychiatric Association, is used throughout North America to classify mental health disorders. The manual provides detailed diagnostic criteria, symptom duration, and clinical descriptions to ensure accurate multidisciplinary evaluation.
由美国精神医学学会出版的《精神障碍诊断与统计手册（第五版）》（DSM-5）在全北美及全球范围内被广泛用作精神健康障碍的权威分类与诊断指南。该手册提供了详尽的诊断标准、症状持续时间以及临床描述，以确保跨学科评估的科学性与一致性。

Mental Health Versus Mental Illness

心理健康与精神疾病的本质区别
Mental health and mental illness are not merely opposites; they exist along a dynamic continuum. Mental health is a state of mind in which a person copes with and adjusts to the stressors of everyday living in socially acceptable ways. Mentally healthy individuals experience distress, sadness, and anger, but they possess adaptive coping mechanisms to manage these feelings.
心理健康与精神疾病并非简单的对立二分，而是存在于一个动态的连续谱系之上。心理健康是一种个体能够以社会认可且具有适应性的方式，积极应对和调节日常生活压力源的良好心智状态。心理健康的人同样会体验到痛苦、悲伤和愤怒，但他们拥有适应性的应对机制来化解并调节这些情绪。
Mental illness (or mental health disorder, emotional illness, psychiatric disorder), in contrast, involves enduring disturbances in cognition, emotion regulation, or behaviour that reflect a dysfunction in the psychological, biological, or developmental processes underlying mental functioning. These conditions cause clinically significant distress or impairment in social, occupational, or other important activities.
相比之下，精神疾病（亦称精神健康障碍、情绪疾患、精神病学障碍）涉及认知、情绪调节或行为方面的持久紊乱，反映了支撑心智功能的心理、生物或发育过程中的病理功能失调。这些病理状态会在社交、职业或其他重要生命活动中造成具有临床显著意义的痛苦或功能损害。

Repression and Unconscious Coping Mechanisms

压抑与潜意识心理防御机制
When faced with overwhelming stress, anxiety, or traumatic conflict, the human mind often employs psychological defence mechanisms. One of the most common is repression: the keeping from the conscious mind of (or "burying of") unpleasant, frightening, or painful thoughts and memories.
当面临压倒性的压力、严重焦虑或创伤性冲突时，人类的心智往往会调动潜意识心理防御机制。其中最常见的是压抑（Repression）：即无意识地将令人不快、恐惧或痛苦的想法和记忆“深埋”并排除于意识知觉之外。
Although repression can provide temporary relief from emotional pain, repressed feelings do not simply vanish. Instead, they often resurface indirectly as unexplained physical ailments (somatization), persistent anxiety, phobias, or sudden emotional outbursts. Professional psychotherapy helps clients safely unearth and resolve these repressed conflicts.
尽管压抑能够在短期内提供对情感痛苦的暂时缓解，但被压抑的情绪绝不会自行消失。相反，它们往往会以躯体化症状（无法解释的身体疼痛与不适）、顽固性焦虑、恐惧症或突发的情绪爆发等形式间接暴露出来。专业的心理治疗能够协助服务对象安全地探索并化解这些被深埋压抑的内心冲突。

Causes of Mental Health Disorders

精神健康障碍的致病原因与诱发因素
Mental health disorders do not arise from a single cause, nor are they a sign of personal weakness, moral failing, or lack of willpower. Modern medical science demonstrates that they result from a complex interplay of biological, genetic, psychological, and environmental factors.
精神健康障碍绝非由单一因素所致，更不是个人软弱、道德沦丧或意志力缺乏的标志。现代医学科学证实，它们是生物学、遗传基因、心理特质以及环境应激等多维因素高度复杂的相互作用产物。

Biological and Genetic Factors

生物学与遗传学致病机制
  • Chemical imbalances in the brain: Neurotransmitters such as dopamine, serotonin, and norepinephrine regulate mood, thought, and sensory perception. Imbalances in these chemical messengers can directly trigger depression, anxiety, or psychosis.
    大脑神经化学递质失衡：多巴胺、五羟色胺（血清素）和去甲肾上腺素等神经递质在调节心境、思维及感官知觉中起关键作用。这些化学信使的浓度或受体功能失衡可直接诱发抑郁症、焦虑症或精神分裂症。
  • Genetic predisposition: Many mental health disorders, such as bipolar disorder, major depression, and schizophrenia, tend to run in families, suggesting a strong hereditary vulnerability.
    遗传易感性：许多精神健康障碍（如双相情感障碍、重度抑郁症和精神分裂症）具有明显的家族聚集倾向，表明存在强烈的遗传性患病易感基因。
  • Brain injury and physical trauma: Traumatic brain injury (TBI), stroke, brain tumours, or chronic infections can alter neural circuits and precipitate cognitive and behavioural disorders.
    脑损伤与躯体创伤：创伤性脑损伤（TBI）、中风卒中、颅内肿瘤或慢性感染会直接改变大脑神经回路，从而引发认知与行为方面的继发性障碍。

Psychosocial and Environmental Triggers

心理社会与外部环境诱发因素
  • Childhood abuse or neglect: Physical, sexual, or emotional trauma in early childhood severely alters brain development and stress response systems.
    童年期虐待或忽视：儿童早期的身体虐待、性虐待或情感忽视会严重破坏大脑的正常发育进程与应激神经内分泌系统。
  • Severe life stressors: Loss of a loved one, divorce, financial ruin, persistent unemployment, or chronic discrimination can trigger latent mental health conditions.
    重大生活压力源：亲人丧亡、婚变破裂、经济破产、长期失业或持续的社会歧视与边缘化，均可成为击溃心理防线并激活潜在精神障碍的诱发引信。

Think About Safety: Home and Community Protocol / 安全警示：居家与社区执业安全核心规范

  • Always maintain a clear, unobstructed path to exit any premises suddenly. Position yourself between the client and the door whenever feasible.
    在进入任何执业场所时，始终确保有一条清晰通畅、无障碍的快速撤离逃生路线。在可能的情况下，始终让自己处于服务对象与出口房门之间。
  • If you observe weapons, extreme aggression, severe intoxication, or suspect imminent violence, do not enter or remain in the home. Retreat to a safe location immediately and notify your supervisor and emergency services (9-1-1).
    如果你目击危险武器、极度暴力攻击迹象、严重醉酒药物中毒或怀疑即将发生暴力事件，切勿擅自进入或滞留屋内。立即撤离到绝对安全区域，并通过手机迅速向机构主管和紧急求援热线（9-1-1）汇报。

跨学科精神卫生医疗照护团队协作
The care-planning process aims at addressing the needs of clients with mental health disorders, including their physical safety and emotional needs, and usually requires input from various members of the health care team. The health care team may include:
照护计划制定流程旨在全面满足精神健康障碍服务对象的全人需求，包括身体生理安全与心理情绪需求，通常需要跨学科团队各专业成员的紧密协作。团队成员包括：
  • Psychiatrists: Physicians specializing in mental health disorders who can formulate medical diagnoses, perform assessments, and prescribe psychiatric medications.
    精神科专科医师（Psychiatrists）：专攻精神健康障碍的医学博士，具备执业处方权，能够制定权威医学诊断、进行生化评估并开具精神类药物。

Forms of Psychotherapy

心理治疗的主要临床流派
Psychotherapy is a form of therapy in which a client explores thoughts, feelings, and behaviours with help and guidance from a mental health specialist. Common modalities include Psychoanalysis (exploring unconscious conflicts from childhood), Behaviour therapy (reconditioning maladaptive habits), Cognitive-behavioural therapy (CBT, reframing distorted thoughts), and Group therapy (sharing experiences with peers facing similar challenges).
心理治疗是在精神卫生专家的指导与支持下，服务对象系统探讨其思维认知、情感体验与行为模式的临床过程。常见流派包括：精神分析（探讨童年潜意识冲突）、行为疗法（纠正适应不良行为习惯）、认知行为疗法（CBT，重塑歪曲负面认知）以及团体治疗（与面临相似困境的同伴分享支持）。

BOX 37.2: Principles of Mental Health Care / 专栏 37.2：精神健康照护核心实践准则

  • 1. Follow the care plan. Tell your supervisor if support measures are not working or if the client's condition deteriorates.

    2. 绝不伤害（Do no harm）：为服务对象提供绝对安全、舒适的环境；时刻觉察并规范自己的肢体语言与面部表情。
  • 3. Accept the client as a whole person with inherent dignity, regardless of symptoms or past behaviours.
    3. 接纳全人：无论其精神症状有多严重或过往有何行为，始终将其视为具有与生俱来尊严的完整人予以接纳。
  • 4. Communicate with clarity, patience, and active listening. Never argue with or mock a client.
    4. 清晰耐心沟通：运用积极倾听技巧；绝不能与服务对象争辩对错，严禁任何形式的嘲讽或蔑视。

Pharmacotherapy (Drug Therapy)

精神科药物疗法（药物治疗）
Medications are frequently prescribed to manage psychiatric signs and symptoms by modifying neurotransmitter concentrations in the brain. Common classes include Antidepressants (elevating serotonin and norepinephrine), Antipsychotics (blocking excess dopamine to control delusions and hallucinations), Anti-anxiety agents / anxiolytics (reducing nervous tension), and Mood stabilizers (such as lithium, preventing extreme swings in bipolar disorder).
精神科处方药物通常用于通过调节大脑中神经递质的浓度来控制精神病理体征与症状。常用药物大类包括：抗抑郁药（提升五羟色胺和去甲肾上腺素水平）、抗精神病药（阻断过量多巴胺以控制妄想和幻觉）、抗焦虑药（缓解高度神经紧张与恐慌）以及心境稳定剂（如锂盐，用于预防双相情感障碍的极度波动）。
Support workers do not prescribe or alter medications, but you play a critical frontline role in monitoring for therapeutic efficacy and adverse effects. Many psychiatric medications cause severe side effects such as drowsiness, dizziness, dry mouth, blurred vision, muscle stiffness, tremors, or gastrointestinal distress. Report any observed changes promptly.
支持照护人员虽无权开具或调整药物，但在监测药物疗效与不良反应方面承担着至关重要的前线职责。许多精神科药物会引起严重的副作用，如嗜睡、眩晕、口干、视力模糊、肌肉强直、震颤或胃肠道不适。一旦观察到异常变化，必须立即如实汇报。

Stigma and Discrimination in Mental Illness

精神疾病的社会病耻感与歧视
Stigma refers to social shame, negative stereotyping, and discredit associated with a particular circumstance, quality, or person. Sadly, mental health disorders remain among the most stigmatized medical conditions in society. Misguided portrayals in the media often falsely equate mental illness with violence, unpredictability, or moral weakness.
病耻感（Stigma，亦称污名）是指附着于特定处境、特质或个体之上的社会耻辱、负面刻板印象与贬低排斥。令人遗憾的是，精神健康障碍依然是当今社会中遭受最严重污名化的医学疾患之一。大众媒体的不实描摹往往错误地将精神疾病与暴力危险、不可预测或道德败坏划上等号。
Stigma creates immense barriers to recovery. It causes individuals to hide their symptoms out of shame, delay seeking professional medical help, and suffer in silence. Discrimination in housing, employment, and social relationships further compounds their suffering. Support workers must actively dismantle stigma by treating every client with unconditional positive regard and dignity.
病耻感为疾病康复制造了巨大的阻碍。它导致患者因羞耻感而隐瞒病情、拖延求医，并在痛苦中默默忍受。在住房、就业以及人际交往中遭遇的歧视进一步加剧了他们的绝望。支持照护员必须通过对每位服务对象展现无条件的积极关注与尊重，主动破除病耻感与偏见。

Cultural Influences on Mental Health

文化对心理精神健康观念的深远塑造
Culture strongly influences how individuals understand mental health, express emotional distress, and perceive treatment. What is viewed as an emotional disorder in one culture may be regarded as normal grief, spiritual communication, or a moral trial in another. Some cultures encourage open expression of feelings, whereas others emphasize stoicism and emotional restraint.
文化背景深刻塑造了个体如何理解心理健康、表达情感痛苦以及看待医学治疗。在一种文化中被视作情绪障碍的表现，在另一种文化中可能被视为正常的哀悼仪式、灵性沟通或道德考验。某些文化鼓励坦率宣泄内心情感，而另一些文化则推崇坚忍克制与情感内敛。

Supporting Mrs. Hill: The Effect of Past Experiences / 专栏：支持希尔夫人——过往历史经历对行为的深远影响

  • Mrs. Hill, 88, lived through the extreme privation of the Great Depression. She is extremely hesitant to throw anything away, hoarding old rubber bands, bread tags, and washed plastic containers throughout her apartment.
    88岁的希尔夫人在年轻时曾经历过经济大萧条时期的极度物资匮乏。她极其抗拒扔掉任何物品，在公寓里囤积着大量的旧橡皮筋、面包封口夹和洗净的塑料盒。
  • While a worker unfamiliar with her background might label this as bizarre hoarding disorder, understanding her life history reveals it as an adaptive survival mechanism born of profound hardship. Support workers must never discard a client’s belongings without their consent, which would violate their dignity and provoke acute panic.
    若是不了解其人生背景的照护人员，可能会草率地将其定性为怪异的囤积障碍；然而，深入探寻其历史经历就会发现，这实际上是源于深刻苦难的适应性生存习惯。支持照护员绝不能在未经服务对象允许的情况下擅自丢弃其物品，否则会严重践踏其尊严并引发急性恐慌。

Marginalized Populations Within Canada

加拿大境内的边缘化群体与精神健康差距
Marginalized groups in Canada—including Indigenous peoples, refugees, racialized communities, and 2SLGBTQ+ individuals—face disproportionate rates of mental health challenges. These disparities are rooted in structural inequities, intergenerational trauma from residential schools, systemic racism, and social exclusion rather than inherent biological vulnerability.
加拿大境内的边缘化群体——包括原住民、难民、有色人种群体以及 2SLGBTQ+ 群体——面临着不成比例的高发精神健康挑战。这些健康差距深深植根于结构性不平等、寄宿学校制度造成的代际创伤、系统性种族歧视以及社会排斥，而非源于内在的生物学劣势。

The Effect of Mental Health Disorders on Families

精神健康障碍对家庭系统的全面冲击
Mental health disorders rarely affect an individual in isolation; they exert profound emotional, physical, and financial strains on the entire family unit. Family members often experience overwhelming feelings of sorrow, guilt, embarrassment, helplessness, and anger. Spouses and parents frequently wonder if they are somehow to blame for the client’s illness.
精神健康障碍极少仅孤立地影响患者个人；它会对整个家庭系统造成深远的情感、生理与经济压力。家属往往长期承受着巨大的悲痛、内疚、羞耻、无助以及愤怒情绪。配偶和父母常常反复自责，怀疑是否是自己在某些方面做错才导致亲人患病。
Caregiver burnout is a critical risk. Family caregivers often provide exhausting around-the-clock supervision, manage volatile behaviours, and coordinate medical appointments while neglecting their own health, leading to chronic illness and depression.
照顾者倦怠（Caregiver burnout）是极度严峻的现实风险。家庭照顾者常常需要承担筋疲力竭的昼夜看护、应对反复无常的情绪行为并协调繁杂的医疗随访，同时严重忽视自身的生理与心理健康，最终诱发慢性疾病与抑郁症。

How Support Workers Can Support Families

支持照护人员协助家庭的核心路径
  • Practice non-judgmental listening: Allow family members to express their exhaustion, frustration, and fears without fear of criticism.
    践行非评判性积极倾听：允许家属毫无顾忌地倾诉其疲惫、沮丧与恐惧，绝不进行道德评判。
  • Provide dependable respite: By delivering skilled, reliable care for the client's ADLs, you grant family members vital hours of rest to recharge their physical and emotional reserves.
    提供可靠的喘息照护：通过为服务对象提供熟练可靠的日常生活照护，为家庭照顾者争取宝贵的休息时间，以重建其身心能量。
  • Reinforce DIPPS principles: Ensure the client is treated with dignity so that the family feels assured their loved one is in compassionate, capable hands.
    贯彻 DIPPS 原则：确保服务对象始终获得充满尊严的专业对待，让家属真切感受到亲人得到了富有同理心与高水平的照护。

Acquired Brain Injury (ABI) and Behaviour

获得性脑损伤（ABI）对行为与情绪的损害
An acquired brain injury (ABI) is damage to the brain that occurs after birth and is not related to a congenital disorder or a degenerative disease. Causes include traumatic brain injuries (falls, motor vehicle collisions, sports concussions, assaults) and non-traumatic causes (stroke, aneurysm, anoxia, brain infections).
获得性脑损伤（ABI）是指在出生后发生且与先天性畸形或退行性神经疾病无关的大脑结构受损。常见病因包括创伤性脑损伤（跌倒撞击、车祸碰撞、运动脑震荡、暴力袭击）以及非创伤性病因（中风卒中、动脉瘤破裂、脑缺氧、颅内感染等）。
ABI can profoundly affect emotional regulation and personality. Damage to the frontal lobes often causes disinhibition, sudden emotional lability, impulsivity, severe anger outbursts, apathy, and impaired social judgment. Support workers must remember that these behaviours stem from damaged neural architecture, not deliberate malice.
ABI 会严重损害情绪调节能力与人格特征。大脑额叶受损往往会导致去抑制（言行失控）、突发的情绪不稳、极度冲动、狂暴发怒、冷漠无动于衷以及社会判断力严重丧失。支持照护员必须牢记，这些异常行为完全源于大脑神经回路的器质性破坏，绝非服务对象故意刁难或恶意挑衅。

Anxiety Disorders

焦虑障碍谱系全景解析
Anxiety is a vague, uneasy feeling in response to stress. In normal amounts, anxiety is adaptive and alerts us to danger. Anxiety disorders, however, are a group of mental health disorders whose main symptom is anxiety, with fears and worries so disproportionate to the situation as to impair normal daily functioning.
焦虑是面对压力时产生的一种模糊、局促不安且难以名状的不适情绪。在适度范围内，焦虑具有适应性价值并能警示潜在危险。然而，焦虑障碍是以病理性焦虑为核心表现的一组精神健康障碍，其恐惧与担忧程度与实际危险极不相称，严重损害个体的正常日常生活功能。
  • Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD): Chronic, excessive, and uncontrollable worry about everyday events and activities, accompanied by muscle tension, restlessness, fatigue, and insomnia.
    广泛性焦虑障碍（GAD）：对日常普通事务产生长期的、过度的且无法自控的病态担忧，并伴有肌肉持续紧张、坐立不安、易疲劳和顽固失眠。
  • Panic Disorder: Characterized by recurrent, unexpected panic attacks—sudden surges of intense terror, racing heart, chest pain, choking sensations, shortness of breath, trembling, and overwhelming fear of losing control or dying.
    惊恐障碍（Panic Disorder）：以反复突发、不可预测的惊恐发作为特征——突如其来的濒死感与极度恐惧、心动过速心悸、胸痛窒息感、呼吸急促、浑身颤抖以及对彻底失控或即将死亡的极端惊骇。
  • Phobic Disorders: Intense, irrational, and persistent fears of specific objects, animals, or situations (e.g., acrophobia—fear of heights; claustrophobia—fear of enclosed spaces; agoraphobia—fear of being in places where escape might be difficult during a panic attack).
    特定恐惧症（Phobic Disorders）：对特定物体、动物或处境产生强烈的、非理性的且持久的恐惧回避（例如：恐高症、幽闭恐惧症、广场/场所恐惧症——极度恐惧处于惊恐发作时难以迅速逃离的开阔或拥挤场所）。

Trauma- and Stressor-Related Disorders

创伤及应激相关障碍
Trauma- and stressor-related disorders are mental health conditions that develop after an individual has experienced, witnessed, or been confronted with a traumatic or severely stressful event, such as military combat, physical or sexual assault, hostage taking, catastrophic natural disasters, or severe motor vehicle accidents.
创伤及应激相关障碍是个体亲身经历、目睹或直接面临极其严重的灾难性创伤或应激事件（如军事作战、严重身体或性侵害、人质劫持、特大自然灾害或恶性车祸事故）后所发展演变出的一组精神健康障碍。

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)

创伤后应激障碍（PTSD）的核心临床特征
Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) can occur at any age, including in young children. Symptoms may begin within 3 months of the event or may be delayed for months or even years. PTSD involves four core symptom clusters:
创伤后应激障碍（PTSD）可在任何年龄发生，包括幼童。其病理症状可能在创伤事件发生后 3 个月内迅速显现，也可能潜伏数月甚至数年之久才延迟暴发。PTSD 具有四大核心症状群：
  • Intrusive memories: Recurrent, involuntary, and distressing memories of the trauma, terrifying nightmares, or dissociative flashbacks in which the individual feels as though the trauma is happening all over again.
    侵入性再体验：反复发生、非自主且令人极度痛苦的创伤记忆闯入、噩梦惊魂，或发生解离性闪回（Flashbacks，使患者身临其境般感觉惨剧正在眼前重演）。
  • Avoidance: Persistent efforts to avoid distressing memories, thoughts, feelings, people, places, conversations, or objects that serve as reminders of the traumatic event.
    持续性回避：极力回避与创伤事件相关的痛苦记忆、思绪、感受、特定场所、人物、对话或外界物品刺激。
  • Negative alterations in cognition and mood: Persistent negative emotional states (fear, guilt, shame), inability to feel positive emotions, feeling detached from loved ones, memory gaps regarding the event, and distorted blame toward oneself or others.
    认知与情绪的负面改变：持续被恐惧、内疚、自责和羞耻等消极情绪笼罩，丧失体验快乐的能力，与亲人疏离脱节，遗忘创伤关键细节，并产生扭曲的归因与自责。
  • Hyperarousal and reactivity: Being constantly on guard (hypervigilance), exaggerated startle response, irritability, aggressive outbursts, reckless or self-destructive behaviour, and severe difficulty concentrating and sleeping.
    高度唤醒与过度警觉：时刻处于防备戒备状态（过度警觉）、惊跳反射过强、易怒暴躁、突发攻击性言行、鲁莽或自我毁灭性举动，以及注意力严重涣散与顽固失眠。

Supporting Clients With Anxiety Disorders

支持焦虑障碍服务对象的照护规范
  • Avoid situations known to trigger anxiety: For example, if a client experiences acute panic in small, enclosed spaces (claustrophobia), avoid elevators or cramped utility rooms.
    规避已知诱发焦虑的情境：例如，如果服务对象在狭小封闭空间内会出现急性惊恐发作（幽闭恐惧症），应带其走宽敞楼梯，避免乘坐狭窄电梯或进入拥挤杂物间。
  • Avoid discussing topics that heighten distress: Redirect conversations gently toward neutral, soothing, or comforting subjects.
    避免探讨加剧痛苦的话题：温和地将对话引导至中性、舒缓或令其感到安心的积极话题。
  • Provide calm presence during panic episodes: Stay with a client experiencing severe anxiety or a panic attack. Use comforting, reassuring touch if culturally and personally acceptable. Offer limited, simple choices so decision-making does not induce further panic. Report the episode to your supervisor immediately.
    在惊恐发作期间提供镇定陪伴：当服务对象经历极度焦虑或惊恐发作时，寸步不离予以守护。在符合文化与个人习惯的前提下，使用温和轻抚予以安抚。提供极简明晰的选择，减轻决断压力；事后立即向主管护士详细汇报。

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder in Children and Older Persons

儿童与老年群体中的创伤后应激障碍特征
Children who experience traumatic events often manifest distress differently than adults. They may develop severe fear of the dark, fear of being alone, separation anxiety, night terrors, bedwetting, or chronic somatic complaints (stomach aches, headaches). They frequently conceal their distress from parents for fear of upsetting them.
经历过创伤性事件的儿童往往表现出与成年人截然不同的痛苦体征。他们可能出现严重的恐黑、害怕独处、分离焦虑、夜惊梦魇、尿床遗尿，或频繁主诉慢性躯体不适（胃痛、头痛）。他们常常因害怕父母难过而刻意隐瞒内心的恐惧。
In older adults, PTSD symptoms may resurface decades after military combat, persecution, or catastrophic loss, triggered by sensory reminders (loud fireworks, sirens), physical frailty, cognitive changes, or retirement-induced lack of distraction.
在老年人群体中，PTSD 症状可能在军事作战、战乱迫害或重大丧失数十年后死灰复燃，往往由感官刺激（如烟花巨响、警笛声）、身体虚弱、认知功能衰退或退休后缺乏日常分心事项而再次被诱发。

Bipolar and Related Disorders

双相及相关障碍（躁狂-抑郁症）
Bipolar and related disorders, formerly known as manic-depressive illness, are a group of related brain disorders that cause unusual shifts in a person’s mood, energy, activity levels, and ability to function, vastly different from normal daily ups and downs. Approximately 1% of Canadians live with this condition. Like diabetes or hypertension, bipolar disorder is a chronic, long-term illness requiring lifelong management.
双相及相关障碍（过去称为躁狂抑郁症）是一组脑部神经生物学障碍，会导致个体的心境、精力、活动水平及日常功能出现非同寻常的剧烈波动，与普通人日常的情绪起伏有着本质区别。全加拿大约有 1% 的人口患有该障碍。正如糖尿病或高血压一样，双相情感障碍是一种需要终身严谨医学管理的慢性长期疾患。
A person with bipolar disorder alternates between emotional lows (depressive episodes) and emotional highs (manic or hypomanic episodes), alongside periods of normal mood. A manic episode is diagnosed if an abnormally elevated, expansive, or irritable mood lasts at least 1 week, accompanied by heightened energy, grandiosity, decreased need for sleep, rapid pressured speech, racing thoughts, and reckless impulsive behaviours (e.g., spending sprees, dangerous investments).
双相障碍患者在情绪低谷（抑郁发作期）与情绪顶峰（躁狂或轻躁狂发作期）之间交替波动，其间可夹杂心境正常的间歇期。若异常高涨、膨胀或极易激惹的心境持续至少 1 周，并伴有精力过度旺盛、夸大妄想、睡眠需求急剧减少、言语滔滔奔逸、思维飞速奔跑以及鲁莽冲动行为（如挥霍无度、危险投资等），即可临床诊断为躁狂发作。
In children and adolescents, mood episodes are often characterized by rapid, volatile mood swings occurring multiple times within a single day, rather than the sustained weeks-long episodes seen in adults.
在儿童和青少年中，双相发作的特征往往表现为在单日内多次发生极度迅猛、反复无常的情绪骤变，而非成年人常见的那种持续数周的稳定单一发作期。

  • During depressive episodes: Follow all established major depression care guidelines, monitor for suicide risk, and assist with basic self-care activities.

  • During manic episodes: Provide a calm, low-stimulus environment without distractions. Offer simple, structured routines and limited choices. Encourage frequent short rest breaks and nutritious finger foods. Never argue, debate, or confront a manic client, which will provoke agitation.

Introduction to Depressive Disorders

抑郁障碍谱系导论
Depressive disorders are characterized by persistent sad, empty, or irritable moods accompanied by significant somatic and cognitive changes that severely impair the client’s ability to function. Normal grief (reactive sadness following the loss of a loved one or job) is an expected human emotion; major depressive disorder, however, is a debilitating medical illness requiring comprehensive clinical intervention.
抑郁障碍以持久的悲伤、空虚或易激惹心境为特征，并伴有显著的躯体与认知功能改变，严重损害服务对象的正常生活功能。正常的哀伤（如失去亲人或失业后的反应性悲痛）是人类可以理解的情感反应；然而，重度抑郁障碍则是一种剥夺生活能力的医学疾病，必须接受全面的临床干预。

Major Depressive Disorder and Treatment

重度抑郁障碍临床特征与治疗方式
Major depressive disorder consists of severe feelings of worthlessness, self-blame, sadness, disappointment, and emptiness that persist for weeks or months. It robs individuals of energy, disrupting sleep, appetite, concentration, and emotional affect. Up to 80% of individuals improve significantly when depression is recognized and properly treated with psychotherapy, antidepressant medication, or both.
重度抑郁障碍表现为持续数周乃至数月的极度无价值感、深刻自责、极度悲伤、绝望与精神空虚。它剥夺了个体的生命活力，严重扰乱睡眠、食欲、专注力与情感表达。一旦得到及时识别并接受心理治疗、抗抑郁药物或联合干预，高达 80% 的患者病情能获得显著改善。
Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) is an effective short-term medical treatment for severe, treatment-resistant depression or acute suicidal crisis. Under general anaesthesia and muscle relaxation, brief electrical impulses are applied to the temples to produce a controlled therapeutic cerebral seizure, believed to reset neurotransmitter pathways.
电休克疗法（ECT，电抽搐治疗）是针对难治性严重抑郁症或极度自杀危机的高效短期临床手段。在全身麻醉与肌肉松弛剂保护下，通过附着在患者颞部的电极向大脑释放短暂电脉冲，诱发可控的脑神经治疗性抽搐，重塑大脑神经递质传导通路。
Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD) is a subtype of depression that recurs annually during specific seasons—typically emerging in late autumn or winter when daylight hours decrease. SAD is effectively managed with daily phototherapy (light therapy boxes emitting white fluorescent light).
季节性情感障碍（SAD）是一种在每年固定季节（通常是秋冬日照缩短的寒冷月份）周期性复发的抑郁亚型。SAD 通常通过每日接受特制荧光光疗箱的高强度白光照射（光照疗法）得到有效缓解。

FOCUS ON OLDER PERSONS: Depression / 聚焦老年长者：老年抑郁症的隐匿特征与防范策略

  • Depression is common in older adults due to compounding losses: death of spouses, retirement, loss of physical independence, memory impairment, and transition to long-term care.
    抑郁症在老年人群体中极为常见，这与叠加的重大丧失密切相关：丧偶、失亲、退休脱节、身体活动独立性丧失、记忆减退以及迁入长期照护机构的适应压力。
  • Older persons rarely report feeling "depressed." Instead, they complain of persistent somatic aches, chronic fatigue, or extreme irritability. Depression is frequently misdiagnosed as dementia (pseudodementia) or delirium (see Chapter 38), leaving them untreated.
    老年长者极少主动承认自己“抑郁”。相反，他们往往表现为顽固的躯体各处酸痛、慢性疲乏或极度烦躁易怒。老年抑郁症极易被误诊为认知症痴呆（假性痴呆）或谵妄（参见第38章），导致病情长期得不到有效医治。
  • Support strategies: Ensure nutritious meals and hydration, monitor medication adherence, provide outdoor fresh air and sunlight, facilitate pet visits, encourage gentle physical movement, and create opportunities for reminiscence and feeling needed.
    支持照护策略：保障充足营养与水分、监测服药依从性、提供户外新鲜空气与阳光、安排温顺宠物探访、鼓励温和肢体活动，并创造怀旧叙事与让长者感受自身价值与被需要的温馨情境。

Other Depressive Disorders

其他特定抑郁障碍亚型
  • Disruptive mood dysregulation disorder (DMDD): Diagnosed in children under 10, characterized by severe, persistent irritability and frequent temper outbursts vastly disproportionate to the situation.
    破坏性心境失调障碍（DMDD）：见于10岁以下儿童，表现为持续严重的易激惹以及与实际诱因极不相符的狂暴发脾气。
  • Persistent depressive disorder (Dysthymia): A chronic depressed mood present for most of the day, for at least 2 years, accompanied by low energy, low self-esteem, poor concentration, and hopelessness.
    持续性抑郁障碍（恶劣心境）：持续至少 2 年以上、绝大部分时间心境低落的慢性抑郁状态，伴有低自尊、精力衰退、注意力不集中与绝望感。
  • Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD): Marked affective lability, irritability, dysphoria, and severe tension emerging regularly during the premenstrual week and resolving post-menses.
    经前期烦躁障碍（PMDD）：在月经周期的前一周规律性出现严重的情绪波动、暴躁易怒、抑郁心境与紧张焦虑，并在经期后完全缓解。
  • Substance/medication-induced depressive disorder: Depressive mood directly precipitated by alcohol/drug intoxication, withdrawal, or prescription medications.
    物质/药物所致抑郁障碍：直接由酒精毒品中毒、戒断反应或特定处方药物药理作用引发的突发抑郁心境。

BOX 37.3: Signs and Symptoms of Depression / 专栏 37.3：抑郁症的临床体征与症状全景表

  • Physical symptoms: Chronic fatigue, profound insomnia or hypersomnia, loss of appetite or overeating, unexplainable muscle aches, headaches, dry mouth, nausea, constipation, and psychomotor agitation or retardation.
    生理躯体症状：慢性极度疲乏、严重失眠或嗜睡、食欲骤减或暴饮暴食、查无实据的肌肉酸痛、头痛、口干、恶心呕吐、顽固便秘，以及运动精神迟滞或坐立不安。
  • Emotional and cognitive symptoms: Persistent sadness, emotional emptiness, crying spells, anhedonia (inability to experience pleasure), feelings of worthlessness, excessive guilt, paranoia, morbid preoccupation with death or suicide.
    情绪与认知症状：持久的深层悲伤、内心空虚麻木、无故哭泣、快感缺失（对所有爱好彻底丧失兴趣）、无价值感、病理性过度自责、多疑偏执、反复出现死亡意念或自杀计划。
  • Behavioural changes: Social withdrawal, neglect of personal hygiene, messy living space, refusal to complete ADLs, and inability to make decisions.
    行为与社交改变：全面避开人群社交、严重忽视个人清洁卫生、居住环境凌乱不堪、拒绝执行日常活动，以及甚至连最简单琐事都无法做出决断。

Supporting Clients With a Depressive Disorder

支持抑郁症服务对象的核心行动准则
  • Show authentic warmth: Express genuine care and listen attentively without rushing.
    传递真诚温暖：表达发自内心的关怀，耐心专注地倾听其诉说，绝不匆忙催促。
  • Never minimize their pain: Never utter platitudes like "Cheer up," "Snap out of it," or "Look how lucky you are." These imply the client is faking or responsible for their medical illness.
    切勿淡化其病痛：绝不能说“高兴点”、“振作起来”或“看看你有多幸运”等敷衍套话；这些话暗示服务对象是在装病或自找苦吃，会极大加剧其自责与绝望。
  • Promote gentle physical activity, nutrition, and rest: Balance structured routines with rest breaks, ensuring adequate protein and fluid intake.
    鼓励温和活动、营养与休息：在规律作息中穿插充足休息，确保优质蛋白质与水分摄入。
  • Maintain constant vigilance for suicidal warning signs (BOX 37.4): Report any sudden mood change or suicidal verbalization immediately.
    时刻警惕自杀先兆警报（专栏37.4）：对服务对象突然出现的情绪反常或言语线索立即向主管报告。

Disruptive, Impulse-Control, and Conduct Disorders

破坏性、冲动控制及品行障碍
Disruptive, impulse-control, and conduct disorders involve persistent problems in the self-control of emotions and behaviours that violate the rights of others or bring the individual into significant conflict with societal norms or authority figures. They manifest across three primary clusters: (1) angry/irritable mood, (2) argumentative/defiant behaviour, and (3) vindictiveness.
破坏性、冲动控制及品行障碍涉及在情绪和行为自我控制方面的持久缺陷，其行为公然侵犯他人基本人身权利，或使个体与社会准则、权威人物发生激烈冲突。其临床表现涵盖三大核心维度：（1）愤怒/易激惹心境；（2）争辩/违抗挑衅行为；（3）报复复仇心理。

Oppositional Defiant Disorder (ODD)

对立违抗性障碍（ODD）
Oppositional defiant disorder (ODD) is a recurrent pattern of angry, irritable, argumentative, defiant, and vindictive behaviour toward parents, teachers, and caregivers, persisting for at least 6 months. Symptoms include frequent temper loss, touchiness, arguing with adults, actively defying rules, deliberately annoying others, and blaming others for one’s own mistakes.
对立违抗性障碍（ODD）是指针对父母、教师及照护者表现出的愤怒、易激惹、好争辩、挑衅违抗以及报复性行为的反复发作模式，持续至少 6 个月以上。症状表现包括：频繁大发脾气、极度敏感易怒、经常与成年人顶撞争论、公然对抗拒不服从指令、故意激怒骚扰他人，以及将自己的过错推卸怪罪于别人。

BOX 37.4: Warning Signs of Suicidal Intent / 专栏 37.4：自杀意图与危机的高危警示信号

  • Verbal statements of death: Statements such as "I wish I were dead," "Everyone would be better off without me," or "I won't be a burden much longer."
    死亡言语表达：直接说出“我真希望自己死了”、“没有我大家都解脱了”或“我很快就不会再连累大家了”。
  • Giving away prized possessions: Handing over cherished electronics, jewelry, pets, or organizing wills and final affairs.
    赠送最珍贵财物：无缘无故将心爱的电子设备、传家珠宝、宠物送人，或突然立遗嘱交代后事。
  • Sudden uncharacteristic calmness: A sudden lift in depression or sudden happiness—indicating the person has finalized their suicide plan and made peace with death.
    反常的突然释然平静：长期重度抑郁者突然显得格外欢快或平静——这往往意味着其已下定自杀决心，在心理上对死亡达成和解，属于最危急信号。
  • Emergency action: NEVER leave the person alone. Call 9-1-1 or crisis lines immediately. Remove all sharp objects, ropes, belts, and medications.
    应急救助程序：绝不能让该个体独处！立即拨打 9-1-1 或危机干预热线。迅速收缴并锁闭所有锋利刀剪、绳索、皮带及药品。

Intermittent Explosive Disorder & Conduct Disorder

间歇性暴怒障碍与品行障碍（CD）
Intermittent explosive disorder involves recurrent, impulsive, sudden behavioural outbursts representing a failure to control aggressive impulses. The degree of aggressiveness is vastly disproportionate to any provocation. These explosive episodes result in assault, destruction of property, and severe legal and interpersonal consequences.
间歇性暴怒障碍表现为反复突发的冲动性暴力行为爆发，代表了个体在控制攻击冲动方面的严重失调。其狂暴攻击程度与诱因极不相称，常导致暴力殴斗、财物毁灭以及严重的法律制裁与人际破裂。

Conduct Disorder (CD)

品行障碍（CD）的恶性病理表现
Conduct disorder is a severe disorder in which the individual repeatedly violates the basic rights of others and major age-appropriate societal rules. It typically escalates from childhood lying and shoplifting to severe adolescent felonies: physical cruelty to animals and people, using lethal weapons, initiating physical fights, forced sexual activity, deliberate arson, breaking and entering, and severe deceitfulness.
品行障碍是一种极为严重的外化行为障碍，个体反复公然侵犯他人基本人身权利，肆意践踏社会年龄基本规范。其行为通常从童年期的撒谎、小偷小摸逐步升级为青春期的严重重罪：残害虐待动物与同伴、使用致命凶器、主动挑衅斗殴、强迫性侵害、故意纵火焚烧、破门入室盗窃以及欺诈勒索。

BOX 37.5: Dealing With Your Own Anger / 专栏 37.5：支持照护人员自身愤怒与情绪失控的疏导管理

  • Understand the hurt and fear underlying anger: Anger is almost always a secondary protective emotion rooted in fear, helplessness, feeling disrespected, or losing control.
    理解愤怒背后的恐惧与受伤：愤怒几乎始终是一种次生防御情绪，其根源往往深植于内在的恐惧、无助、感受到被贬低或局势失控感。
  • Take a therapeutic timeout: When provoked by an aggressive or defiant client, do not react defensively. Take slow, deep diaphragmatic breaths, step back to maintain safe physical space, and avoid shouting.
    进行治疗性情绪暂停：当遭到挑衅性或攻击性服务对象激怒时，切勿以防御姿态针锋相对。进行缓慢深腹式呼吸，主动后退一步保持安全身体间距，绝不高声叫嚷。
  • Debrief with supervisor: Unresolved anger leads to professional burnout and compromises client safety. Discuss difficult encounters with your team to formulate objective coping plans.
    与主管督导复盘倾诉：未能消解的愤怒会导致职业倦怠并危及服务对象安全。在团队复盘中探讨棘手冲突，共同制定客观理性的应对预案。

Supporting Clients With Conduct Disorders

支持品行障碍服务对象的临床照护原则
Caring for individuals with conduct disorder requires unwavering professional composure, firm boundaries, and consistent multidisciplinary rules. These clients frequently test limits, manipulate staff, and seek to provoke emotional reactions.
照护品行障碍服务对象需要极其沉稳的专业定力、坚定的行为界限以及跨团队高度一致的规则执行。这些服务对象常常会故意试探底线、操纵工作人员，并蓄意激惹照护者的情绪反应。
  • Establish clear, enforceable limits: Clearly define acceptable behaviours and predictable consequences in simple, unemotional language. Consistency among all staff members is vital; inconsistency invites manipulation.
    设立明确且可执行的行为界限：用简明客观、不带情绪的语言界定可接纳的行为与可预期的违规后果。所有团队成员执行标准必须完全一致；任何不一致都会为操控与钻空子打开大门。
  • Maintain non-confrontational communication: Speak in a calm, controlled, and respectful tone. Avoid power struggles, arguing, or matching the client’s aggression.
    保持非对抗性平静沟通：始终使用平静、沉稳且尊重的语调发声。坚决避免权力争夺、辩驳对质或以牙还牙式的对抗。
  • Protect personal safety: Position yourself near exits, maintain more than arm's-length distance, remove dangerous projectiles from the room, and call for immediate backup if violence erupts.
    严密保障人身安全：始终站在靠近出口的位置，保持至少一臂以上的安全防卫距离，提前清理房间内的危险抛掷物，一旦暴发肢体暴力立即呼叫安保与后援。

Feeding and Eating Disorders

进食与喂食障碍全景概述
Feeding and eating disorders are characterized by persistent disturbances of eating behaviours and severe altered body image perceptions, accompanied by an abnormal preoccupation with body weight and shape. While historically diagnosed predominantly in adolescent girls and young women, they increasingly affect men and older adults across all demographic groups. Eating disorders carry the highest mortality rate of any psychiatric condition.
进食与喂食障碍以持续存在的异常进食行为以及对体象认知的严重扭曲为特征，伴有对体重和体型的病态过分关注。尽管历史上多见于青春期少女与年轻女性，但近年来男性及年长者的发病率持续攀升。在所有精神医学疾患中，进食障碍具有最高的病死率。

Anorexia Nervosa

神经性厌食症（Anorexia Nervosa）
Anorexia nervosa is a life-threatening disorder characterized by: (1) restriction of energy intake relative to requirements, leading to significantly low body weight; (2) intense fear of gaining weight or becoming fat; and (3) disturbance in self-perceived body weight or shape. Even when emaciated and skeletal, individuals with anorexia look in the mirror and genuinely perceive themselves as "fat."
神经性厌食症是一种危及生命的严重疾患，具有三大核心特征：（1）持久严苛地限制能量摄入，导致体重显著低于正常维持生命的下限；（2）极度恐惧增重或发胖；（3）对自身体重和体型存在严重的妄想式认知扭曲。即使已经极度骨瘦如柴，厌食症患者在镜中看到自己时仍真切坚信自己“太胖了”。
Two subtypes exist: Restricting type (weight loss accomplished purely through dieting, fasting, or excessive compulsive exercise) and Binge-eating/purging type (engaging in recurrent episodes of binge eating followed by self-induced vomiting or misuse of laxatives, enemas, or diuretics).
临床分为两大亚型：限制型（纯粹通过极度节食、绝食或过量强迫运动达到消瘦）与暴食/清除型（反复发生暴饮暴食，随后通过自我诱发催吐、滥用导泻剂、灌肠剂或利尿剂排出食物）。

Physical Complications of Anorexia Nervosa

  • Severe emaciation, brittle nails, thinning hair, dry yellowish skin, and growth of fine downy lanugo hair over the body to conserve heat as subcutaneous fat vanishes.
    极度消瘦恶病质、指甲变脆、头发稀疏脱落、皮肤干燥发黄，且全身长出细软的胎毛（Lanugo），这是皮下脂肪消失后机体维持微弱体温的代偿反应。
  • Amenorrhea (cessation of menstrual periods), hypothermia (constant cold intolerance), bradycardia (dangerously slow pulse), hypotension, severe osteoporosis, and sudden fatal cardiac arrhythmias due to electrolyte collapse.
    闭经（停经）、严重低体温（常年畏寒冰冷）、心动过缓（脉搏极缓危险）、低血压、严重骨质疏松，以及因电解质崩溃引发的突发致死性恶性心律失常。

Bulimia Nervosa

神经性贪食症（Bulimia Nervosa）
Bulimia nervosa is characterized by recurrent episodes of binge eating (consuming abnormally massive amounts of food in a discrete period with a sense of lack of control) followed by inappropriate compensatory behaviours to prevent weight gain: self-induced vomiting, abuse of laxatives/diuretics, fasting, or excessive compulsive exercising.
神经性贪食症以反复发作的暴食（在短时间内吃下极其惊人数量的食物，并伴有失控感）为特征，随后采取病态的代偿行为以防发胖：自我催吐、滥用泻药/利尿剂、绝食或强迫性超负荷运动。
Unlike clients with anorexia, clients with bulimia typically maintain normal or slightly above-normal body weight, making the disorder harder to detect. However, repeated acid exposure causes severe erosion of tooth enamel, chronic sore throat, swollen salivary glands, esophageal tears, severe gastric rupture, and fatal potassium deficits.
与厌食症患者不同，贪食症患者的体重通常处于正常或略微偏高范围，导致该病更具隐蔽性。然而，胃酸反复倒流会导致牙釉质严重溶解腐蚀、慢性咽痛、唾液腺肿大酸痛、食管裂伤出血、胃穿孔破裂以及致命的低血钾症。

Pica and Rumination Disorder

异食癖与反刍障碍
  • Pica: The persistent, compulsive eating of non-nutritive, non-food substances (e.g., dirt, clay, paint chips, chalk, paper, ice) for at least 1 month. Can lead to lead poisoning, bowel obstructions, and lethal intestinal perforations.
    异食癖（Pica）：持续至少 1 个月以上病态嗜食非营养性非食用物质（如泥土、黏土、油漆皮、粉笔、纸张、冰块等）。极易引发铅中毒、肠梗阻与致死性消化道穿孔。
  • Rumination Disorder: Repeated regurgitation of food back into the mouth after eating, which is then re-chewed, re-swallowed, or spit out, not caused by a gastrointestinal condition.
    反刍障碍：进食后反复将食物非自主反流至口腔，重新咀嚼、吞咽或吐出，且排除了器质性胃肠道疾病。

Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders

强迫及相关障碍谱系概述
Obsessive-compulsive and related disorders are mental health disorders characterized by recurrent obsessions and compulsions that interfere significantly with a person’s daily life, occupational functioning, and social relationships.
强迫及相关障碍是一组以反复发作的强迫观念与强迫动作为主要特征的精神健康障碍，显著干扰个体的日常生活、职业工作与社会人际关系。
An obsession is a persistent, intrusive, unwanted thought, urge, or image that causes marked anxiety and distress (e.g., constant fear of contamination by germs). A compulsion is a repetitive, rigid behaviour or mental act that the person feels driven to perform in response to an obsession, aimed at preventing anxiety or dread (e.g., washing hands until raw, checking door locks dozens of times).
强迫观念（Obsession）是无法通过意志驱除、反复侵入大脑的侵入性念头、冲动或意象，引发极度焦虑与痛苦（如对病菌污染的恒久恐惧）。强迫行为（Compulsion）是个体为了减轻强迫观念带来的恐惧痛苦而被迫反复执行的刻板仪式动作或心理活动（如洗手直至脱皮出血、反复数十次检查门锁是否锁好）。
PAGE 879 · 第 879 页 Obsessive-Compulsive Spectrum & Related Disorders / 强迫症谱系、躯体变形障碍与囤积障碍

Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders

强迫及相关障碍谱系临床特征
Obsessions are recurrent, persistent, and unwanted thoughts, urges, or images that cause intense distress. The person attempts to ignore, suppress, or neutralize them by performing a compulsion—an uncontrollable repetitive act or ritual. Examples include fear of contamination, intense symmetry demands, or distressing forbidden thoughts.
强迫观念是反复出现、持久侵入且违背意愿的念头、冲动或意象，引发极度痛苦与焦虑。个体试图通过执行强迫行为（一种无法抑制的刻板重复仪式动作）来忽视、压抑或中和这些观念。常见例子包括：对病菌污染的恐惧、对对称排列的极致苛求，或令人痛苦的侵入性禁忌念头。

Related Obsessive-Compulsive Disorders

强迫相关障碍亚型
  • Body Dysmorphic Disorder: Preoccupation with one or more perceived defects or flaws in physical appearance that are unnoticeable or appear slight to others, driving repetitive mirror-checking or grooming rituals.
    躯体变形障碍：对自身外貌中某种在他人看来微不足道或根本不存在的“缺陷”产生病态过度关注，促使其反复照镜子或进行仪式性打扮遮掩。
  • Hoarding Disorder: Persistent difficulty discarding or parting with possessions, regardless of actual value, resulting in hazardous accumulation that clutters living spaces and endangers safety.
    囤积障碍：持久难以丢弃或舍弃物品（无论其实际价值如何），导致居住空间堆积如山，引发严重的跌倒与火灾安全隐患。
  • Trichotillomania (Hair-Pulling) and Excoriation (Skin-Picking): Recurrent pulling out of one’s hair or picking at one’s skin resulting in hair loss or severe skin lesions.
    拔毛癖与抓痕障碍：反复拔除自己的毛发或反复抠抓自己的皮肤，导致大面积脱发斑秃或严重的皮肤破溃感染。

Supporting Clients With OCD

支持强迫症服务对象的照护准则
  • Do not interrupt rituals abruptly: Forcibly preventing a compulsion elevates anxiety to unbearable panic levels. Allow the client to complete their ritual while the clinical team works on desensitization.
    切勿强行突然打断仪式：暴力阻断强迫动作会使患者的焦虑瞬间飙升至难以承受的惊恐崩溃状态。在专业团队实施脱敏治疗期间，应给予其必要时间完成仪式。
  • Recognize accomplishments: Acknowledge that resisting even a minor compulsion takes monumental courage and exhaustion for the client.
    认可每一步进步：深刻理解对强迫症患者而言，哪怕只是推迟或抵抗一次微小的强迫动作，都需要付出巨大的勇气与心力消耗。

Personality Disorders

人格障碍的本质与临床界定
Personality disorders are a group of psychiatric conditions involving rigid, enduring, and deeply ingrained maladaptive patterns of thinking, feeling, interpersonal relating, and impulse control that deviate markedly from cultural expectations. These patterns cause profound impairment in relationships and social functioning.
人格障碍是一组以僵化、持久且根深蒂固的适应不良性思维、情感、人际交往及冲动控制模式为特征的精神疾患，明显偏离个体所属文化的常态期望。这些顽固模式会导致严重的人际关系破裂与社会功能损伤。

Common Types of Personality Disorders

常见人格障碍临床亚型
  • Paranoid Personality Disorder: Pervasive distrust and suspiciousness of others, interpreting benign motives as malevolent, hostile, or demeaning.
    偏执型人格障碍：对他人抱有普遍的无端猜疑与不信任，将善意或中性的言行恶意曲解为故意贬低、敌意或迫害。
  • Antisocial Personality Disorder (ASPD): Pervasive pattern of disregard for and violation of the rights of others, deceitfulness, impulsivity, aggressive criminal behaviour, and complete lack of remorse or empathy.
    反社会型人格障碍（ASPD）：漠视并肆意侵犯他人权利、极度善于撒谎欺骗、行事冲动鲁莽、频繁从事违法暴力活动，且完全缺乏悔意或同理心。
  • Borderline Personality Disorder (BPD): Marked instability in interpersonal relationships, self-image, and affects, alongside acute fears of abandonment, chronic feelings of emptiness, and recurrent suicidal or self-harm gestures.
    边缘型人格障碍（BPD）：在人际关系、自我体象及情绪调节方面表现出剧烈的不稳定性，伴有对被抛弃的极端恐惧、慢性格局空虚感，以及反复出现的自伤或自杀威胁。
  • Narcissistic Personality Disorder (NPD): Pervasive grandiosity, need for excessive admiration, sense of entitlement, exploitative behaviours, and lack of empathy for others.
    自恋型人格障碍（NPD）：表现为极度的夸大狂妄、渴望无休止的崇拜赞美、特权阶层妄自尊大感、在人际交往中剥削利用他人，且极度缺乏对他人感受的同理心。

Supporting Clients With Personality Disorders

支持人格障碍服务对象的专业边界
Support workers must maintain firm, professional boundaries. Do not share personal details, home addresses, or phone numbers. Treat the client with non-judgmental respect, remain objective, and do not get drawn into "splitting" (where the client plays staff members against one another).
支持照护人员必须严守清晰的专业界限。严禁向服务对象透露个人隐私、家庭住址或私人电话号码。始终以客观、非评判的尊重态度提供照护，绝不卷入服务对象挑拨离间员工的“分裂心理机制”（Splitting）。

Schizophrenia Spectrum and Other Psychotic Disorders

精神分裂症谱系及其他精神病性障碍
Schizophrenia is an extremely complex, severe, and chronic brain disorder characterized by psychosis—a mental state in which an individual’s ability to perceive reality is impaired. Typical onset occurs between late teens and early thirties.
精神分裂症是一种极其复杂、严重且慢性的脑部器质与生化障碍，以精神病态（Psychosis，即个体感知和检验客观现实的能力严重受损）为核心特征。该病通常在青春期晚期至三十岁出头首次暴发。

Positive Symptoms of Psychosis

精神分裂症阳性症状
  • Delusions: Fixed, false beliefs firmly held despite overwhelming evidence to the contrary. These include persecutory delusions (believing oneself targeted or poisoned), grandiose delusions (believing oneself to be a deity or billionaire), and delusions of reference (believing ordinary media messages contain coded personal orders).
    妄想（Delusions）：即使面对确凿的相反事实证据，仍坚信不疑的固定错误信念。包括：被害妄想（坚信自己被暗中监控或投毒）、夸大妄想（自称神明或拥有无限财富），以及关系妄想（坚信广播电视新闻在向自己传达秘密代号指令）。
  • Hallucinations: Perception-like experiences that occur without an external stimulus. Auditory hallucinations (hearing voices whispering, criticizing, or commanding acts) are most common, though visual, olfactory, and tactile hallucinations also occur.
    幻觉（Hallucinations）：在没有任何客观外界刺激的情况下产生的生动知觉体验。其中以言语性幻听（听见有人低语、斥责嘲笑或下达命令）最为普遍，视幻觉、嗅幻觉及触幻觉亦可发生。
  • Disorganized Thinking and Speech: Loose associations where conversation derails from one topic to an unrelated one, or "word salad" (incoherent strings of words).
    思维和言语紊乱：联想散漫破裂，言语内容在毫无逻辑关联的话题间随意跳跃，严重时甚至出现“词语杂拌”（杂乱无章、完全无法理解的断裂语句）。

Negative Symptoms and Course of Illness

精神分裂症阴性症状与病程演变
Negative symptoms represent a diminution or absence of normal emotional and behavioural functions. These include: Flat affect (immobile facial expression, monotone voice, lack of eye contact), Avolition (inability to initiate and persist in goal-directed self-care activities), Alogia (poverty of speech), and Anhedonia/Asociality (profound withdrawal from human contact).
阴性症状代表正常情绪和行为功能的减退或丧失。具体表现包括：情感淡漠/平淡（面部毫无表情、语调刻板单调、缺乏眼神接触）、意志减退（丧失发起并坚持日常自我照护活动的能力）、言语贫乏（说话极其简略），以及快感缺失与社交退缩（极度避开人群孤立独处）。
Schizophrenia typically follows a fluctuating course of acute psychotic episodes interspersed with periods of remission, where symptoms lessen significantly with consistent pharmacotherapy.
精神分裂症的病程通常呈现波动性，表现为急性精神病发作期与缓解期间歇交替；在长期规律服用抗精神病药物治疗下，多数患者的症状可获得显著减轻。

Care Guidelines for Clients With Schizophrenia

精神分裂症服务对象的照护原则
  • Do not argue with delusions: Never attempt to debate or logically disprove a delusion. Arguing increases anxiety and damages trust. Acknowledge the client's distress without validating the false belief (e.g., "That sounds frightening, but I do not see insects on the wall. You are safe here with me.").
    绝不与妄想争辩对质：切勿试图用逻辑去反驳或辩倒服务对象的妄想；争辩只会加剧其惊恐并破坏信任。承认其内心痛苦的真实感受，同时温和表明现实立场（例如：“那听起来确实很吓人，但我并没有看到墙上有虫子。在我身边你是绝对安全的。”）。
  • Do not play along with hallucinations: Never pretend you see or hear what the client is experiencing, which reinforces psychosis.
    绝不附和顺应幻觉：绝不能说谎假装自己也看到了或听到了服务对象的幻觉内容，那会进一步加深强化其精神病态。
  • Provide calm structure and assist with ADLs: Support personal hygiene and nutrition gently, as negative symptoms often lead to severe self-neglect.
    提供平静规律的生活并协助ADLs：温和协助其完成个人清洁与进食营养，因为阴性症状往往会导致严重的自我忽视。

Self-Harm (Non-Suicidal Self-Injury)

非自杀性自伤行为（NSSI）
Self-harm (also called non-suicidal self-injury, NSSI) is the deliberate, direct destruction of body tissue without conscious suicidal intent. Methods include cutting, burning, hitting oneself, picking at wounds, or head-banging. Although not an attempt to die, self-harm is a serious manifestation of profound emotional distress.
自伤行为（亦称非自杀性自伤，NSSI）是指在没有明确自杀意图的情况下，故意直接破坏自身肉体组织的行为。常见手段包括用刀片划伤皮肤、用烟头烫伤、拳击撞墙、撕扯伤口或剧烈撞击头部。尽管自伤通常并非为了致死，但它是内心深处遭受极度情感创伤与痛苦的严重外化体现。
Why do people self-harm? Individuals often report using physical pain to: (1) escape intense, unbearable emotional numbness; (2) release overwhelming anxiety, anger, or guilt; (3) punish themselves for perceived failings; or (4) regain a sense of control over their bodies when their life feels totally chaotic.
个体为何会选择自伤？患者往往解释，他们试图借由肉体剧痛来：（1）摆脱令人窒息的内在情感麻木感；（2）宣泄排解压倒性的焦虑、狂暴愤怒或内疚感；（3）对自认为的过错实施自我惩罚；或（4）在整个现实生活陷入彻底失控时，重新夺回对自身肉体的一丝掌控感。

Supporting Clients Who Self-Harm

支持自伤服务对象的核心规范
  • Respond with calm compassion: Never express disgust, anger, horror, or scold the client. Shaming increases their emotional pain and drives the behaviour further underground.
    以平静同理心应对：绝不能表现出厌恶、愤怒、惊恐或当面斥责服务对象。羞辱只会加深其内疚痛苦，并将自伤行为推向更隐蔽的深渊。
  • Ensure medical wound care: Treat any physical wounds professionally and promptly to prevent cellulitis and sepsis.
    确保专业医疗清创包扎：迅速对肉体创伤进行规范的消毒与医疗处置，杜绝蜂窝织炎与严重感染。
  • Focus on emotional triggers, not the cuts: Discuss what feelings precipitated the urge. Encourage healthy replacement coping strategies (e.g., holding ice cubes, journaling, deep breathing).
    关注心理诱因而非单纯盯着伤口：温和探讨引发自伤冲动的内在情绪。鼓励运用健康的替代性应对技巧（如手握冰块、书写日记宣泄、深呼吸等）。

Sleep–Wake Disorders

睡眠-觉醒障碍
Sleep-wake disorders involve problems with the quality, timing, and amount of sleep, resulting in daytime distress and impairment in social and occupational functioning. Common conditions include Insomnia (difficulty initiating or maintaining sleep), Obstructive Sleep Apnea (repeated breathing pauses during sleep), and Narcolepsy (uncontrollable daytime sleep attacks). Support workers should track sleep patterns, maintain dark and quiet bedroom environments, and observe medication effects.
睡眠-觉醒障碍涉及睡眠质量、时相及总量的病理异常，导致明显的日间身心痛苦与社会功能受损。常见病症包括：失眠障碍（入睡困难或易醒早醒）、阻塞性睡眠呼吸暂停（睡眠期间呼吸反复骤停）以及发作性睡病（白天不可抗拒的突发猝倒入睡）。支持照护员应详细记录睡眠日志、营造安静黑暗的卧室环境并密切观察助眠药物反应。

Substance-Related and Addictive Disorders

物质相关及成瘾障碍
Substance-related and addictive disorders involve the deliberate misuse of and inability to cease abusing substances despite catastrophic physiological, legal, financial, and personal consequences. Substances include alcohol, prescription narcotics/opioids, sedatives, street drugs (cocaine, methamphetamine, heroin, fentanyl), and cannabis.
物质相关及成瘾障碍是指明知已造成灾难性的生理器官损害、法律诉讼、经济破产和人际破裂，仍故意滥用且无法自主停用成瘾物质的慢性脑部疾患。涉及物质包括酒精、处方阿片类麻醉镇痛药、镇静催眠药、街头违禁毒品（可卡因、冰毒、海洛因、芬太尼）及大麻。
  • Drug Tolerance: The biological state where the body adapts to a substance, requiring larger and larger doses to achieve the original psychological or physical effect.
    药物耐受性（Drug Tolerance）：机体对特定物质产生生化适应，必须持续大幅增加药量才能达到最初同等心理刺激或麻醉效应的生理状态。
  • Drug Withdrawal: A cluster of distressing, painful, and potentially life-threatening physiological symptoms that emerge when a person abruptly ceases or reduces substance consumption after chronic dependence.
    药物戒断反应（Drug Withdrawal）：长期严重依赖成瘾物质的个体在骤然停用或减量时，身体和神经系统所爆发的一系列剧烈痛苦乃至危及生命的躯体反跳反应。

Detoxification and Addiction Support

医疗脱瘾治疗与支持照护人员伦理
Detoxification (detox) is the medically supervised process of clearing toxins from the body while managing acute withdrawal symptoms safely. Severe alcohol withdrawal, for example, can cause delirium tremens (DTs)—characterized by severe shaking, acute confusion, seizures, and visual hallucinations—which carries a high mortality rate without hospital care.
脱瘾治疗（Detox）是在严密医疗监护下，安全清除体内毒物并对症处理急性戒断反应的临床过程。例如，严重的酒精戒断可诱发震颤谵妄（DTs）——表现为剧烈颤抖、极度谵妄混乱、癫痫大发作与生动视幻觉——若无专业住院抢救具有极高的致死率。
  • Avoid confrontation: Never argue with a client regarding their addiction. Report signs of intoxication or covert alcohol storage to your supervisor immediately.
    避免正面对质：绝不能就成瘾问题与服务对象发生言语争辩。一旦发现醉酒迹象或隐匿藏酒，立即向主管护士汇报。
  • Strict professional ethics: NEVER purchase alcohol, cigarettes, or drugs for a client, regardless of pleas. Doing so violates the law and professional standards.
    恪守职业伦理红线：无论服务对象如何苦苦哀求，绝对禁止替其代购任何酒精、香烟或药物，违者将触犯法律并被开除吊销资格。

Suicidal Behaviour Disorder

自杀行为障碍
Suicidal behaviour disorder involves recurrent thoughts of, preoccupation with, or attempts at ending one’s own life. Suicide is never a random event; it is almost always the tragic culmination of unbearable psychological agony combined with untreated psychiatric illness, severe depression, chronic pain, or overwhelming isolation.
自杀行为障碍以反复出现自杀念头、过度沉溺于终结自身生命或实施自杀企图为特征。自杀绝非偶然发生的孤立事件；它几乎始终是无法承受的剧烈心理剧痛与未获医治的精神疾患、重度抑郁、慢性躯体绝症或极度孤独绝望相互叠加的悲剧性结果。

Older Adults and Suicide Risk

老年高龄群体的极高自杀风险
Did you know that older adults over the age of 85 have the highest suicide rate of any age group in Canada? Older men, in particular, face extremely elevated risk due to chronic physical illness, loss of autonomy, grief from spousal death, and social isolation. Their suicide attempts are far more lethal because they rarely communicate intent as a cry for help.
你是否知道，全加拿大85岁以上的高龄长者群体拥有所有年龄段中最高的自杀率？尤其是高龄男性长者，因饱受慢性顽疾折磨、生活自理能力丧失、丧偶孤寂以及严重的社会脱节，面临着极高的自杀风险。他们实施自杀的手段往往极其致命果决，极少作为呼救信号展现，因此防范难度极高。

Immediate Actions for Suicidal Crisis

自杀危机的即时应急干预生命准则

Emergency Suicide Protocol: Life-Saving Steps / 生命守护：自杀危机应急处置四大硬门禁

  • 1. NEVER leave the client alone: If a client voices suicidal thoughts, states they have a plan, or you suspect immediate danger, stay with them continuously. Do not leave the room.
    1. 绝对寸步不离：如果服务对象吐露自杀念头、自称已有具体计划或你怀疑存在即时生命危险，必须寸步不离陪伴监护，严禁让其独处！
  • 2. Call for immediate emergency assistance: Contact your supervisor and emergency response services (9-1-1 / Crisis Hotline) immediately via mobile phone.
    2. 立即呼叫紧急救援：立刻掏出手机拨打主管电话及紧急急救热线（9-1-1 / 自杀危机干预热线），通报精确地址与现场危机。
  • 3. Remove means of self-harm safely: Without endangering yourself, remove medications, sharp cutlery, razors, ropes, or firearms from the immediate vicinity.
    3. 安全排险消除隐患：在确保自身安全的前提下，迅速将服务对象触手可及范围内的所有大量药品、刀具、剃须刀片、绳索或枪支转移锁闭。
  • 4. Provide calm, reassuring presence: Speak in a gentle, caring voice: "I care about you, you are not alone, and we are going to keep you safe."
    4. 给予沉着温和的心理锚定：用坚定充满关切的语调发声：“我很在乎你，你绝不是孤身一人，我们会一直陪着你、确保你的安全。”

Review Question 21 & Conflict De-escalation

原版考题 21 与成瘾群体家庭肢体冲突现场化解规范
Question 21 tests a critical frontline emergency: two residents in an addiction recovery group home engaged in physical violence over a TV remote. As the first arriving support worker, how do you safely intervene without escalating aggression?
第 21 题重点考核前线紧急突发危机处置：在成瘾康复群居家庭中，两位新住户因抢夺电视遥控器发生肢体暴力互殴。作为首位赶到现场的支持照护员，如何在不激化冲突的前提下安全化解危机？

Official Publisher Rationale for Question 21 / Elsevier 官方权威深度临床精解（第 21 题）

  • Answer: B. You should separate the two clients and talk to them separately. Begin with Bob, the aggressor. You should speak in a firm but soft voice that is both professional and friendly.
    官方标准答案：B。首要任务是将两名服务对象物理分隔开，并分别与他们单独沟通。首先与发起攻击的鲍勃交谈，照护员应用坚定但温和、专业且友善的语调发声。
  • De-escalation mechanics: NEVER grab an aggressive client, as doing so might increase the client’s anger. Distance yourself so that you are just beyond arm’s length in case the client tries to hit you.
    降温降维机制：切勿身体强行拉扯或抓住激动的服务对象，这极易激化其狂怒并导致暴力转向自身；始终保持在一步以外的安全臂长距离防备突袭。
  • Defusing and skill reinforcement: By separating the two from each other and from other clients, you can help defuse this situation. Try to discuss ways that they can deal with their issues in a nonconfrontational manner. After they calm down, you can try role-playing with them to reinforce what you have discussed.
    化解事态与技能重构：将双方彼此隔离并远离其他群体，能迅速降温局势；随后以非对抗的方式引导他们探讨解决分歧的建设性途径，并在情绪彻底稳定后通过角色扮演强化良性应对技能。
