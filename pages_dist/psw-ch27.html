p. 592 基础营养学生理过程与六大营养素 (Basic Nutrition & Key Terms)
Food and water are necessary for life and health. The amount and quality of foods and fluids in the diet are important, as they affect a person’s current as well as future health and well-being. For all age groups, a poor diet will affect physical and mental growth and function, increase the risk for disease, and slow healing. Poor physical and mental functioning increases the risk for accidents and injuries. Food and drink contribute to social and emotional health also. Eating and drinking are part of social activity with family and friends (FIGURE 27.1). As a support worker, you will serve food and fluids to clients and assist them with eating. In home care, you may also prepare meals for clients. This chapter introduces you to the basics of nutrition.
食物与水分是维持生命、保障健康的不可或缺之源。膳食中食物与体液的供给总量及其营养品质至关重要，直接塑造并深远影响一个人当前乃至未来的长久身心福祉。在人生的所有年龄阶段，营养匮乏或失衡的劣质饮食均会阻滞生理与心理的健全发育，损害机体各项生理机能，显著增加慢性疾病的易感风险，并极大延缓组织与创口的康复进程。不良的身心状态还会增加跌倒滑脱等意外伤害的发生概率。饮食不仅供给养分，更是维系社交互动与情感健康的核心纽带。与家人、挚友围桌共餐是人类不可替代的社交愉悦体验（见图 27.1）。作为支持人员，你将承担为案主分发餐盘、协助其体面进食的职责；在居家照护中，你还需亲自为案主科学烹饪膳食。本章将系统引领你步入基础营养学的专业殿堂。

BASIC NUTRITION

基础营养学生理过程（Basic Nutrition）

Nutrition refers to the processes involved in the ingestion, digestion, absorption, and use of foods and fluids by the body. Ingestion is the process of taking food and fluids into the body. Digestion is the process of physically and chemically breaking down nutrients so that they can be absorbed for use by the cells. Absorption is the process by which substances pass through the intestinal wall into the blood. Review the description of the digestive system in Chapter 32.
营养（Nutrition）是指机体摄取、物理咀嚼与化学消化分解、黏膜吸收以及在全身细胞水平利用食物与水分的全部动态生理生化过程的总和。摄入（Ingestion）是指将食物与体液吃入或饮入口腔消化道的过程。消化（Digestion）则是指通过胃肠道机械蠕动与各种消化酶的化学作用，将复杂大分子营养物质逐步分解为微小分子以便细胞吸收利用的过程。吸收（Absorption）是指小分子营养素穿透小肠绒毛黏膜上皮细胞屏障、渗透进入血液微循环或淋巴管的生理转运过程（可复习第32章消化系统的解剖生理学阐述）。
Good nutrition is needed for growth, healing, and the maintenance of body functions. Selected foods must provide a well-balanced diet and the correct amount of calories to sustain a healthy weight range. A diet high in fat and empty calories (lacking in nutrients) can cause unwanted health problems. Those who follow Canada’s Food Guide (2019) will obtain all of the nutrients they need for a balanced diet. Health care providers can refer to Health Canada’s document entitled Canada’s Dietary Guidelines, which accompanies the 2019 version of Canada’s Food Guide. These guidelines explain how health care workers and policymakers can promote healthy eating and overall nutritional well-being for all Canadians.
均衡优质的营养是机体生长发育、创伤愈合以及维系各系统平稳运转的生理基石。日常膳食所选取的食材必须构建起结构平衡的营养体系，并提供恰到好处的卡路里热量，以维持机体处于理想的健康体重区间。长期摄入高脂以及充斥着“空热量”（Empty Calories，即仅提供单纯热量而极度匮乏维生素与矿物质的高糖高油垃圾食品）的膳食，将诱发严重的肥胖、心血管硬化及代谢性疾患。遵循 2019 年版《加拿大膳食指南》的人群，将能够全面获取满足平衡膳食所需的全部核心营养素。医护人员可参阅加拿大卫生部发布的配套专业技术文献《加拿大膳食准则》（Canada's Dietary Guidelines），该指南为医护人员与公共政策制定者提供了如何全方位促进全体国民健康饮食与营养福祉的权威实操指引。

Nutrients and Calories

营养素分类与卡路里能量代谢（Nutrients & Calories）

Foods and fluids contain nutrients—substances that are ingested, digested, absorbed, and used by the body. Nutrients are grouped into proteins, carbohydrates, fats, vitamins, minerals, and water. Proteins, fats, and carbohydrates give the body fuel for energy. The amount of energy provided by a nutrient is measured in calories. A calorie is a unit of measure of the energy produced as the body burns food. For example: 1 g of carbohydrate supplies the body with 4 calories; 1 g of protein supplies the body with 4 calories; 1 g of fat supplies the body with 9 calories.
食物与体液中蕴含着维持生命所必需的营养素（Nutrients）——即能够被机体摄入、消化、吸收并利用的活性化学成分。营养素科学划分为六大核心类别：蛋白质、碳水化合物、脂肪、维生素、矿物质以及水。其中，蛋白质、脂肪与碳水化合物是人体的三大能量供给燃料（宏量供能营养素）。营养素所能释放的热量大小以卡路里（Calorie，即大卡 kcal）为法定计量单位。卡路里是度量人体在代谢氧化分解食物分子时所释放能量的物理单位。其能量转换公式为：每 1 克碳水化合物氧化释放 4 大卡能量；每 1 克蛋白质氧化释放 4 大卡能量；每 1 克脂肪氧化释放高达 9 大卡的高密度能量。

Protein

1. 蛋白质（Protein）：机体生长与修复的基石

Protein is needed for tissue growth and repair. Protein sources include meat, fish, poultry, eggs, milk and milk products, cereals, beans, peas, and nuts. Animal products are the best sources of protein because they contain all essential amino acids. People who do not eat animal products must consume sufficient protein from plant sources, which are best eaten in combinations—for example, beans and rice. Protein deficiency can result in severe malnutrition. Children and older people who do not eat properly are at the greatest risk.
蛋白质是人体全身所有组织器官生长发育与创伤修复不可或缺的首要物质。丰富的蛋白质来源涵盖畜肉、鱼类水产、禽肉、蛋类、乳制品、谷物、大豆、豌豆以及坚果。动物性食品是公认的完全优质蛋白质的最佳源泉，因为其氨基酸组成模式与人体高度吻合。完全不食用动物产品的严格素食者，必须通过互补搭配摄入多样化的植物性蛋白质以获取全部必需氨基酸——例如经典的豆类与糙米混合食用（蛋白质互补效应）。长期的蛋白质摄入不足将导致严重的低蛋白血症、免疫崩溃与重度营养不良；缺乏科学照护的儿童与咀嚼吞咽困难的老年群体面临最严峻的致病风险。

Carbohydrates

2. 碳水化合物（Carbohydrates）：清洁高效的能量中枢与肠道纤维

Carbohydrates provide energy for the body and fibre for bowel elimination and to maintain bowel regularity. Most carbohydrates come from plants. There are three main kinds of carbohydrates: Simple sugars (found in table sugar, fruits, and fruit juices); Starches (found in bread, pasta, rice, and potatoes); and Fibre (found in bran, nuts, seeds, and raw fruits with skins). Fibre cannot be digested by human enzymes and passes through the intestines undigested, adding bulk to stool. During digestion, most carbohydrates (except fibre) are broken down into sugars (glucose), which are then absorbed into the bloodstream.
碳水化合物是人体脑力与体力活动最核心、最经济的清洁供能中枢，同时提供不可或缺的膳食纤维以促进规律排便。自然界中的绝大多数碳水化合物来源于植物。碳水化合物在结构上划分为三大主流形态：1. 单糖与双糖（Simple sugars）：天然存在于食糖、新鲜水果及纯果汁中，吸收迅速；2. 复合淀粉（Starches）：广泛存在于面包、全麦面食、米饭、马铃薯与豆类中，水解平缓；3. 膳食纤维（Fibre）：富集于麦麸、粗粮、坚果、种籽以及带皮的生鲜果蔬中。人体消化道缺乏水解纤维素的消化酶，因此膳食纤维不被小肠吸收而直接进入结肠，增加粪便体积并吸附毒素，是维持正常肠道蠕动、预防结肠癌与憩室炎的关键卫士。在消化过程中，除膳食纤维外，所有可消化碳水化合物均被逐步水解为葡萄糖，随后吸收入血供能。

Fats

3. 脂肪（Fats）：能量储备、脂溶性维生素载体与美味源泉

Fats provide concentrated energy, help the body absorb fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), and add flavour and satiety to food. Some dietary fat is necessary for life. Dietary fat that is not burned by the body is stored in adipose tissue as body fat. There are three main types of dietary fat: • Saturated fat: Found in animal and dairy products (e.g., meat, butter, milk, cheeses). Excess intake raises blood cholesterol. • Trans fat: Found in commercial margarine, shortening, store-bought cookies, cakes, pies, doughnuts, and deep-fried foods. Trans fat is created through industrial hydrogenation to solidify liquid oils and extend shelf life; it is the least healthy fat and significantly increases coronary heart disease. • Unsaturated fat: Found in fish, nuts, seeds, and vegetable oils (canola oil, olive oil). Unsaturated fat is heart-healthy and helps lower LDL cholesterol.
脂肪不仅提供高密度的储备能量，更是运载并协助机体吸收脂溶性维生素（A、D、E、K）的必需生理载体，同时赋予食物浓郁的香气与持久的饱腹感。适量的健康脂肪是维系神经髓鞘与激素合成的生命必需品。饮食中未被消耗的剩余热量均会转化为脂肪储存于脂肪组织中。膳食脂肪划分为三大形态： • 饱和脂肪（Saturated fat）：广泛存在于红肉、牛油、猪油、黄油、全脂奶酪及棕榈油中，过量摄入会导致低密度脂蛋白（LDL 坏胆固醇）升高。 • 反式脂肪（Trans fat）：充斥于人造植物奶油、起酥油、商业烘焙糕点、甜甜圈及深层油炸食品中。系植物液态油经工业氢化反应硬化而成，用以延长货架期与增加松脆感；反式脂肪是最具心血管毒性的危险脂肪，不仅大幅飙升坏胆固醇，更强力压低保护性的好胆固醇（HDL），已被加拿大卫生部全面明令禁止工业添加。 • 不饱和脂肪（Unsaturated fat）：大量富集于深海鲑鱼、鲭鱼、核桃、牛油果以及双低菜籽油、特级初榨橄榄油中；有助于调节血脂、抗炎并强力护卫心脑血管健康。

Vitamins, Minerals, and Water

4. 维生素、矿物质与生命之水

Vitamins: Needed daily in minute amounts for biochemical enzyme functions and tissue vitality, but they provide zero calories. Vitamins A, D, E, and K are fat-soluble vitamins; excess doses are stored in liver and fatty tissues and can accumulate to toxic levels. Vitamins C and B-complex are water-soluble vitamins; excesses are excreted harmlessly in urine and require daily replenishment. Minerals: Inorganic chemical elements (e.g., calcium, iron, potassium, sodium) required for fluid electrolyte balance, muscle contractions, and nerve conduction. Water: The single most vital nutrient for human survival. Death ensues in mere days without water.
维生素（Vitamins）：参与全身数百种生化酶促反应与细胞代谢，虽不直接提供热量，但机体每日必不可少。维生素 A、D、E、K 属于脂溶性维生素，随脂肪吸收，过量摄入会在肝脏和脂肪组织中蓄积引发蓄积性中毒；维生素 C 与 B 族属于水溶性维生素，随尿液排出，必须每日通过新鲜果蔬与粗粮持续补充。矿物质（Minerals）：维持体液渗透压、电解质平衡、神经肌肉兴奋性传导（如钙、磷、铁、钠、钾）所必需的无机元素。水（Water）：人类生存最无可替代的头号关键营养素，人体若断水数日即可导致器官衰竭与死亡。

Plant-based proteins contain beneficial unsaturated fats and fibre without cholesterol. Lean meats, poultry, eggs, fish, and lower-fat dairy products also provide protein. • Make water your drink of choice: Water hydrates the body without adding sugars, sodium, or saturated fats found in soft drinks, juice cocktails, and sweetened coffees.

Healthy Eating Habits: Beyond Just the Food on Your Plate

健康饮食行为习惯：超越餐盘食物本身的饮食哲学

Canada's Dietary Guidelines recognize that healthy eating is not just about what you eat—it is also about how you eat. The guidelines outline four essential behavioural habits and three critical food choices for everyday life:
加拿大官方膳食指南深刻认识到，真正的健康饮食绝不仅仅关乎你吃入肚中的食物成分，更关乎你的饮食态度、环境与行为习惯。指南精炼概括了四大健康生活习惯与三大食材抉择原则：
Seven Foundational Pillars of Healthy Eating (Canada's Food Guide) · 加拿大膳食指南：健康饮食的七大基石性行动指引
[Behavioural Habits / 良好进食行为习惯]
1. Be mindful of your eating habits: Take time to eat, savour flavours, and listen to internal hunger and fullness cues.
2. Cook more often: Cooking meals at home allows control over fresh ingredients, reduces reliance on processed foods, and builds lifelong culinary skills.
3. Enjoy your food: Culture, food traditions, and personal taste are essential parts of eating well.
4. Eat meals with others: Sharing meals strengthens social relationships, fosters communication, and promotes healthier eating across generations.

[Food Choice Recommendations / 科学食材抉择准则]
5. Use food labels: Read Nutrition Facts tables and ingredient lists to compare and select foods lower in sodium, sugars, and saturated fat.
6. Limit highly processed foods: Restrict consumption of sugary drinks, fast food, hot dogs, processed cold cuts, chips, and commercially baked pastries.
7. Be aware of food marketing: Recognize that commercial advertising on television and social media frequently promotes foods high in sugar, fat, and sodium.
【四大良好进食行为习惯】
1. 专注细品你的饮食习惯（Be mindful）：放慢节奏从容进餐，悉心品味食材本真风味，敏锐感知身体内部真实的饥饿与饱腹信号，拒绝狼吞虎咽或边看手机边机械进食。
2. 经常在家亲自下厨（Cook more often）：亲手料理膳食能完全掌控新鲜天然食材的品质，大幅削减对外卖加工食品的依赖，并促进家庭健康饮食技能的世代传承。
3. 尽情享受美食的乐趣（Enjoy your food）：将民族文化、家族饮食传统与个人口味偏好完美融入健康饮食，享受烹饪与品尝带来的情感滋养。
4. 与家人及同伴围桌共餐（Eat meals with others）：共同进餐能增进人际亲密关系，提供温馨的语言交流契机，研究证实能显著提升老年人及儿童的营养达标率。

【三大科学食材抉择准则】
5. 熟练看懂食品营养标签（Use food labels）：主动查阅商品外包装上的营养成分表与配料清单，明智挑选纳盐、精制糖及饱和脂肪含量极低的优质产品。
6. 坚决限制超加工食品（Limit highly processed foods）：严格节制高糖汽水、快餐汉堡、热狗香肠、重盐腌腊熟食、油炸薯片及商业高糖糕点。
7. 警惕商业食品营销陷阱（Be aware of food marketing）：保持清醒头脑，识别电视与社交媒体广告针对高糖、高脂、高盐不健康零食所展开的营销包装诱惑。

BOX 27.1 Canada’s Dietary Guidelines: Core Scientific Principles · 专栏 27.1 加拿大卫生部《膳食指南》三大核心科学规约
[Guideline 1: Foundational Nutritious Foods]
Nutritious foods are the foundation for healthy eating. Vegetables, fruit, whole grains, and protein foods should be consumed regularly. Among protein foods, consume plant-based proteins more often. Foods that contain mostly unsaturated fat should replace foods that contain mostly saturated fat.

[Guideline 2: Processed Foods and Harmful Nutrients]
Processed or prepared foods and beverages that contribute to excess sodium, free sugars, or saturated fat undermine healthy eating and should not be consumed regularly. Chronic excess intake of sodium causes hypertension and stroke; excess free sugars cause dental caries and obesity; saturated fat increases atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.

[Guideline 3: Food Skills and Supportive Food Environments]
Food skills—such as menu planning, understanding nutrition labels, preparing ingredients safely, and cooking—are vital for healthy eating. Environmental contexts (supportive care facilities, school nutrition programs, community gardens) must empower individuals to make healthy food choices easily.
【指南原则 1：天然高营养密度食材是健康饮食的基石】
天然纯粹、富含营养的食材是健康生活的根基。新鲜蔬菜、水果、全谷物及优质蛋白质食物应当作为每日膳食的常规核心。在蛋白质食物的选择上，大力提倡优先增加植物性蛋白质的摄入频次；坚决以富含健康不饱和脂肪酸的食用油替代富含饱和脂肪的动物油与固体脂。

【指南原则 3：强化烹饪实操技能与构建支持性膳食生态】
熟练的膳食生活技能——如制定平衡食谱、看懂成分标签、食材卫生清洗与安全下厨——是践行健康饮食的核心能力。无论在养老照护机构、医院还是家庭社区，都必须营造支持性的健康饮食环境，让健康美味的天然食物变得唾手可得。

p. 600 全生命周期营养需求演变 (Nutritional Requirements Across the Life Cycle)

Nutritional Requirements Across the Life Cycle

全生命周期营养需求的生理演变规律

Nutrient and caloric requirements evolve dramatically throughout the human lifespan, influenced by growth rates, metabolic speed, hormonal shifts, and physical activity levels: • Infants (Birth to 1 year): Rapid growth phase. Breast milk is the ideal, exclusive food for the first 6 months, supplemented with Vitamin D. Iron-fortified solid foods are introduced around 6 months. High fat requirements support brain and myelin sheath development. • Toddlers & Preschoolers (Ages 1 to 5): Growth slows compared to infancy, but activity escalates. Appetite varies day to day. Smaller, nutrient-dense portions and healthy finger foods are essential. • School-Age Children & Adolescents (Ages 6 to 18): Pubertal growth spurts require increased calories, protein, calcium (for peak bone mass development), and iron (especially for menstruating females and expanding muscle mass in males). • Adults (Ages 19 to 50): Caloric needs stabilize based on occupational and physical activity. Nutrient density must remain high while moderating total energy intake to prevent adult-onset obesity. • Older Adults (Ages 51 and older): Metabolic rates decline, lean muscle mass decreases, and physical activity often diminishes, reducing overall caloric needs. However, the requirement for vitamins and minerals DOES NOT decrease; in fact, needs for protein, calcium, Vitamin D, and Vitamin B12 increase due to reduced intestinal absorption.
人体在不同生命周期的营养素与热量需求呈现显著的生理差异，受到生长速率、基础代谢率、内分泌激素以及日常体力活动强度的深刻调控： • 婴儿期（0至1岁）：人一生中生长发育最为迅猛的阶段。母乳是前6个月最完美的天然口粮，需每日口服补充400国际单位维生素D；约6个月时逐步引入富铁泥状辅食。婴儿期对健康脂肪的相对需求极高，以全力支持大脑神经元突触与髓鞘的高速发育。 • 幼儿与学龄前期（1至5岁）：生长速率较婴儿期平缓，但探索体力消耗大。食欲往往随情绪波动，需提供少食多餐、营养丰富且易于用手抓握的健康软食。 • 学龄期与青少年期（6至18岁）：青春期生长突增高峰期，对优质蛋白质、能量摄入要求骤增。尤需补充大量钙质以构建一生的“峰值骨量（Peak Bone Mass）”，女性因月经初潮、男性因肌肉爆发式扩容而对铁元素的需求显著提高。 • 成年期（19至50岁）：能量消耗取决于职业性质与运动强度。需严格控制高糖高脂热量以防范成年期进行性肥胖与代谢综合征。 • 老年期（51岁及以上）：基础代谢率随增龄逐步降低，骨骼肌自然萎缩退化，体力消耗减少，因而总卡路里热量需求相应减少。然而，长者对微量营养素的需求绝未降低，甚至因胃肠黏膜萎缩、胃酸分泌减少导致吸收率下降，对优质蛋白质、钙质、维生素 D 以及维生素 B12 的实际推荐供给量反而显著攀升！
p. 601 影响进食与营养的多维因素 (Factors Affecting Eating and Nutrition)

Factors Affecting Eating and Nutrition

影响案主进食与营养摄入的多维身心因素

A support worker must appreciate that eating is not merely a biological act of fuel consumption. Many complex, interrelated factors influence what, when, how, and why a client eats: • Personal Preferences: Lifelong habits, familiar tastes, and comfort foods. • Allergies and Food Intolerances: An allergy is a severe immune hypersensitivity (e.g., peanuts, shellfish) that can trigger anaphylaxis; a food intolerance (e.g., lactose intolerance) causes digestive discomfort (bloating, diarrhea, gas) without an immune response. • Culture and Religion: Dietary laws govern acceptable foods and preparation (e.g., Kosher dietary laws in Judaism, Halal rules in Islam, vegetarianism in Hinduism, fasting during Ramadan or Lent). • Finances: Poverty and fixed pensions force many clients to buy cheap, nutrient-poor starches while foregoing fresh vegetables, fruits, and lean meats. • Appetite and Emotional State: Loneliness, grief, depression, and chronic pain suppress appetite; anxiety may cause overeating or complete food refusal. • Illness and Medication Side Effects: Diseases alter taste buds; chemotherapies, antibiotics, and cardiac drugs frequently cause severe nausea, dry mouth (xerostomia), and metallic taste. • Aging Body Changes: Loss of teeth, poorly fitting dentures, reduced saliva production, diminished taste and smell senses, and decreased gastrointestinal motility directly hinder chewing, swallowing, and digestion.
支持工作者必须深刻领悟，进食绝非单纯摄入热量的生物学机械过程。案主吃什么、如何吃、何时吃，受到极其复杂且交织的多维因素制约： • 个人口味偏好（Personal preferences）：长年累月形成的饮食传统、童年记忆与抚慰性食物（Comfort foods）。 • 食物过敏与食物不耐受：过敏（Allergy）是免疫系统对致敏原（如花生、坚果、甲壳类水产）产生的急性变态反应，严重时数分钟内导致过敏性休克窒息；而食物不耐受（Food intolerance，如乳糖不耐受）则是因体内缺乏特定消化酶（乳糖酶）导致的消化不良（腹胀、腹泻、肠绞痛），不涉及免疫抗体反应。 • 文化传统与宗教规约：各大宗教与民族拥有严明的饮食禁忌（如犹太教洁食 Kosher 规范、伊斯兰教清真 Halal 规约与斋月禁食、印度教戒牛肉与素食传统、天主教四旬期守斋）。 • 经济拮据与贫困陷阱：微薄的养老金迫使许多孤寡长者只能购买廉价的面包挂面充饥，被迫割舍昂贵的新鲜蔬果与优质鱼肉。 • 情绪心理与食欲：孤独独居、丧偶哀伤、抑郁症及顽固慢性疼痛会严重抑制下丘脑食欲中枢；焦虑紧张可能导致暴饮暴食或彻底厌食拒餐。 • 疾病与药物毒副作用：严重心力衰竭、肝肾功能衰竭会直接改变味觉；抗生素、化疗药及心脏药物常引发剧烈恶心、顽固性口干（Xerostomia）以及口中持久不散的铁锈金属异味。 • 老年期生理退行性衰变：牙齿脱落松动、全口假牙不贴合、唾液腺萎缩口干、舌乳头味蕾与嗅觉细胞大幅减少退化，以及胃肠道蠕动迟缓，直接造成咀嚼费力、吞咽困难与习惯性便秘。
p. 602 食品标签与营养成分表 (Food Labels & Nutrition Facts)
• Finances. People with limited incomes often buy cheaper foods, so their diets may lack proteins and certain vitamins and minerals.
• 经济状况（Finances）：经济收入有限的人往往会购买更便宜的食物，因此他们的膳食中可能缺乏蛋白质以及某些维生素和矿物质。
• Appetite. Appetite is the desire for food. When hungry, a person seeks food and eats until the appetite is satisfied. Aromas and thoughts of food can also stimulate appetite. However, illness, medications, anxiety, pain, and depression, as well as unpleasant sights, thoughts, and smells, can cause lack of appetite.
• 食欲（Appetite）：食欲是对食物的渴望与欲望。饥饿时，人会寻找食物并进食直至食欲得到满足。食物的香气和联想也能刺激食欲。然而，疾病、药物、焦虑、疼痛、抑郁，以及令人不适的景象、想法和气味，都会导致食欲缺乏。
• Illness. Appetite usually decreases during illness and during recovery from injuries, even though nutritional needs increase. The body must fight infection, heal tissues, and replace blood cells and nutrients lost through vomiting and diarrhea. Some diseases and medications can cause a sore mouth, which makes eating painful. Loss of teeth affects chewing, especially protein foods. Illness makes preparing and serving meals difficult. Long-term care residents need good nutrition to correct or prevent health problems; however, poor nutrition is still common among this population.
• 疾病（Illness）：尽管患病和伤后康复期间机体的营养需求实际上有所增加，但食欲通常会减退。身体必须抵抗感染、修复受损组织，并补充因呕吐和腹泻丢失的血细胞和营养素。某些疾病和药物可能导致口腔疼痛，使进食变得痛苦。牙齿脱落会影响咀嚼，尤其是富含蛋白质的食物。疾病还使得准备和端送餐食变得困难。长期照护机构的住客需要充足的营养来纠正或预防健康问题；然而，营养不良在该人群中仍然非常普遍。
• Aging. Factors that can affect the appetite of older persons are loss of teeth; poor-fitting dentures; vision, hearing, or perceptual impairments; fatigue; and loss of control over their choices for meals (see the Focus on Long-Term Care: Food and Quality of Life box).
• 衰老（Aging）：影响老年人食欲的因素包括牙齿脱落、假牙不合身、视力/听力或知觉障碍、疲倦乏力，以及对餐食选择失去自主控制权（参见“聚焦长期照护：饮食与生活质量”专栏）。

FOOD LABELS

食品标签
Food labels are useful in planning a healthy diet and following special diets ordered by physicians, dietitians, or nurses. Nutrition labels are now mandatory on packaged food. Food labels have three components: a list of ingredients, nutrition facts, and nutrition claims.
食品标签在规划健康膳食以及执行医生、营养师或护士开具的特殊饮食医嘱时非常有用。目前，包装食品上的营养标签是强制性标示的。食品标签主要包含三个组成部分：配料表、营养成分表和营养声称。

List of Ingredients

配料表 (List of Ingredients)
Ingredient lists usually start with the major ingredient and list other ingredients in descending order of quantity in the product. Use the list to compare two or more products. For example, Brand 1 may list wheat flour as the first ingredient, whereas Brand 2 may list bran first. Use the lists on food products also to check for ingredients that a client may be allergic to or have an intolerance to (see page 601).

Food Safety

食品安全与食源性疾病 (Food Safety)
Food safety is an important consideration for all people because foodborne illnesses can cause serious illness or death. A foodborne illness is an illness caused by improperly cooked or stored food. Diarrhea, nausea, and vomiting are common signs and symptoms of a foodborne illness. Some people are at particularly high risk for severe symptoms—for example, infants, children, older persons, people with chronic illnesses, and people with weakened immune systems.
食品安全关乎每个人的健康，因为食源性疾病可导致严重疾病甚至死亡。食源性疾病（Foodborne illness）是指因烹饪不当或储存不当的受污染食物引起的疾病。腹泻、恶心和呕吐是食源性疾病的常见体征和症状。部分人群发生重症的风险极高——例如婴儿、儿童、老年人、慢性病患者以及免疫系统功能低下者。
Pathogens are disease-causing microbes (see Chapter 18), which are commonly called germs. When pathogens are present in or on food, the food is said to be contaminated. Many foods naturally have pathogens in them in their raw state—for example, meat, fish, poultry, and eggs. When food is cooked properly, however, most pathogens are killed. However, raw food may spread pathogens to other, ready-to-eat foods—referred to as cross-contamination. For example, when fluids from raw chicken meat drip onto vegetables in the refrigerator, the vegetables become contaminated. If they are eaten before they are properly washed, they could cause illness.
病原体（Pathogens）是致病微生物（参见第18章），通常俗称为病菌。当食物内部或表面存在病原体时，该食物就被称为被污染。许多生鲜食材天然携带病原体——例如生肉、鱼类、禽肉和蛋类。然而，通过彻底烹饪，绝大多数病原体都会被杀灭。但是，生鲜食物可能会将病原体传播到其他即食食品上——这被称为交叉污染（Cross-contamination）。例如，生鸡肉的汁水滴到冰箱里的蔬菜上，蔬菜就会受到污染。如果在未彻底清洗的情况下食用，就可能引发疾病。
p. 606 食品温控、特殊饮食与限钠饮食 (Food Temps, Special Diets & Sodium Restriction)
It is usually hard to tell by sight, smell, or taste if something is contaminated. Safe food-handling practices and effective cooking can prevent cross-contamination and foodborne illnesses. Most pathogens thrive at room temperature, but they die at temperatures below 4°C (40°F) and above 60°C (140°F). Therefore, it is important to cook foods well and preserve them in the refrigerator. Never leave foods sitting out at room temperature for more than a few minutes.
通常很难通过视觉、嗅觉或味觉直接判断食物是否受到污染。规范的安全食品操作流程与充分烹调可以有效预防交叉污染与食源性疾病。大多数病原体在室温下迅速繁殖，但在4°C（40°F）以下或60°C（140°F）以上会被抑制或杀灭。因此，彻底烧熟食物并及时冷藏保存至关重要。切勿将熟食置于室温下超过数分钟。
If you prepare and serve meals to clients, you must know safe food-handling practices. Food safety training is a very educational course for all support workers to take and is available in most cities and towns. Observe safety practices when grocery shopping and when storing, cooking, reheating, and serving food (BOX 27.2).
若你需要为服务对象准备和端送餐食，必须熟练掌握安全食品操作规范。食品安全培训是对所有支持人员极具教育意义的实用课程，几乎在各大城镇均有开设。在食品采购、储存、烹调、重新加热和端送过程中，都必须严格遵守安全规程（参见框 27.2）。

SPECIAL DIETS

特殊饮食医嘱 (SPECIAL DIETS)
Physicians may order special diets for clients because of a nutritional deficiency or a disease, for the purpose of eliminating or decreasing certain substances in the diet, or for weight control (see TABLE 27.4 on page 608). Special diets are common before and after surgery and for people with diabetes or an intolerance to gluten (page 610). People with diseases of the heart, kidneys, gallbladder, liver, stomach, or intestines may receive special diets. Allergies, food intolerances, obesity, and other disorders also require special diets.
由于营养缺乏、特定疾病、排除或减少某些膳食成分，或为了控制体重，医生可能会为服务对象开具特殊饮食医嘱（参见第608页表 27.4）。特殊饮食在手术前后、糖尿病患者以及对麸质不耐受的人群中非常普遍（第610页）。患有心脏、肾脏、胆囊、肝脏、胃或肠道疾病的人群也可能需要特殊饮食。过敏、食物不耐受、肥胖及其他病症同样需要针对性的饮食安排。
When a special diet is ordered, a nurse and dietitian work together to create a plan for meeting the client’s nutritional needs. The plan takes into consideration the client’s preferences, culture, religion, and food allergies and intolerances, as well as any eating problems such as dysphagia (see page 610).
开具特殊饮食医嘱后，护士与营养师会协同制定满足服务对象营养需求的个性化方案。该方案会综合权衡服务对象的饮食喜好、文化背景、宗教信仰、食物过敏与不耐受情况，以及是否存在吞咽障碍等进食问题（参见第610页）。
In facilities, the terms regular diet, general diet, and house diet mean that there are no dietary limits or restrictions. Three common special diets are the sodium-controlled diet, diabetes meal planning, and the gluten-free diet.
在医疗照护机构中，常规饮食（Regular diet）、普食（General diet）和全食（House diet）指的是没有任何饮食限制或特殊禁忌的普通膳食。三种最常见的特殊饮食包括：限钠饮食、糖尿病配餐计划以及无麸质饮食。

The Sodium-Controlled Diet

限钠饮食 (The Sodium-Controlled Diet)
The average amount of sodium found in the daily diet of a person is 3 000 to 5 000 mg, twice the daily amount required by the body. Healthy people excrete the excess sodium in the urine. Heart and kidney diseases, as well as some drugs and some complications of pregnancy, cause the body to retain the extra sodium.
普通人日常饮食中平均钠摄入量为3,000至5,000毫克，是人体生理需求量的两倍以上。健康人通过尿液排出多余的钠。但心脏病、肾脏疾病、某些药物以及妊娠并发症会导致体内钠滞留。
Sodium causes the body to retain water. If there is too much sodium, tissues can swell with water, and excess fluid can build in the blood vessels, making the heart work harder. The extra workload for the heart can cause serious complications and even death. Restricting sodium in the diet helps the body retain less water, and less water in the tissues and blood vessels reduces the amount of work for the heart.
钠会导致体内水分潴留。体内钠含量过高时，组织会发生水肿，血管内容量负荷增加，迫使心脏承担更重的泵血负荷。心脏负荷过重会诱发严重并发症甚至危及生命。限制饮食中的钠有助于减少体液潴留，减轻组织水肿和血管负荷，从而显著降低心脏的工作负担。
The physician may order sodium control (restriction) for the client. Many low-salt or salt-free foods are available on the market; use food labels to determine salt content.
医生可能会为服务对象开具控钠（限钠）饮食医嘱。市面上有许多低盐或无盐食品，可通过食品标签查看确切的含盐量。
• 2 000- to 3 000-mg sodium diet—called the low-salt diet or no-added-salt diet. Sodium restriction is mild, but all high-sodium foods are omitted. A minimum amount of salt is used for cooking, and salt is not added to foods at the table.
• 2,000至3,000毫克限钠饮食：称为低盐饮食（Low-salt diet）或无额外加盐饮食（No-added-salt diet）。限钠程度较轻，但必须排除所有高钠食品。烹饪时仅允许使用极少量的盐，餐桌上严禁额外添加食盐。
• 1 000-mg sodium diet—sodium restriction is moderate. Food is cooked without salt, and foods high in sodium are omitted. Canned vegetables that are high in sodium due to canning are restricted in amount. Salt-free products such as salt-free bread are used. Diet planning is necessary.
• 1,000毫克限钠饮食：属于中度限钠。烹调时不加盐，严格禁食高钠食物。因加工而含高钠的蔬菜罐头必须严格限量。食用无盐面包等专用无盐食品。必须制定周密的配餐计划。
• 500-mg sodium diet—sodium restriction is severe. Restrictions for the mild and moderate sodium diets are followed. In addition, vegetables high in sodium are omitted. Other restrictions are as follows: milk—1 cup per day; egg—1 per day; and meat—120 g (4 oz) per day. Diet planning is essential.
• 500毫克限钠饮食：属于极严格限钠。除遵循轻度和中度限钠的所有规则外，还必须彻底剔除天然高钠蔬菜。其他明确限制包括：每日牛奶最多1杯；鸡蛋每日限1个；肉类每日限120克（4盎司）。专业的膳食计划在此必不可少。

Diabetic Diet Meal Planning

糖尿病配餐计划 (Diabetic Diet Meal Planning)
Diabetic diet meal planning is done for clients with diabetes. Type 1 diabetes is caused by the lack of insulin. Type 2 diabetes is caused by insulin resistance (see Chapter 33). In healthy people, the pancreas produces and secretes insulin, which enables the body to use the sugar consumed. In people who lack insulin, sugar builds up in the bloodstream rather than being used by cells for energy. Diabetes is usually treated with insulin or medication, diet, and exercise.
糖尿病配餐计划专为患有糖尿病的服务对象制定。1型糖尿病是由胰岛素绝对缺乏引起的；2型糖尿病是由胰岛素抵抗引起的（参见第33章）。健康人体内，胰腺合成并分泌胰岛素，使机体能够利用摄入的糖分。在胰岛素缺乏或利用障碍的人体内，葡萄糖在血液中积聚，无法被细胞摄取作为能量。糖尿病通常采用胰岛素或降糖药物、饮食控制和运动疗法综合治疗。
The dietitian and the client develop a meal plan together. Consistency in following it is key. The plan involves the following: The client’s food preferences. It may be necessary to limit amounts or change the way food is prepared.
营养师与服务对象共同制定配餐计划。持之以恒地执行是关键。该计划涵盖以下核心要素：尊重服务对象的饮食喜好，但可能需要限制摄入分量或改变食物的烹饪方式。

Shopping:
• Packaging should be secure. Do not buy packages that are ripped or have broken seals, and do not buy dented cans.
• Check the “best before” date. Do not buy expired items.
• Select meat, poultry, and fish last. Place them in separate plastic bags to avoid dripping on other foods in the cart.
• Keep raw meat, poultry, fish, and eggs separate from fresh produce and ready-to-eat foods in your shopping cart and bags.
食材采购：
• 包装必须完好严密。切勿购买包装撕裂、封条破损的商品，切勿购买有凹陷的罐头。
• 查验“最佳食用期/保质期”。切勿购买过期物品。
• 最后再选购肉类、禽肉和鱼类。将其装入单独塑料袋，防止血水汁液滴落到手推车中的其他食物上。
• 在购物手推车和购物袋中，确保生肉、禽肉、鱼类和蛋类与新鲜果蔬及即食食品彻底分开放置。
Storing:
• Refrigerate or freeze meat, poultry, and fish immediately after shopping (within 2 hours, or within 1 hour if the weather is warm).
• Set refrigerator temperature to 4°C (40°F) or lower, and freezer to -18°C (0°F) or lower.
• Store raw meat, poultry, and fish in clean, sealed containers or bags on the bottom shelf of the refrigerator so juices cannot drip onto other foods.
• Consume leftovers within 2 to 3 days, or freeze them.
食物储存：
• 购物后立即将肉类、禽类和鱼类冷藏或冷冻（2小时内；若天气炎热须在1小时内完成）。
• 冰箱冷藏室温度设定在4°C（40°F）或以下，冷冻室设定在-18°C（0°F）或以下。
• 将生肉、禽肉和鱼类存放在干净密封的容器或保鲜袋中，并置于冰箱最底层，严防汁水滴落在其他食物上。
• 剩饭剩菜应在2至3天内食用完毕，或置于冷冻室保存。
Preparing:
• Wash hands thoroughly with warm water and soap for at least 20 seconds before and after handling food, especially raw meat.
• Sanitize cutting boards, utensils, and countertops with a mild bleach solution (5 mL [1 tsp] unscented bleach in 750 mL [3 cups] water) or sanitizing spray.
• Use one cutting board for fresh produce and bread, and a separate board for raw meat, poultry, and seafood.
• Wash all fresh fruits and vegetables thoroughly under cool running drinking water before peeling, cutting, or eating.
• Thaw foods in the refrigerator, in cold water (changing water every 30 minutes), or in the microwave—never at room temperature on the counter.
备料加工：
• 处理食物前后（尤其是处理生肉后），务必用温水和肥皂彻底洗手至少20秒。
• 使用稀释漂白水（750毫升水中加5毫升无香漂白剂）或消毒喷雾对砧板、刀具和台面进行消毒。
• 准备新鲜果蔬和面包使用一块专用砧板，生肉、禽肉和海鲜使用另一块独立砧板，严格生熟分开。
• 所有新鲜果蔬在削皮、切块或食用前，必须在流动的饮用凉水下彻底冲洗干净。
• 解冻食品应在冰箱冷藏室、冷水中（每30分钟换一次水）或微波炉中进行——严禁在厨房台面上室温解冻。
Cooking & Reheating:
• Cook foods to safe internal temperatures. Use a digital food thermometer:
- Ground meat: 71°C (160°F)
- Whole poultry: 82°C (180°F); poultry pieces: 74°C (165°F)
- Pork, beef, veal, lamb: 71°C (160°F)
- Fish: 70°C (158°F)
• Reheat leftovers thoroughly to at least 74°C (165°F). Soups, gravies, and sauces should be brought to a rolling boil.
烹调与重热：
• 将食物烹煮至安全内部核心温度，使用数字食品温度计测定：
- 碎肉馅类：71°C（160°F）
- 整禽：82°C（180°F）；禽肉分割块：74°C（165°F）
- 猪肉、牛肉、小牛肉、羊肉：71°C（160°F）
- 鱼类：70°C（158°F）
• 剩菜重新加热时中心温度必须达到至少74°C（165°F）。汤类、肉汁和酱汁应重新煮沸至彻底沸腾翻滚。
Serving:
• Keep hot foods hot (at or above 60°C [140°F]) and cold foods cold (at or below 4°C [40°F]).
• Never leave cooked food at room temperature for more than 2 hours (1 hour in hot weather).
• Use clean dishes and utensils to serve food. Never place cooked food on an unwashed plate that previously held raw meat or poultry.
端餐服务：
• 热食保温（保持在60°C [140°F] 或以上），冷食保冷（保持在4°C [40°F] 或以下）。
• 熟食置于室温下的时间切勿超过2小时（炎热天气下不超过1小时）。
• 使用干净餐盘和餐具端送食物。绝不能将熟食盛放在此前盛装过生生肉或生禽肉且未清洗的盘子里。

The Gluten-Free Diet

无麸质饮食 (The Gluten-Free Diet)
Celiac disease is an autoimmune disorder in which the immune system attacks and damages the small intestine when gluten is consumed. Gluten is a protein found in wheat, barley, rye, and triticale. Consuming gluten causes inflammation and damages the villi (tiny hair-like structures in the small intestine that absorb nutrients), preventing the body from absorbing essential nutrients (malabsorption).
乳糜泻（Celiac disease）是一种自身免疫性疾病，患者摄入麸质时，机体免疫系统会攻击并损害小肠黏膜。麸质（Gluten）是小麦、大麦、黑麦和小黑麦中存在的一种植物蛋白。摄入麸质会导致小肠炎症反应并破坏小肠绒毛（吸收营养素的微细毛状结构），从而阻碍机体吸收必需营养素（吸收不良）。
A gluten-free diet is the only effective treatment for celiac disease. All foods containing gluten must be strictly avoided. Gluten is found in breads, pasta, cereals, baked goods, beers, and many processed foods (e.g., gravies, salad dressings, and soy sauce containing wheat). Gluten-free grains include rice, corn, quinoa, millet, and certified gluten-free oats. Carefully read labels: look for certified “Gluten-Free” logos and check ingredient lists for hidden wheat, rye, or barley derivatives.
无麸质饮食是乳糜泻唯一确切有效的终身治疗手段。必须严格禁止食用一切含有麸质的食品。面包、面条、谷物早餐、烘焙糕点、啤酒以及许多加工食品（如肉汁、沙拉酱及含小麦的酱油）中均含有麸质。无麸质天然替代谷物包括大米、玉米、藜麦、小米和认证无麸质燕麦。仔细阅读包装标签：寻找权威认证的“Gluten-Free”标识，并审查配料表是否存在隐藏的小麦、黑麦或大麦衍生配料。

Other Special Diets

其他特殊饮食 (Other Special Diets)
Food allergies and food intolerances also require special dietary management. A food allergy is an abnormal immune response to a specific food protein (e.g., peanuts, shellfish, tree nuts, milk, eggs, soy). In severe cases, an allergic reaction can trigger anaphylaxis, a life-threatening medical emergency. A food intolerance (e.g., lactose intolerance) is an adverse physiological reaction that does not involve the immune system but causes digestive distress (such as gas, bloating, and diarrhea).
食物过敏与食物不耐受同样需要严格的特殊饮食管理。食物过敏（Food allergy）是对特定食物蛋白（如花生、贝类、坚果、牛奶、鸡蛋、大豆）产生的异常免疫反应。在严重情况下，过敏反应可引发全身性过敏性休克（Anaphylaxis），这是一种危及生命的医疗急症。食物不耐受（Food intolerance，如乳糖不耐受）则是指非免疫机制引起的生理不良反应，通常导致消化系统不适（如肠胀气、腹痛和腹泻）。

ASSISTING CLIENTS WITH EATING

协助服务对象进餐 (ASSISTING CLIENTS WITH EATING)
Eating is an essential physiological need and a social experience. Factors such as weakness, tremors, visual impairment, or cognitive loss can interfere with eating. When assisting clients, always promote dignity, comfort, and independence while maintaining vigilance for safety risks like aspiration and choking.
进餐不仅是人体维持生命的必需生理需求，也是一种重要的社交体验。虚弱无力、手部震颤、视力减退或认知功能受损都会阻碍自主进食。在协助服务对象时，应在确保生命安全（防范误吸和窒息）的前提下，最大程度维护其尊严、舒适感与独立自主性。

Feeding Clients With Swallowing Challenges

吞咽困难服务对象的进餐照护规范
• Offer plenty of fluids according to care plan orders.
• 严格遵照护理计划医嘱提供适量液体。
• Offer small mouthfuls. For clients who have swallowing challenges with eating, it is generally recommended that you give them one third of a teaspoon at a time. However, being fed such a small amount would involve many spoonfuls, which could tire the client. Follow the care plan regarding the amount you should feed at a time.
• 每次仅给予少量一小口食物。对于有吞咽困难的服务对象，通常建议每次喂食量控制在三分之一茶匙左右。然而，每次喂食量过小意味着需要频繁舀送，可能会让服务对象感到疲惫。请严格依照照护计划中的具体规定把控每次喂食量。
• Give the client time to chew and swallow before offering the next bite. Observe their throat to confirm swallowing has occurred.
• 在喂下一口之前，给予服务对象充裕的时间咀嚼并吞咽。观察其喉部喉结活动，确认已完全完成吞咽动作。

Swallowing Difficulty (Dysphagia)

吞咽困难 (Dysphagia)
Clients can have difficulty (dys) swallowing (phagia) (see Chapters 32 and 43) for many reasons. Certain conditions, such as stroke, Parkinson’s disease, multiple sclerosis, Alzheimer’s disease, head injury, and throat cancer, can impair the nervous system or muscles involved in swallowing.
服务对象可能由于多种病因导致吞咽困难（Dysphagia：dys表示困难，phagia表示吞咽；参见第32章和第43章）。脑卒中、帕金森病、多发性硬化症、阿尔茨海默病、颅脑创伤及咽喉部肿瘤等病症，都会损害参与吞咽生理反射的神经系统或咽喉肌肉群。
Clients with swallowing difficulties are at risk for choking and aspiration. Aspiration is the inhalation of fluid or an object into the lungs. If a client is unable to talk or cough, they may be choking, and you should call for help immediately. If no help is available, follow the emergency measures for choking (foreign-body airway obstruction) that you have been taught in your first-aid and CPR (cardiopulmonary resuscitation) course.
吞咽困难的服务对象面临窒息和误吸的严重风险。误吸（Aspiration）是指液体、食物或异物被意外吸入气管和肺部。如果服务对象突然无法说话、不能咳嗽或面色发绀，可能处于急性气道梗阻状态，必须立即高声呼救。若现场无其他人员协助，必须立即按照急救与心肺复苏（CPR）培训中所学的成人气道异物梗阻急救法（如海姆立克急救法）进行紧急施救。
Dietary textures for clients with dysphagia are ordered by a speech-language pathologist (SLP) or physician: thickened liquids (nectar-thick, honey-thick, or pudding-thick consistency) flow more slowly and are easier to control in the mouth, reducing aspiration risk. Pureed or minced foods eliminate the need for extensive chewing.
吞咽困难服务对象的食物质地与稠度由言语病理学家（SLP）或主管医生严格开具医嘱：增稠液体（花蜜稠度、蜂蜜稠度或布丁稠度）流动速度慢，在口腔内更容易被舌肌包裹控制，从而显著降低误吸风险。糊状泥餐（Pureed）或细碎餐（Minced）则免除了剧烈咀嚼的生理负担。

Weakness

虚弱无力者的进餐照护 (Weakness)
Some clients are too weak to chew and swallow their food, and when they do not eat well, they become even weaker and have even less energy to eat. Never force a client to eat. If the client cannot eat, tell your supervisor. To encourage a client to eat, offer frequent, small, high-calorie meals, and serve nutritious drinks, including liquid dietary supplements. Soft foods that do not need much chewing are preferred. Follow the care plan, and do the following:
部分服务对象极度虚弱，缺乏咀嚼和吞咽的体力；而进食不足又会导致机体更加衰弱，形成恶性循环。切勿强迫服务对象进食。如果服务对象无法进食，必须立即报告主管护士。为了鼓励进餐，可提供少食多餐的高热量膳食，并供给包括液体全营养补充剂在内的营养饮料。优先选择无需过度咀嚼的软质食物。遵循照护计划，并执行以下要点：
• Allow the client to rest before and after meals.
• 安排服务对象在餐前及餐后充分休息储备体力。
• Use the appropriate feeding utensils. (For example, the Ministry of Health and Long-Term Care [MOHLTC] in Ontario requires that clients be fed using a teaspoon.) Check with your supervisor and the client’s care plan.
• 使用适宜的辅助进餐工具。（例如，安大略省卫生及长期照护厅 [MOHLTC] 明确规定必须使用茶匙进行人工喂餐）。请向主管护士确认并核对照护计划要求。
• Provide a straw to drink (if allowed) so the client does not need to lift the glass. Check with your supervisor and the client’s care plan.
• 在医嘱允许的情况下提供吸管饮水，使服务对象无需费力举起水杯。必须提前核对主管医护指示与照护计划（注：吞咽障碍严重者禁用普通吸管以防呛咳）。
• Provide cups, glasses, and utensils that are light and easy to handle.
• 提供质地轻便、便于稳定握持的水杯、玻璃杯及进用餐具。

视力障碍者的时钟定位进餐法 (Vision Impairment)

If a client wishes to eat in their room, a team conference to discuss the safety issues and risks of eating alone, especially if the client has dysphagia, must be scheduled and must involve the family, client, and staff. A staff member has to be with the client to assist and monitor safety during meals in the room.
若服务对象坚持要在个人房间内进餐，必须召开多学科团队会议，深入研讨独自进餐的安全隐患与风险（尤其是存在吞咽困难时），会议须有家属、服务对象本人及医护团队共同参与。在房间内就餐时，必须有照护工作人员全程在场协助并密切监护其安全。

Between-Meal Nourishments

两餐间营养点心 (Between-Meal Nourishments)
Some special diets involve between-meal nourishments, such as crackers, milk, juice, a milkshake, cake, wafers, a sandwich, gelatin, and custard. Provide nourishments according to the care plan. Prepackaged nutritional liquid food supplements such as Ensure and Boost are meant only to be supplements and not meal replacements for an extended period, as they do not provide adequate nutritional balance. In home care settings, you may have to prepare the between-meal nourishments. In facilities, they are supplied to the unit. Provide eating utensils, a straw, and a napkin to the client. Follow the procedures for serving meals and feeding clients.
某些特殊饮食包含两餐间加餐，如苏打饼干、牛奶、果汁、奶昔、蛋糕、威化饼、三明治、明胶果冻和蛋奶冻等。根据照护计划提供加餐。像 Ensure（安素）和 Boost 这类预包装液体全营养补充剂仅用作膳食补充，不能长期完全替代正餐，因为它们无法提供全面的膳食平衡。在居家照护中，你可能需要亲手准备两餐间点心；在机构中，加餐由营养科统一配送。为服务对象备好餐具、吸管和餐巾，遵循端餐和协助喂食规范。

Calorie Counts

卡路里摄入计数 (Calorie Counts)
For some clients, it is important to keep a record of calorie intake. A flow sheet is provided for this purpose, and you must note what the client ate and how much. For example, a client is served a chicken breast, a baked potato, green beans, a roll, pudding, and two pats of butter. You record on the form that the client finished all the chicken, half the potato, the roll, and one pat of butter but left the beans and the pudding. The care plans tell you which clients require calorie counts.
对于某些服务对象，详细记录卡路里摄入量至关重要。机构会为此提供专用流转记录表，你必须准确记录服务对象吃了什么以及吃了多少。例如，端送的餐食包括一块鸡胸肉、一个烤土豆、四季豆、一个小面包卷、一份布丁和两小块黄油。你记录在表单上：该住客吃完了全部鸡肉、半个土豆、整个面包卷和一块黄油，但四季豆和布丁未吃。照护计划会指明哪些住客需要进行热量计数。

FLUID BALANCE

体液平衡 (FLUID BALANCE)
Harm, even death, can result from too much or too little water in the body, so fluid balance is essential for health. The amount of fluids taken in (intake) and the amount lost (output) must be equal. If fluid intake exceeds fluid output, tissues swell with water—a condition called edema (see Chapter 33). Edema is common in clients with heart and kidney diseases. Dehydration is a decrease in the amount of water in body tissues, which occurs when fluid output exceeds intake. Common causes are low fluid intake, vomiting, diarrhea, bleeding, excess sweating, and increased urination.
体内水分过多或过少都会对机体造成严重伤害甚至危及生命，因此维持体液平衡（Fluid balance）对健康至关重要。摄入的体液量（Intake）与排出的体液量（Output）必须保持动态平衡。若摄入量超过排出量，水分在组织间隙积聚造成肿胀——称为水肿（Edema；参见第33章）。水肿常见于心脏病和肾脏疾病患者。脱水（Dehydration）则是指机体组织中水分减少，当体液排出量大于摄入量时发生。常见诱因包括摄水量不足、呕吐、腹泻、出血、大汗及多尿等。
p. 615 喂餐仁爱原则与进餐技巧 (Compassionate Feeding & Bite-Bite-Sip)
PROVIDING COMPASSIONATE CARE: Assisting a Client With Eating · 倾注仁爱关怀：协助服务对象进食的核心准则
• Dignity. Clients who cannot feed themselves may feel embarrassed, humiliated, or angry, and some clients can become depressed or resentful. Some of these clients may refuse to eat. Be gentle, patient, and encouraging, and ensure comfort. Sit facing the client, as it is more relaxing and shows that you have time for the client. Standing communicates that you are in a hurry, and clients should never be made to feel rushed. Sit at a slight angle from them in case they cough or expel food during the meal. Clean up dribbles and spills in a tactful way to save the client from embarrassment. Remember to speak to the client in a respectful manner. Identify the type of food that the client is eating with each spoonful, to improve their appetite. This is especially important if the client’s food texture has been ground or puréed. Refrain from mixing the foods together, so that the client can taste each food separately.
• 维护尊严（Dignity）：无法自主进食的服务对象常感到窘迫、屈辱或愤怒，有些人甚至产生抑郁或怨恨情绪，进而抗拒进餐。必须保持温和、耐心与鼓励，确保环境舒适。坐着面对服务对象，这样显得放松并表明你有充足的时间陪伴；站立则传达出匆忙仓促的信号，绝不能让服务对象产生被催促的压迫感。呈微小角度就座，以防对方在进餐中突发咳嗽或喷出食物。以委婉体贴的方式擦拭嘴角流淌或遗洒，消除其尴尬。始终以尊重的语气交流。喂每一勺食物时说明菜品名称，激发其食欲；对于肉糜或糊状泥餐尤为重要。切忌将各种菜肴搅拌混杂在一起，让服务对象能够分别品尝每种食物的独特风味。
• Independence. Encourage the client to participate in some part of the meal, and allow the client to set the pace. The client may be able to gesture or nod to show preferences.
• 促进自主独立（Independence）：鼓励服务对象参与进餐的某些环节（如手拿面包或自持水杯），由服务对象决定进餐节奏。他们可以通过手势或点头表达偏好。
• Individualized care. For care to be truly client-centred, it must be planned or adapted to fit the client’s food choices, physical and cognitive strengths and limitations while eating, personality, and care plan (see the Supporting Mrs. King: Respecting Religious Food Practices box). The meal should be paced according to the client’s ability to swallow to avoid choking.
• 个体化照护（Individualized care）：以服务对象为中心的照护必须契合其食物喜好、身心机能限制、个性特点及照护计划（参见“支持金夫人：尊重宗教饮食习俗”专栏）。根据其吞咽能力调节进餐节律，严防呛咳窒息。
• Preferences. If the client wants to pray before eating, provide time and privacy for it. Describe the food on the tray, and ask the client the order in which foods and fluids should be served. Ask questions and use paraphrasing (see Chapter 9) to make sure that you understand the client’s feelings and wishes. Engage the client in pleasant conversation.
• 尊重个人偏好（Preferences）：若服务对象希望餐前祈祷，留出时间并维护隐私。描述餐盘中的菜肴，询问希望先吃哪道菜、先喝哪种水。通过提问和意译转述确认理解其意愿。进行愉快轻松的交谈。
• Privacy. Some clients do not like others to see them being fed. Be sensitive to your client’s needs and feelings. Provide for privacy if desired by the client.
• 保障隐私（Privacy）：部分服务对象不希望被人目睹被他人喂食的过程。体恤其敏感自尊，若需要应提供私密空间。
• Safety. Follow the care plan. Use spoons, not forks, as they are less likely to cause injury. The spoon should be only one-third full (FIGURE 27.8) each time, as this portion is easy to chew and swallow. Some clients need even smaller portions. Sit in relation to the client so that you can immediately see signs of choking or any problems with eating, chewing, or swallowing. Give the client enough time to chew and swallow. Remember to offer fluids during the meal as they help with chewing and swallowing. A good rule of thumb is to provide something to drink after two spoonfuls of solid food (“bite, bite, & sip”). Some clients may tend to fall asleep during their meals, which may pose a swallowing risk. Attempt to stimulate the client by gently stroking the cheek and addressing the client by name. If the client does not awaken, do not attempt to feed the client. Report your observations and record this situation in the appropriate manner.
• 确保安全（Safety）：严格遵循照护计划。使用汤匙而非餐叉，汤匙不易划伤口腔。每次勺内食物仅装三分之一满（图 27.8），该分量极易咀嚼和吞咽；某些严重障碍者需要更小的分量。坐在能直视其面部表情的位置，以便第一时间捕捉窒息迹象或咀嚼吞咽异常。给予充分咀嚼吞咽时间。进餐中适时喂饮液体，有助湿润吞咽；实用的经验法则是每喂两勺固体食物提供一次饮水（“两口干食、一口水” bite, bite & sip 原则）。某些住客可能在进餐中打瞌睡，这极易引发误吸；可通过轻抚面颊并唤其姓名尝试唤醒。若无法唤醒，绝不能强行喂食！如实汇报并规范记录。

Special Fluid Orders

特殊体液摄入医嘱 (Special Fluid Orders)
• Encourage fluids. The client needs to drink more fluids. The order states the amount to ingest and provides a variety of allowed fluids. Fluids are kept within the client’s reach and are offered regularly to clients who cannot feed themselves. Intake records are kept.
• 鼓励饮水（Encourage fluids）：服务对象需要摄入更多体液。医嘱会明确每日建议饮用总量及允许饮用的饮品种类。将水杯置于伸手可及处；对于无法自理者定时主动喂水。严格记录出入液量。
p. 616 限水与禁食医嘱、出入量监测 (Fluid Restrictions, NPO & I&O Measuring)
• Restrict fluids. Fluids are restricted to a specific amount. They are offered in small amounts and in small containers. The water pitcher is removed or kept out of sight. The client needs frequent oral hygiene, as it helps keep the mucous membranes of the mouth moist. Intake records are kept.
• 限制饮水（Restrict fluids）：摄入的体液被严格限制在特定限额内。每次使用小容量容器提供微量饮品。撤走水壶或移至视线之外。由于口渴口干，需要频繁提供口腔护理以保持口腔黏膜湿润。严格记录出入液量。
• Nothing by mouth (NPO). The client cannot eat or drink anything—NPO stands for the Latin term nil per os, meaning “nothing (nil) by (per) mouth (os).” Clients are usually NPO before and after surgery, before certain laboratory tests and X-ray procedures, and in the treatment of certain illnesses. Clients who are tube-fed may be NPO, in which case an NPO sign is usually posted above the bed and the water pitcher and glass are removed. Frequent oral hygiene is important, but you must ensure that the client does not swallow any fluid.
• 禁食禁饮（NPO）：服务对象绝对严禁由口进食或饮用任何液体——NPO 源自拉丁文“nil per os”，意为“任何东西（nil）都不经由（per）口腔（os）进入”。常见于手术前后、某些化验检查和胃肠道造影检查前，以及急性胰腺炎等胃肠疾患治疗期。接受管饲的患者也可能遵医嘱禁食，床头必须悬挂明显的“NPO”警示标牌，撤走水壶和水杯。加强口腔护理至关重要，但必须严格监护，防止误吞漱口水。

Calculating Daily Fluid Intake

每日出入液量监测与计算 (Calculating Daily Fluid Intake)
Some clients must have their daily fluid intake calculated to ensure they receive enough fluids each day. Other clients have their daily fluid intake calculated against their daily urinary output (see Chapter 29). The physician or nurse may want a client’s fluid intake and output measured, in which case you will have to keep intake and output (I&O) records. I&O records are used to evaluate fluid balance and kidney function and to plan medical treatment. They also are kept when the client has special fluid orders.
部分服务对象需要计算每日摄入的体液总量以确保补液充足；另一些服务对象则需要将摄入量与每日尿量进行对比（参见第29章）。当医生或护士开具出入量监测医嘱时，必须严密维持出入量（I&O）记录表。I&O 记录用于客观评估体液平衡和肾功能，并为临床医疗方案的调整提供核心依据。
All fluids taken by mouth, as well as fluids given in intravenous (IV) therapy and tube feedings (see Chapter 28), are measured and recorded. The obvious fluids are measured—water, milk, coffee, tea, juices, soups, and soft drinks—as well as the fluid content in soft and semi-solid foods such as ice cream, sherbet, custard, pudding, jelly, and popsicles. Output measure includes urine, vomitus, diarrhea, and wound drainage.
所有经口摄入的液体，以及静脉输液（IV）和管饲补液（参见第28章），都必须精确计量并记录。不仅包括显而易见的饮用水、牛奶、咖啡、茶、果汁、浓汤和饮料，还包括在体温或室温下会融化为液体的半固态食物，如冰淇淋、果汁雪葩、蛋奶冻、布丁、果冻和冰棒。排出量（Output）则包括尿液、呕吐物、腹泻水样便以及伤口引流液。
Intake and output are measured in millilitres (mL) or in cubic centimetres (cc). These metric-system measurements are equal in amount (1 mL = 1 cc). Common conversions include: 30 mL equals 1 ounce; 500 mL is about 1 pint; 1 000 mL is about 1 quart. A container called a graduate (FIGURE 27.9) is used to measure fluids. When intake or output is measured, the amount is recorded on the I&O record (FIGURE 27.10). Amounts are totalled at the end of the shift.
出入量以毫升（mL）或立方厘米（cc）计量，二者在数量上完全等同（1 mL = 1 cc）。常见换算关系包括：30毫升约等于1盎司（oz）；500毫升约等于1品脱；1,000毫升约等于1夸脱。使用带有刻度的量筒/量杯（Graduate，图 27.9）精准测量液体。每次测量后将数值登记在出入量记录表（图 27.10）对应栏目中，并在每班次结束时统计班次总量。

Mrs. King is a client who lives in a long-term care facility. She was diagnosed with Alzheimer’s-related dementia (see Chapter 38) several years ago. She no longer remembers her family or friends and although she can no longer speak, she is able to swallow foods that are minced and fed to her by spoon. Mrs. King is Jewish, and her family has requested that she be given only foods that comply with traditional Jewish laws, which includes avoiding pork.
金夫人居住在长期照护机构中。数年前她被确诊为阿尔茨海默病型痴呆（参见第38章）。她已记不清家人或朋友，虽然无法说话，但仍能吞咽用勺子喂给她的肉糜软食。金夫人是犹太教徒，其家属明确要求为她提供的食物必须严格符合犹太传统饮食戒律（Kosher），其中严禁食用猪肉。
Today, one of the mealtime food choices was a meatloaf made from ground pork. While feeding your own clients, you notice that Mrs. King was being fed the meatloaf by Finn, her support worker. After being gently reminded that the meatloaf contained meat that was against Mrs. King’s religion, Finn replied, “So what? She can safely swallow it and she seems to like it. Besides, she doesn’t know what she’s eating!”
今天，餐食选项之一是用猪肉馅制作的肉饼。在协助自己负责的服务对象进餐时，你注意到支持人员芬恩（Finn）正在把猪肉肉饼喂给金夫人吃。在被你委婉提醒该肉饼含有违反金夫人宗教信仰的猪肉后，芬恩却回答：“那又怎样？她吞咽得很顺利，而且看起来挺爱吃的。再说，她根本不知道自己在吃什么！”

FOCUS ON CHILDREN: Fluid Requirements · 聚焦儿童：体液生理需求特征
Infants and young children have more body water than do adults and, therefore, need higher fluid intake. Excessive fluid loss can quickly cause death in infants and children.
婴儿和幼儿体内水分占体重的比例远高于成年人，因此按体重计算需要更高的液体摄入量。过度的体液丢失（如剧烈呕吐或腹泻）会在极短时间内导致婴儿和幼儿严重脱水甚至危及生命。
FOCUS ON OLDER PERSONS: Fluid Requirements · 聚焦老年人：体液平衡与脱水风险
Older persons are at risk for diseases that affect fluid balance, such as heart disease, kidney disease, cancer, and diabetes. Many older people also take medications that cause the body to lose fluids or retain water. Older persons are thus at risk for edema and dehydration (see Chapter 33).
老年人易患影响体液平衡的慢性病，如心脏病、肾脏疾病、恶性肿瘤和糖尿病。许多老年人还在服用利尿剂或引起水钠潴留的药物。因此，老年人发生水肿和脱水的风险极高（参见第33章）。
Older persons may have a decreased sense of thirst. Their bodies need water, but they may not feel thirsty. In addition, fluids lost by perspiration or through expiration cannot be measured. Offer water at least every 1 to 2 hours to your older clients.

Clinical Tips for Accurate Intake and Output Measurement

出入量精确测量临床核心技巧
• Know the container sizes used by your facility: A standard coffee mug is usually 240 mL (8 oz); a small juice glass is 120 mL (4 oz); a water pitcher holds about 1 000 mL (32 oz); a gelatin/pudding cup is typically 100 mL.
• 牢记所属机构常用器皿容量标准：标准咖啡马克杯通常为240毫升（8盎司）；小果汁玻璃杯通常为120毫升（4盎司）；大号冷水壶约为1,000毫升（32盎司）；单个果冻/布丁杯通常为100毫升。
• Ice chips count as 50% fluid: A full cup of ice chips melts into approximately half a cup of water (e.g., a 200-mL cup of ice chips equals 100 mL of fluid intake).
• 冰块按50%液体体积折算：整整一杯碎冰融化后体积约为杯容量的一半（例如，摄入200毫升一杯碎冰，实际计入100毫升液体摄入量）。
• Record immediately: Do not rely on memory. Record amounts promptly on the bedside sheet or portable chart.
• 测量后立即记录：切勿依赖记忆！测定后须立即登记在床旁表单或便携终端上。

CHAPTER REVIEW: KEY POINTS

本章核心知识点总结 (CHAPTER REVIEW: KEY POINTS)
• Nutrition is the many processes involved in the ingestion, digestion, absorption, and use of foods and fluids by the body.
• 营养（Nutrition）是机体摄入、消化、吸收和利用食物与体液的全部复杂生理过程。
• Nutrients are substances that are ingested, digested, absorbed, and used by the body. They are grouped into proteins, carbohydrates, fats, vitamins, minerals, and water.
• 营养素（Nutrients）是机体摄入、消化、吸收并利用的活性物质，分为蛋白质、碳水化合物、脂肪、维生素、矿物质和水六大类。
• A well-balanced diet provides sufficient calories and the proper amounts of carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, minerals, and water each day.
• 均衡膳食每日提供充足的热量，以及适量的碳水化合物、脂肪、蛋白质、维生素、矿物质和水分。
• Health Canada’s Canada’s Dietary Guidelines (2019) promotes wise food choices. The Guidelines are available in 28 languages, and a tailored version is available for Indigenous people in Canada.
• 加拿大卫生部的《加拿大膳食指南（2019）》倡导明智的食物选择。该指南提供28种语言版本，并专门为加拿大原住民群体定制了契合传统文化的版本。
• Nutritional requirements differ throughout a person’s life cycle.
• 人体在生命周期的各个阶段具有显著不同的营养需求。
• Be aware of the amount of caffeine you and your clients consume daily. Too much caffeine can cause adverse effects.
• 密切关注自身及服务对象每日咖啡因摄入总量。过量摄入咖啡因会诱发失眠、头痛及焦虑等不良反应。
• Factors that can affect eating and nutrition are personal choices, allergies, food intolerances, culture, religion, finances, appetite, illness, and age.
• 影响进餐与营养的多元因素包括个人选择、食物过敏、不耐受、文化、宗教、经济收入、食欲、疾病状态及年龄。
• In the case of clients with type 1 diabetes, it is very important to record the intake for every meal and snack to ensure that their dietary intake is adequate for the amount of insulin they must take.
• 对于1型糖尿病患者，极为关键的是准确记录每餐和每次加餐的实际摄入量，确保热量摄入与注射的胰岛素剂量精准平衡。
• Follow the care plan carefully, especially if you are to assist a client with eating.
• 严格执行照护计划，尤其是在协助服务对象进食时。
• Recording intake and output is necessary when caring for a client who is ill. Harm, even death, can result from too much or too little water in the body.
• 照护患病服务对象时监测出入液量至关重要。体内水分过量或匮乏均可引发机体严重损伤甚至死亡。
