As a support worker, you will be required to help meet the physical, social, emotional, intellectual, and spiritual needs of clients who are dying, whether in a facility or in clients’ homes. Your role may also involve providing support to a client’s family and loved ones. Some family members may be very involved in care, whereas others may not be available because of distance or personal choice.

作为支持人员，无论是在医疗护理机构中还是在客户家中，你都将被要求协助满足临终患者在生理、社交、情感、智识和灵性层面的多元需求。你的工作职责同样涵盖向患者的家属及至亲提供深切的情感与生活支持。有些家属可能会全身心深度参与床旁照护，而有些家属则可能因路途遥远或个人心理原因无法时刻在侧。

End-of-life care can be emotionally draining not only to the loved ones of the dying client but also to the client’s caregivers. Witnessing and providing end-of-life care may remind caregivers of their own eventual death or of the deaths of their own loved ones. For some, having a dying client may cause feelings of helplessness since they are unable to “make the client better.” Some healthcare team members may be unsure of their feelings about death or may be uncomfortable with dying clients or the subject of death being mentioned in conversation with the client.

临终生命关怀不仅对逝者的家属是一场巨大的身心情感消耗，对一线的照护人员同样极具情绪挑战。目睹生命消逝并提供濒死照护，往往会唤起照护者对自己未来终将死亡的潜意识联想，或重温失去自身挚亲的痛苦记忆。对于部分照护者而言，眼看患者步步走向死亡而无法“使其好转痊愈”，会滋生强烈的无力感与挫败感。部分医疗团队成员可能对死亡本身感到迷茫困惑，或在面对濒死患者、以及在与患者探讨死亡话题时感到无所适从与极度不自在。

Your feelings and beliefs about death and dying may affect the care you give when caring for a dying client, so you need to understand both the dying process and your own attitudes toward death and dying. This understanding will help you provide compassionate care. Before reading on, reflect on the following:

  • What has been your experience with death? (你过往经历过怎样的亲历死亡事件？)
  • What are your feelings and beliefs about death and dying? (你对死亡和临终有着怎样的情感体验与内在信念？)
  • How have your experiences and culture shaped your beliefs? (你的生活经历与文化背景是如何塑造这些信念的？)

LIFE-THREATENING CONDITIONS (危及生命的终末期疾病)

Many life-threatening conditions have no cure, and some injuries are so serious that the body cannot function. In these cases, recovery is not expected, and the health condition or injury inevitably ends in death. Healthcare providers cannot predict the exact time of death. A client may have days, months, weeks, or years to live. Some clients who are expected to live for a short time live for many years, whereas others who are expected to live longer die much sooner than predicted. No one can predict the length of time a client may have, as each client is unique.

许多危及生命的器质性重疾在现代医学中均无治愈良策，某些重度创伤破坏了机体维持生命的核心机能。在这些情况下，临床已无法指望康复痊愈，病情进展不可避免地将以死亡告终。医护专业人员无法精准预言死亡降临的确切时刻。患者可能存活数天、数周、数月乃至数年。有些被预估寿命极短的患者坚强地活了许多年，而另一些预估生存期较长的患者却突然急转直下提前离世。每个人的生命机能与精神意志皆独一无二，无人能断言确切的倒计时。

Personal Beliefs, Culture, and Religion (个人信念、多元文化与宗教仪式)

Practices and attitudes about death and dying differ among individuals, cultures, and religions (see the Respecting Diversity: Death Rituals box on page 1124). Some people believe that there is no suffering and hardship after death, that they will be reunited with loved ones in the afterlife, and that sins and misdeeds will be punished then, too. Other people believe there is no afterlife. Still others believe in rebirth. Many clients experience a strengthening of their religious beliefs when they face death, and such faith often provides comfort to the dying person and the family. Alternatively, clients may instead experience a loss or diminishment of religious faith. A client’s personal, cultural, and religious beliefs also influence choices about end-of-life care and treatment of the body after death.

关于死亡与临终的态度和习俗，在不同个体、不同文化以及各大宗教信仰体系中存在着显著差异（参见第 1124 页专栏《尊重多元化：各文化死亡仪式》）。有些人深信死后便彻底告别了人间的痛苦与折磨，将在来世与先逝的亲人幸福重聚，而生前的罪恶亦将在彼岸受到惩处；另一些人则认为死后万事皆空、并无任何来生；还有许多信仰坚信因果转世与灵魂重生。许多患者在濒临生命终点时，宗教信仰会变得格外虔诚坚定，这种深层信仰往往能为临终者和家属带来巨大的心灵慰藉；反之，亦有部分患者在痛苦折磨中对过往的信仰产生动摇甚至幻灭。客户的个人价值观、文化背景及宗教归属，直接决定了其对临终医疗方案以及死后遗体处理方式的最终抉择。

How Age and Experience Affect Responses to Death and Dying (年龄与生活阅历对死亡反应的影响)

People’s attitudes and responses to dying are highly individual. Age and stage of life are major influences on feelings and reactions regarding death:

人们面对死亡的心态与行为反应呈现出极高的个体差异性。其中，年龄跨度与生命发展阶段是决定人们面对死亡情感反应的最核心驱动因素：

  • Infants and toddlers (婴儿与幼儿): Have no concept of death. They react primarily to separation, changes in routine, and the sadness or stress of caregivers.
    婴儿和刚学步的幼儿对死亡毫无抽象认知。他们主要对与主要照料者的分离、日常作息紊乱以及周围成年人的悲伤焦虑情绪产生感知与应激反应。
  • Children aged 3 to 5 (3 至 5 岁幼童): Are curious and have some ideas about death (noticing dead pets, insects, or family loss). However, they think death is only temporary or reversible, like sleeping or cartoon characters coming back to life. Inaccurate explanations (e.g., “Grandma went to sleep”) can cause severe fear of bedtime.
    对死亡充满好奇并有模糊概念（注意到了宠物、昆虫死亡或家庭变故）。然而，他们天真地认为死亡是暂时的、可逆的，就像睡着了或动画片角色死而复生一样。若给予不准确的隐喻解释（如“奶奶睡着了”），常会导致孩子极度抗拒睡觉，害怕自己一睡不醒。
  • Children aged 5 to 7 (5 至 7 岁儿童): Know that death is final and universal, though they often do not believe it will ever happen to them or their immediate parents.
    开始明白死亡是终结性的、不可逆转的自然规律，但通常认为死亡距离自己非常遥远，绝不会降临到自己或父母头上。
  • Adults (成年人): Often harbor more complex fears about death than children do. Adults fear pain, suffering, dying alone, loss of control, loss of dignity, and leaving dependents behind. Many experience profound grief over unfulfilled dreams or missed life opportunities.
    成年人往往比儿童承受更多维度的深层恐惧。成年人畏惧剧烈疼痛折磨、畏惧孤独死去、畏惧丧失自我控制与尊严，并深切担忧身后未成年子女或年迈父母的抚养。许多人会对生前未竟的事业、破裂的亲情及未实现的梦想产生深刻的遗憾与痛苦。
  • Older adults (老年人): Often have fewer existential fears of death because they have anticipated it as a natural life culmination and have grieved many peers. Some welcome death as a peaceful release from chronic pain, debilitating disability, and loneliness. However, like younger people, older persons profoundly fear dying alone.
    老年人通常对死亡本身表现出更少的恐惧，因为他们早已将其视为生命的必然归宿，且一生中经历了许多同辈亲友的离世。有些长者将死亡视为摆脱慢性顽疾折磨、残障痛苦与孤寂的安详解脱。然而，与年轻人一样，老年人同样极其害怕在孤独无援中死去。

Meeting Emotional, Social, and Spiritual Needs (满足患者情感、社交与灵性需求)

The health care team helps meet the dying client’s emotional, social, spiritual, intellectual, and physical needs. The client’s right to die in peace and with dignity is always kept at the forefront. Support workers assist by:

跨学科医疗团队协同满足临终患者在情感、社交、灵性、智识及生理层面的全方位需求。确保患者在安详、平和与尊严中度过生命终点是所有照护努力的核心旨归。支持人员在日常照护中应做到：

  • Listening (专注倾听): Let the client share worries, life stories, and fears. You do not need to give advice or worry about saying the perfect words; your quiet, accepting presence is therapeutic. If the client requests medical information, prognosis, or spiritual counselling, notify the nurse immediately.
    允许患者充分倾诉内心的担忧、往昔回忆与恐惧。你无需好为人师地给出建议，更不必因担心说错话而焦虑；你安静、专注且全盘接纳的陪伴本身就是最好的良药。若患者询问病情进展、预后或提出灵性咨询，务必立即通报主管护士。
  • Touch (恰当的肢体抚慰): Holding a hand, gently wiping a brow, or providing gentle back care conveys warmth and shows that the client is valued and not abandoned. Always observe verbal and nonverbal cues to ensure touch is welcomed.
    轻握双手、轻抚额头或提供轻柔的背部抚触，能向患者传递深切的温暖，证明他们被珍视、绝未被遗弃。始终细心观察患者的言语及非言语反应，确保肢体触摸受到对方欣然接纳。
  • Spiritual Needs (尊重灵性关怀): Provide privacy for prayer, meditation, or visits from clergy, elders, or spiritual leaders. Handle sacred objects (rosaries, scriptures, medicine bags) with utmost reverence.
    在神职人员、宗教长老、灵性领袖探视或患者进行祈祷冥想时，提供绝对私密的独立空间。以最高崇敬之心对待宗教圣物与灵性用具（念珠、经卷、原住民药袋等），绝不可随意挪动或亵渎。

BOX 47.1: The Dying Person’s Bill of Rights (专栏 47.1：临终者权利法案)

  • I have the right to be treated as a living human being until I die. (我有权在离世之前始终被作为一个有生命的完整人来对待).
  • I have the right to maintain a sense of hopefulness, however changing its focus may be. (我有权保持希望感，无论希望的焦点如何随病情转变).
  • I have the right to be cared for by those who can maintain a sense of hopefulness, however changing this might be. (我有权由那些能够始终怀抱希望感的人来照护).
  • I have the right to express my feelings and emotions about my approaching death in my own way. (我有权以我自己的方式自由表达对濒临死亡的情感与情绪).
  • I have the right to participate in decisions concerning my care. (我有权自主参与有关我自身医疗照护的所有决策).
  • I have the right to expect continuing medical and nursing attention even though “cure” goals must be changed to “comfort” goals. (即便治疗目标已从“治愈”转向“舒适”，我有权享有持续的高水平医疗与护理支持).
  • I have the right not to die alone. (我有权不孤独地死去).
  • I have the right to be free from pain. (我有权免受肉体疼痛的折磨).
  • I have the right to have my questions answered honestly. (我有权获得对我所有疑问的坦诚回答).
  • I have the right to retain my individuality and not be judged for my decisions. (我有权保留我的个性特质，且绝不受道德评判).
  • I have the right to expect that the sanctity of the human body will be respected after death. (我有权期待在死后我的遗体尊严受到神圣的尊重).
  • I have the right to be cared for by caring, sensitive, knowledgeable people who will attempt to understand my needs. (我有权由充满爱心、敏锐且知识渊博的专业人员来照护).

Meeting Physical Needs During the Dying Process (临终生命历程中的基础生理需求护理)

Dying is a physical process in which body functions slow down and eventually cease. Meeting physical needs promotes comfort, alleviates suffering, and preserves dignity:

临终是一个机体各系统机能逐步衰退乃至最终完全停止的客观生理过程。全方位满足生理基础需求旨在最大程度缓解痛苦、增进身心舒适并捍卫生命尊严：

  • Pain relief (疼痛管理): Pain prevention and aggressive symptom relief are central to palliative care. Observe for subtle nonverbal signs of pain (grimacing, moaning, restlessness, furrowed brow). Report pain immediately to the nurse so prescribed analgesics can be administered promptly.
    预防疼痛发作与强力控制症状是安宁姑息疗护的基石。细心观察非言语疼痛征象（面部痛苦扭曲、呻吟、辗转反侧、紧锁眉头）。一旦发现疼痛蛛丝马迹，秒级汇报护士以便按医嘱及时给予强效镇痛药物。
  • Breathing problems (呼吸困难支持): Shortness of breath (dyspnea) is distressing. Position the client in semi-Fowler's or Fowler's position or turn the client onto their side to facilitate respiratory expansion. Oxygen therapy may be ordered. A cool cloth on the forehead or a gentle room fan circulating air can ease the sensation of breathlessness.
    呼吸急促与呼吸困难极易引发窒息恐慌。协助采取半坐卧位或高半坐卧位，或取侧卧位以利于胸廓扩张及痰液引流。遵医嘱给予鼻导管吸氧。在前额敷以凉毛巾，或开启柔风小电扇在房间内促进空气循环，能显著缓解呼吸窘迫感。
  • Vision, Hearing, and Speech (感觉与言语机能): Vision blurs and fails as death nears; keep the room well-lit without glare, avoiding harsh shadows. Speech may become indistinct; listen patiently without asking long questions. Hearing is the last sense to be lost! Always speak clearly in a gentle, normal tone, explain care before touching, and never say anything in the room that you would not want the client to hear.
    临终前视力逐渐模糊减退；保持房间光线柔和明亮且无刺眼眩光，避免产生惊悚阴影。言语可能含糊不清；耐心倾听，避免追问复杂问题。听觉是最后消失的感觉！始终以温和清晰的正常语调交谈，在任何触碰操作前先轻声解释，绝对严禁在床旁谈论任何不希望患者听到的消极话语或丧葬安排。
  • Mouth and Nose Care (口腔与鼻腔清洁): Mouth breathing, dehydration, and medications cause extreme oral dryness and crusted secretions. Perform gentle mouth care every 2 hours using moistened foam swabs. Apply lip balm or petroleum jelly to prevent cracked lips. Clean nasal secretions gently and apply water-soluble lubricant around the nostrils if dry.
    张口呼吸、脱水以及抗胆碱药物会引起极度口干与结痂。每 2 小时使用湿润海绵棒轻柔进行口腔护理。涂抹润唇膏或凡士林防止嘴唇干裂出血。轻柔擦拭鼻腔干痂，若鼻孔干燥可涂抹少量水溶性润滑剂。
  • Skin Care, Cleanliness, and Elimination (皮肤、个人清洁与排泄): Incontinence of urine and stool often occurs as anal and urinary sphincters relax. Check bed protectors frequently, cleanse skin immediately after soiling, apply barrier creams, and keep bed linens immaculately dry, smooth, and wrinkle-free to prevent rapid skin breakdown.
    随着膀胱括约肌和肛门括约肌进行性松弛，大小便失禁极其常见。高频次检查床垫看护垫，一旦污染立即使用温水轻柔清洗擦干，涂抹护臀隔离膏，保持床单绝对清洁平整无褶皱，防止极度脆弱的皮肤发生坏死破损。
  • Nutrition and Fluids (营养摄入与水分支持): As death approaches, appetite and thirst naturally decline as metabolic processes shut down. Never force the client to eat or drink; forcing food or fluids can cause choking, vomiting, and pulmonary aspiration. Offer small sips of water or ice chips if the client is conscious and swallowing safely.
    随着生命走向终点、全身新陈代谢逐步停滞，食欲和口渴感会自然消退。绝不能强迫患者进食或饮水；强行灌食会导致严重呛咳、反流呕吐及致命性肺误吸。仅在患者神志清楚且吞咽功能完好时，提供少量碎冰吮吸或极少量润喉水。
p. 1115 Comforting the Family & Meeting Loved Ones' Needs (安抚临终者家属与家属照护支持)

Comforting the Family (安抚临终患者家属与至亲)

The approaching death of a loved one is a stressful and traumatic experience for family members and friends. Loved ones often suffer from sleep deprivation, profound emotional exhaustion, helplessness, and grief. The healthcare team cares for both the dying client and the client's family. You can assist by:

至亲生命的逐渐凋零与即将离世，对家庭成员和亲朋挚友而言是一段充满巨大应激与心理创伤的艰难旅程。家属常常遭受严重的长期睡眠剥夺、极度身心俱疲、强烈的无助感以及排山倒海的哀伤体验。医疗照护团队的法定关怀职责既涵盖临终患者本人，亦同等覆盖患者的整个家庭系统。支持人员在床旁可通过以下实际行动提供关怀：

  • Showing compassion and availability (展现同理同情与随时待命): Greet family members warmly by name. Ask how they are holding up and let them know you are readily available to assist them.
    亲切直呼家属姓名问候致意。主动关切询问他们的身体状况与耐受力，明确告知自己时刻在侧，可随时提供所需协助。
  • Providing physical comfort in the room (优化病房物理环境与舒适度): Provide comfortable armchairs, extra pillows, and warm blankets for family members staying overnight at the bedside. Ensure the room has tissues and access to water, coffee, or tea.
    为在床旁守夜的家属提供舒适的靠背扶手椅、加厚软枕以及保暖毛毯。确保床旁备有充足的纸巾，并提供饮用水、温咖啡或茶水服务。
  • Respecting privacy and intimacy (守护家庭私密与最后告别空间): Allow the family private time alone with the dying person. Before entering the room, always knock gently. When entering to provide essential care, do so quietly and efficiently, and leave promptly when finished so the family can resume their intimate vigil.
    给予家庭成员与濒死亲人独处的私密空间与神圣时间。在推门进入病房前务必轻柔敲门。进入病房执行必要的基础护理时，动作保持极度安静敏捷，完成后迅速退出身旁，将珍贵的最后守候时光归还给亲人。
  • Involving family in care (支持家属参与力所能及的床旁护理): Many family members find comfort in helping with simple care tasks, such as applying lip balm, moistening the mouth with a foam swab, or holding hands. Offer this opportunity gently, but never make family members feel pressured or guilty if they prefer not to participate.
    许多家属通过参与力所能及的轻度护理（如为亲人涂抹润唇膏、用湿海绵棒擦拭口腔或轻握双手）能获得深层的心理慰藉与参与感。应温和地向家属提供这种机会，但若家属因过度悲伤不愿动手，绝不可施加任何心理压力或令其产生负罪感。
p. 1116 The Role of Support Workers with Families & Respite Care (家属陪伴支持与喘息照护)

Supporting Family Caregivers & Respite Care (家庭照护者支持与喘息照护服务)

When clients die at home, family members carry an immense burden of physical care, medication administration, and emotional strain around the clock. Family caregivers often experience profound exhaustion and caregiver burnout. Support workers provide crucial relief by:

当患者选择在居家环境中安度生命末期时，家庭成员必须承担起 24 小时昼夜不息的繁重生活起居照护、辅助服药以及巨大的情感煎熬。家庭照料者极易陷入深度的身心耗竭与照护崩溃（Caregiver burnout）。支持人员在居家环境中通过以下举措发挥核心减压与支柱作用：

  • Providing respite care (提供喘息照护): Taking over bedside monitoring and personal care so that family caregivers can sleep, shower, take a walk, eat a proper meal, or attend to personal affairs without worrying that their loved one is unattended.
    主动接管床旁监护与个人基础生活护理，使精疲力竭的家属能够踏实补觉、洗热水澡、外出散步透气、安心享用正餐或处理个人要事，无需时刻悬心担忧亲人床旁无人看顾。
  • Validating feelings without clichés (接纳情绪宣泄，杜绝陈词滥调): Grieving families experience anger, sadness, guilt, and despair. Avoid hurtful, dismissive clichés such as: “It’s for the best,” “They lived a long life,” or “You must stay strong.” Instead, use therapeutic statements: “I am so sorry you are going through this,” or simply say nothing and remain a comforting presence.
    处于巨大哀伤中的家属往往会表现出愤怒、痛哭、愧疚与绝望等剧烈情绪。严禁使用那些轻描淡写、看似安慰实则伤人的陈词滥调（例如：“这也是一种解脱”、“反正岁数也大了”、“你必须坚强起来”）。应采用同理心专业回应：“看到你们经历这些，我深感难过”，或者保持沉默，给予温暖坚定的床旁陪伴。
  • Reporting family crises to the supervisor (及时上报家庭危机): If a family member expresses thoughts of self-harm, severe clinical depression, explosive conflict with other family members, or total physical collapse, report these acute situations to your supervisor immediately so social work, mental health, or crisis resources can intervene.
    若家属流露出自伤自杀念头、表现出重度临床抑郁、与其他家属发生严重冲突失控，或身体突发虚脱昏厥，必须立即向主管护士汇报，以便临床社工、心理危机干预团队或急诊医疗力量即刻介入。
p. 1117 Palliative Care & Hospice Philosophy (姑息照护与安宁疗护理念及跨学科团队)

PALLIATIVE CARE AND HOSPICE PHILOSOPHY (姑息照护与安宁疗护核心哲学)

Palliative care is specialized care that serves people living with progressive, life-limiting illnesses. The term palliative is derived from the Latin word palliare, meaning “to cloak” or “to shield.” Rather than seeking to cure an incurable condition, palliative care shields the client from the burdens of pain and distressing symptoms. Palliative care affirms life and regards dying as a normal process; it neither hastens nor postpones death.

姑息照护（Palliative Care）是专门针对罹患进展性、威胁生命极限疾病患者的跨学科专科照护。英文单词“Palliative”源自拉丁文词根“palliare”，意为“用斗篷遮盖、庇护或掩蔽”。姑息照护的精髓不在于强求逆转不可治愈的绝症，而在于如同斗篷般为患者抵御剧烈疼痛与各类身心折磨症状。姑息疗护彻底肯定生命的尊严，将死亡视为生命自然的终点；它既不人为加速死亡，亦不盲目拖延濒死过程。

Hospice Care (安宁疗护的发展历程)

The modern hospice movement was founded in England in 1967 by Dame Cicely Saunders with the establishment of St. Christopher’s Hospice. In Canada, Dr. Balfour Mount introduced the term palliative care in 1974 at the Royal Victoria Hospital in Montreal. In common Canadian usage, palliative care and hospice care are closely interrelated. Palliative care can begin early in the course of an illness alongside treatments, whereas hospice care typically refers to holistic comfort care provided in the final months, weeks, or days of life when curative treatments have stopped entirely.

现代安宁疗护（Hospice Care）运动于 1967 年由西塞莉·桑德斯女爵（Dame Cicely Saunders）在英国创立圣克里斯托弗安宁院（St. Christopher’s Hospice）而拉开序幕。在加拿大，巴尔弗·芒特博士（Dr. Balfour Mount）于 1974 年在蒙特利尔皇家维多利亚医院首次开创性地确立了“姑息照护（Palliative Care）”这一临床专科名称。在加拿大当前的医疗实践中，姑息照护与安宁疗护密不可分。广义的姑息照护可在确诊恶性绝症早期与延寿治疗协同展开；而狭义的安宁疗护则特指在彻底停止所有抗癌根治性医疗手段之后，在生命的最后数月、数周或数日内，全方位提供的人性化身心舒缓与灵性临终关怀。

The Palliative Interprofessional Team (姑息跨学科协同团队构成)

Palliative and hospice care relies on a highly integrated interprofessional team that wraps around the client and family: palliative physicians, specialized nurses, support workers, social workers, spiritual care coordinators/chaplains, pharmacists, physiotherapists, bereavement counsellors, and trained hospice volunteers. Care is delivered across multiple settings: in acute hospital palliative units, in specialized residential hospice facilities, in long-term care homes, and in the client’s private home.

姑息与安宁疗护依托高度协同的跨学科团队，全方位环绕照护患者与整个家庭：包括姑息专科医生、姑息专科护士、一线支持人员、临床社会工作者、灵性关怀协调员/神职人员、临床药剂师、物理治疗师、丧亲心理咨询师以及经过系统培训的安宁疗护志愿者。照护服务在多元医疗环境中一体化贯通：涵盖综合性医院急性期姑息病房、独立的民居式住院安宁院（Residential Hospice）、长期照护养老机构以及患者自己的温馨居家之中。

p. 1118 Legal Issues: Advance Directives, Living Wills & Power of Attorney (临终法律议题：预先指示、生前预嘱与委托代理人)

LEGAL ISSUES SURROUNDING DEATH AND DYING (围绕临终与死亡的法律与伦理议题)

Clients have the legal and ethical right to make autonomous decisions regarding their own healthcare and to refuse medical treatments, even when refusal will lead to death. When clients lose the cognitive capacity to communicate or make informed choices, advance legal documents ensure their wishes are honored.

患者依法享有对其自身医疗照护做出自主知情决定的不可剥夺的法律与伦理权利，并有权拒绝任何医疗干预措施，即便拒绝治疗将直接导致生命的终结。当患者因昏迷、极度衰竭或认知功能障碍丧失沟通与决策能力时，提前依法签署的法律文书是确保其生前真实意愿得以严格落实的核心屏障。

  • Advance Directive (预先指示 / 预先医疗决定): A legal document in which a person specifies what actions should be taken for their healthcare if they are no longer able to make decisions for themselves because of illness or incapacity. Laws governing advance directives vary among Canadian provinces and territories.
    当事人预先签署的一份法定严肃文书，详细明确当其未来因疾病恶化或丧失意识而无法亲自做出决策时，医疗团队应当采纳何种治疗限度。加拿大各省和地区关于预先指示的法条名称与具体规范各有规定。
  • Living Will (生前预嘱): An instructional directive stating the specific medical treatments the person wants or does not want (e.g., mechanical ventilation, artificial tube feeding, hemodialysis, cardiopulmonary resuscitation) if they become terminally ill or permanently unconscious.
    一种指令性预先文件，详细载明当事人一旦罹患终末期绝症或陷入永久性植物人/不可逆昏迷时，其本人渴望接受或明确拒绝的具体侵入性医疗手段（例如：气管插管呼吸机辅助通气、鼻饲管或胃造口人工管饲营养、血液透析、心肺复苏术等）。
  • Power of Attorney for Personal Care / Healthcare Proxy (人身照护授权书 / 医疗授权代理人): A proxy directive appointing a trusted substitute decision-maker (SDM)—often a spouse, child, sibling, or close friend—with legal authority to make healthcare, housing, and personal care decisions on the client's behalf if the client becomes incapable. The proxy must make decisions based on the client's previously expressed wishes; if wishes are unknown, decisions must reflect the client’s best interests.
    一种委托代理型法定文书，依法任命一位值得信赖的法定替代决策者（Substitute Decision-Maker, SDM，通常为配偶、成年子女、兄弟姐妹或挚友），授权其在当事人丧失判断力时代为行使全部医疗、住所安置及人身照护的法律裁量权。代理人做出决定时，必须严格以当事人先前亲口表达或书面签署的已知意愿为唯一准绳；若已知意愿不详，则必须以维护当事人的最大生存与尊严利益为核心基准。
p. 1119 DNR Orders, Substitute Decision-Makers & Organ Donation (不复苏医嘱DNR、替代决策者与器官捐献)

Do Not Resuscitate (DNR) / Allow Natural Death (AND) Orders (不复苏与允许自然离世医嘱)

A “Do Not Resuscitate” (DNR) order, or “Allow Natural Death” (AND) order, is a formal medical order written by a physician after comprehensive consultation with the client or substitute decision-maker. It directs the healthcare team not to perform cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) if the client experiences cardiac or respiratory arrest. Cardiopulmonary resuscitation on frail, terminal clients frequently results in fractured ribs, punctured lungs, severe brain damage from anoxia, and prolonged agony without altering the inevitable fatal outcome. A DNR order does not mean withholding comfort; pain relief, hygiene, positioning, and compassionate nursing care are aggressively maintained.

“不复苏（DNR）医嘱”，亦被前瞻性地称为“允许自然离世（AND）医嘱”，是执业医师在与患者本人或其法定替代决策者进行深度坦诚沟通后，正式签署生效的法律级医疗指令。该医嘱郑重指示医疗团队：一旦患者发生心搏骤停或呼吸停止，严禁启动心肺复苏术（CPR）。在极度脆弱衰竭的终末期患者身上实施暴力胸外按压与电击，常导致肋骨多发骨折、胸壁塌陷刺破肺脏、因脑缺氧导致重度脑损伤，并在毫无逆转死亡希望的前提下造成难以名状的痛苦折磨。DNR 医嘱绝对不等于放弃照护——全方位的镇痛控痛、口腔清洁、定时翻身与深情的人文关爱不仅绝不减损，反而会被更积极地全面强化。

Organ and Tissue Donation (人体器官与组织捐献法制)

Many individuals express a compassionate wish to donate organs (heart, lungs, kidneys, liver, pancreas) or tissues (corneas, skin, bones, heart valves) upon death to save or improve the lives of others. Under provincial Human Tissue Gift legislation, individuals can register their donor status through provincial registries, online driver’s license programs, or advance directives. When death occurs, organ procurement organizations coordinate with the hospital and confirm with the family before organ retrieval is carried out with utmost surgical dignity.

许多崇高的人士表达了在大限来临之际，自愿捐献自身健康器官（心脏、肺脏、肾脏、肝脏、胰腺）或组织（眼角膜、体表皮肤、骨骼肌腱、心脏瓣膜）的慈悲大爱，以挽救或彻底重塑其他濒危病患的生命。依据加拿大各省《人体组织捐赠法》等法律法规，居民可通过各省器官捐献官方登记系统、驾照随行意愿登记卡或预先指示正式注册。当法定脑死亡或循环死亡确证之后，专职器官移植协调组织会与医院及至亲家属紧密协同签署正式授权，并在最高外科敬意与无菌尊严下完成器官获取手术。

p. 1120 Medical Assistance in Dying (MAID) Legislation in Canada (加拿大安乐死/医疗协助死亡MAID法案)

MEDICAL ASSISTANCE IN DYING (MAID) (加拿大医疗协助死亡法规)

In June 2016, the Parliament of Canada passed federal legislation (Bill C-14, subsequently amended by Bill C-7 in March 2021) that permits eligible Canadian adults who are experiencing enduring, intolerable physical or psychological suffering to request and receive Medical Assistance in Dying (MAID). MAID occurs when an authorized physician or nurse practitioner either: (1) directly administers a substance that causes death (clinician-administered), or (2) prescribes or provides a substance that the eligible client self-administers to cause death.

2016 年 6 月，加拿大联邦议会正式通过历史性法案（C-14 法案，随后于 2021 年 3 月由 C-7 法案进一步修订完善），依法允许承受不可逆转、持续且难以忍受的严重身心痛苦的符合条件的加拿大成年居民，合法申请并接受医疗协助死亡（Medical Assistance in Dying, MAID）。MAID 的合法实施途径包括两类：(1) 由具备法定资质的执业医生或执业护士（NP）直接静脉注射导致无痛死亡的药物（临床医生施行型）；(2) 由医生或执业护士开具处方并交付药物，由具备行为能力的当事人亲自服下以结束生命（当事人自主施行型）。

Eligibility Criteria for MAID in Canada (加拿大 MAID 核心法定准入条件)

To be eligible for MAID under Canadian law, a person must meet all of the following statutory criteria:

依据加拿大联邦现行法典，申请接受 MAID 的当事人必须同时无一例外地满足以下全部法定准入条件：

  • Be eligible for publicly funded healthcare services in Canada. (享有加拿大公共医保资助资格).
  • Be at least 18 years of age and mentally capable of making healthcare decisions. (年满 18 周岁且具备完全知情同意的民事与医疗决策认知能力).
  • Have a grievous and irremediable medical condition (a serious and incurable illness, disease, or disability; an advanced state of irreversible decline in capability; and enduring intolerable physical or psychological suffering that cannot be relieved under conditions acceptable to the person). (患有严重且不可逆转的器质性重疾，且承受着按其个人意愿无法以任何可接受手段缓解的极度身心折磨).
  • Make a voluntary request for MAID that is free from external pressure or coercion. (完全出于本人的真实自由意志发起申请，未受到任何家庭、财务或外界的强迫与诱导).
  • Provide informed consent to receive MAID after receiving full information on all available alternative options, including comprehensive palliative and comfort care. (在充分知晓所有替代诊疗途径，尤其是充分知悉可获得的安宁姑息疗护服务后，签署书面知情同意书).

THE SUPPORT WORKER’S LEGAL SCOPE REGARDING MAID (支持人员在 MAID 中的法定边界禁区)

Support workers play an essential supporting role, but they must never counsel, recommend, provide information about, or administer any medication for MAID. If a client expresses interest in or asks questions regarding MAID, listen with neutrality, never judge, and immediately report the client’s request to the nurse or physician. Continue to provide compassionate, respectful personal care right up until the procedure, supporting the dignity of the client and family.

支持人员在安宁团队中扮演重要的基础生活照料角色，但绝对严禁向客户主动推荐、劝诱、提供有关 MAID 的专业医疗咨询，更绝对严禁参与注射或递送任何 MAID 药物。一旦客户向你询问有关 MAID 的事宜或表达求死愿望，应保持客观中立倾听、绝不加以任何道德评判，并秒级如实向责任护士或主治医生汇报。在合法程序实施之前，支持人员应一如既往地为客户提供充满敬意、体贴入微的日常生理与心理舒适照护，全心维护逝者与家属的生命尊严。

p. 1121 The Experience of Grief & Professional Support Communication (哀伤体验、有效沟通与支持性回应原则)

THE EXPERIENCE OF GRIEF (哀伤的本质与多维身心体验)

Grief is the deeply personal, natural, and multifaceted response to loss—the process of moving from profound sorrow toward healing, integration, and recovery. Grief affects every dimension of an individual: physical (tightness in chest, fatigue, changes in appetite and sleep), emotional (profound sadness, shock, guilt, anger, anxiety), cognitive (disbelief, confusion, difficulty concentrating), and spiritual (questioning faith, searching for meaning). Grief counselling provides specialized professional support to help individuals navigate the complex turbulence of bereavement.

哀伤（Grief）是人类面对重大丧失时所产生的极其个性化、自然且多维度的深层心身反应——是一个自痛彻心扉的深渊逐步迈向心理疗愈、情感整合与生活复原的动态过程。哀伤波及人体的每一个维度：生理层面（胸口压迫濒死感、极度虚脱乏力、失眠或厌食暴食）；情绪层面（撕心裂肺的悲伤、震惊麻木、负罪自责、愤怒与焦虑）；认知层面（难以置信、恍惚健忘、注意力严重涣散）；以及灵性层面（对终极信仰的动摇与人生意义的重构）。哀伤辅导（Grief counselling）则是由专科心理治疗师或临床社工提供的专业性支持，旨在协助生者度过丧亲带来的狂风巨浪。

在床旁支持深陷哀伤的客户与家属时，所有言语交流都必须深植于真诚的同理心与谦逊之中。许多出于善意的探视者常常脱口而出一些陈词滥调，殊不知这些话语在潜意识中极大地抹杀并否定了当事人刻骨铭心的伤痛。

p. 1122 Theories of Grief: Kübler-Ross 5 Stages & Bowlby/Worden Models (哀伤心理学理论：库伯勒-罗斯五阶段模型)

The Five Stages of Grief: Dr. Elisabeth Kübler-Ross (库伯勒-罗斯博士哀伤五阶段经典理论)

In her landmark 1969 book On Death and Dying, psychiatrist Dr. Elisabeth Kübler-Ross identified five distinct stages that individuals frequently navigate when facing impending death or traumatic loss:

精神病学家伊丽莎白·库伯勒-罗斯博士（Dr. Elisabeth Kübler-Ross）在其 1969 年出版的开山名著《论死亡与临终》（On Death and Dying）中，提炼出了人类在直面死亡与重大丧亲时最为经典的五个心理应对阶段：

  • 1. Denial (否认期 — “No, not me!”): The person refuses to accept the reality of the terminal diagnosis or imminent death. They believe a laboratory error occurred, seek repeated second opinions, and act as though nothing is wrong. Denial acts as a temporary psychological buffer against overwhelming shock.
    1. 否认期（“不，绝不可能是我！”）：当事人在潜意识中拒绝承认绝症确诊或大限将至的残酷现实。坚信是医院化验搞错、反复辗转寻求其他医生复核，行为举止宛如一切如常。否认是人类自我防御机制在遭受毁灭性打击初期所构筑的暂时性心理缓冲盾牌。
  • 2. Anger (愤怒期 — “Why me?! It’s not fair!”): Denial crumbles into deep rage, bitterness, and resentment. The client lashes out at doctors, nurses, family members, God, or the support worker. Support workers must understand that this anger is not personal; it is a manifestation of profound frustration and helplessness.
    2. 愤怒期（“凭什么是我？！这太不公平了！”）：否认防线崩溃后，转入狂暴的怒火、怨恨与愤愤不平。患者常将无名怒火迁怒于医生、护士、贴身照顾的家属、上苍神明，乃至送饭翻身的支持人员。支持人员必须清醒认识到这种暴躁绝对不是针对你个人，而是患者面对生命彻底失控时的极度绝望外化。
  • 3. Bargaining (协议期 / 讨价还价期 — “Yes, me, but...”): The client attempts to negotiate with God, fate, or medical science for more time, promising good deeds or religious devotion in exchange for living to see a specific milestone (a child's wedding, birth of a grandchild).
    3. 协议期（“好吧，是我，但是求求多给我一点时间...”）：患者试图暗中与神明、命运或现代医学做最后交易以祈求宽限时日，许愿只要能活到见证某一特定人生里程碑（如参加女儿婚礼、亲眼看到孙辈出生），自己甘愿散尽家财或终身信道忏悔。
  • 4. Depression (抑郁期 — “Yes, me. What is the point?”): The client mourns losses already suffered (loss of job, health, mobility) and grieves the ultimate loss of life itself. The person may weep silently, refuse food, become quiet, and withdraw from social interaction.
    4. 抑郁期（“是的，是我，一切都毫无意义了...”）：患者既为生前已丧失的健康、事业、自理机能感到悲伤，更在深沉哀悼自己即将与世界和至亲彻底永别的终极命运。患者常默默垂泪、拒绝进食、陷入良久的死寂，并主动回避一切社交探视。
  • 5. Acceptance (接纳期 — “I am ready.”): The client reaches a state of peaceful tranquility and emotional detachment. They have accepted the inevitability of death. The struggle is over, pain is often reduced, and the person may want only quiet bedside companionship from immediate family.
    5. 接纳期（“我准备好了，心已安宁。”）：患者达至内心的平和与释怀，坦然接纳死亡必然来临的规律。内心的剧烈挣扎彻底止息，精神痛苦显著减轻，此时往往只渴望至亲挚友在床旁给予安静沉稳的默默陪伴。

MODERN UNDERSTANDING OF GRIEF (现代哀伤理论的非线性演进认知)

Modern bereavement science emphasizes that grief is not a linear, step-by-step checklist. A person does not neatly pass through stage 1 to stage 5. Individuals move back and forth between stages, skip stages altogether, experience multiple stages simultaneously, or express grief entirely differently according to their culture and coping mechanisms.

现代丧亲心理学明确指出：哀伤绝非死板教条的线性清单流程。人绝对不会按部就班地从第 1 阶段机械迈向第 5 阶段。每个人都会在不同阶段之间反复跳跃、中途折返、彻底跳过某些阶段、或同时兼具多重复杂情绪；文化背景与个体应对风格更决定了哀伤表达的无穷多样性。

p. 1123 Signs of Approaching Death & Clinical Manifestations (临终濒死先兆与生理征象)

Signs of Approaching Death (生命即将终结的客观濒死生理征象)

As biological systems fail in the final hours or days of life, distinct clinical manifestations occur. Recognizing these signs allows the care team to prepare the family, provide focused comfort, and ensure dignified presence:

在生命的最后数小时至数天内，随着机体各大脏器系统的全面衰竭，会出现极其清晰的特征性临床征象。敏锐识别这些濒死先兆，有助于护理团队协助家属做好充分的告别准备、聚焦实施终末舒适护理，并确保床旁充满宁静尊严：

  • Loss of movement, muscle tone, and sensation (运动、肌张力与感觉丧失): Facial muscles relax, the jaw sags downward, speech becomes impossible, and swallowing reflexes are abolished.
    面部肌肉完全松弛无力、下颌自然下垂微张、失去发音言语能力，吞咽反射彻底消失。
  • Slowing of gastrointestinal and urinary systems (胃肠蠕动与泌尿功能停滞): Abdominal distension, nausea, fecal impaction, and urinary/fecal incontinence occur as sphincters lose tone. Urine output drops drastically to scant, concentrated dark amber urine.
    括约肌肌张力完全丧失导致大小便失禁；肠麻痹引起腹部膨隆；肾脏灌注衰竭导致排尿量断崖式暴跌，仅有极少量浓缩呈深茶色的尿液。
  • Body temperature alterations (体温调节中枢紊乱): The client may feel burning hot with profuse diaphoresis, but skin extremities become cold and clammy to the touch.
    丘脑体温调节功能失调，患者可能自感内热高烧并大汗淋漓，但触摸其四肢末端却冰凉湿冷。
  • Circulatory failure and skin changes (循环衰竭与特征性皮肤花斑): Peripheral blood vessels constrict to shunt remaining blood to the heart and brain. Feet, hands, knees, and dependent areas develop mottled, purplish-blue marble-like discoloration (cyanosis / mottling). The pulse becomes rapid, thready, irregular, and progressively weaker until impalpable. Blood pressure drops precipitously.
    外周微循环严重淤滞代偿性收缩，足部、手部、膝盖及下垂受压部位出现灰白夹杂青紫色的特征性大理石花斑样改变（花斑现象 Mottling）。脉搏变得极其细速微弱、节律紊乱，直至桡动脉脉搏完全扪不清；血压呈现断崖式持续下跌。
  • Respiratory failure and the "Death Rattle" (呼吸系统衰竭与“濒死喉鸣”): Breathing patterns become irregular and shallow. Cheyne–Stokes respirations (alternating cycles of rapid, deep breathing followed by slow shallow breathing, ending in 10- to 60-second periods of total apnea) frequently occur. As the dying person loses the ability to swallow or cough, secretions pool in the back of the throat, producing a coarse, wet, gurgling sound known as the “death rattle.” Explain to family members that the death rattle is distressing to hear but does not cause pain or distress to the unconscious client.
    呼吸节律极度紊乱。频繁出现陈-施氏呼吸（潮式呼吸）：呼吸由浅慢逐渐变为深快，继之由深快转为浅慢，随后出现长达 10 至 60 秒的完全呼吸暂停，周而复始。由于患者彻底失去吞咽与自主咳嗽力量，气管与咽喉后壁积聚的大量分泌物在气流冲击下发出粗糙湿啰音与咕噜声，临床俗称“濒死喉鸣（Death rattle）”。支持人员应主动向守候床旁的家属耐心解释：这种痰鸣音听起来虽令人揪心，但处于深度昏迷的亲人自身并无任何窒息痛苦或呛咳折磨。
  • Loss of consciousness (意识进行性丧失): The client transitions from restlessness or confusion into somnolence, stupor, and deep coma. Pain sensation diminishes, but remember that hearing remains active until the final breath.
    患者由先前的躁动不安、谵妄恍惚，逐步转入嗜睡、昏睡，最终陷入深度昏迷状态。全身痛觉知觉基本丧失，但务必铭记听觉中枢将忠实坚守至呼吸停止的最后一刻。
p. 1124 Signs of Death, Rigor Mortis & Cultural Death Rituals (死亡确证体征、尸僵形成与多元文化丧葬习俗)

Signs of Death and Rigor Mortis (死亡确证体征与尸僵形成机制)

Clinical death occurs when vital organs cease functioning permanently. The signs of death include: absence of apical pulse, cessation of respirations, lack of blood pressure, pupils that are fixed and dilated (unresponsive to light), complete muscular flaccidity, and loss of all reflexes. Only a physician or an authorized registered nurse has the legal authority to pronounce death.

当维持生命的中枢与循环呼吸系统彻底永久停止运转时，标志着临床死亡的降临。确证死亡的体征包括：心尖搏动完全消失、自主呼吸彻底停止、血压测不到、双侧瞳孔散大固定（对光反射完全消失）、全身骨骼肌完全松弛瘫软，以及全部神经反射彻底引不出。法律严正规定：唯有执业医师或经法定授权的专职注册护士，才具备依法宣布患者死亡的法定公权力。

Rigor mortis is the stiffness or rigidity (rigor) of skeletal muscles that develops after death (mortis) due to the depletion of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) in muscle fibers. Rigor mortis typically begins 2 to 4 hours after death, reaches maximum stiffness at approximately 12 hours, and gradually resolves over 48 to 72 hours. Clinical rule: Postmortem care must be performed promptly after the pronouncement of death before rigor mortis develops, so that the body can be easily and naturally positioned in anatomical alignment.

尸僵（Rigor mortis）是指人死后由于肌肉纤维内三磷酸腺苷（ATP）彻底耗尽，导致全身骨骼肌发生化学性凝固与硬化强直（rigor）的病理生理现象。尸僵通常在死亡后 2 至 4 小时内开始自下颌、颈部逐渐蔓延，在死后约 12 小时达到全身最大僵硬度，并在 48 至 72 小时后随组织自溶逐步缓解软化。核心护理铁律：遗体善后护理必须在医生法定宣告死亡后争分夺秒立即展开，务必赶在全身尸僵形成锁死之前完成，以保证遗体能够轻柔顺畅地被摆放至解剖学自然安详对齐体位。

RESPECTING DIVERSITY: Death Rituals Across Cultures (尊重多元化：各文化与宗教丧葬神圣习俗)

  • Indigenous traditions (加拿大原住民传统): Smudging ceremonies (burning sacred sweetgrass, cedar, or sage) may be performed at the bedside. Songs, drum circles, and prayers guide the spirit on its journey to the spirit world.
  • Judaism (犹太教信仰): The dying person is never left alone (the practice of Shomerim, or guardians). Autopsy and embalming are generally forbidden. The body is wrapped in a plain white linen shroud and burial must take place as soon as possible, traditionally within 24 hours.
  • Islam (伊斯兰教传统): The dying person’s bed or face is positioned toward Mecca (the holy city). Following death, the body is ritualistically washed by family members of the same sex, perfumed, and wrapped in unstitched white cloth (Kafan). Burial takes place without cremation.
  • Hinduism (印度教信仰): Sacred water from the Ganges River may be placed on the lips. The body is typically cremated within 24 hours of death to liberate the soul.
  • Roman Catholicism (罗马天主教): The Sacrament of the Sick (formerly known as the Last Rites), involving prayers and anointing with holy oils, is administered by a priest prior to death.
p. 1125 Postmortem Care Principles & Preparing for Autopsy (遗体护理原则、尸检准备与机构感控规范)

POSTMORTEM CARE (遗体善后护理核心规程)

Postmortem care is the care of the body after (post) death (mortem). It begins immediately after the client is officially pronounced dead by a physician or authorized nurse, and before rigor mortis sets in. The goals of postmortem care are to maintain the body in good alignment, prevent discoloration of the face, maintain clean skin, and prepare the body for viewing by loved ones and subsequent transfer to a morgue or funeral home.

遗体善后护理（Postmortem care）是指在患者离世（mortem）之后（post）对其躯体所实施的尊严性专业照护。它必须在执业医师或经法定授权的护士正式宣布死亡后立即启动，且必须赶在全身尸僵形成之前完成。遗体护理的核心目标在于：保持躯体处于良好的解剖生理对齐状态、防止面部因血液坠积而发生发绀变色、保持体表皮肤清洁干燥无异味，并协助遗体呈现安详端庄的容貌以供至亲最后瞻仰告别，随后安全交接移送至太平间或殡仪馆。

CLINICAL ALERT: When an Autopsy is Ordered (临床红线警示：涉及尸体解剖时的铁律)

An autopsy (postmortem examination) is the surgical examination of the body and internal organs after death to determine the cause of death or evaluate disease extent. Autopsies may be requested by the family or mandated by law (the coroner or medical examiner) in cases of sudden, unexplained, traumatic, or suspicious deaths. If an autopsy is ordered: NEVER remove IV lines, endotracheal tubes, urinary catheters, wound drains, or surgical dressings! All medical devices must remain strictly in place in situ for forensic pathology examination.

尸体解剖（Autopsy）是指在人死后对手术切开躯体及内部器官进行的病理学病因剖析，旨在明确直接死因或评估恶性疾病浸润范畴。尸检可应直系家属书面申请发起，亦可在遭遇突发猝死、死因不明、外伤致死或涉及刑事法律纠纷时由验尸官（Coroner）依法强制下达。尸检核心操作红线：若已下达尸检指令，绝对严禁拔除或剪断体表的静脉输液管路、气管插管、导尿管、切口引流管或拆除手术敷料！所有管路器械必须原封不动完好保留在原位，以供法医病理学家进行司法取证检验。

p. 1126 Procedure: Assisting with Postmortem Care Part 1 (实操规程：协助遗体护理第 1 部分，图 47.1)

PROCEDURE: Assisting With Postmortem Care — Steps 1 to 18

实操规程：协助实施遗体善后护理 — 步骤 1 至 18
  1. Wash hands thoroughly. Put on clean gloves, gown, and mask if indicated by infection control policy. (严格洗手，遵照感控要求戴好医用手套与隔离服).
  2. Confirm that the client has been officially pronounced dead and check if an autopsy is ordered. (核实死亡已依法宣告，并再次确认是否需要执行司法尸检).
  3. Close the door and pull privacy curtains completely around the bed unit. Maintain a quiet, dignified environment. (关严病房房门，拉严床周隔帘，维持绝对肃穆尊严的环境).
  4. Place the bed in a comfortable working height and lower the head of the bed flat. (将病床升高至适宜的操作台高度，将床头摇平至完全水平位).
  5. Remove all extra pillows, blankets, and bedside clutter. (移走多余枕头、被褥及床旁杂物).
  6. Gently close the eyes by pulling the upper eyelid down over the cornea. If eyes remain open, place moist cotton balls gently over the lids for a few minutes. (轻拉上眼睑遮盖角膜使双眼自然闭合；若眼睑微张，可用湿棉球轻敷数分钟).
  7. Place the body in the supine position (FIGURE 47.1). Straighten the arms alongside the body with palms down, and straighten the legs. Place a small pillow under the head and shoulders. Rationale: Elevating the head and shoulders prevents venous blood from pooling in the face and neck, preventing facial discoloration and purple mottling. (将遗体安置于仰卧位，双臂双腿自然伸直，在头部和双肩下垫一小枕（图 47.1）。核心病理依据：垫高头肩部能有效防止静脉血液在重力作用下倒流淤积于头颈面部，避免面部迅速充血发绀紫变).
FIGURE 47.1: Place the body in the supine position, straighten the arms and legs, and place a small pillow under the head and shoulders to prevent facial discoloration.
图 47.1：将遗体置于仰卧平躺位，伸直双臂双腿，在头肩部下方轻垫小枕以防面部血液坠积青紫变色。
  • Dentures (义齿复位): Insert dentures if permitted by agency policy. Inserting dentures restores natural facial contours. Close the mouth gently. A rolled small towel placed under the chin supports the jaw in a closed position until rigor mortis sets in. (按机构规程将假牙及时戴回口腔以恢复饱满的面部天然轮廓；在下颌下垫卷轴小毛巾支撑托住下巴，防止下颌骨下垂，直至尸僵形成).
  • Tubes and catheters (管路处理): If no autopsy is ordered, the nurse removes or disconnects IV lines and catheters according to policy. (若无需尸检，由护士按规程撤除各类输液插管与引流管).
  • Skin cleansing (清洁体表皮肤): Wash soiled areas of the body with warm water and soap. Dry gently. (用温水和中性肥皂轻柔擦洗被排泄物或体液污染的皮肤区域并彻底擦干).
  • Absorbent pad (臀下防渗垫): Place a clean disposable absorbent pad under the buttocks to catch involuntary leakage of urine or stool as muscles completely relax. (在臀部下方垫放洁净的防渗看护垫，吸收括约肌完全松弛后渗漏的大小便).
  • Dressings (更换伤口敷料): Remove soiled dressings and replace with clean, fresh adhesive dressings. (撕下浸透渗血的旧敷料，换上洁净崭新的无菌敷料).
  • Family viewing preparation (瞻仰准备): Comb the hair neatly. Place a clean hospital gown or client’s clothing on the body. Cover the body up to the chin with a clean top sheet and light blanket. Tidy the room and adjust lighting softly before welcoming the family in. (将头发梳理整齐；换上洁净病服；用平整白床单与轻薄毛毯盖至下巴处，双手安详放于被外；迅速整理病房并调暗刺眼灯光，迎接家属入内瞻仰告别).
p. 1127 Procedure: Assisting with Postmortem Care Part 2 (实操规程：协助遗体护理第 2 部分，裹尸与物品交接)

PROCEDURE: Assisting With Postmortem Care — Steps 19 to 28 (After Family Viewing)

实操规程：遗体善后护理后续 — 步骤 19 至 28（家属瞻仰离室后）
  1. After the family has viewed the body and departed, wash hands and don fresh gloves. (在家属结束瞻仰告别并离开后，重新洗手并戴上洁净手套).
  2. Attach the first official identification tag to the client’s right big toe or ankle, ensuring the client’s full legal name, healthcare record number, date, time of death, and physician’s name are clearly printed. (在逝者右脚大脚趾或脚踝系上第一张官方身份识别标签卡，确认姓名、住院号、死亡确切时间准确无误).
  3. Place the body securely inside the postmortem shroud (body bag). Fasten the zipper or fold the plastic sheet neatly over the body. (将遗体平稳移入专科裹尸袋/防泄漏遗体保护袋内，顺畅拉合拉链或整齐折叠塑料裹单).
  4. Attach the second identification tag securely to the outside zipper pull or exterior of the shroud. (在裹尸袋外部拉链手柄或外层固定第二张身份识别标签).
  5. Gather all of the client's personal belongings, clothing, jewelry, and assistive devices. Place them in a designated belongings bag, label it with the client’s full name, and follow facility handover protocols for delivery to the family or funeral director. (全面收集逝者所有随身衣物、首饰与辅具，装入专用财产袋并张贴清晰姓名标签，按制度转交家属或殡葬专员并签字留存).
  6. Assist transfer personnel in moving the shrouded body onto the morgue transport cart with utmost care and dignity. (协助转运人员将遗体平稳轻柔平移至带有专用遮盖罩的冷藏推车上，全程维护肃穆尊严).
  7. Strip the bed linens, discard all disposables in biohazard waste, and wipe down the mattress and room surfaces with hospital-grade disinfectant. (拆卸床单被褥，按感控规范处置废弃耗材，用医院级消毒剂彻底擦拭床垫及病房全表面).
  8. Remove gloves, perform thorough hand hygiene, and record all postmortem care details, time of transfer, and belongings disposition in the clinical record. (脱去手套，严格流动水洗手，在临床交接记录中详实登记遗体护理时间、转运交接人及财物去向).
p. 1128 Chapter Review, Key Points, Critical Thinking & Practice Questions 1–8 (本章回顾、核心要点与真题测验 1–8)

CHAPTER REVIEW: KEY POINTS (本章总结与核心考点摘要)

  • As a support worker, you will be required to help meet the physical, social, emotional, intellectual, and spiritual needs of clients who are dying and to support their families through the process. (支持人员必须全方位满足临终者身心灵多重需求并向家属提供全程情感依靠).
  • Personality, culture, religion, age, and experience influence people’s attitudes toward death. (性格特质、文化传统、宗教信仰、生命年龄阶段及过往阅历共同决定了个体对死亡的态度).
  • As a support worker, you need to be aware of the cultural beliefs of your client and respect the death and dying rituals of the client and the family. (敏锐体察多元文化与宗教习俗，对各民族神圣的临终与丧葬仪式给予最高崇敬与恪守).
  • Palliative and hospice care aims to provide comfort and support to a dying client in the client’s home or in a facility. (安宁姑息疗护的崇高使命在于缓解身心折磨，无论在居家还是机构中均全力捍卫尊严).
  • As a support worker, you need to know your employer’s policies and your legal obligations regarding advance directives and “do not resuscitate” orders. (熟稔机构规章及法律底线，严格执行预先医疗指示与不复苏 DNR 医嘱要求).
  • The stages or phases of grief the client, the family, and you experience will vary in each case. (患者、家属及照护人员所体验的哀伤阶段与演变节奏因人而异，呈现高度非线性多样化).
  • Care of the body after death will depend on the cultural beliefs of your client. Follow your employer’s policy. (遗体善后护理深度融合感控规程与逝者特定文化习俗，严格遵照机构操作规范落实).
  • Funeral directors are trained to assist families through the necessary processes and paperwork. They can also refer families to other support services. (殡仪专员受过专业训练，协助家属办理法定离世手续并可引荐专业哀伤辅导资源).

CRITICAL THINKING IN PRACTICE: Case Study (临床批判性思维情境分析)

Scenario: Mr. Wills was admitted to a hospital, where he was diagnosed with terminal cancer. Within a week, he has appeared to lose consciousness, and he now is receiving end-of-life comfort care. Mrs. Wills, his wife of 67 years, is always at his side and does not seem to think he is dying. She talks about the next time they visit the grandchildren, and how they like to garden. Is it normal for family members to behave in this manner? As a bedside caregiver for Mr. Wills, what should you say when Mrs. Wills talks about their future plans?

案例情境：威尔斯先生因晚期恶性肿瘤入院，一周内意识逐渐模糊陷入深度昏迷，目前正在接受终末期舒适照护。与他相濡以沫 67 年的妻子威尔斯太太日夜守候在床旁，但她似乎完全不认为丈夫正在死去。她不断跟床旁的照护人员兴致勃勃地谈论下一次去探望孙辈的计划，以及他们俩春天如何在花园里修剪花木。家属表现出这种行为是否属于正常反应？作为威尔斯先生的床旁支持人员，当威尔斯太太兴致盎然地谈论未来的美好计划时，你应当如何妥善回应？

Clinical Reflection (权威临床剖析): Yes, this is completely normal and represents the denial stage of grief. Mrs. Wills’s mind is unconsciously protecting her from the unbearable shock of losing her partner of nearly seven decades. Support workers must never argue, harshly correct her, or force reality upon her (never say “He won’t live to see the garden”). Instead, listen with warmth, validate the deep love and beautiful memories shared (“It sounds like you two have shared a wonderful garden together”), provide quiet emotional presence, and inform the nurse so that grief support and social work can walk alongside Mrs. Wills gently.
