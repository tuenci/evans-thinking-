Chapter 6: Reader Reflection and Group Discussion

### 【中文】
（编者附。以下问题为编者所拟，非王明道原文，供个人默想或小组讨论。这些问题是邀请，不是考试，也不用来给人定性。）

读完这本书，最要紧的不是多明白一些道理，而是像作者在自序里所盼望的：拿这些道理来看自己的经验和生活。请安静、诚实地思考，不必急着给出"标准答案"。

### 【English】
(Editor's Note: The following reflective questions have been prepared by the editor rather than being part of Wang Mingdao's original text. They are offered for personal meditation or small group discussion. These questions are an invitation rather than an examination, and should never be used to label or categorize others.)

Having completed this book, the supreme priority is not merely to amass more theological doctrine in our heads, but—as the author earnestly prayed in his preface—to bring these living truths to bear upon our own concrete experience and daily walk. Please reflect quietly and honestly, without rushing to supply "standard textbook answers."

---

### 【中文】
1. "重生"和"决心做个好人"，是同一回事吗？

书中提到：有人把重生理解为悔改自新、决心做好人。书中指出，重生是神借着真道和圣灵，在人里面赐下新生命，就像人不能自己生自己。悔改信靠是人要做的条件，不等于重生本身。

请思考：你原来怎么理解"重生"？你的信主，更像"我决定改好"，还是"神给了我新生命"？

### 【English】
1. Are "Being Born Again" and "Resolving to Be a Good Person" the Same Thing?

As highlighted in the book: Some interpret regeneration merely as moral reformation or turning over a new leaf. The author clarifies that regeneration is God's sovereign act of imparting a brand-new spiritual life through His living Word and the Holy Spirit—just as no human being can give birth to himself. Heartfelt repentance and faith are the conditions required of man, but they are not identical with the act of regeneration itself.

For reflection: How did you previously understand "being born again"? Does your coming to faith resemble "my personal decision to reform myself," or "God imparting divine life within me"?

---

### 【中文】
2. 人的问题是"行为不好"，还是"生命有问题"？

书中提到：罪不是从环境来的，而是从人心里出来的。所以靠教育、道德、改良社会，都改不了根本，好比树根枯了，修剪树叶没有用。因此主说"你们必须重生"，是要给人新生命，而不是修补旧生命。

请思考：回想你曾用过什么办法改变自己（努力、立志、换环境、学做好人），结果如何？这说明了什么？

### 【English】
2. Is Humanity's Core Problem "Flawed Behavior" or "A Corrupted Nature"?

As highlighted in the book: Sin does not originate from outward societal environment, but gushes forth from the human heart. Therefore, education, moralism, and social reform can never cure the root dilemma—just as trimming withered leaves can never revive a dead root. When our Lord declared, "You must be born again," He came to bestow an entirely new life, not to patch up the ruined old nature.

For reflection: Looking back, what methods have you employed to improve yourself (willpower, resolutions, changing environments, striving to be virtuous), and what was the outcome? What does this reveal about your spiritual need?

---

### 【中文】
3. 四种"称为基督徒的人"，我属于哪一种？

书中提到（自序）：作者把称为基督徒的人分成四种。
① 明白重生的道理，也有重生的经历。
② 有过重生的经历，却不明白道理，所以常怀疑自己得救，缺少平安。
③ 明白道理，却没有真正经历，只有头脑知识，容易自满自欺。
④ 既不明白，也没有经历。

请思考：你觉得自己最像哪一种？你的把握来自"我知道这些道理"，还是"我真的仰望过基督"？
提醒：这一题只用于自我省察，不要用来给别人贴标签。

### 【English】
3. Among the Four Classes of "Professing Christians," Which One Am I?

As highlighted in the book (Author's Preface): The author classifies those bearing the name of Christian into four categories:
① Those who comprehend the doctrine of regeneration and have personally experienced it.
② Those who have experienced regeneration but do not clearly grasp the doctrine, hence frequently doubting their salvation and lacking lasting peace.
③ Those who understand the doctrine with their intellect but have never experienced the reality, possessing only head knowledge and lapsing into pride and self-deception.
④ Those who neither understand the doctrine nor possess the spiritual experience.

For reflection: Which category do you most closely resemble? Does your assurance rest on "I know these doctrines," or on "I have truly looked upon and trusted in Christ"?
Reminder: This inquiry is intended solely for personal soul-searching, never for labeling or judging fellow believers.

---

### 【中文】
4. 我当初为什么信主、受洗？

书中提到（第四章）：书中列举了常见的入教动机：为生计、为婚姻、欣赏耶稣的教训、觉得基督教是好宗教、盼望改良社会。作者指出，这些动机的共同点是都没有真正认识到：我是罪人、罪的结局是死、我无法自救、我需要信靠基督。

请思考：诚实回想，你的动机里有哪些？有没有一个时刻，你真切地意识到"我在神面前有罪，需要被拯救"？

### 【English】
4. What Was My Original Motive for Believing in Christ and Being Baptized?

As highlighted in the book (Chapter 4): The author enumerates prevalent motivations for joining the church: for employment, marriage, intellectual admiration of Jesus' ethical teachings, regarding Christianity as the finest world religion, or hoping to improve society. The author underscores that all these motives share a fatal commonality: none of them involves a true conviction of being a doomed sinner, facing eternal death, powerless to self-rescue, and desperately needing Christ.

For reflection: Looking back with radical honesty, were any of these mixed motives present in your journey? Was there ever a specific turning point when you were pierced with the conviction: "I am a guilty sinner before Holy God, and I must be saved"?

---

### 【中文】
5. 一个人是怎样得救重生的？（四个认识）

书中提到：民数记21章记载，以色列人被火蛇咬，神吩咐摩西做一条铜蛇挂在杆子上，被咬的人一望铜蛇就活了。主耶稣用这件事比喻：人仰望被钉十字架的基督，就得生命。书中指出，"仰望"包含四个认识：
① 知道自己犯了罪。
② 知道罪的结局是死。
③ 知道自己没有办法自救。
④ 相信神的话，仰望基督。

请思考：这四个认识中，哪一个对你最模糊，或者曾被跳过？你是否曾想"先把自己变好，再来到神面前"？

### 【English】
5. How Does a Person Obtain Salvation and the New Birth? (The Four Vital Recognitions)

As highlighted in the book: Numbers 21 records that when the Israelites were bitten by fiery serpents, God instructed Moses to make a bronze serpent and set it upon a pole; anyone who looked upon the bronze serpent lived. The Lord Jesus employed this historical event as a vivid type: whoever looks in faith upon the crucified Christ receives eternal life. The author explains that this saving "look" encompasses four vital recognitions:
① Knowing that one has committed sin against God.
② Knowing that the inevitable wages of sin is eternal death.
③ Knowing that one is utterly destitute of any ability to self-rescue.
④ Believing God's promise and casting one's helpless soul upon Christ.

For reflection: Among these four recognitions, which was the haziest for you, or perhaps bypassed altogether? Have you ever entertained the subtle snare of "trying to clean myself up first before coming to God"?

---

### 【中文】
6. 我的"得救确据"，建立在什么上？

书中提到：书中强调"一望就活"：人信靠的那一刻就得了生命，不需要等将来，也不是靠表现。确据来自主的应许（约三14–15："叫一切信祂的都得永生"）。许多真信的人因为没人讲清楚，常常怀疑自己得救。

请思考：你有平安的时候，和怀疑的时候，各是因为什么？是因为"我今天表现好或不好"，还是因为"基督已经为我做成了"？

### 【English】
6. Upon What Is My "Assurance of Salvation" Anchored?

As highlighted in the book: The text emphasizes "look and live immediately": the very moment a sinner looks in faith to Christ, he receives eternal life—he does not need to wait for the future, nor does it hinge upon his performance. Assurance flows unshakably from the Lord's promise (John 3:14–15: "that whoever believes in Him should have eternal life"). Many genuine believers continually doubt their salvation simply because no one had clearly expounded this scriptural ground to them.

For reflection: In seasons of peace and in moments of doubt, what drives your inner disposition? Is it based on "how well or poorly I performed today," or on "what Christ has once and for all accomplished for me"?

---

### 【中文】
7. 哪些变化能证明有新生命，哪些不能？

书中提到：书中把变化分成两类。
外在、可以学会的：会背圣经书卷、会祷告唱诗、按时聚会奉献、戒掉烟酒赌博。
内在、生命上的：从爱罪变成恨罪；从不愿被指出过错，变成愿意被劝责；从自私变成爱人；从贪恋世界，变成盼望天上的事；从厌恶圣经，变成喜爱圣经；从躲避圣徒，变成愿意亲近；从远离神，变成信靠神。
作者说，第一类不足以证明重生，第二类才是。同时他也说明，重生的人仍会有缺欠，要看的是方向性的改变，而不是完美。

请思考：对照内在的几项，哪些已经有变化的迹象，哪些几乎没有变化？你过去是否曾把外在习惯当成了确据？

### 【English】
7. Which Evidences Demonstrate Divine Life, and Which Do Not?

As highlighted in the book: The author divides spiritual changes into two distinct categories:
Outward, acquirable disciplines: Memorizing books of the Bible, reciting prayers, singing hymns, attending church, giving offerings, and giving up smoking, drinking, or gambling.
Inward, spiritual transformations: Turning from loving sin to abhorring it; turning from resenting reproof to welcoming godly rebuke; turning from selfish gain to loving others; turning from worldly lusts to yearning for heavenly realities; turning from disdaining the Scriptures to delighting in them; turning from shunning godly saints to cherishing their fellowship; turning from fleeing God to clinging to Him in trust.
The author warns that the former cannot prove regeneration; only the latter can. Yet he comforts us by noting that a regenerate believer still possesses flaws; what matters is the fundamental trajectory of life, not sinless perfection.

For reflection: Examining these inward marks, which ones show unmistakable signs of change, and which seem virtually unchanged? Have you in the past mistakenly mistaken outward habits for inward life?

---

### 【中文】
8. 我追求的方向对吗？

书中提到：一个人即使能背整本圣经、会讲道、做过长老牧师，如果里面没有生命的改变，仍不足为证。圣经说，"里面的人"需要被圣灵加力刚强起来（弗三16）。

请思考：你现在最投入的是什么（研读圣经知识、事奉表现、教会活动）？这些是在喂养"里面的人"（与主的关系、对罪的敏感、对人的爱），还是主要在装备外面的表现？

### 【English】
8. Am I Pursuing the Right Spiritual Trajectory?

As highlighted in the book: Even if an individual can recite the entire Bible from memory, preach eloquently, and serve as an elder or pastor, if there is no inward transformation of life, these outward achievements are completely void of spiritual proof. The Scriptures declare that the "inner man" must be strengthened with power through the Holy Spirit (Ephesians 3:16).

For reflection: Where is your greatest energy currently invested (theological study, ministry performance, church programs)? Are these endeavors nurturing your "inner man" (intimacy with Christ, sensitivity to sin, sacrificial love for brothers and sisters), or are they predominantly polishing outward religious appearance?

---

### 【中文】
9. 信主后又犯罪，是不是说明我没有得救？

书中提到：很多信徒因为再犯罪，就以为自己没有重生，或以为不能再被赦免。书中指出，约翰壹书一8–9和二1–2是写给已重生的信徒的：认罪就蒙赦免；若有人犯罪，在父那里有一位中保耶稣基督。使徒保罗在罗马书七章也说自己里面有新旧两种性情在交战，这恰恰说明里面有了新生命。

请思考：犯罪之后，你的第一反应是什么？是躲开神、陷入绝望，还是痛心，并回到主面前？

### 【English】
9. If I Sin After Believing, Does That Prove I Am Not Truly Saved?

As highlighted in the book: Many sincere believers, upon stumbling into sin after coming to faith, despairingly conclude that they were never born again, or that they can never be forgiven again. The author demonstrates that 1 John 1:8–9 and 2:1–2 were written explicitly to regenerate children of God: upon confession, forgiveness and cleansing are guaranteed; if anyone sins, we have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous. Furthermore, the Apostle Paul's warfare of two opposing natures in Romans 7 confirms that a new divine life is actively at war within.

For reflection: When you stumble into sin, what is your instinctive reaction? Do you flee from God in hopelessness, or do you grieve with godly sorrow and run straight back to His throne of grace?

---

### 【中文】
10. 恩典是"创伤药"，会不会变成犯罪的借口？

书中提到：作者打了一个比方：朋友送你一瓶创伤药，没有人因为有药，就故意割伤自己。对真正重生的人来说，犯罪比受伤更痛苦，所以不会故意拿恩典当放纵的理由。

请思考：你有没有想过"反正会被赦免"？这种想法和"恨恶罪"的新生命，冲突在哪里？

### 【English】
10. If Grace Is a "Healing Balm," Could It Become an Excuse for Presumptuous Sinning?

As highlighted in the book: The author presents a poignant parable: If a physician gifts you a potent bottle of healing ointment, no sane person would purposely slash his own face simply because the balm is in his pocket! To a truly regenerate person, stumbling into sin produces far deeper agony than physical lacerations; therefore, he will never cynically turn grace into a license for lawlessness.

For reflection: Have you ever harbored the reckless presumption: "It does not matter, for God will forgive me anyway"? How does such a mindset violently contradict the heartbeat of the new nature that genuinely abhors sin?
